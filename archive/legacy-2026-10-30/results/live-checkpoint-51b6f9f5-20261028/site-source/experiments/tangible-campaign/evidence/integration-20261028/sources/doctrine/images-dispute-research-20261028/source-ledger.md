# Primary-source ledger

All listed core passages were opened and read on 28 October 2026. Brief quotations below are the proposed quotation pool, not a request to reproduce long excerpts. Labels and invented cases must distinguish source speech, attributed paraphrase, and editorial inference.

## C1 — Calvin: images and instituted signs

[Institutes I.11, Henry Beveridge translation](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.iii.xii.html), especially §§9–13. Final Latin edition: 1559; English translation: 1845.

Calvin permits depictions of visible things and values historical scenes, but rejects devotional honour through images. In §13 he contrasts church pictures with baptism and the Supper as word-consecrated signs. His claims about the prevalence and history of idolatry are polemical claims, not archaeological findings.

Short quotation: **“sculpture and painting are gifts of God”** (§12; 7 words).

Do not infer that Calvin approves every image or church installation. Do not generalise his position to all Protestants.

## T1 — Trent: four categories, not one material mechanism

[Session XXV, decree on invocation, relics and images, Waterworth 1848](https://history.hanover.edu/texts/trent/ct25.html), printed pp.233–236; paragraphs beginning “The holy Synod enjoins”, “Also”, “Moreover”, and “And that these things”.

The council affirms invocation through Christ, honours saintly bodies, and directs image-honour to the represented person rather than resident divinity. It requires episcopal approval for recognition of new relics and miracles and for unusual images. Its statements about images must not erase its distinct claim that God bestows benefits through holy bodies.

Short quotation: **“our alone Redeemer and Saviour”** (5 words).

The decree condemns contrary positions; do not recast it as modern pluralism. The research does not verify any miracle or relic.

## C2 — Calvin: authenticity does not settle veneration

[Treatise on Relics, Krasinski translation, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/32136/pg32136-images.html). Original: 1543. Translator's preface dated 1854; this digitised volume identifies its title-page edition as 1870. Use the on-page printed pagination, not a guessed chapter number.

**Critical boundary:** Calvin's text starts at printed p.217, under the heading *Calvin's Treatise On Relics, With Notes By The Translator*. The extensive earlier history and later numbered notes are the translator's work. They are not Calvin's comments about Trent.

At pp.254–256 Calvin contrasts reports about John the Baptist's face and whole head. At pp.276–277 he acknowledges having no travelling commissioners. At p.278 he explicitly separates his prohibition from whether relics are genuine.

Short quotation: **“whether true or false”** (4 words, p.278).

If used, the two catalogue slips must begin “Calvin reports…”. Their juxtaposition challenges the claims if accurately described; it does not establish the objects' nature, provenance, present location, or fraud.

## C3 — Calvin: living intercession versus invoking the departed

[Institutes III.20, Beveridge translation](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.v.xxi.html), §§19–24, especially §§19 and 24.

He permits mutual prayer among the living under Christ's mediation. Even granting heavenly saints pray, he denies that invocation follows: his questions include scriptural warrant and whether they hear particular requests. His allegations about Catholics' motives remain his allegations.

Short quotation: **“ears capable of listening to the sounds of our voice”** (§24; 11 words).

Do not make his position “all intercession competes with Christ,” or claim he demonstrates that no saint ever hears anything.

## B1 — An adjacent biblical tension, not an instant solution

[1 Timothy 2:1–5, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Timothy+2%3A1-5&version=KJV%3BESV), using only the KJV text on this page.

Short quotation: **“one mediator between God and men”** (7 words, v.5). Verse 1 also calls for intercessions. This makes the “any other prayer must rival Christ” shortcut untenable for this text; it does not alone decide invocation of departed saints.

KJV is public domain in the United States; its UK Crown-rights status differs. Retain translation attribution and keep the quotation short, consistent with the existing candidate. Do not accidentally copy the page's ESV column.

## G1 — An optional older witness, not a proxy for Trent

[Gregory the Great, Register IX.105, to Serenus, James Barmby translation](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/360209105.htm).

Gregory praises opposition to adoration but criticises breaking the pictures, retaining their instructional role. This is a valuable third position. Calvin explicitly argues against Gregory's teaching-image defence in I.11.5. The short letter is not a complete history of Christian images, and Gregory must not be flattened into Trent's later formulation.
