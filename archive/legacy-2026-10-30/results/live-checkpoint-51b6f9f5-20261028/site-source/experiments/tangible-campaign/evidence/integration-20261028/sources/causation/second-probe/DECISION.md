# Second experiment: more than one fitting form

## Keep the first result

The fresh Workshop Bucket controller recognized the same object and the reversal from keeping water in to letting it through. It found that pleasant. This is a **positive conceptual result**, not a failed game. It also found the actions prescribed, wanted to try resealing, and did not want many more identical jobs. The parent verified exact source hashes and replay in `../fresh-probe-replay.json`.

Keep the first pilot and those findings unchanged. Its explicit-place-at-spring friction came from the logical interface, not the browser's drop resolution. A second runner must use `M.dropAction`. Do not add a casual inverse recipe: the reusable-discovery grammar does not yet give us a clean reversible finite-object model.

## Decision

**One small second probe is justified, but not a longer campaign.** Test whether a need with more than one materially distinct solution makes the end guide making, rather than dictate a single transformation. Comfortable, obvious crafting remains welcome; every move need not be a strategic dilemma.

The new probe has three differences from the first:

1. **One end admits different forms.** A closed crate and an open-weave basket both carry coarse stones. Neither is an arbitrarily invalid answer.
2. **A later end does not always require an upgrade.** For separating sand from gravel, an earlier basket already works. An earlier crate needs perforation—or the player can make a basket using their previously created rope.
3. **The consequence is physical.** Perforation removes the crate's ability to retain fine sand. Reusing the basket leaves the closed crate intact, if both were made. There is no score, forced quota, exclusive choice, or invented resource penalty. Making both is allowed.

The two fitting sieves are materially different: interwoven willow with gaps, versus boards deliberately pierced. One is not merely the other's renamed token. The two routes are short: three combinations if using a basket throughout; four if adapting the crate. Optional exploration can create both.

This is **not a balanced strategy problem**. The basket route is shorter, and preserving an intact crate can be better. We will not pretend that the extra drilling is a difficult trade-off merely because another recipe exists. Its research value is whether the player realizes that a new purpose can justify reuse, adaptation, or a different means—not “always combine again.”

The narrative consequence is the **condition of the workshop's remaining kit**, not a branching human fate: either the closed crate survives, or it has become a sieve. Both routes supply the same path makers. This does not yet test competing human priorities or claim a richer consequential story than the data actually implements.

## Why this concerns final cause—and the limit

The need is given before choosing means. It explains why an open arrangement is fitting for separation, while skilled work and material still explain how the artifact is produced. The practical end does not uniquely dictate a single design, and it is never a magical ingredient. This is a design inference from Aristotle's end-directed craft and conditional necessity, not an additional historical assertion. See [Physics II.2–3, II.9](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/physics.2.ii.html) and [Metaphysics VII.7](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.7.vii.html); the source discussion is in `../RETHINK.md`.

**Ordinary thoughtful crafting also does this.** Success cannot establish a uniquely Aristotelian mechanism or teach the whole four-causes account. If it is enjoyable but nobody reasons about suitability beyond following highlighted partners, keep the craft result and do not claim the philosophical target was achieved.

## Continuity and scope

The opening makes a rope coil and at least one carrier. Every route to the second job requires a carrier bound with that earlier-created rope; the ordinary routes directly reuse the carrier delivered in chapter one. Even making a different carrier later requires the earlier rope, not just old raw stock. A source-aware dependency test checks both direct reuse and this fallback.

The shared rope coil contains enough bindings for both carriers; stock use is explicit, not a duplicated finite component. Crafting is compressed fiction, not a history claim or construction lesson. Carrying a carrier to the quarry stands for the short collection trip; the next scene provides the gravel that trip brought back. Separation is compressed into combining gravel with the appropriate sieve. There is no simulated grain size, physics, labor time, or dangerous real-world instruction.

No new runtime or UI mechanics. No edits to the first probe, recovered app, live Site, or remote repository.

## What would justify continuing

- Positive: the player chooses or recognizes a different fitting form; understands why an old basket does not need “upgrading”; enjoys at least some ordinary making; or deliberately preserves the closed crate rather than blindly drilling it.
- Mixed: pleasant construction, but still only a visible recipe chain. Preserve that finding; do not multiply jobs.
- Negative: the rope step feels like bureaucracy, the quarry trip feels like parking, or alternatives feel nominal. Remove/rethink that model rather than adding explanatory text.

One player who sees only one branch may give inconclusive evidence. Do not turn absence of comparison into success. No human enjoyment claim follows from a controller session.
