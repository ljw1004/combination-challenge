# Images, saints and relics: a bounded continuation concept

Research/design only · 28 October 2026 · **not an approved extension or playable candidate**

## Useful finding and recommendation

The promising new question is **who authorised this use of this object?** The ten existing desks have made material mediation intelligible. They have not made every material devotional practice interchangeable. A second, distinct question is **would establishing an object's identity settle the permitted use?**

Recommend a small two-desk concept, Calvin followed by Trent, built around cases rather than a vocabulary ladder. The second desk should change the disposition of the same cases, not simply add the opposite slogan. Do not implement merely to increase chapter count. If a human reader finds the current ending sufficient, these notes can remain unused.

The research covers invocation of saints as well, but **do not squeeze all three disputes into two short desks**. The saint-prayer case below is a substitution for the relic branch, not an automatic extra branch.

## Why this could add something distinct

- An inherited answer becomes useful but insufficient: Aquinas's account explains dependent material agency; it does not establish a warrant for an arbitrary new devotional object.
- A remembered person, an honoured image, an actual bodily remain, and an instituted sacrament require different arguments. This is a meaningful limit on the earlier keepsake comparison, not a decorative callback.
- The player can reach a concrete result such as **hold this new claim for examination**, rather than only producing a longer doctrinal label.
- A genuine relic would not answer Calvin's objection. Conversely, an unverified relic does not make Trent reject relic veneration as a class. These are different questions, not symmetrical teams accumulating points.

## Narrative and chronology

**Desk A — What belongs in this church?**

Speaker: *Calvin's manuscripts · Institutes, 1559; his 1543 relic treatise reopened.*

Goal: prepare an annotated case-file separating a craft, a devotional gesture, and a contested relic claim. This is an editorial reconstruction, not an order issued by Calvin to a documented congregation.

**Desk B — What should the bishop permit?**

Speaker: *Trent · Session XXV, 3–4 December 1563; earlier case-file reopened.*

Goal: review those cases under the council's distinctions and recognition procedure. The player must preserve the unresolved dispute while making a practical decision about an unsupported new claim. The fictional clerk and sample petition are explicitly editorial; the decree is historical.

The sequence follows 1547 → 1559 → 1563. The 1543 treatise is identified as an older leaf. If Gregory's letter is used, label it an older source reopened, not a later Catholic response to Calvin. Neither desk is a transcript of a meeting between the writers.

## Reasons not to ship this yet

1. **The carry-in recipes are still comparisons.** They risk becoming the familiar pattern “old distinction + new text = new distinction.” Their value must be tested by whether readers actually change how they handle the particular panel or relic, not by a dependency count.
2. **Visible source leaves can disclose much of an answer.** Keep normal highlighting. Do not create difficulty by hiding source text, withholding combinations, or turning labels cryptic. The interest should be in consequences and changed applications.
3. **A practical disposition is not a historical verdict.** “Hold for examination” is an application to a fictional newly submitted object, not evidence that Trent rejected a named relic. No new laboratory result or bishop's ruling may be invented.
4. **The two-decision ending could still be predictable.** A normal-highlight human read should test this before any third branch or new chapter is added. The v10 simulated reader is evidence to consider, not a numerical optimisation target.
5. **Saint invocation deserves space.** If that is the user's priority, substitute its branch; do not make one easy “one mediator + saints” combination purport to resolve the doctrine.
