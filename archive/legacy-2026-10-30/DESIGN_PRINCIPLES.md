# Combination games: design principles

These are the user's design decisions, accumulated during playtests. This is the central authoring reference for existing games and new work, not text to display to players. Read it before changing content, runtime behaviour or validation.

## Why these rules matter

The audience is a player making sense of a world, not an author checking off a recipe graph. A consistent conceptual structure lets the player predict how things will behave, become better informed, and make meaningful decisions: that is the intended source of **fun**. Preserve the pleasure of recognising, trying and discovering; satisfying this document mechanically is not sufficient.

The user's latest explicit direction controls when older designs conflict. Direct human enjoyment is stronger evidence than an AI player's report of boredom; use simulated trials to expose concrete confusion or inconsistency, not to overrule the human's enjoyed experience or manufacture difficulty by obscurity. Recipe totals, completion and test passes are not fun metrics.

**Design direction is not implementation status.** These principles govern new work; preserved versions retain their original behavior and save identities. The location-artwork successor implements fixed panels, one authored cue per unfinished chapter, and distinct place/chapter data for seven new versions. Logical route and synthetic controller evidence is not browser, native touch, human comprehension or enjoyment evidence. See [the implementation record](docs/persistent-locations-and-artwork.md). Older audits and releases remain evidence of their own versions, not claims that they already implemented this direction. The [preserved live61 checkpoint](docs/live-checkpoint-61eaca8a-20261028.md) and Iron quarry successor at `f780990cdff4557aeb46dcbf7d0d622e9f6310e2` remain historical records. At private root, use `campaign-start.html`/`campaign.html`; the unrelated root app is preserved.

## The working set is a clue, not just a space budget

Latest clarification: 29 October 2026.

**The smaller working set does more than save space: it gives the player a clue about which things matter.** The current location's visible things should suggest relevant ingredients and meaningful options for its planned work. Carrying unnecessary old objects weakens that guidance and makes the puzzle harder to read, even when everything fits on screen.

- Author location boundaries and state-sensitive carry together to curate a manageable, relevant working set, generally around **5–10 things**. This is a design target, not a universal hard cap or a claim that every current scene already meets it. Preserve worthwhile optional combinations and room for discovery rather than showing only the one required pair.
- Prefer the earlier, successful small-scene cadence where it served the puzzle. When work needs separation, establish genuinely different places or clearly explained later visits; do not invent an arbitrary chapter reset or disguise a supply drip as travel. Supply all ordinary stock belonging to that actual place.
- Bring useful existing things for the destination visit's planned work, considering what the player has already made. Leave unrelated creations tangible in their earlier scrollback locations, accessible for deliberate reuse. Do not delete, invisibly shelve or retroactively consume leftovers to make a smaller board.
- Review both arrival and peak working sets, including alternative preparation orders. Ask whether the visible options still communicate what matters—not merely whether the route completes or the objects fit. Same-location chapter changes still preserve the settled board and cannot perform cleanup.

## Place coherence and the working surface

Latest clarification: 29 October 2026, morning playtest.

- A location must make sense as a whole: actual background, indoor/outdoor setting, hotspots, new supplies and carried objects. A text label must not explain away an exterior ice deposit or mast pictured inside a room. Check physical access, scale and story purpose together.
- Fixed structures stay at their actual place. A change of worktable must not pack a whole greenhouse, refuge or kiln as if it were a portable supply. If a later task concerns a fixed structure, author a truthful way to work there or use an actual portable subsystem; do not solve it by renaming the same building.
- Keep hash-bound metadata describing the VERIFIED image, separately from its intended prompt: indoor/outdoor setting, viewpoint, prominent features and rough positions, meaningful absences and ambiguities. Reuse this knowledge for unchanged art; re-inspect when its hash changes or a mismatch is suspected. Correct clearly wrong backgrounds now; the broader furniture/hotspot-art overhaul remains a separate task.
- On first entry, aim the movable items' visual center of weight around one-third of the playable screen height. Preserve the player's relative arrangement, compress genuine vertical whitespace proportionately as needed, and put new arrivals beneath carried things. Stay within bounds and do not create new overlaps. This is a feasible layout aim, not permission to shuffle objects on every creation, same-place chapter, resize or reload.
- Hotspots are NOT part of that centering rule. Place them generally lower, beneath the movable working set/new-arrival division, anticipating identifiable furniture or features in future art. Keep them clearly labelled/highlighted and fixed during play. “Lower” does not require offscreen scrolling. Explicit artwork anchors constrain placement.
- Richer backgrounds are welcome. Partner highlights must remain unmistakable over them, using strong contrasting shape/outline rather than a subtle color change alone. Apply this equally to tiles and hotspots.
- Dense Ideas scenes need the same focus discipline as physical stories. A new location may be a neighboring table within the same era; it need not always be a new city or date. A later evening or next morning at the desk can also be a truthful fresh work session: say why the desk is clear, preserve the earlier panel and its tangible creations, and carry the relevant work forward. Do not invent a city, date or room for each tiny step, or use an unannounced same-panel reset. Preserve meaningful competing ideas and earlier-created connections while reducing simultaneous options.
- A map or survey must answer a story question and help someone act. Expeditions can begin with plausible existing regional/orbital maps. Local observations should resolve a meaningful uncertainty (such as access, terrain or resources), not manufacture successive generic map categories. Research what the equipment can establish and keep the result's limits honest.

## Locations, hotspots and chapters have different jobs

Latest clarification: 28 October 2026.

- A **location** is the whole continuing place: its persistent background, arrangement of things and ordinary supplies. It can contain several chapters. On arrival, expose all plausible authored stock that belongs there, not just the few bespoke ingredients needed for the next task. A kitchen has its ordinary ingredients and equipment from the get-go. A substantial facility may have genuinely distinct, clearly established workrooms; do not invent a new room merely to disguise a chapter-by-chapter supply drip.
- A **hotspot** is a particular interactive spot within a location: for example a tray, forge or signal mast. It may be a recipe operand, a catalyst setting or a placement-goal destination. A hotspot is not the whole location and does not itself define a chapter or a journey.
- A **chapter** is a story beat: a new purpose, radio contact, dialogue or development, not necessarily a journey. Every chapter actually entered with unfinished work should have a brief story-beat popup, such as a radio exchange, dialogue or narrative card. Arrival at a new location will often have one too; an arrival and chapter beat can share the same introduction rather than duplicate it. A popup is not a demand to read the prose to understand the mechanics.
- **Exception: chapters already completed on entry retain the explicit skip/bridge policy below.** Do not restore a popup or a task for every skipped chapter. Important authored bridges join the next unfinished beat or ending; a partially complete chapter still has a real introduction and remaining task.
- A new chapter in the **same location** keeps every surviving item and the player's layout. The chapter boundary must never automatically carry/import earlier-location items, prune items, rearrange the work surface or re-grant existing stock. It does not start a fresh board or pretend to travel.
- **Only an actual location move triggers carry.** Here, the destination's **current scene means the actual location visit's planned work across all its chapters**, not merely its first chapter or an arbitrary chapter boundary. On arrival, bring existing things useful across that planned work, preserving the carried group's arrangement; do not silently import missing old items when a later same-place chapter begins. Leave other surviving things physically in their earlier locations, accessible for later reuse; do not haul the entire future campaign catalogue forward or hide leftovers. Do not carry an existing placement witness away merely to manufacture an unfinished task.
- A genuine new delivery or discovery at the same place should be rare and visibly explained by a minimal discovery/delivery popup. It is not an excuse for an arbitrary new pile each chapter. Actual fresh items use the new-items policy below; old-location spillover and previously existing objects are not new grants.
- **Artwork is now authorized:** use vivid, clearly visible persistent background art for each setting, shared across its chapters; preserve contrast with local backing behind labels and controls. Decorative scenery must not imply inventory or completed work that is not present. Movable objects, labels and tasks remain independently legible.
- **Fixed phone-like panels:** a location has a bounded 390 × 844 logical reference area, responsive down to narrow phones and compact on desktop. It never grows to fit more objects. Previously visited panels stay visible above it, with vertical scrollback and deliberate dragging between discovered locations. Storage ownership is separate from the story's active location; moving an object backward does not restart travel, grant stock or change its provenance.
- Created objects may overlap reasonably; each must remain reachable for dragging or double-tap explanation. Normal authored play should not demand housekeeping. Arrival-stock packing must not create new overlaps by squeezing a carried arrangement; removing genuine empty gaps and moving rigid clusters on first arrival is different from rearranging residents. Source cards retain their full readable size.
- A genuine commercial kitchen station (pastry bench, hot line, confectionery counter) may be a distinct location. An explicit later shift can also have a new panel in the same physical setting: explain the time change and cleared workspace, retain the earlier panel, and bring actual prepared food forward. Never use an arbitrary chapter reset as a disguised cleanup.
- Mars remains a single-narrator survival journey. Use minimal necessary people and radio/contact explanation to establish who is speaking and what matters; do not force alternating rover/farming narrators or two threads into every game.

## A small, predictable interaction language

- The core is **binary A + B = C**, with recognisable, authored, named results. Do not turn arbitrary pairs into unnamed compound bags or secretly require a third ingredient. Fitting an actual component into a named construction is the explicit construction case, not generic accumulation.
- The two player-facing roles are **things** and **hotspots**: ordinary rounded thing tiles and distinct hotspot frames. Component items remain things and stay visibly identifiable when fitted. A named hotspot within a location is not itself an instruction to change chapters or travel.
- Keep the three hotspot uses distinct: **catalyst**, where the receiving thing B's actual placement supplies context for A+B; **operand**, where A+hotspot creates a named portable C; and **placement**, where moving a thing onto a hotspot tests its own goal eligibility. Neither context nor placement accumulates prior occupants' properties. No sticky ingredients, cumulative hotspot recipe or invented context override.
- Things remain movable. Moving a placed witness away changes current goal truth; historical achievement is separate. Context can make the two drag directions differ, so communicate the actual legal receiving direction rather than pretending both are legal.
- Normal partner highlighting is a core affordance: show it transiently on press and throughout a thing-drag, including hotspot operands and relevant current-goal things. Hotspot frames are fixed targets, not draggable things. Show legal existing partners and valid drops, not future output-name previews. Highlights must not disappear merely because a source is gold or a target is a hotspot; they must not promise an illegal direction or an already-existing result.
- Never create another C when that output already exists, including in an earlier location or fitted construction. Moving, fitting or incorporating an output is not permission to reconstruct a duplicate. Alternative routes should converge on existing output identities where appropriate; distinct real objects in different kitchens can still share a familiar label.
- After a successful creation, surviving dragged A returns to its original position; B stays put. New C appears close to the receiving B, or **inside the hotspot target** when the hotspot is the operand, and remains portable rather than permanently attached. The explicit transformation and installation rules below take precedence over this ordinary birth rule. A same-token drop is a no-op/rejection, never deletion.

## Understand from the task and visible things

Clear, simple object names are a mandatory baseline, not an optional polish pass. Assume the player may skip chapter prose, cache narrative and double-tap explanations. The task list, object/hotspot names, text printed on tiles, visible fitted components and normal interaction feedback must make the action intelligible. A short quotation already printed on a tile counts; a crucial premise hidden behind an explanation does not.

- Ask of every required action: **Does this make sense from the task and the things the player can see?** Put a necessary premise on the surface through a truthful short name, visible component, diagram, observed result or meaningful state change. Do not repair a missing apparatus, ingredient, evidential premise, purpose or order constraint by lengthening prose.
- Review recipes **forwards**, not only backwards: would the visible things and task naturally suggest trying this pair, and is its result recognisable and useful for a purpose the player can understand? A technically possible after-the-fact story does not make a combination intuitive. For example, Footbridge + Paper → Crossing map is not justified merely by saying that paper can record a bridge crossing: why would the player want or need that map? This is not a ban on maps or observational recipes; give them a genuine, visible job.
- Accept plausible alternative tools, evidence and preparation orders that genuinely reach the result. A stronger compatible tool should not arbitrarily lose its simpler form's useful capability; independent preparations should not have a secret sequence. Preserve real ingredient, causal and evidential distinctions: related names alone do not make a valid recipe.
- Keep physical identity and prerequisites consistent across alternatives: no duplicated trays, missing covers, vanished samples or finished food without its ingredients. Use bounded states or existing construction components rather than every imaginable permutation.
- Chapter prose, explanations and wonders use plain, direct English. No mysterious, quirky or cryptic clue voice: understanding the wording is not a game challenge. Explain unfamiliar ideas and necessary qualifications without jargon; the interesting work is combining things. Do not use a long explanation to rescue an irrational action. Keep research citations and source apparatus outside player-facing text; an essential distinction between named thinkers or traditions belongs in the ordinary explanation itself.

## Play without administration

- Chapter introductions should state the new purpose or information directly. Avoid routine “I will…” or “I need to…” narration, and do not retell events already shown through play. Let combinations demonstrate continuity.
- Teach through what objects do and how they change. Remove explanatory gameplay toasts, including “Moved”, and temporary recipe instructions. Do not make reading instructions necessary for ordinary interactions.
- Keep the scene open. Put terse objectives at the bottom right, without an “Objectives” heading or selected state. They sit visually and interactively below movable tiles: an overlapping tile remains draggable. Clicking an unobstructed objective opens the current location/chapter popup. Keep the bare bug control, accessible names and adequate touch targets.
- Ordinary story cards contain clear, simple prose, with no chapter title, duplicated speakers, decorative subtitles or goal checklist. A meaningful historical place/era may appear as an italic first line, for example a genuinely appropriate Athens date. An adjacent worktable or arbitrary riverside view needs no such header. Keep essential chronology and perspective intelligible without citation apparatus.
- Do not advance automatically when objectives finish. Leave the created result available to read and let any earned wonder appear. Show a conventional animated down-arrow button at the bottom for a new location, or a right-arrow button for a same-location chapter or return to an already known place. Name a return accurately for assistive technology and scroll to its actual destination; do not imply downward travel when returning upward. Activating it advances through the existing skip/bridge rules, scrolls to the actual next location where appropriate, and opens the next real popup. Respect reduced motion and do not add a completed-chapter recap.
- There is no selected-tile concept and no click-to-combine mode. Drag things to move/combine them. Double-tap (or desktop double-click) opens the associated wonder again, or an ordinary explanation/quotation for an item without a wonder. Primitive items may need explanations but do not gain fake creation awards. Reading/replay preserves scroll and progress and does not earn or acknowledge a reward. Keep double-tap distinct from drag and accidental combination.
- A key quotation can be the item itself: 5–12 words need not collapse into an icon. All long labels—including observations and cases—need matching line space in rendering and packing. Explanations must explain the object or idea in ordinary language, not expose authoring instructions, recipes, research citations or dense source notes. Preserve genuine quotations faithfully and explain their meaning plainly.
- Keep the work surface compact and responsive, with readable labels and usable touch targets. Fit the available width and use vertical scrolling rather than a horizontally sprawling board. Compress genuinely empty space only when necessary; scroll between fixed locations, not into an endlessly taller location. Do not hide objects, scramble a settled arrangement or claim phone usability from coordinate checks alone.
- Keep full-height location art visibly present rather than washing it away. Do not trade readable labels, usable controls or tangible inventory for decorative detail.

## Wonder screens explain and celebrate

- When converting old informational text to Markdown's unified explanation/wonder, review each promotion individually. A confusing intermediate step can deserve an immediate explanation even if a later wonder revisits it; Divinity's intermediate popups are intentional. An obvious preparation such as Salt-preserved fish needs neither a wonder nor an informational panel. Review the newly promoted texts rather than reopening all existing wonders, and leave uncertain cases intact for author judgment. Former reader-only status identifies a timing change, not whether the popup is worthwhile.
- Chapters carry the story; wonders explain or celebrate meaningful advances. Frequency is story-specific. In Ideas, deliberately try wonders for most substantial abstract advances (perhaps 80% or more), using them to explain what the card means. Consider the same approach where useful in Divinity. Do not turn mundane procedural combinations into awards just to hit a quota.
- In physical stories, choose wonders for significance and their role in opening combinations: Plant is a strong Elements candidate; merely making juice is not. Give them a coherent rhythm rather than one unexplained surprise. Cooking need not have wonders. Do not use outdated route counts or a universal percentage as a design rule.
- Celebrate an actually achieved invention. An unfinished Rover shell is not a working Rover; required fitted parts and the authored capability must be present before celebrating it.
- Wonder text is clear, plain and concise. Explain what the new thing makes possible or understandable, and why that matters. A marked rain collector, for example, makes weather observations comparable over time; its wonder should convey that significance rather than read as operating instructions. A gentle indication of real future possibilities is welcome, but no cryptic hints, recipe sequence, technical manual, unlimited-resource promise or unearned capability claim.
- Let the creation remain tangible, then show its wonder with a clear dismissal. Do not force waiting for animation. After dismissal, the explicit chapter arrow remains available; do not silently advance. Double-tap can revisit an applicable wonder without changing achievement/acknowledgment state.
- Keep mundane procedural combinations uncelebrated and never replay an old-save backlog as newly earned. A dismissed celebration happens once per run. Pending rewards and acknowledgement survive saving; Undo removes unearned pending events but does not repeat a dismissed triumph. Reset starts a fresh run; debug checkpoints identify their replayed achievements as historical.

## Explanations teach; their item names are captions

Latest clarification: 29 October 2026, extended morning playtest; attribution clarified 30 October 2026 during Markdown review.

- An educational wonder must make sense before the reader sees its item name. Write a self-contained explanation of the idea, discovery or useful distinction in ordinary English, including the subject and the necessary context. Do not rely on a heading to supply the missing premise, offer only a teasing slogan, or make the player decode the prose.
- Present that body first, then the actual current item name on a separate italic line below it. The caption identifies what was made; it is not a rhetorical title above the explanation. Ordinary double-tap explanations follow the same body-first, italic-name pattern. Keep physical component-led celebrations and genuinely useful illustrations where appropriate rather than flattening all wonders into one format.
- Essential differences among thinkers or traditions still matter, but bibliography, source chrome and repeated attribution labels are not gameplay chores. Keep research provenance intact in source/evidence, preserve quotations accurately, and explain an essential attribution naturally in the body when it changes the claim.
- Contextual short labels should follow the player's accumulated story context. In the opening Augustine chapters, an established Augustine context can remove repetitive Augustine prefixes. Once later chapters introduce other thinkers, restore the attribution everywhere, including in earlier scrollback: the player now has more ideas in mind and needs to recognise whose claim they are revisiting. In Markdown, use the chapter-based rename rule; scrolling back does not revert the name. This supersedes the earlier location-scoped naming direction. Do not strip Case: or Paul: distinctions. Item identity, recipes and permanent explanations stay the same.

## Objects retain identity

Latest clarification: 29 October 2026, Markdown authoring review. **Fates should fit the action, and goals should explain the useful work.** Cooking dough into pastry, roux into sauce and cooked cauliflower into a dish are transformations. This supersedes the earlier choice to leave those batches permanently just to preserve optional dishes. Use meaningful goal alternatives for different acceptable dishes, or a conjunction when both preparations have a purpose. The existing goal-protection rule can keep a batch available while another required preparation still needs it. Cut an unhelpful optional branch instead of inventing a goal solely to keep its recipe. This guides current Markdown authoring; the preserved runtime and its earlier evidence are unchanged.

Unexplained differences in fate can break the player's mental model of an ingredient. A visible difference in the item's role or an unfinished preparation can explain why some of it stays. The Markdown spec proposes a warning for mixed retention declarations, including implicit `preserve` defaults and slot consumption; review both the physical action and the goals, rather than automatically preserving everything. This warning is not implemented. In Markdown, write `[consume]`, `[transform]` or `[preserve]` beside the reagent, as in `A [transform] + B [preserve] -> C`. Omit the default `[preserve]` annotation unless the item is a reagent in only one composition in the story, when the explicit-fate lint requires it.

- Each recipe explicitly declares every object input as `transform`, `incorporate`, or `retain`, with a reason. An upgrade or physical incorporation consumes its original at the event when no independent use needs an outside copy; reusable tools and supplies remain when another use still needs them. This is not a finite-stock simulation or a blanket everything-stays rule. Exhausting an input's future uses is a warning to review its lifecycle, never permission to delete it automatically.
- Apply authored removal only at the actual combining or installing event. If another use or objective requires an outside copy/supply to remain then, it stays tangible until a later action explicitly transforms or incorporates it. Do not retroactively absorb it into an old owner when an unrelated action, alternative recipe, chapter advance or reload exhausts its remaining uses. Leave finished leftovers in their locations; scene-local carry controls clutter.
- Preserve a declared construction's fitted contents visibly at the combining/installing event even when its outside source copy remains useful. The new assembly receives that event's actual construction snapshot, not later additions to the separate copy. Preserving the new assembly's visible parts does not move or alter the retained source copy.
- A transformation that retires its primary input replaces it at exactly that object's position, with a brief creation animation, in either drag direction. If the primary source survives for an independent use, the additional result is born near the receiver instead; it is not a replacement. Contextual hotspot results belong at their actual destination.
- Context matters: putting material into a seed tray should leave the result at the tray. A reusable tool used there should remain a tool. Do not apply a blanket “everything stays” rule.
- Soup pouch → warmed soup is one evolving object. Its empty earlier state should not remain as a useless duplicate. Heater, references and other reusable equipment survive. Shared supplies can remain while another authored branch needs them.
- Adding to a fitted rover should keep the same recognisable rover and its fitted parts. Prefer installation in its existing slots. Never collapse a compound into an apparently simple object. When joining assemblies or genuinely changing an assembly's identity, explicitly preserve and display its existing components through subsequent transformations. A change of name is not a disassembly.
- In Markdown constructions, allow independent parts to be fitted in any order. Require the working configuration in the goal instead of imposing an unnecessary installation sequence. A rover's navigation component should be a physical navchip, not an abstract route; its preparation should update existing hardware.
- Any genuine removal/disassembly needs a deliberately authored action and understandable visible result. Do not hide lost parts in bookkeeping or explain them away in a paragraph.
- A terminal creation does not disappear after its animation, a timer or its last use. Every surviving object stays tangible; no hidden shelf, presentation archive or invisible inventory solves clutter. Scene-local carry controls what travels, not what exists.

## Goals express the next useful action

- In Markdown authoring, give a goal a useful purpose and accept plausible alternative results: "Cook a cauliflower dish" can accept either gratin or buttered cauliflower. Alternatives should give the player more ways to discover a satisfactory answer. Require both outputs only when both have a purpose, such as plain pastry for a later preparation and cheese straws for the current meal. Optional dead-end products must earn the attention they ask for; do not make every existing recipe required merely to preserve it.
- Aim for no more than two active goals. Use `next:` chains to stage required work when helpful; their later goals still protect ingredients and do not lock recipes against early discovery.
- Gold means **a currently fulfilled objective of the current chapter**, not a historical achievement, new delivery or permanently prestigious object. A placement objective gilds its actual correctly placed witness and hotspot; moving it away removes current satisfaction. Advancing clears prior-chapter gold, not the surviving objects. Avoid making a gold object a reagent for a subsequent goal in the same challenge.
- Represent intermediate goals explicitly and reveal their successor after completion: “Bake bread” becomes “Pack a picnic basket”. Remove the completed intermediate from the visible list, and do not permanently gild its object.
- Completing an intermediate remains recorded even after it is incorporated. Undo restores both objects and goal progress.
- Phrase Ideas and Divinity objectives as concise, open-ended questions about a useful purpose or uncertainty. Invite the player to work out an answer rather than naming the required output in a disguised “make X” instruction. Keep the wording clear, not cryptic; keep the actual authored goal identity and acceptance conditions precise. Do not turn equipment parking, every optional recipe or a replacement checklist into the question.
- An enhanced thing satisfies the function it genuinely retains: an upgraded radio or growing shelter must not fail merely because its name changed. Preserve real functional distinctions; a radio's little supply is not automatically a rover's traction power. No exact-name barrier should manufacture extra work.

## Chapters belong to a continuing story

- Every non-opening chapter must meaningfully use at least one, preferably several, objects or ideas created in an earlier chapter. An item merely remaining in inventory, or an old raw supply, does not satisfy this.
- The source chapter need not be the immediately preceding one. Earlier creations may return after a gap. Evaluate competing perspectives selectively, especially for Doctrine; do not force two threads into every journey.
- Carry useful existing creations forward on actual travel, under the location/chapter distinction above; do not conjure missing prerequisites or replace continuity with an explanatory paragraph. Players should recognise what they made.
- Distinguish actual new supplies spatially: existing objects above a dotted “new items” line, actual deliveries below. Do not use New/Carried labels or divide the screen into arbitrary halves. Compact genuine empty vertical gaps only when needed, without introducing new overlaps; fixed locations remain bounded. The first intentional committed crossing or successful cross-group combination removes the line; cancellation, rejection, reading, resizing and reload do not. Undo restores the earlier split. The lower group contains only this arrival's actual fresh grants, never old-location spillover or existing things reclassified as new. Crossing is scoped to those groups in that location. With no fresh delivery, do not add a divider or rearrange settled objects; old saves are not retroactively relaid out.
- The food journey needs the same continuity. Preserve the value of older practices rather than portraying every later technique as simply superior.
- A kitchen or cooking station should support several services, with ordinary ingredients and tools introduced when its stores become available, not a fresh box of bespoke materials for each chapter. Keep the active work surface manageable; a substantial facility may have small, clearly established workrooms. New chapters on the same work surface must not repack or relocate settled objects.
- Familiar equipment labels may repeat across distinct kitchens. Do not force uniqueness into names such as “Salad oven”. Let the story and physical identities distinguish facilities.
- Let the cooking journey earn its conclusion through useful earlier preparations and a concrete act of feeding people. Do not force a branded destination or invent industrial props when the actual journey remains grounded in cooking.

## Chapters react to work already done

- Chapter divisions do not invalidate earlier invention. A chapter whose goals are all already fulfilled on entry disappears, including its objectives and routine acknowledgement. Do not lock recipes or add chores to prevent the player racing ahead.
- Author each active chapter’s `onAlreadyComplete` policy explicitly: `skip`, or `bridge` with a short important story paragraph. Most chapters skip silently. Bridges preserve genuinely important story turns, not carry lists, mechanical recaps or future recipe spoilers; they appear in order with the next unfinished chapter, or the ending if everything remaining is done.
- A partial chapter remains: already fulfilled goals are visibly complete, while the remaining task is real. Preserve existing placement witnesses rather than automatically carrying them away to manufacture work.
- Entering a skipped chapter still introduces its genuine caches and hotspots. Supplies remain tangible with their original provenance and new-versus-old classification through a cascade; never fabricate combinations or invention rewards. Empty source locations do not retain a delivery divider.
- Normal chapter completion is distinct from being complete on entry. The shared logical engine owns this distinction and the bounded cascade, not a hidden UI shortcut. Bridge facts follow Undo; acknowledgements persist once per run. Reset starts fresh; replayed debug checkpoints mark their bridges and inventions historical. Old policy-less snapshots retain their original behavior and save identities.

## Ideas have histories

- Show what builds on what: transmission, adaptation, criticism, synthesis and cross-fertilisation, rather than isolated historical vignettes.
- Respect chronology. Athens predates formal early-modern scientific method; intellectual history also predates Athens. Research earlier cosmologies, practical knowledge and observation where relevant.
- Do not turn “the will of the gods” → “the universe ticks” into an inevitable secular ladder. Religious thought and natural explanation can coexist and interact.
- Distinguish documented historical influence from a useful modern comparison. Keyword matches and episode abstracts supply research leads, not proof of a connection.
- Doctrine should span a broad, continuing history, including Augustine, Aquinas and Luther. Alternate strands and perspectives; always use earlier created ideas, and allow disagreements to remain substantive rather than crowning each later chapter the winner.
- For religion, use substantive doctrinal tensions with consequences. The Benedictine fasting exception was too niche. Attribute claims to particular texts/traditions; completing a recipe reconstructs a position, not a proof that a religion is true or false. Cathar sources and beliefs need careful attribution rather than a single universal “Cathar doctrine”.

## Enforcement and review

- Every recipe should earn its place through a meaningful, recognisable result or use, not graph padding. Every non-opening chapter must meet the earlier-created-item continuity rule above; merely carrying an item is not meaningful reuse. Historical influence, analogy and physical lifecycle are different claims and need different evidence.
- Cut technically defensible but fiddly processing when its result is not an interesting composition. Do not rescue it with a longer label, attachment kit or calibration chore merely to preserve recipe count. Mars's scale-card/core-rack family is the concrete example: photograph the actual core and compare existing geological photographs directly, keeping the useful observation and later story connection without inventing dimensions or evidence of life.
- Keep graph/audit warnings visible. Resolve a warning or record an explicit, specific waiver explaining why that exception is intentional; do not silently ignore it, weaken the rule to obtain green tests or present a waiver as a fix. This review requirement does not claim that every desired graph check is automated today.
- Default discoverability testing is **name-and-task-first**, with prose and double-tap explanations closed and normal highlights available. Begin from a fresh state or clearly labelled earned checkpoint, not memorised recipe knowledge. Record the exact visible words and rejected attempt; distinguish a valid alternative rejected, a hidden premise, a misleading label, a missing prerequisite, an unavailable object and an already-existing output.
- For an accepted content fix, verify local input availability and undiscovered output, the original and alternative routes, both drag directions where applicable, downstream uses, objectives, arrival/carry, duplicate prevention, Undo and save/restore. Do not let an alternative erase necessary physical parts or make another order unwinnable.
- Grade **factual/authenticity accuracy** and **forward player intuition/useful purpose** separately. A historically or physically defensible account can still fail the player-facing test; an authenticity reviewer must not approve a contrived recipe merely because it can be explained afterwards.
- State the evidence level: source review, logical/engine replay, visual name/task-only pass, native interaction or independent human play. Full-text fact-checking is valuable but not discoverability evidence. Mechanical completion and AI difficulty ratings do not certify comprehension or fun.

The release build checks input lifecycles, compound preservation and objective progression. Regression tests cover position, consumption, fitting, saved progress and authored routes. Existing saves should remain usable; a redesigned campaign gets a new identity, while old direct routes remain available.

### Objectives stay readable while dragging

Keep objectives fully visible at normal opacity throughout pointer down, movement, cancellation and drop. Dragging must not hide, dim or grey out the objective text. A tile may cover only the text it actually overlaps, because tiles sit above the objectives; the rest remains readable.
