# Named Mars: an explicit field scenario, not a 102-thing board

**Design-only architecture experiment.** No deployed source or UI changes. The independently reviewed continuous two-mission artifact remains preserved, but is no longer the preferred presentation plan: its ordinary endpoint of 102 things plus 29 places contradicts the uncluttered direct-workspace aim.

The user explicitly welcomed different levels sharing a large combination tree. This proposal therefore treats a **level as a distinct, openly selected work scene**, not another expanding region of the same visible board. The previous home state remains saved and accessible. A new field expedition has its own goals and a short, explicit manifest of actual equipment borrowed from home. No generic “camp kit,” invisible reset, destruction, automatic garbage collection or secretly duplicated learned thing is involved.

## The boundary the player would see

The home project is finished. The player chooses the separate **Ice Field Observations** scenario: shelter the field stop, photograph a close ice sample and the distant cliff, preserve a specimen, and leave a map annotated with that photograph. This is an expedition setup and evidence-return task, not another request to equip every room of a permanent base.

The packing screen must name the proposed equipment individually and show its source. The authored loadouts are witnesses, **not evidence that players selected or enjoyed them**:

- With an earned field camera: **frame, habitat fabric, solar lamp, field camera, map** — five things.
- With a simple/binocular home route: **frame, habitat fabric, solar lamp, camera, tripod, map** — six things.
- The generous condition borrows its existing lantern rather than a solar lamp; otherwise it uses the camera/tripod loadout.

The home navigation table retains its survey map. The packed map is its original, separately named underlying map token; in the binocular route it is explicitly removed from the overlook, not duplicated. The field camera and solar lamp are genuine earlier inventions, now useful for new purposes. The first field objective introduces skis and the icy approach; subsequent objectives introduce only their own fresh supplies and places.

The saved completion snapshot is historical evidence of how home was left. It is **not a second active copy** of the borrowed equipment. The live home scene loses each transferred token. All ten previously authored home endpoints happen to remain true because these loadouts do not borrow their deployed goal objects. They are checked to make the transition honest, not displayed as another set of field objectives or required to occupy the field workspace. If a future loadout borrows a deployed object, its home truth must change openly; preserving a snapshot cannot conceal that change.

The field board contains the packed actual things, new relevant supplies, and its own locations. A visible scene switch would return to the saved live home, with the loaned objects marked as being at the field scene. This navigation/access rule is a **new explicit design contract**, not an implemented UI feature. Merely silently concealing old things behind a catalogue would not satisfy it. The user should always know which scene is active, what has been borrowed and where a particular actual thing is.

Global one-kind novelty remains in force across both active scenes. If a result already exists at home, the field cannot fabricate a duplicate; the player must bring the existing thing if it is needed. The present witnesses were checked against this rule, not merely local uniqueness.

## A smaller, different purpose

1. Frame + skis → sled; place it at the icy approach.
2. Frame + habitat fabric → shelter tent; + insulation → insulated tent; place it at the field stop.
3. Sealed windowed box with glove ports + gloves → glovebox; earned solar lamp or lantern + glovebox → illuminated glovebox; camera/field camera + illuminated glovebox → imaging glovebox. Scoop + glacier → ice sample; ice sample + imaging glovebox → ice photograph. Deliver the portable photograph.
4. Ice sample + sample jar → sealed ice sample; store the specimen.
5. Lens + tube → telescope. Earned field camera + telescope → telescope camera. With a bare camera instead, telescope + tripod → field telescope, then + camera → telescope camera. Telescope camera + distant lookout → cliff photograph.
6. Cliff photograph + map → annotated survey map; leave the map at the field table.

These are conventional photographic operations. A windowed glovebox permits contained handling/photography; an existing supported field camera can accept the telescope optical setup. Photographs do not prove sample chemistry or a safe route. The annotated map is a photographic annotation, not inferred geological expertise. The scenario does not claim to model a breathable atmosphere or actual astronauts.

The imaging apparatus no longer needs its own compulsory equipment-delivery goal: it is useful because the next combination takes a picture. The distant photograph is a historical milestone that becomes an operand in the annotated map, not another forever-occupied delivery slot. Those are purposeful changes from the earlier camp checklist, not renamed chores.

## Mastery versus repetition

**Zero previously known outputs are reconstructed** in every witness. A field-camera player uses that earned invention twice: for contained imaging and to support the telescope camera. Earlier light also becomes useful again without remaking a charger/lamp network. The simple home route is still respected: its camera/tripod equipment reaches the same result with one extra genuinely new construction, not a punishment demanding the old photograph chain.

That establishes non-repetition mechanically, not enjoyment. The route remains quite linear, and five placements can still feel like errands. Useful empirical questions are whether packing known equipment feels like agency or administrative transfer; whether earned equipment recognition is rewarding; whether photographing two different scales feels like observation rather than issuing two receipts; and whether the player can comfortably find compatible things among 26–28 endpoints. A later prospective whole-scenario evaluation should retain the simple-tool control and allow shorter valid solutions. No tiny detached photography pilot is required by this artifact.

## What remains unsolved

The first home scene itself reaches 70 things in the ordinary witness. This architecture **does not solve that earlier crowding**. Its earlier fifteen objectives should be considered for similarly explicit, independently finished scenario boundaries before implementation—not merely conceal the same seventy objects. The field endpoint of 26–28 things plus seven places also needs interaction evidence; “less than 102” is not proof of legibility.

A large shared tree can support many scenarios, but this artifact authors only this six-goal field scenario against four genuine inherited home states. It does not invent a 200-decision total by multiplying an eighteen-operation level by hypothetical future levels. The next content should offer different useful purposes and choices; repetition of these same construction/output steps would need a clear reason and fresh evidence, not an assumption that practice is always fun.

The previous continuous campaign stays available as a stringent retention counterexample and historical design evidence. It is not the preferred UI plan, and neither artifact authorizes changing the deployed checkpoint.
