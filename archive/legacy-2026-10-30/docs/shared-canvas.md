# One universe, one canvas

Current interaction experiment, 25 October 2026. The owner suggested removing the shelf/workspace split: exactly one object per concept, with both ingredients remaining when a third is introduced. This is implemented in TypeScript/HTML at `?experiment=constellation`. It is a testable substrate, not a conclusion that the surrounding game is solved.

The shared canvas is now the default `/` route as well. The model is constellation-1; the current interface is canvas-3. Earlier interface sources are preserved under `experiments/snapshots/canvas-1` and `canvas-2`, and captures retain their original interface versions.

Hover retains the praised partner/result disclosure. Drag one concept onto another; their echoes fuse, the parents remain in their original positions, and a novel child appears nearby without covering an existing concept. A completed pair loses its discovery invitation. Repeating it focuses the established result. Alternate routes can record another relationship without manufacturing another copy. Drag into empty space to arrange, drag background to pan, scroll to zoom, and Fit to recover the whole board. Arrangement Undo and reload preserve discoveries. Switching modes preserves each board.

| Mode | What is implemented | What remains unproved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elements | Six origins, sixteen total concepts and fifteen disclosed relationships; persistent ingredients and immediate reuse | A worthwhile challenge beyond catalogue exploration |
| Forms | Physical cell geometry changes the result; three visible target outlines, rotations, retained parents and all legal edge contacts | Whether the spatial insight feels like creation rather than a conventional shape puzzle; whether precision obscures reasoning |
| Ideas | Five starting disciplines/methods and six illustrative bridges; checking and experimentation are distinct from prediction | A surrounding game; the free catalogue is explicitly a sketch |

The modes test different meanings of combination. They are not three completed campaigns. No finite material supplies, action allowance, stockpile or waiting requirement is present. No human enjoyment total is produced.

## Mechanical evidence

`experiments/constellation-screen.ts` constructs actual model-action witnesses under the new unique-node rules. All three Forms targets are reachable with one, four and three fusions respectively. Each fusion retains its ingredients and adds at most one canonical shape; self-combination is not required. These are privileged reachability witnesses, not shortest-path claims or blind play. The earlier unlimited-copy solver would not have established reachability for this representation.

Model tests cover immutable transitions, exact retained parents, repeated/alternate outputs, new spatial results from the same pair, color provenance through rotation, a board beyond the previous forty-piece limit, arrangement Undo, replay tampering and invalid contacts. Browser checks cover real pointer/touch contact, preview-before-release, deduplication, completed highlights, pan/zoom, persistence, goal progression and rotation during dragging. Screenshots are in `results/constellation-ui`.

Only the visible region is rendered. A synthetic thousand-node fixture was used during view development to inspect rendering and Fit. This is technical probing, not a thousand-concept game, an authored possibility space or evidence about large-board enjoyment. In particular, grouping a thousand things and long-run persistence remain separate design/engineering questions.

## Closed-loop evidence and rejection

The fresh screen-only `canvas-a` player uses only screenshots, visible controls and actual mouse/key commands. Its saved report and command record live in `results/gesture-players/canvas-a`. Isolation is by instruction, not a process sandbox. Two live development reloads interrupted the original test; the coordinator identified them, paused mutations and resumed on frozen source. Those interruptions remain in the record and are excluded from conclusions about game clarity.

`experiments/grade-constellation.ts` replays each mode, checks retained parent positions and unique concepts, and reports exact contacts and subsequent result reuse. These establish interaction continuity, not pleasure or strategic depth. The owner’s earlier praise for hover remains direct human evidence about that affordance, and is not averaged away by an AI preference.

The first canvas-a pass made four Elements fusions and nine Forms fusions in50 play commands, completed elbow/key, and left crossing unresolved. Its concrete failures were accidental click-based shape fusion, a child clipped beyond the viewport, and a target too small for accurate comparison. Canvas-2 made shape clicks select only, kept children visible, and enlarged the target. The same player returned in a fresh session as explicitly **experienced**, not independently blind: canvas-b completed all three Forms goals with seven fusions in32 play commands. It verified the exact former click failure no longer fused anything. The larger crossing revealed that its previous eleven-cell plan had misread a ten-cell outline; the player revised the decomposition and physically built it.

This is a useful correction loop, not a causal estimate of learning or enjoyment. Prior experience, changed controls and changed visibility all differ. The second pass also found excessive automatic recentering: seeing the child pushed an ingredient out of view. Canvas-3 changes camera motion to only the correction needed to reveal the child, with a browser bound on edge-contact movement. That final refinement has mechanical browser checks, not another fresh/experienced player session. Fit remains available for larger arrangements. Shape variants still share generic names and are distinguished by their actual geometry.

The negative challenge screens matter more than more catalogue content:

- The interdisciplinary toolkit can be completed by the same novelty-saturation routine for all31 goal subsets. A weaker direct-target baseline would falsely suggest deep planning. Rejected as a challenge.
- The intention-driven creature model has a planner gap and a local consequence reversal, yet one common six-fusion routine wins every authored context. Rejected.
- Optional safeguards before society-wide publication admit one safe ordering across all eight toy contexts. Rejected as a full challenge; no action quota was added to rescue it.
- Gravity/wind placement produces genuine consequence reversals, but its repeated active copies do not validate this unique-node substrate. Retained only as a separate probe.

The [designer source revisit](challenge-research-revisit.md) gives the interpretation: public tools can support construction and prediction, but repeated solved situations, redundant rules and mere nominal goals are weak evidence. A specific intended spatial insight can still be worthwhile; a universal policy-gap threshold is not a law of fun.

## Next substantive question

The owner’s faculty-scaling lens makes a promising interpretation of progress: combinations change what a world can remember, coordinate, judge or propagate. A singularity route could have consequences different from another route, including failures. This does not establish a universal history or a scientific forecast.

Before building a large system, ask what actual relationship makes choices matter when every concept and safeguard remains available. If “learn everything safe, then proceed” always wins, a new list of institutions merely postpones catalogue saturation. [The interdisciplinary research and exact counterexamples](interdisciplinary-ideas.md) retain both the opportunity and the failed attempts. The player should physically compose the consequential relation; selecting a policy from an administrative dashboard would repeat the original failure.
