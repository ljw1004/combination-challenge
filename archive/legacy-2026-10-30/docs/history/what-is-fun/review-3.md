# Generative and narrative revisions

The owner identified two substantial limitations after revision3. First, the framework explained how to reject weak gameplay better than how to invent an absorbing sequence. Second, it abstracted away personal motivations in narrative sweep, beauty, discovery and philosophical concepts.

Revision4 reorganizes the doctrine around intention, imagined construction, consequential response and renewed intention or satisfying closure. It separates a speculative designer from an evidence-seeking critic, supplies experience engines, and requires a real optional-play checkpoint rather than equating instructed completion with appetite. The independent source researcher supplied a parallel review of those mechanisms and warned that elective AI behavior is still not human motivation.

Revision5 makes narrative and conceptual choice part of the intended experience. It corrects an overreach in the cosmetic-variety test: mechanically equivalent content can have narrative or aesthetic value, even though it does not establish a new strategic problem. A logical prototype may contain a written world and character; it need not be a contextless algebra.

These revisions precede evaluation of the new continuing-world prototypes. The progression rerun had already begun under revision3 and must be labeled accordingly. The next independent audit should assess the actual evaluator and experiences against revision5, including whether its critics have again displaced the generative and narrative questions.
