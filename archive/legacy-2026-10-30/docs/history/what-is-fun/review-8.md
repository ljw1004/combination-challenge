# Revision 8: different goals in a shared ontology

Author's coherence and actionability review after the owner's 2026-10-26 clarification. This is a self-review, not an independent audit or a new playtest. Revision 7 remains archived unchanged; revision 8 applies prospectively.

## The correction

The owner asked whether each level would be extremely narrative-heavy, then explicitly proposed ontology-heavy levels: a huge combination tree reused between levels with different goals. We interpret the dictated final clause as a different goal within the shared sea of combinations, not a demand for simulation, permanent world state or endless play.

Our recent prototypes overemphasized making each world generate its own next purpose. That is one legitimate hypothesis, not the test every level must pass. A bounded goal can organize the player's work, and satisfied completion can be a successful ending. Another level may supply the next purpose without authored drama.

## Integrated changes

| Before | Revision 8 |
| --- | --- |
| Renewal dominates the opening theory and the required experience account | Intention, actual construction and valued response lead to completion or continuation; declare which experience is being tested |
| A new independent puzzle cannot supply a reason to care | Different explicit goals over a shared ontology are a central design route; test their value rather than dismissing their supplied purpose |
| Primitive-generated development reads as a requirement for every candidate | Combination must carry the activity; any narrative must arise in primitives, but every level need not generate a narrative |
| Reuse chiefly means a retained construction acquiring a new world role | Also test concepts and methods transferred across goals; distinguish knowledge transfer from physical persistence |
| Optional appetite risks becoming a universal pass gate | Use it for the specific renewal or continued-interest hypothesis; evaluate finite goal-directed play on its declared terms |
| The long-sweep section asks one prototype to cover 180–240 opportunities | Preserve the earlier whole-experience ambition, potentially across levels; do not impose it on each level or manufacture time from commands |
| Ontology size and eventual saturation risk functioning as rejection tests | Neither size nor eventual exhaustive solvability proves success or failure; investigate actual inference, enactment, routine and attention |

The opening theory, experience engines, testing instructions, evaluator obligations, F1/F7/F10, longer-scope section and closing next test now use this distinction consistently. `docs/design-contract.md` narrowly records the same current direction and retains the existing interaction constraints.

## What stays fixed

- The player performs combinations; selecting a finished output and delegating its construction is not equivalent.
- Recipes, results and actual eligibility remain inspectable. The challenge is not guessing the author's ontology.
- Established concepts are inexhaustible; no inventory, depletion or artificial action-budget economy is introduced.
- One insight may be followed by several understood combines. Every move need not be strategic, novel, irreversible or one of several equally good alternatives.
- A large catalogue is permitted but not evidence of fun. A cheap goal-insensitive routine can challenge a claimed construction problem; an eventual exhaustive solution alone cannot reject it.
- UI work remains frozen. No new evaluator implementation, model or play evidence is claimed.

## How the next tests change

Use a representative connected ontology slice under several distinct explicit goals. Before play, state each objective, public rules, proposed pleasure and valid endpoint. Observe which concepts and plans become relevant, the actual construction inference, transferred understanding, appreciated execution and unwanted repetition. A fresh level may supply a fresh objective without having to emerge from the prior level's events. Test optional appetite separately only when it answers the declared question. Whole-experience scope needs evidence across the sequence, not a multiplication of recipe or level counts.

Frozen prior protocols remain intact. Confluence, Frontier and Faculties retain their original long-scope outcomes and finite appreciations; Drifting Gardens' already-started mobile/anchored comparison remains a renewal diagnostic under revision 7. None becomes a retrospective multi-goal test, and none is promoted into a long game by this correction. Revision 8 does not claim the current evaluator has adopted its new distinctions.
