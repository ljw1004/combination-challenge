# Testing a longer combination game

The owner's target is approximately 30–40 minutes at one decision every ten seconds, or 180–240 decision opportunities. This is a proposed experience scale. Action count, annotated episodes and human duration are different measurements. No action quota belongs in the game.

Use a frozen version, recorded scenario and seed. All legal combinations, functional prerequisites and effects must be inspectable. Players may inspect rules, current state, legal actions and single-action previews. They may reason and make their own plans; they may not read implementation, designer reports, other plays or search future states with a script. Log invalid attempts and inspections separately from committed actions. A public CLI is sufficient for this phase.

## Two conditions

**Long diagnostic.** Ask a fresh player to pursue a coherent creation story through the available middle and return, with an upper examination range of 180–240 interventions. This instruction supplies a reason to keep examining the game; it does not establish appetite. Stop earlier if the player has reached a wanted ending and can no longer identify a worthwhile unresolved intention. Do not require permutations, manufactured damage or every branch to meet the target. At roughly 30, 60, 120 and 180 interventions, record a short account before selecting the next act. If the arc ends earlier, record its endpoint and why the remaining possibilities are not wanted projects.

**Elective interest.** Require only the opening encounter and enough construction to see a consequential response, up to roughly twelve interventions. At a valid stopping point, ask neutrally what the player wants to do now, including ending the session. Record any further intention before the first elective act. If play continues, revisit the question after another substantial response or about thirty interventions. Stopping satisfies the task. Do not suggest which receiver, moral answer or unresolved question ought to attract the player.

The conditions answer different questions. A mandated arc can reveal promising middle-game construction without showing that the player chose it over stopping. An elective stop can be satisfying local closure while falsifying a particular renewal prediction. A story direction chosen during an assigned block is a discretionary choice within prompted play, not a verified unprompted return.

## Brief checkpoint account

Record these in the player's own vocabulary:

- What do you currently want to make happen, and who supplied that intention?
- What response mattered since the previous checkpoint? Was it already understood, a new fact, a changed interpretation or an appreciated enactment?
- What remains unresolved, if anything? What would your next action attempt?
- Did an old combination or creation acquire a different role? Name the relevant actions or places.
- What felt like useful construction, expressive enactment, investigation, routine or overhead?
- Would you end here? If continuing, state the intention before acting again.

An episode may span several combinations or end in one. Annotate a new episode when the question or approach changes, rather than awarding an episode to each command. Exact prose appreciation and philosophical resonance belong in these accounts; a novel tactical dilemma is not required at every step.

## Review and next invention

Replay the capture exactly. Preserve the full report and intermediate notes, including mismatched expectations and positive closure. Check functional claims against actual transitions: an eloquent report of independent action cannot establish an institution or right that the model did not create.

The critic then selects concrete mechanisms to keep, change or reject, and gives the designer a new experiment. The current hypothesis concerns history expressed through primitive state: inspect whether an earlier place or creation acquires a different use; whether actual traffic, temporal phase or another actor changes what a later combination does; and whether the player adopts a resulting purpose. Try the obvious harmless-copy, saturate-everywhere, always-counter and total-isolation policies before declaring a commitment difficult. A bypass can invalidate a strategic claim without erasing reported appreciation. Authored dialogue or a stage queue cannot supply the missing development.

If the second arc is still short, identify where its ambitions exhaust. Do not replace that finding with a procedural 200-action walk, a large legal-action menu or a scene-count estimate. Those may test capacity, but the next design question remains what makes performing the next combination worth this player's attention.
