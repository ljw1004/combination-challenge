# Prospective protocol: Glasshouse Inquiry 3

Prepared 26 October 2026, before fresh recruitment. Freeze package: `results/ideas-history-v3-freeze/`. Candidate `ideas-history-inquiry-3`; publication precedes recruitment. V2 and the delivered Working Materials browser game remain unchanged.

## Changed hypothesis

The V2 pilot genuinely appreciated multi-purpose evidence and reuse, but independent review correctly classified its operations mainly as provenance/scope bookkeeping. Its 18-combine completed campaign is retained as a short success, not reinterpreted as explanatory theory play. The next question is whether a constructed **method** can change what an intelligible causal question distinguishes.

The fictional seedling offers three explicit explanations for leaf opening: direct light, immediate warmth, retained warmth. A player constructs a lamp pulse, thermostat conditions and an opening record. Its intermediate state matters: retained warmth persists after light; fixing warmth can remove the very route one wants to investigate. Full forecasts are public. Combining results creates a discriminating study, not a hidden-answer discovery.

The original V3 sketch had five static rivals (light, warmth, both, either, watering). Pre-build criticism showed it was the old Boolean response-table exercise under new labels: two fixed-temperature lamp trials already separated all rivals. That breadth was dropped. The temporal fixture is the changed mechanism. The mechanics critic helped propose this fixture, so its source audit is **not independent of the hypothesis**. A later experience reviewer must be separate.

## Scope and falsifiers

This is one tiny inquiry, not a long campaign. A developer witness solves it in **five combines**, and an exhaustive screen exposes four valid two-method solutions. Six method states exist. None alone separates all explanations even with the full diary; an uncontrolled pulse plus any thermostated method works. That fixed short diagnostic is retained. Do not create a quota or extra boards to disguise its size. The long-play target remains unestablished.

A clear short exercise may be worthwhile. It fails the proposed new direction if the player experiences only arranging already-obvious labels/groups, if the temporal/control distinction is merely recited rather than understood, or if the result feels like mandatory confirmation of a named demonstration. No requirement names the pulse or a particular control: actual prediction separation decides completion. Inspecting complete forecasts is legitimate and may make the answer obvious; do not hide them to rescue challenge.

The compact CLI is also a response to V2's reported output burden: full laws appear once and remain inspectable, while subsequent responses show the created object. This is a coupled exploratory change, not a clean causal comparison of verbosity alone.

## Capture and interpretation

Raw chronological events include exact delivered outputs, operands, before/after states and hashes, accepted/rejected outcomes and final report. Count selected accepted combines only; inspections, previews, automatic moments, report and finish are excluded. The capture has no hidden world state. A separate reviewer checks every action and claimed effect against source and forecasts, distinguishes actual player report from inference, and compares any shorter valid route. Claim no more than a small agent-diagnostic result, never human pleasure or duration.

At most one relevant neutral follow-up after the final report may ask what a particular control changed, if the player's account leaves understanding genuinely unclear. It must not supply the answer or demand an appreciation story. Missing appreciation stays missing. Do not recruit repeatedly until favorable prose appears.

If this tiny method construction is appreciated and the report concerns the actual changing prediction, retain that narrow direction and design a substantive next shared inquiry, not multiplied fixtures. If it is merely obvious demonstration enactment or unwanted syntax, retain the result and change or reject the method-game hypothesis. Either outcome leaves the approximately-200-decision destination open.
