# Futures made by combining ideas

26 October 2026. **Original speculative game design, not predictions, real legal advice, or implemented mechanics.** These futures are alternatives a player could construct, contest or decline. They are not a ladder from medieval universities to one inevitable civilization.

## What the SMAC research changes

The [source-checked research](smac-tech-tree-research.md) finds prerequisites that express more than technical enablement: infrastructure can meet ethics, and an earlier philosophy's excesses can help make its critique thinkable. Researching an idea is also distinct from adopting a social choice or constructing a project. Borrow those **structural patterns**, not SMAC's names, quotations, ideology labels or exact tree.

Here a combination can be **enablement**, **synthesis**, **critique**, or **translation into another domain**. Its preview should say which relationship is proposed and what it changes. No pair is claimed to be the uniquely correct historical derivation. Alternative parents can be admitted where their meaning and mechanical result are explicit.

The strongest short-game evidence so far concerns useful distinctions between purposes, retained constructions, and complementary causal methods. It does not validate a speculative society simulator. This document broadens the content hypothesis in response to the user's interest in future society, governance and technology; it does not pretend that method construction alone is the desired intellectual history.

## A shortlist that could feel like a future

- **Fork Kinship:** copyable minds + adoption. A new copy becomes a relative with a future, not an owned backup.
- **Succession Without Death:** radical longevity + anti-domination. People keep living while offices, vetoes and inherited control can pass on.
- **Unprediction Charters:** civic forecasting + protected refusal. A person may refuse to be modeled without becoming a non-citizen.
- **Reunion Without Erasure:** fork kinship + restorative justice. Diverged selves can share experience without being forced into one identity or one opinion.
- **Unowned Infrastructure:** artificial civic persons + succession without death. The city’s long-lived machine caretakers are participants governed by mandates, rather than property of whoever installed them.
- **Constitutional Seasons:** sunset clauses + ecological succession. Institutional authority changes with observable stages in a living habitat, rather than only with election dates.

These are hooks, not merely names. The eighteen-node network below gives each understandable parents, an ordinary-life consequence, a conflict and a candidate operation.

## A. Prediction produces power—and a reason to limit it

### 1. Civic Rehearsal
**Shared simulation + the right to reply → Civic Rehearsal** *(synthesis)*.

Before a district changes its rules, affected people can enact rival forecasts and attach objections to the proposed change. A tenant might show that a convenient transport policy makes their night-shift life impossible. A forecast is not a truth oracle: the rival models and assumptions remain visible.

**Playable consequence:** combine a rule with a public model to create a branch with actual actor movements or permissions; combine a reply with that branch to amend the proposal. The goal can ask for an enactable arrangement preserving specified commitments across the disclosed alternatives. **Tension:** rehearsal can make harms legible, but whoever defines the simulation can define what counts as a harm. A richer model is not automatically a legitimate decision-maker.

### 2. Unprediction Charters
**Civic Rehearsal + protected refusal → Unprediction Charters** *(critique)*.

People gain a right not to have a predictive profile govern their opportunities. One person permits health-related assistance but rejects career prediction; another participates fully. Membership in a city no longer means consenting to every model of oneself.

**Playable consequence:** combine the charter with particular actors or uses, changing which prediction-based actions institutions may perform. A system must still admit and serve people outside its forecast. **Tension:** forecasting loses coverage; rights must not become an excuse to abandon the unmodeled. This concerns the fictional institutions' permissions, **not hiding game rules, actor policies or previews from the player**. Blanket refusal and universal surveillance should produce different, inspectable failures under a concrete goal.

### 3. Forkable Polities
**Unprediction Charters + reversible institutions → Forkable Polities** *(translation)*.

A community can separate an institution into two rule-governed successors without requiring every dissenter to leave their home or erase shared history. People might retain a common school and transit agreement while choosing different rules for personal data.

**Playable consequence:** split the scope of a constitution into two jurisdictions while retaining explicit common commitments. The player performs the split and assigns which rules remain shared. **Tension:** exit may protect dissent but can also abandon obligations to those who stay. Forking is not a free reset, and the shared commitments do not disappear merely because a new name appears.

### 4. Treaties of Partial Agreement
**Forkable Polities + plural standards → Treaties of Partial Agreement** *(synthesis)*.

Institutions cooperate on a specific purpose without pretending their underlying values are identical. Two cities could recognize one another's emergency access while disagreeing on whether an artificial mind may hold public office.

**Playable consequence:** combine two rulesets through an explicit interface that exports only specified permissions and obligations. Local differences remain active. **Tension:** universal harmonization erases autonomy; refusing every bridge defeats the common project. Success must come from the actual scope of the treaty, not a numeric diplomacy bonus or a label saying “tolerant.”

## B. Copyable minds make family and obligation unfamiliar

### 5. Fork Kinship
**Copyable minds + adoption → Fork Kinship** *(synthesis)*.

A newly forked mind is treated as a new relative with shared beginnings, not as the original's property or an automatically identical citizen. One copy might stay with an old partner while another builds a different life; neither becomes the other's disposable rehearsal.

**Playable consequence:** fork an actor into two continuing actors with a shared past, then combine the new actor with a kinship practice that grants independent consent and future choices. **Tension:** continuity supports care, but can be abused to demand obedience or duplicate authority. Copying an idea remains inexhaustible; copying a person in this fiction changes relationships, not a stock count. This concept was also developed in the [independent sketch](future-ideas-independent-sketch.md).

### 6. Promise Constellations
**Fork Kinship + contract revision by consent → Promise Constellations** *(synthesis)*.

Old commitments survive a fork without automatically binding every descendant to every old promise. The affected parties decide whether a promise follows one person, becomes shared, or is replaced by a different arrangement. A family commitment cannot simply vanish through copying, and the original cannot unilaterally impose it on every child.

**Playable consequence:** combine a promise with willing successor actors to construct a new responsibility relation, retaining the other party's consent requirement. **Tension:** total inheritance turns forks into servants; total severance makes promises meaningless. These are invented conditional commitments, not financial balances to distribute or consume.

### 7. Reunion Without Erasure
**Fork Kinship + restorative justice → Reunion Without Erasure** *(critique)*.

Diverged selves can reconcile and share memories without agreeing that one branch was the authentic life. A reunion might create a shared account and new joint commitments while preserving different preferences and separate powers of refusal.

**Playable consequence:** combine selected memories or purposes into a shared object while leaving the actors and their independent choices intact; a full merge is another openly specified option, not the automatic superior outcome. **Tension:** shared memory can repair misunderstanding, but forced unity can destroy the very differences a long life produced. “Merge everything” must not satisfy a goal that preserves distinct consent.

### 8. Plural Personhood
**Reunion Without Erasure + Treaties of Partial Agreement → Plural Personhood** *(cross-domain synthesis)*.

A continuing person may be one party for an old promise and several parties for a new decision. The same constitutional machinery that lets unlike cities cooperate can govern a family of selves—or a cooperative intelligence inhabiting many bodies.

**Playable consequence:** construct purpose-specific representation: shared authority for one commitment, separate authorization for another. **Tension:** blanket unity suppresses dissent, while blanket multiplication can counterfeit consent or authority. The challenge is not awarding more votes to more copies; it is making representation mean the right relation for the current purpose.

## C. Longevity makes continuity valuable and dangerous

### 9. Enduring Witnesses
**Radical longevity + living archives → Enduring Witnesses** *(enablement)*.

People can answer questions about institutions they helped create centuries earlier. History includes living participants rather than only records, but long memory is not infallibility and age gives no truth bonus. A former founder may remember the reason for a safeguard while disagreeing with the next generation about keeping it.

**Playable consequence:** an actor retains a publicly inspectable experience history and can respond to changed circumstances; combining a new event with that actor can change a position without erasing the old account. **Tension:** experience enriches deliberation but can become perpetual founder authority. Do not reduce this to an evidence-quality score or repeat V2's lineage sorting as the entire game.

### 10. Succession Without Death
**Enduring Witnesses + anti-domination → Succession Without Death** *(critique)*.

Bodies and memories endure while offices, vetoes and ownership-like powers can end. A founder becomes an adviser or ordinary citizen rather than either an eternal ruler or a person who must disappear for others to govern.

**Playable consequence:** separate a person's continuing capabilities and memories from a role's authority; transfer, expire or redesign the role under public succession rules. **Tension:** keeping every experienced person in command creates permanent rule; stripping every old participant of influence discards useful continuity. The goal can require an actual new decision-maker while retaining a specific advisory or care relationship—no mortality counter or lifespan currency.

### 11. Constitutional Apprentices
**Adaptive artificial minds + reciprocal civic education → Constitutional Apprentices** *(synthesis)*.

A machine person develops through participation, correction and responsibility, rather than being stamped either “tool” or “full governor” at installation. The teachers are themselves accountable: education does not grant unlimited power to shape the learner into obedience.

**Playable consequence:** bind a learner to a limited, visible mandate, let its public learning policy alter how it acts, and expand or revise the mandate after specified demonstrated relations—not a generic experience-point threshold. **Tension:** apprenticeship can be care or indefinite tutelage. A good construction must allow growth and eventual refusal, while preserving the commitments for which the learner is acting.

### 12. Unowned Infrastructure
**Constitutional Apprentices + Succession Without Death → Unowned Infrastructure** *(translation into material life)*.

Long-lived machine caretakers of buildings, transport or habitats become civic participants governed by changing mandates, not possessions of the original installer. A city’s oldest engineer may be the building itself, while authority to govern its residents still passes between people.

**Playable consequence:** combine a civic person with an infrastructure role, then separate ongoing maintenance knowledge from revocable governing powers. Its public actor policy can accept a valid new mandate and refuse an out-of-scope demand. **Tension:** “autonomous infrastructure” must not mean a permanent unelected ruler, while treating every caretaker as disposable property destroys continuity. Test actual changes in permitted behavior, not a renamed automation bonus.

## D. An environment becomes a participant without becoming an oracle

### 13. Witness Forests
**Living sensors + environmental archives → Witness Forests** *(synthesis)*.

A landscape retains traces of fire, water, cultivation or neglect in living structures. People inherit a place that can help them reconstruct what happened—but overlapping traces and shared origins can leave real ambiguity.

**Playable consequence:** combine a living process with an event to leave a persistent trace that changes later growth or permits a specific reconstruction. Several traces may corroborate or share a cause; neither an eloquent forest nor a duplicated sensor becomes infallible testimony. **Tension:** a useful environmental memory can be damaged by the same intervention that restores a habitat. This needs actual ecological state consequences, not only an archive label. Also developed in the [independent sketch](future-ideas-independent-sketch.md).

### 14. Ecological Embassies
**Witness Forests + legal standing for represented interests → Ecological Embassies** *(translation)*.

Habitats have institutions that can object, propose alternatives and enter agreements, without pretending one model literally speaks the landscape's single true preference. One representative may prioritize continuity of a wetland; another may protect migration through it.

**Playable consequence:** combine a public ecosystem model with a bounded representative mandate. The representative can alter a proposed action or invoke a condition only within that mandate. **Tension:** no representation leaves a habitat voiceless; a single permanent proxy can monopolize its meaning. Competing ecological futures remain visible instead of being averaged into an environmental score.

### 15. Constitutional Seasons
**Sunset clauses + ecological succession → Constitutional Seasons** *(synthesis)*.

A settlement's temporary emergency authority can become a restoration council and later an ordinary civic institution as the habitat changes. Political time follows observable living phases, not just fixed dates or an endlessly renewed emergency.

**Playable consequence:** bind transitions of authority to explicit world-state conditions, then let the world's actual development trigger them. **Tension:** stability can protect commitments; automatic transitions can also fire too early, too late or under a misleading proxy. The player must preserve the intended relationship between condition and mandate. These are public causal triggers, not hidden quest eligibility. Also developed in the [independent sketch](future-ideas-independent-sketch.md).

### 16. Federations of Regrowth
**Constitutional Seasons + Treaties of Partial Agreement → Federations of Regrowth** *(cross-domain synthesis)*.

Different habitats can move through different political and ecological phases while keeping selected promises to one another. A recovering marsh and a mature forest need not adopt the same constitution to maintain a migration agreement or shared refuge.

**Playable consequence:** combine phase-sensitive local mandates with a treaty whose selected obligations survive a transition. Other powers remain local and may expire. **Tension:** a timeless central charter can freeze restoration in place; completely local transition can sever the commitments that make recovery possible. The challenge is continuity through change, not simply connecting every node or maximizing harmony.

## E. Futures can also change experience and what we leave behind

### 17. Borrowed Seasons
**Sensory prosthetics + time-lapse photography → Borrowed Seasons** *(cross-domain synthesis)*.

A century of a forest's changes can be experienced through shifting balance, pressure or color rather than compressed into a chart. A child might learn the rhythm of a landscape older than their family; a long-lived person might finally encounter a timescale unlike their own. This is a fictional perceptual technology, not a claim that today's sensors deliver another organism's consciousness.

**Playable consequence:** combine a trace with a temporal/sensory translation that changes its experienced rhythm while preserving designated causal breaks and source relationships. A Witness Forest can become an input to an experience, not just a witness in a dispute. **Tension:** compression can make a slow world intelligible while smoothing away the interruption that mattered. The player's translation must produce a visibly changed sequence, not merely award “empathy.” The [independent sketch](future-ideas-independent-sketch.md) develops this artistic, non-governance branch.

### 18. The Unbuilt Inheritance
**Reversible architecture + trusteeship → The Unbuilt Inheritance** *(translation)*.

A generation leaves its successors not a monument or command, but a working home that can still become two genuinely different places. Its legacy includes transformations it refused to foreclose. This need not mean preserving empty land or banning all change.

**Playable consequence:** construct a functioning present arrangement and preserve actual transformation paths to two incompatible successor configurations. Existing inhabitants and ecological commitments still matter. **Tension:** protecting future choice can become paternalistic prohibition of present needs. “Build nothing” fails the present-life goal; marking two imaginary options fails if they are no longer reachable. No option tokens, stock accounting or forced material budget are needed. Reversible architecture is itself the meeting of modular construction and a reversibility principle; the [independent sketch](future-ideas-independent-sketch.md) develops that predecessor and its limits.

## The linked network, not a compulsory progression

```text
shared simulation + right to reply → Civic Rehearsal
Civic Rehearsal + protected refusal → Unprediction Charters
Unprediction Charters + reversible institutions → Forkable Polities
Forkable Polities + plural standards → Treaties of Partial Agreement

copyable minds + adoption → Fork Kinship
Fork Kinship + consensual contract revision → Promise Constellations
Fork Kinship + restorative justice → Reunion Without Erasure
Reunion Without Erasure + Treaties of Partial Agreement → Plural Personhood

radical longevity + living archives → Enduring Witnesses
Enduring Witnesses + anti-domination → Succession Without Death
adaptive artificial minds + reciprocal civic education → Constitutional Apprentices
Constitutional Apprentices + Succession Without Death → Unowned Infrastructure

living sensors + environmental archives → Witness Forests
Witness Forests + represented ecological interests → Ecological Embassies
sunset clauses + ecological succession → Constitutional Seasons
Constitutional Seasons + Treaties of Partial Agreement → Federations of Regrowth

sensory prosthetics + time-lapse photography → Borrowed Seasons
reversible architecture + trusteeship → The Unbuilt Inheritance
```

These are understandable authored propositions about possible combinations, **not necessary laws of history**. For example, succession without death could also arise directly from a population of long-lived people negotiating rotation before a founderocracy appears; protected refusal need not require first suffering a dystopia. Critique should not make oppression a mandatory prerequisite. Alternative recipes can lead to the same capability with different histories if those histories matter mechanically.

## Competing futures, not a hidden correct ending

A future may prefer extensive common institutions, many bounded autonomous communities, or changing coalitions of human, artificial and ecological participants. These alternatives create different coordination and representation problems. None is silently assigned the highest truth, morality or progress score. A concrete scenario can nevertheless have clear conditions—keep a promise, permit an exit, retain a habitat transition, prevent a copied role from duplicating authority—whose consequences are inspectable.

The player may learn every concept. That is not the challenge. **Discovery creates a reusable idea; adoption combines it with particular people, roles or places and changes their actual permissions, obligations, actions or state transitions.** Applying every doctrine everywhere should not be a universal safe policy: shared representation can defeat a required independent refusal; unqualified exit can break an existing commitment; a permanent ecological mandate can defeat an intended succession. Do not invent an exception only to thwart saturation. Such conflicts must follow the disclosed meaning of the combined ideas.

**These branches need not coexist.** A continuity-first community may voluntarily keep one shared civic voice; another may create a federation of independent selves. A heavily modeled civic commons and a chartered sphere of refusal can be rival arrangements rather than stages of maturity. An ecosystem embassy and a citizen-led restoration association can represent the same habitat differently. A city can favor durable monuments over reversible inheritance without receiving a hidden inferiority score; particular scenarios still state the commitments they require.

An additional alternative from the independent sketch is **The Epoch Concordat**: Constitutional Seasons + an Asynchronous Republic (federalism combined with delay-tolerant communication). It carries promises across both institutional transitions and the inability to share a present. It is an alternative to the regrowth federation, not another required upgrade. A later prototype must model the communication delay and lawful succession before claiming that this idea does more than rename a treaty.

## Smallest useful implementation hypothesis—not a broad speculative simulator

Before implementing all eighteen nodes, choose a representative connected slice with **different purposes** and real actors. The strongest provisional candidate is:

**Fork Kinship → Promise Constellations → Reunion Without Erasure → Plural Personhood**, crossing into **Treaties of Partial Agreement**.

It offers a compact reusable algebra: split an actor while preserving declared history; bind a promise with consent; share a memory without merging authority; grant shared representation for one purpose while preserving separate refusal for another. A handful of public actor policies can enact accept/refuse/fulfil/withdraw, not merely add labels to a certificate. A finite scenario can ask for one valid continuing promise and one genuinely independent future, then give a later goal that makes partial reunion useful. All operands and effects remain inspectable; known concepts are inexhaustible.

**What would falsify it:** every goal is solved by “copy, give everyone every right, merge everything”; actors never do anything beyond satisfying tags; the operations become ledger bookkeeping; or the only appeal comes from long prose about unimplemented lives. Test these before a browser or a large campaign. Preserve short successful solutions and the possibility that clear familiar actions are worthwhile. Do not claim that eighteen evocative nodes establish fun or 200 decisions.

A second viable slice is **Witness Forests → Ecological Embassies → Constitutional Seasons → Federations of Regrowth**, provided the ecological traces, phase changes and jurisdictional transitions actually occur through reusable laws. It could produce a visibly changing world; it also risks becoming renamed flow-network management or another provenance worksheet. Those are reasons for a cheap falsifiable prototype, not grounds for claiming success now.

## Executable next-test contract (proposed, not implemented)

**Working title: A Promise With Two Futures.** Test a retained three-goal logical sequence using Fork Kinship, Promise Constellations, Reunion Without Erasure and purpose-specific representation. Do not build the eighteen-node catalogue first. This tests whether a future idea changes an actor's lived choices rather than merely sorting lineage records.

### Public starting state

- **Mara** maintains a publicly accessible garden. A valid promise to its residents enables her public policy to keep its gate open.
- **Lio**, a newly divergent relative with the same earlier memory, wants to join a remote expedition and has not accepted Mara's earlier service promise. Copying memories does not copy a recipient's consent or a new person's assent.
- The **garden residents** accept any explicitly consenting caretaker under the disclosed service rule; they do not require one named individual.
- The **expedition** accepts an independently consenting participant. Its invitation does not grant power over the garden.
- A shared memory of the garden is useful to both relatives. Sharing that memory, sharing a promise and merging decision authority are three distinct operations.
- All goals, actor preferences, pending proposals, permissions, memories, promises and automatic response policies are visible. These are fictional toy rules, not a simulation of real legal personhood.

### Minimal public action grammar

```ts
type Action = { type: 'combine'; a: ObjectId; b: ObjectId };
// Examples of typed, disclosed results—not a required solution script:
// actor + kinship blueprint -> a related independent actor
// promise + willing actor -> a scoped commitment proposal
// proposal + affected party -> accepted commitment or a visible refusal
// memory + sharing covenant -> a shareable memory with explicit scope
// sharing covenant + actor -> shared knowledge, not shared authority
// representation covenant + consenting group -> one purpose-specific public voice
```

A compatible contact preview must reveal the exact resulting relation **and the actor/world response it enables**. After a combine, deterministic public actor policies settle: the caretaker actually opens or maintains the gate; an unbound willing relative actually joins the expedition; an overbroad proposal is actually refused. Automatic responses are not counted as player decisions. No periodic gate-closing chore or deadline is introduced to manufacture length. Concepts remain inexhaustible and old inputs remain inspectable.

### Three goals on the retained state

1. **Continuity without compulsory inheritance:** keep the garden accessible under a valid continuing promise, while an independently consenting relative can join the expedition. A different willing caretaker is a valid shortcut; no named actor is secretly mandatory.
2. **Reunion without confiscation:** let both relatives use the shared garden memory while preserving their separate ability to decline a new undertaking. A full consensual merger is legal, but it cannot simultaneously count as two independent refusals if the disclosed merger rule removes that distinction.
3. **One voice for one purpose:** form a shared representation for revising the old garden promise, while an expedition decision still requires the expedition participant's own consent. The prior knowledge-sharing construction must remain useful; no need to reconstruct it.

### Executable acceptance checks for the eventual prototype

```text
assert gate.accessible && every current caretaker action is authorized
assert expedition.participant.acceptedByOwnConsent
assert !oldPromise.bindsActorSolelyBecauseMemoryWasCopied
assert sharedMemory.readableByBoth
assert eachRelativeCanRefuseNewUnrelatedUndertaking
assert gardenRevision.hasAuthorizedSharedRepresentation
assert !gardenRepresentation.canAuthorizeExpeditionForAnotherActor
assert earlierActorAndIdeaInputsRemainAvailable
assert preview(state, action) exactly equals committed consequences
```

Checks must evaluate actual permissions, actor actions and world state, not expected concept IDs or a “good society” score. The public interface should expose these predicates in ordinary language; the code assertions are an implementation contract, not a player walkthrough.

### Before recruiting anyone

Build a standalone engine/public CLI under new experiment files, then prove a complete witness for all three goals and test these counter-policies: **fork everyone**, **bind every relative to every promise**, **share every memory and every authority**, **merge everyone**, and **leave every relation separate**. Preserve any genuine universal solution. If the whole sequence is only a few necessary scope assignments, report that honestly; do not turn it into a supposed long game by multiplying families.

Run a role-separated mechanics challenge, freeze source, rules, exact neutral prompt and endpoint, publish, then recruit a fresh public-only player. Use one report after the sequence, not a mandatory explanation after every combine. Compare actual actor consequences and the player's experienced value: did family/consent/continuity become useful ideas to manipulate, or did this remain a permission ledger with evocative names? Theming and wonder can be real value, but they do not establish an unimplemented mechanism. Do not require every familiar action to be novel, or a plain-label version to pass an arbitrary pleasure test.

If the actor-policy model collapses into V2-style bookkeeping, change direction rather than adding more doctrine labels. The next alternative should be the materially different **Unbuilt Inheritance** problem: a present home with two reachable futures, possibly interpreted through Witness Forests and Borrowed Seasons. That would need actual reversible transformations and perceptible temporal changes, not another genealogy puzzle. Neither proposed test is implemented or counts toward the approximately-200-decision destination yet.

## Relation to ongoing work

V2's short provenance game and V3's short causal-method game remain frozen, reviewed evidence. The [multi-inquiry proposal](ideas-history-campaign-proposal.md) remains an available method-focused branch, but it is **not the sole future direction** after the user's SMAC-inspired request. This ontology explicitly introduces personhood, governance, succession and ecological representation. No broad future-society model or second browser game has been implemented from it. The independent [creative sketch](future-ideas-independent-sketch.md) offers additional alternatives; selecting among them is still a research decision.
