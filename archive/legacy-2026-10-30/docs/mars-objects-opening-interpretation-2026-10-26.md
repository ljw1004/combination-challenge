# Mars Objects 1 — postplay interpretation addendum

26 October 2026, after the owner's 05:40:30 UTC correction. **Interpretation only, not a new playtest or a replacement verdict under the original protocol.** The [original review](mars-objects-play-review.md), [prospective protocol](mars-objects-play-protocol.md), raw captures and frozen models remain unchanged.

## What was too negative

The owner pointed out that there can be comfort in making progress by doing clear things, that not every decision should be meaningful, and that early meaning can come partly from learning the mechanics. Our presentation of straightforward Mars play as a weakness was too strong **if judging it as an opening or tutorial**. Low inference burden does not alone establish unwanted play. Getting oriented, seeing an understandable effect and simply progressing are legitimate possibilities, not consolation prizes for missing strategy.

This does not retroactively designate the original test as an onboarding study or declare its teaching successful. It tested familiar-object functions and purposeful construction under doctrine 8. The correction changes the next question, not what those players did or reported.

## What the evidence still says

- **Ordinary: four combines** — two blueprint syntheses and two whole-object placements. **Complete catalogue: two combines** — two known-object placements, not synthesis of their interiors.
- Both selected a clear shell for garden and a reflective shell for signal. Their effects were intelligible, and both agents recognized the functional contrast while describing the answers as obvious/routine application rather than discovery.
- Every public observation exposed exact legal-action previews. Correct predictions and completion therefore do not isolate newly learned mechanics or independently discovered implications. The two-command difference reflects omitted blueprint acquisitions, not a measured learning or enjoyment effect.
- Neither redesigned a construction, used a powered lamp, reused the carried clear shell on signal, or continued beyond the assigned goals. Assigned completion is not elective appetite; stopping was valid under the original protocol.

The plays still do not establish a substantial construction challenge. They also do not establish that humans find this opening comfortable, boring or fun. The owner's general design preference is not a report of playing this slice; the agents' routine reports must not be rewritten as comfort.

## How to judge the opening differently

Keep **clear progress**, **mechanics learning**, **comfortable enactment**, **pacing** and **later purpose** distinct from strategic dilemma density. Learning may supply part of the opening's value; comfortable progress need not depend on new learning. Familiar steps can remain worthwhile toward the same goal without acquiring a new role. No individual combine owes us an interesting dilemma, a separately articulated meaning or a promised later payoff.

Pacing and later purposes belong to the experience across passages: a later goal may make familiar actions useful differently, while a calm stretch may remain simple. These are possibilities to examine, not requirements to attach to every action. Do not hide previews, add quotas, invent obstacles or enlarge the ontology merely to make the opening less obvious.

## One small next evaluation question

**For a first-time player, do these clear early combines provide welcome progress or orientation, or feel like unwanted instructions to copy?**

If evaluated later, ask once after the short opening what it was like and what, if anything, became clearer. Preserve the answer alongside representative actions; do not demand a report from every combine or require both learning and comfort. Any later-use or continuation question is separate, not a mandatory opening pass gate. Agent diagnostics can inform interpretation but cannot establish human comfort.
