## Physics and source checks

- **fields-r01/02, compass/current:** the compass detects a magnetic effect around a current-carrying wire; it does not measure the electric current's direction by aligning along the wire. Above/below observations are explicitly presented as recorded positions with orientation marks. [IOP, Oersted's experiment, Teaching Notes](https://spark.iop.org/oersteds-experiment) directly supports the opposite directions. The module does not reproduce apparatus-power instructions or assert the contested dramatic-discovery story.
- **fields-r03/04, direction map:** compass directions form a representation of local magnetic direction, not literal threads or a measurement of magnitude. [OpenStax University Physics §11.2, Representing Magnetic Fields, rule 1 and fig.11.6](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/11-2-magnetic-fields-and-lines) directly supports this use of the compass. Comparing different spatial patterns is meaningful; the game does not confuse current direction with field-line direction.
- **fields-r05/06, moving versus resting magnet:** the same magnet orientation and coil are preserved; approach/rest/withdrawal give opposite/nonexistent responses in these supplied trials. [OpenStax College Physics §23.1, fig.23.4 and adjacent explanation](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics/pages/23-1-induced-emf-and-magnetic-flux) supports these contrasts. It is correctly scoped to this arrangement, not the universal claim that any mechanical motion creates electricity.
- **fields-r07/08, stationary ring:** connection and disconnection produce transient responses despite neither winding moving; steady current does not sustain the secondary response. [OpenStax §23.1, fig.23.3](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics/pages/23-1-induced-emf-and-magnetic-flux) supplies the operational distinction. [Royal Institution object record, date and Description](https://www.rigb.org/explore-science/explore/collection/michael-faradays-ring-coil-apparatus) independently dates the apparatus to August 1831 and describes the brief secondary current. Museum object evidence and a modern teaching reconstruction are properly distinguished.
- **fields-r08/09/10, linkage and generator:** changing magnetic linkage is the common account; mechanical motion is one route, not a universal requirement. The hand supplies energy and the magnet is not fuel. [IOP Generators, explanation of changing linkage and mechanical/electrical pathways](https://spark.iop.org/generators), and [OpenStax §23.1, fig.23.5 and flux discussion](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics/pages/23-1-induced-emf-and-magnetic-flux), support those claims. The module does not claim its compass map proves induction or completes electromagnetic theory.
- **Historical object:** [Science Museum Group, Ørsted compass needle](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co145363/oersted-compass-needle-1828), date and object description, supports an 1828 demonstration instrument. The scenario does not mistake it for the first 1820 discovery apparatus.
