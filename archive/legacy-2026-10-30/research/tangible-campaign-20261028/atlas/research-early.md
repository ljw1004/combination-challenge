# Early Atlas: useful findings first

## Recommended spine

Use one chronological journey with **converging textual and mathematical lineages**, not alternating disconnected civilizations. A strong early sequence is Athens (fourth century BCE) → Hellenistic geometry/editing (third–second centuries BCE) → Baghdad arithmetic and algebra (c. 820–830 CE) → Hunayn's translation workshop (mid-ninth century) → Ibn al-Haytham's optics (early eleventh century) → Latin optical manuscripts (c. 1200) → Florence's perspective workshop (1435) → Venice's printed geometry (1482).

**The safest tangible continuity is a sequence of books, diagrams and worked examples being translated, corrected, recombined and printed.** It is not an immortal notebook physically carried through 1,800 years. The game can call the tokens reconstructed copies and use the existing scene-local carry mechanism without explaining carry in every chapter.

India is a **distinct tributary**. Introduce Indian place-value practice in the Baghdad reception chapter; do not imply Athens or Euclid caused it. Similarly, do not portray Arabic scholars as a holding pen for Greek discoveries: the optics chapter changes the received account of vision, and the algebra chapter develops reusable techniques in its own setting.

## Most important historical cautions

1. Remove the undated modern scientific-method opening. Ancient reasoning does not need to be a defective version of a modern laboratory protocol.
2. Socratic question-and-answer, Aristotelian demonstration and Euclidean proof are related intellectual practices, not interchangeable stages of a single algorithm. Do not say Socrates invented skepticism, Aristotle invented logic everywhere, or Euclid deduced all geometry from flawless axioms.
3. Do not label the entire translation movement “the House of Wisdom.” Specialist scholarship rejects treating the court library as the central institution for all Greek-Arabic translation. Patrons, Christian translators, physicians and manuscript networks matter.
4. Hunayn worked through Greek, Syriac and Arabic; not every work traversed every language. His revisions and explanatory additions make translation an active intellectual practice.
5. Do not assert a direct, settled causal arrow “Alhazen invented Renaissance perspective.” Optical texts certainly shaped medieval perspectivists; the specific route to painting is more debated.
6. Do not teach “Europe rediscovered everything because Arabic alone preserved Greece.” Greek, Arabic, Syriac, Hebrew and Latin witnesses form overlapping, uneven traditions.
7. Printing does not mean Gutenberg invented printing globally. A Venice 1482 chapter is a particular technical problem: reproducing text **and diagrams** consistently.

## Source-backed dependency graph

Legend: **D** documented textual/method transmission; **C** conceptual reuse by our reconstruction, not proof of personal historical influence; **Q** relationship needing qualification.

- Athenian definitional dialogue → explicit definition and objection (**C**, primary examples in Plato's *Euthyphro*). Use the resulting definition sheet later to distinguish a geometric definition from a diagram; do not claim a specific Socratic teaching caused Euclid's book.
- Hellenistic Euclidean proposition → Arabic Euclidean manuscript → Latin manuscript → Ratdolt's printed Euclid (**D**). This is the strongest long-range artifact lineage in the early game.
- Greek Galenic/Aristotelian texts → Hunayn and colleagues' Syriac/Arabic translations, revisions and explanations (**D**). A collated reading and translator's note should become useful inputs, not decorative achievements.
- Indian decimal arithmetic → al-Khwarizmi's arithmetic tradition → later Latin arithmetic (**D**). Treat this as a separate incoming stream, not an outgrowth of Greek geometry.
- Greek mathematical optics + anatomical accounts + Arabic optical work → Ibn al-Haytham's new account (**D**, do not present his result as a mere translation).
- Ibn al-Haytham's *Kitab al-Manazir* → Latin *De aspectibus* c. 1200 → Bacon, Pecham and Witelo (**D**).
- Medieval optical geometry → Renaissance pictorial perspective (**Q**). Safe player reuse: a ray diagram is actually useful for constructing a perspective picture. Unsafe claim: the game has proved exactly how Brunelleschi or Alberti acquired every idea.
- Latin Euclidean text + diagram-copying problem → 1482 printed edition (**D**). Particularly good long-range reuse after the player has used a different optical branch.

## Concrete candidate chapter sketches (eight recipes each)

These are authored teaching reconstructions, not claims that historical people used these exact English slips. Bold inputs denote outputs created in an earlier chapter. The first chapter necessarily establishes the initial vocabulary; all subsequent chapters have real reuse. Each completion should yield an inspectable object rather than a generic badge called “critical thinking.”

### 1. Athens: a definition that survives a question — fourth century BCE

Motivation: “Our speakers agree on examples but disagree on the rule. Leave a record that makes their disagreement intelligible.” Objective: an argument transcript and an unresolved question, not a final answer to justice or piety.

1. Example speech + dialogue tablet → recorded example.
2. Recorded example + second accepted example → shared feature.
3. Shared feature + proposed rule → candidate definition.
4. Candidate definition + disputed case → definition objection.
5. Candidate definition + speaker's reasons → stated premises.
6. Stated premises + definition objection → revised argument.
7. Revised argument + speaker's concession → dialogue transcript.
8. Dialogue transcript + remaining disputed case → open question.

Use an openly authored nonreligious illustration if the full *Euthyphro* would overwhelm the narrow UI; do not put invented dialogue in Plato's mouth. Short item descriptions should reveal the actual contested words/cases. Keep definition and argument distinct.

### 2. Alexandria: the diagram is not the proof — c. 300–150 BCE

Motivation: “Two readers should be able to reconstruct this triangle and check why its sides agree.” Objective: a checked proposition sheet and an annotated textual witness.

1. Straight segment + compass → first circle.
2. First circle + second centre mark → intersecting circles.
3. Intersecting circles + straightedge → constructed triangle.
4. Constructed triangle + **candidate definition** → identified equilateral triangle.
5. Equal radii + common notion → equal-side reasoning.
6. Identified equilateral triangle + equal-side reasoning → proposition sheet.
7. Proposition sheet + variant copy → compared witnesses.
8. Compared witnesses + editor's marks → annotated geometric witness.

The definition input is conceptual reuse (C), not a claim of direct personal influence. The proposition is based on *Elements* I.1; the draft should not say the diagram alone proves equality. Editorial work belongs to a related Hellenistic collection, not to a claim that Euclid himself performed every step. A more demanding game can preserve an “intersection assumption” for a much later foundations chapter.

### 3. Baghdad: two mathematical inheritances — c. 820–830

Motivation: “The account needs a reliable calculation; the unknown share needs a reasoned solution.” Objectives: a checked account and a geometric solution sheet.

1. **Annotated geometric witness** + Arabic terminology sheet → Arabic proposition copy.
2. Indian numeral exemplar + place columns → place-value calculation.
3. Place-value calculation + transaction quantities → written account.
4. Written account + reverse calculation → checked account.
5. Square diagram + added strips → incomplete square.
6. Incomplete square + missing corner → completed square.
7. Completed square + Arabic proposition copy → geometric justification.
8. Geometric justification + worked unknown → solution sheet.

No claim that Hindu-Arabic numerals are a prerequisite for al-Khwarizmi's rhetorical algebra. Two strands meet in this reconstructed workshop; arithmetic and geometry keep different jobs. The square example must use positive magnitudes, not casually credit ninth-century algebra with modern unrestricted symbolic equations.

### 4. Baghdad: the translator corrects the book — mid-ninth century

Motivation: “A damaged source and an ambiguous term cannot be repaired by fluency alone.” Objective: an annotated translation whose readers can distinguish source, conjecture and explanation.

1. Greek medical witness + second witness → compared medical copies.
2. Compared medical copies + **annotated geometric witness** → marked textual disagreement.
3. Marked textual disagreement + third reading → preferred source reading.
4. Preferred source reading + Syriac draft → revised Syriac passage.
5. Revised Syriac passage + Arabic draft → compared translations.
6. Compared translations + clinical context → clarified technical term.
7. Clarified technical term + explanatory note → annotated Arabic passage.
8. Annotated Arabic passage + incomplete source notice → accountable translation.

The early geometry witness supplies an actual previously made example of editorial marks, but the medical dossier is newly supplied. This is a reconstruction of comparable philological work, not a claim that one Euclid manuscript caused Hunayn's method. More robust direct historical reuse would make the earlier editorial artifact a Galenic commentary, if campaign breadth permits it. Hunayn's explanatory notes and acknowledged textual problems are documented; a modern full stemma and editorial apparatus should not be presented as his inventions.

### 5. Cairo: where does vision begin? — early eleventh century

Motivation: “A geometrical account of seeing must answer what light actually does.” Objectives: a tested ray diagram and a revised account of vision.

1. Lamp + aperture screen → narrow light path.
2. Narrow light path + **Arabic proposition copy** → ray diagram.
3. Ray diagram + opaque obstacle → predicted shadow.
4. Predicted shadow + observed screen → checked path model.
5. Two lamps + aperture screen → two separated spots.
6. Two separated spots + lamp cover → source-linked spots.
7. Source-linked spots + **accountable translation** → challenged received account.
8. Challenged received account + checked path model → revised optical account.

The imported translation represents a particular inherited account and must describe its claim. Do not use “translation” as a magical generic catalyst. Do not claim a pinhole image proves a complete physiological theory of sight. Object-emitted/reflected light, geometric reasoning and observation work together.

### 6. A Latin reading room: a new optical vocabulary — c. 1200–1270

Motivation: “Our readers can copy the words yet misread the ray. Make the argument travel with its diagram.” Objectives: a Latin optical manuscript and an interpretable demonstration.

1. **Revised optical account** + Latin word list → Latin optical draft.
2. Latin optical draft + **ray diagram** → diagram-linked translation.
3. Diagram-linked translation + ambiguous Latin term → translation query.
4. Translation query + **accountable translation** → translator's annotation.
5. Translator's annotation + copied pages → annotated Latin manuscript.
6. Annotated Latin manuscript + demonstration screen → reading demonstration.
7. Reading demonstration + incorrect copied ray → detected diagram error.
8. Detected diagram error + annotated Latin manuscript → corrected optical copy.

These are reconstructed editorial operations, grounded in the documented Latin reception; do not assign them to an identified translator without evidence. The text is not a neutral parcel: new audiences bring physiological and philosophical questions.

### 7. Florence: a surface that persuades the eye — 1435

Motivation: “The painted floor must fit one chosen viewpoint, not every eye at once.” Objectives: a perspective study and a visible viewing constraint.

1. **Corrected optical copy** + picture plane → picture-plane diagram.
2. Picture-plane diagram + fixed viewpoint → viewing pyramid.
3. Floor grid + viewing pyramid → projected grid.
4. Projected grid + horizon mark → floor study.
5. Upright figure + floor study → scaled figure placement.
6. Scaled figure placement + **ray diagram** → checked perspective study.
7. Checked perspective study + shifted viewpoint → displaced-view comparison.
8. Displaced-view comparison + viewing frame → viewpoint note.

The geometry is an honest dependency. The historical transmission of every painter's technique is not settled. Describe this as a reconstruction inspired by Alberti, not the universal invention or culmination of art. Many valued pictorial traditions had other purposes.

### 8. Venice: the proof has to survive the press — 1482

Motivation: “A thousand identical mistakes are not an improvement. Print a proposition whose diagram agrees with its words.” Objectives: a corrected proof sheet and a reproducible geometry page.

1. **Arabic proposition copy** + Latin Euclid witness → compared geometric versions.
2. Compared geometric versions + **annotated geometric witness** → checked Latin proposition.
3. Checked Latin proposition + type setting → text forme.
4. **Proposition sheet** + diagram block → diagram proof.
5. Diagram proof + text forme → first proof sheet.
6. First proof sheet + **corrected optical copy** → text–diagram inspection.
7. Text–diagram inspection + reversed label notice → corrected proof sheet.
8. Corrected proof sheet + impression run → printed geometry page.

An optical copy is used as an earlier worked example of diagram checking, not as a documented input into Ratdolt's shop. The strongest actual long-range edge is the created Euclidean proposition and its Arabic/Latin versions. Ensure the final page has a demonstrable label/order correction, not merely “accuracy” combined with “printing.”

## Sources and scope

- Plato, *Euthyphro*, primary text (Jowett translation): https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/euthyfro.html . The dialogue distinguishes a proposed general definition from examples and examines whether commitments conflict. It does not yield a simple settled doctrine of piety. Our example can adapt the argumentative structure without quoting or impersonating Plato.
- Aristotle, *Posterior Analytics* I, primary text: https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/posterior.1.i.html . Useful contrast between demonstrations, premises and circular argument. Do not merge this with a modern hypothesis–experiment flowchart.
- Euclid, *Elements* I.1, text plus David Joyce's commentary: https://mathcs.clarku.edu/~djoyce/elements/bookI/propI1.html . Supports the two-circle equilateral construction and equal-radii reasoning. Joyce notes unstated assumptions; this can seed later foundations rather than an inevitable triumph of perfect reason.
- Cristina D'Ancona, “Greek Sources in Arabic and Islamic Philosophy,” Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/arabic-islamic-greek/ . Supports diverse translation groups and textual lineages. Explicitly cautions against treating Bayt al-hikma as the institutional centre of Greek-Arabic translations. This is specialist secondary synthesis, not a contemporary witness.
- Uwe Vagelpohl, “In the Translator's Workshop,” *Arabic Sciences and Philosophy* 21 (2011): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3783994/ . Research using Hunayn's own notes documents source-text difficulties, technical explanations, criticism and attempts to fill gaps. The notes supply a much better playable model than a fictional generic bilingual dictionary. Some reconstructions were conjectural; translation did not automatically secure certainty.
- “Early Texts on Hindu-Arabic Calculation,” *Science in Context* 14 (2001), abstract: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/science-in-context/article/abs/early-texts-on-hinduarabic-calculation/423DA2AEAF78FC7D2C10DFBD326A2EFA . Supports India → Arabic → Latin decimal-arithmetic transmission and al-Khwarizmi's arithmetic c. 825. Abstract inspected, not full paper.
- Oxford History of Science Museum, “Mathematics & Measurement”: https://www.mhs.ox.ac.uk/scienceislam/mathematics.html . Museum context for al-Khwarizmi, practical problems and the distinct contributions made by scholars in Islamic cultures. Use for scope, not precision claims about every recipe.
- A. I. Sabra, Dictionary of Scientific Biography entry on Ibn al-Haytham, hosted by St Andrews: https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/DSB/Al-Haytham.pdf . Scholarly account explicitly discusses inherited Euclidean/Ptolemaic optics and Ibn al-Haytham's re-examination of premises. It is appropriate for the “received account + new optical evidence” edge, not a slogan that one man invented all scientific method.
- A. Mark Smith, *From Sight to Light*, chapter “Developments in the Medieval Latin West,” abstract: https://academic.oup.com/chicago-scholarship-online/book/12932/chapter-abstract/166082710 . Documents *De aspectibus* c. 1200 and its role in Bacon, Witelo and Pecham. Abstract inspected; don't claim detailed reading of the paywalled chapter.
- Smithsonian Libraries, actual 1482 Euclid: https://library.si.edu/digital-library/book/preclarissimusl00eucl . Institutional object record identifies the printed Latin edition from an Arabic source, allowing a concrete final artifact. Do not repeat its broad superlatives as a game claim.
- Library of Congress, Ratdolt's *Elements*: https://www.loc.gov/resource/gdcwdl.wdl_18198/?st=gallery . The object description specifically identifies the technical difficulty of printing geometric diagrams. Its broad claim that medieval readers knew Euclid only through Arabic-to-Latin routes is too sweeping; use the concrete edition evidence, not that generalization.

## Limits

This is research/design guidance, not executable content or a human playtest. The proposed recipes still need small concrete dossiers, count pruning, semantic inspection and scene-load tests. Several reconstruction edges are deliberately labelled C or Q: the parent should favour documented manuscript lineages over these weaker connective devices if it can build a tighter sequence.
