# Molten-world reference and treatment

5 November 2026. The [active brief](../../../../stories/creation/design-brief.md) owns the adopted art direction. This note records the reference evidence behind the revised chapter-two painting.

[NASA's 55 Cancri e concept](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/webb/super-earth-exoplanet-55-cancri-e-artists-concept/) depicts a possible molten rocky planet. NASA explicitly identifies it as an artist's concept based on observations, not a direct photograph of the planet. It supports the broad magma-ocean idea, not a known surface geography for early Earth.

[T. Orr's USGS photograph of Kīlauea's lava lake](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/new-usgs-video-about-kilaueas-summit-eruption-now-online) supplies the stronger material reference. The accompanying account explains how a moving semi-solid surface separates to expose incandescent lava and how circulation produces localized spattering. The inspected photograph shows a nearly level surface, irregular dark skins and angular glowing seams. Its enclosing crater walls are established solid land and were deliberately excluded from this creation stage.

The selected painting extrapolates that surface language into an immense molten plain. It uses transient thin skins, orange/gold separations, localized spattering and heat-lit haze. It does not depict rolling lava hills, mature mountains or a cold foreground platform. The lower working area belongs to the same surface but carries fewer bright seams. Ravilious-inspired simplified planes, directional marks and watercolour remain; stronger light and colour convey the energy of creation.

This is an informed artistic construction, not a reconstruction established by those sources.
