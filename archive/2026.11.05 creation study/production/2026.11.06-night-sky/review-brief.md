# Creation night sky: final review criteria

These criteria accumulate the user’s corrections and govern review of the final scene. The [approved master](masters/c26-rest.png) is the retained visual reference. Earlier attempts and diagnostic images were removed at the user’s request. The [live story brief](../../../../stories/creation/design-brief.md) owns adopted visual intent.

## Required judgments

1. **Abundant stars, one population, narrower Milky Way.** Answer the user’s exact question: “DOES the milky way look like it's formed by the exact same kinds of stars as elsewhere, but gradually getting closer together?” Keep the same mixture of point shapes, sizes, colours and brightnesses inside and outside the band. The band emerges primarily through more of those stars per area, with gradual intermediate densities. It must not switch to smaller, greyer or differently coloured points, or become a smooth graphic, brushed ribbon or blob behind a uniform overlay. Compare equally sized clear-field crops, excluding obscuring dust. The Milky Way occupies a relatively narrow part of a much wider sky; do not magnify a close-up to fill most of the sky. The user’s scale correction was approximately half to one third of the earlier oversized band; the final master establishes the approved proportion. Require abundant individually visible stars throughout the sky at approximately 390-pixel playing width, not only at native resolution. A dark background must not erase the starfield or reduce it to faint grain.
2. **Real, irregular, smoky dust.** Use actual photographed dust structures together with their surrounding stars. Do not invent lookalike clouds. Dust forms relatively few larger, broken, branching complexes with uneven scales and soft, dirty, smoke-like partial opacity. Avoid many small regular holes, sharp black cut-outs, a neat continuous dividing line or two orderly luminous rails. Thin dust veils some stars; denser areas hide them. The slight cigarette-smoke character belongs to obscuring dust, not a bright milky glow. Dust and unresolved smears occur within an already dense field and do not substitute for stellar concentration.
3. **Deep night.** Keep the approved near-black navy background, restrained stellar light and subdued unresolved light. Reject long-exposure brightness, a luminous core, cyan/lavender glow or a bright sky wash. The visible fire motivates the darker sky as artistic intent; this is not a calibrated eye or camera simulation. Preserve distinct stars while keeping the space between them dark.
4. **Readable land and local firelight.** The user permits the landscape a little extra light for readability. Keep its forms discernible and the fire the principal warm focus. Land and sky must share a coherent night palette and contrast, with no harsh colour/value boundary. Do not brighten the sky to match the readable land.
5. **No light pollution.** No city glow, orange/yellow horizon dome, artificial green/cyan wash or sunrise-like stripe. Bright horizons in reference photographs are not the intended treatment. Natural airglow does not justify an unwanted luminous horizon.
6. **Faint, irregular atmospheric transition.** Lower stars become dimmer and less clear, with a few points visible through irregular clearer gaps. Haze dissolves upward without a hard top edge. Its average height is roughly level across the valley and it lies in front of distant mountains; it must not follow ridges, climb behind the nearer slope or form a luminous mountain backing. Keep it barely perceptible so distant terrain retains the same light, colour and material texture as the landscape. Vary local transparency without a formal horizontal fade, regular waves, orderly spacing or parallel streaks. No separate pale-blue distant world and no abrupt land/sky or cloud/clear-sky boundary.
7. **Mostly haze, limited low cloud.** Retain only faint mist in front of or between mountains and very low distant clouds in the central valley. Most of the lower sky uses edgeless haze. Avoid high cloud banks, repeated ridges and clouds shaped like extra mountains.
8. **Preserve the scene.** Five small early Homo sapiens adults, two women and three men, around the central fire; cave with a tiny flame to the right, river to the left, existing terrain, trees, rocks, plants and foreground. Preserve the portrait frame, quiet gathering and registered Social group location. No moon, buildings, extra people, UI or text in the artwork.

## Independent review procedure

Use three concurrent readers. Give each this complete brief, the candidate and available photographic references; omit production prompts, other reviewers’ findings and the assistant’s preference. Inspect the full-resolution image, approximately 390-pixel playing width, enlarged sky detail, equal-size clear-field crops and the horizon transition. Report pass, fail or uncertain for each of the eight criteria, with visible evidence. Explicitly answer the same-star-density question and check playing-size abundance and band width. An attractive scene or photographic provenance alone does not establish a pass. A failure blocks selection; uncertainty requires a focused check. Do not change approved features to solve a different problem.

All three final readers passed all eight criteria, and the user accepted the final appearance on 6 November 2026. Earlier broad passes were sometimes superseded by the user’s more specific criticism; reviewer agreement cannot override the user’s judgment. Detailed reviewer answers and intermediate images are not retained.
