# Creation artwork acceptance

Completed 5 November 2026. **Accept the painted celestial atlas for release.** The final set has 206 ordinary circular illustrations, 12 larger fixed-context exports, 22 portrait backgrounds and one catalogue thumbnail. All raster generation used the built-in image tool. The [production record](../../production/README.md) owns requests, selected masters, necessary inputs and export geometry; the live story's single design brief owns artistic intent.

## Review instructions and scope

Recognition reviewers received shuffled anonymous 64px tokens, without names, recipes, art briefs or earlier reviews. They recorded broad family, exact guess, confidence, alternatives and visible evidence. A separate second stage supplied only ordinary item names and authored readings; first-pass answers were preserved unchanged. Each revision used fresh reviewers. Informed reviewers separately inspected the manuscript, brief, visual catalogue, source masters and selected exports, checking physical/scientific meaning, chronology, family distinctions, crop integrity and continuity. Browser checks used actual pointer actions and decoded runtime artwork.

These are untimed AI interpretations and author-operated Chromium checks. They do not establish human recognition speed, enjoyment, learning, native touch behavior or sustained whole-story engagement. The catalogue's exact/family/context-assisted expectations were declared before the final samples; earlier pilot interpretations remain earlier evidence and are not retrospectively rescored against those expectations.

## Pilot and production revisions

The twenty-icon pilot covered four elemental powers, celestial and cellular families, the cave, forest disturbance, giant browsers and their traces, and the song sequence. Independent recognition and direction reviews supported the luminous gouache, selective ink, lapis ground and thin ivory-gold circles. They exposed a wet cave without a convincing painting surface, inconsistent fresh/fossil track patterns, and a changed song without a sufficiently specific changed ending. Repairs added a dry ledge and upper wall, matched the track arrangement, and confined the song variation to its ending. A second blind track check still read round hollows as craters; the selected trackway uses two alternating pairs of broad oval hind prints and smaller D-shaped foreprints.

The initial browser pilot checked 320×700, 390×844 and 1440×1000 layouts, Fire + Air → Light, actual cave drops and song-making, and reload stability. The cave name badge was obscured by an arrival token at the original anchor. An initial move to 50%, 75% cleared that overlap on tall previews, but subsequent short-viewport checks superseded that geometry. This is local story geometry under the existing UX rules.

Fresh informed batch reviews covered all 186 subsequent ordinary subjects. Adopted corrections removed plants and cave openings from early landscapes, cooled the ground beneath First rain, made the trench show a descending plate, restored the same hooked planet in Axial tilt, removed an axis drawn through the Sun, kept fern forests distinct from flowering woodland, removed new giant tracks from recovery, and replaced prematurely human Social group with a furred tree family. The cold-response sequence retains the same pointed-snouted, long-tailed mammal lineage with pre-existing coat differences. Lunar rhythm depicts differing tidal ranges rather than larger surf waves.

Background continuity review covered every selected scene, full source panels, the cover/ending and catalogue thumbnail. The hooked headland, arched cave, paired-crater Moon, dry painting surface, catastrophe and changed recovery communities remain consistent. Plants, animals and cave art do not appear before their intended scenes. The only remaining scene correction removed sunlight from the deep vent world; a fresh final review accepts the selected dark seafloor. The cover and ending deliberately juxtapose planetary and coastal scales as an atlas image.

## Export failures and corrected selection

Review found three wrongly extracted late icons: Green shoots repeated a neighboring subject, Returning pollinators included several token fragments, and Returning fruit selected another cell. The extractor now restricts alpha detection to each declared cell, averages a narrow scan band, bridges only tiny gaps and rejects non-circular bounds.

A subsequent selection-cache bug let an earlier atlas overwrite a repaired token while a later cached repair was skipped. Final export version 3 determines the winning master for every identity before writing anything, exports each identity once, and validates source hash, bounds, exporter version and output hash. All proofs are assembled after final selection. The two attempted blind trials served during the stale-export condition were excluded from release recognition evidence; fresh reviewers assessed the corrected selected exports. A repeated complete export was byte-identical across all 218 WebPs.

## Final recognition findings

Two fresh reviewers each examined 24 shuffled final tokens at native 64px, then the ordinary context. The natural-object sample strongly supported several families, including stars, galaxy, planetary core and magnetic field, division, whales and bats. Precise chemistry and cellular organization remained less secure. Light could resemble a comet, Dense cloud electrical plasma, and Multicellular life the interior of one cell. Names/readings made the intended use understandable; no definite scientific contradiction was identified.

The state sample clearly conveyed giant browsers, groups of animals, regrowth, deadwood, people vocalizing and people observing the sky. Abstract meanings depended on context. Song, Remembered song and Changed song have distinguishable compositions, but their exact narrative meanings were not independently recognized. Call/Shared warning arcs could read as moons; Memory's echo could look like a flying shape; Varied survivors emphasized shelter more than coat differences. Fossil footprints remained hard for the blind viewer to isolate from stone texture even after naming. Informed inspection confirms the same four anatomically compatible prints in fresh mud and stone, but that does not erase the recognition finding. Tidal prediction and observation need their readings; the pictures do not independently teach the comparison.

These limitations are accepted because ordinary names and readings are part of play, and the images remain consistent with their assigned roles. Human phone validation remains in the editorial backlog. Do not describe the final set as universally recognizable without labels.
