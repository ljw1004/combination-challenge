The [evaluation guide](../../docs/fun-and-evaluation.md) owns the reusable methods, rubrics, independent participants and report format; these are not additional universal rules. Continuing work belongs to the [unreleased manuscript](../../projects/cyberpunk-study/cyberpunk.md) and [active brief](../../projects/cyberpunk-study/design-brief.md).

## Story hypotheses

- The rejected address creates curiosity about an actual place and people. Falsifier: players understand the job but report no specific reason to continue.
- Repair, investigation and neighborhood work support different kinds of satisfying combinations. Falsifier: players repeatedly describe changing labels, assembling documents or following the sole obvious action without a valued payoff.
- Technical distinctions become useful through the job. Falsifier: a reader cannot tell what each operand contributes, or a specialist finds that an action teaches a false implication.
- Moth and the residents acquire continuity through recurring work. Falsifier: a source reviewer identifies unearned affection, contradictory state, an optional creation assumed later, or a final callback without an earlier event.
- The iconic visual convention preserves recognition without promising literal scene placement. Falsifier: representative player views invite nonexistent drops into the background, related state tokens lose identity, or mobile layout obscures a needed token.

## Evaluation sequence

1. Shared lint, structural analysis and legal completion; inspect manageable working sets and alternative orders.
2. A fast pilot across the opening, evidence, evaluation, privacy, hostile-document and ending passages. Fresh narrative and factual/continuity reviews inspect the same frozen manuscript independently.
3. Revise the concrete defects, then a full fast campaign and targeted fresh retests as needed.
4. Full detailed campaign on the strengthened manuscript; revise weak or arbitrary combinations and retest changed passages with fresh players.
5. Once story work is accepted, produce a representative icon pilot and backgrounds. Fresh recognition and informed continuity/art-direction assignments have separate inputs. Expand and repair the selected direction, then verify actual play at phone and desktop sizes, transitions and reload.

Guidance given unchanged to players and graders: `This is an original cyberpunk story for curious teenagers and adults. It aims to make repair and discovery satisfying while offering useful understanding of AI and shared infrastructure. No technical background is assumed. Report candidly; a worthwhile lesson alone does not establish enjoyable play.`

The campaigns used three independent Terra Medium players per tested chapter, fresh Astra High graders and a fresh Astra High chapter synthesizer, with frozen manuscript/runtime and engine-authored public checkpoint history. Fast-mode decision density is diagnostic, not the detailed Fun measure. Mechanical completion, agent testimony, independent interpretation and human enjoyment remain separate claims. The no-goal ending received narrative and browser review rather than a zero-decision score.

Canonical response arrays, chapter reports and their mechanically assembled reports are retained unchanged in timestamped `playtests/` directories. Temporary packets, frozen runtimes, transcripts and browser captures remain disposable under the verification-record policy. The [working notes](working-notes.md) retain version-bound findings and adopted changes; the [archive index](README.md) maps historical production/runtime paths to their current locations.
