# The Travelling Harbour: development outcomes

## Scope

On 6 November 2026 the user selected continued development of the preferred harbour concept into a combination game, aiming for about 200 combinations, repeated fresh reviews and `--fast` playthroughs, followed by artwork. The work remains under this study, outside the published catalogue. The unreleased `cyberpunk.md` is a baseline, not the manuscript being revised.

This development pass is complete: **186 recipes, 215 illustrated identities, 27 playable chapters plus an ending**, and a 94-combination reference route. The project remains active for user review. The dated sequence below records how the concept changed; intermediate plans and artifact counts describe their own versions, not additional outstanding work.

## Method

Test a short playable construction before expanding. Compare materially different ways of sustaining the harbour premise; keep the original ship, bottle and crescent pier recognisable as their roles change. Expand only through substantial new uses and combinations. Reviewer assignments are fresh and receive current artifacts without earlier findings. Whole-story terminal play uses the actual `--fast` interface; detailed sessions test inference where the fast menu may conceal ambiguity. Record the actual scope and distinguish mechanical completion, AI testimony and human enjoyment.

Temporary transcripts, review packets, checkpoints and trial imagery belong in `runs/harbour-development/`. Retain concise outcomes here and requested playtest deliverables under the evaluation policy. Artwork begins after narrative and combination iteration; inspect difficult transformations and related appearances together before expanding the visual family.

## Outcomes

### Expansion candidates

Two independent author assignments considered substantially different structures: an early reversal followed by a travelling harbour with continuing constructions; and one large kinetic exhibition, with the reversal late and a short final movement aboard the ship. Both rejected inflating the original eight operations with minor assembly steps. The early reversal is the current prototype because it gives the distinctive premise an immediate payoff. A separate fresh reviewer is challenging both structures and proposing a third if neither earns the length.

The first playable slice covers six chapters: ship, harbour, reversal, lift, extended route and light. It tests the transition from one memorable reversal into reusable behaviour. It uses text cards and a neutral workbench, with no production artwork yet.

### Playtest experiment

The initial exploratory runs use one fresh participant for an uninterrupted terminal playthrough in `--fast`, followed by independent manuscript review. This deliberately differs from the three-player chapter-scoring campaign: it examines cumulative repetition and hooks in one participant's continuous experience. Participants see only terminal player output, record practical expectations before actions and candid reactions afterward, and never inspect story source. No numerical Fun score is inferred from these runs. Later structured `--detailed` chapter trials will test specific inference risks after the full arc stabilises. Exact versions, commands, coverage and adopted changes will be recorded below.

### First scale review

A fresh informed reviewer received the philosophy, authoring guide, round-4 treatments and the two expansion summaries. It preferred testing the early reversal but rejected either outline as evidence that 200 operations were earned. Its third candidate changes the unit of expansion: several complete digital commissions sharing a harbour and continuing creations. Adopt that admission test for the middle: each work needs its own purpose and payoff, and should reuse what the player already made. Reject a waterwheel/power-transmission detour, because the motion rule copies a route rather than energy or continuous rotation. Keep harbour machinery inside its enclosure; any behaviour used outside must actually be copied.
