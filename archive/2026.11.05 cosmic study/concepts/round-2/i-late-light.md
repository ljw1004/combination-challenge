# I — One encounter, two sources of light

A different adaptation of [What the Darkness Kept](../../narrative/round-3/i-late-light.md): a **single physical combination** inside a short narrated cosmic vignette. This abandons the first concept's argument cards. It is a deliberately small alternative, not a claim to supply a full-length combination game.

## What the player encounters

The opening presents the young red star in the old galaxy as a question. The retrospective passage establishes two bodies that retained hydrogen while other stars spent theirs. The playable scene reaches their final approach, after the long orbital contraction. Two material bodies are visible, named **First cold hydrogen body** and **Second cold hydrogen body**. Their description supplies the relevant distinction: each contains largely unused fuel; separately, their interiors have not sustained ordinary hydrogen fusion.

The visible setting shows a closely bound pair, distorted surfaces and the beginning of material exchange. “Follow the encounter” is the immediate purpose. The player is reconstructing the stated possible history. Dragging is notation for the approaching bodies meeting, not an assertion that a cosmic engineer chose their orbit.

## The entire operation

**First cold hydrogen body + Second cold hydrogen body → New red star.**

Both inputs contribute actual hydrogen-bearing matter and gravitational mass. Their established encounter converts orbital motion into heat and produces a single bound remnant, with some gas escaping. In this selected history, sufficient mass and thermal energy remain for contraction and sustained hydrogen fusion. Neither a portable Heat token nor an “Enough mass” answer card supplies the missing condition after the encounter.

This is a complete natural operation, spanning merger, settling and ignition. Its result reading distinguishes the initial brilliant release of collision energy from the later faint, sustained nuclear light. The whole event has one result identity; the flash is not another collectible object, and the unchanged hydrogen is not declared as a fresh supply. The later red star is the joined material, not an extra star beside two surviving donors.

## What stays out of the inventory

Only the two bodies are movable inputs. There are no inference cards or process stations. The wider galaxy, elapsed time, gravitational radiation and internal reactions belong to the setting and explanation. Circular object art would suit these material bodies. A diagram of their approaching paths can remain scenery; it has no independent ingredient role.

The original prose already contains the necessary causal account. Its mystery structure remains, but the gesture occurs during the reconstruction, before the narrative returns to the star in the opening. No new astrophysical episode is added. The detailed class name “brown dwarf” can be learned in the reading rather than required to understand an inventory label.

## Honest scope and rejection test

The interaction offers enactment and a consequence, with almost no combinatorial search. The result is partly known from the opening; the discovery concerns how its material became capable of sustained light. That may be worthwhile as an interactive vignette, but the prose could also be stronger without a gesture. A prototype must compare that possibility rather than assume interactivity adds value.

Reject this adaptation as a game if participants regard the operation only as a next-page button, or leave believing any two cold bodies inevitably form a star. Ask a transfer question about a remnant that loses too much mass or remains too cold; naming the successful result is insufficient evidence of understanding. A single physical operation gives no basis for expanding this story to dozens or hundreds of recipes. If a sustained making game is required, choose a different narrative premise rather than disguising explanation as extra construction.
