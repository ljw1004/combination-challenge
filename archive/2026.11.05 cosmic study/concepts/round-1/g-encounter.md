# G — Two scales of encounter

Concept candidate for [The Lights That Remain](../../narrative/round-3/g-galaxies.md). Proposed scope: **two combinations**, followed by a narrated ending. No manuscript or artwork is proposed here.

**Player action and question.** The player enacts an encounter first between galaxies, then between their gas clouds. The change of scale exposes why the same encounter redirects stars but physically compresses gas. The question remains: **what can a collision destroy when nearly every star survives?** There is no pilot, telescope, laboratory or imagined hand within the universe. Moving a body is the story's notation for bringing an already established trajectory to its encounter.

**Complete chain.**

1. **First gas-rich spiral + Approaching gas-rich spiral → Galaxies passing through one another.** The first contributes its stars, gas and original arrangement, including the orange star and dark lane. The second contributes another extended mass distribution, its own stars and gas, and the approaching trajectory. Their mutual gravity changes the orbits before their visible disks overlap. The result is one interacting system containing both galaxies, not a claim that their individual stars have coalesced. Its reading follows the orange star into an outward stream while its own light continues.
2. The next scene moves into the overlapping gas. **Gas cloud from the dark lane + Crossing gas cloud from the other spiral → Hot compressed cloud.** Each contributes extended gaseous matter; their established relative motion makes an encounter that gas cannot simply pass through. The result contains material from both inputs. Some bulk motion becomes heat. This result must be hot, not an instantly completed star cluster.
3. Automatic elapsed-time narration follows the same cloud: radiation removes heat; dense regions contract; young stars appear; their radiation and outflows disperse much of the remaining gas. These are consequences within the supplied cloud, not additional things to collect. The narrative returns to the orange star, now turning back toward the changing pair.
4. The ending spans the later passages and eventual merger. The old star and stars born from the cloud now inhabit one rounded galaxy. The disappearance of the spiral pattern and dark lane answers the opening question. There is no final “old star + new star” recipe: proximity does not construct the galaxy.

**What is movable, fixed and automatic.** The two galaxies are the opening movable bodies. At the closer scale, their two clouds are the movable material; the already-created interacting system becomes fixed context establishing where and why those clouds cross. It supplies no additional matter. Shock heating belongs to the cloud collision; subsequent cooling, contraction, fusion, orbital evolution and elapsed ages belong to its readings and chapter transitions. Gravity is never an inventory item. The orange star is a recurring inhabitant, not a reagent or an unused carried token.

**Continuity and supplies.** The closer scene supplies local views of clouds already contained in the two galaxies. They are not newly arrived extragalactic fuel, and the earlier galaxy tokens cannot remain available as duplicate stocks. The cloud result denotes the followed parcel throughout its later division into stars and residual gas; that whole lineage needs only one result, with later states narrated. The scene change must make this change of scale explicit.

**Vocabulary and risk.** New primitive names are limited to two spirals and their two gas clouds. “Shock,” “fusion” and the distinction between an orbit and a star's internal integrity are explained at the relevant consequences. The main risk is that two gestures leave most of the transformation to narration. A decisive falsifier would be a reader interpreting the first action as stars crashing together, or the second as gas becoming stars merely because more gas was added. Additional collectible forces would not repair either misunderstanding.
