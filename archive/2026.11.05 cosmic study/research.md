# Cosmic: scientific premises and research

Scientific-premises review, 2026-11-05. Input: [preserved brainstorming](brainstorming.txt), before a manuscript exists. This reviews the proposed journey from early atoms through stars and planet formation to an early terrestrial sea. It does not establish player comprehension, enjoyment, or enough worthwhile work for 200 combinations. The [authoring guide](../../docs/story-authoring.md#keep-intellectual-and-factual-claims-honest) owns the general factual standard.

The broad journey is defensible. Treat it as following material through representative processes, with explicit gaps between stellar generations, rather than reconstructing a known chain from one first star to the Sun. The greatest risks are turning conditions into ingredients, teaching the wrong chemistry through an attractive binary gesture, and presenting one model of Earth's formation as settled history.

## Premises the draft can build on

| Passage | Supported premise and necessary limit |
| --- | --- |
| Early universe | Light **nuclei** formed in the first minutes; neutral atoms became abundant much later as cooling allowed electrons to remain bound. Opening near recombination, roughly 380,000 years after the Big Bang, permits a comprehensible nucleus–electron interaction without pretending to enact all primordial nucleosynthesis. This is not the moment light first existed. [NASA's cosmological overview](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/overview/), [Particle Data Group's nucleosynthesis account](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2024/reviews/rpp2024-rev-bbang-nucleosynthesis.pdf). |
| First stars | Gravity, gas cooling and accretion can lead from primordial gas to a protostar. Molecular hydrogen supplies an important cooling route in primordial star-formation models; pre-existing rock dust cannot be required before any stars have made its heavy elements. Show a representative massive star without asserting that every first star had the same mass or fate. [Abel, Bryan & Norman, first-star simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0112088). |
| Stellar interiors | Nuclear burning builds heavier nuclei under stellar conditions. Different stellar populations and events contribute different elements; normal stellar evolution, stellar winds and explosions all matter. A supernova can eject elements made earlier as well as synthesize others. Do not teach that every element heavier than helium is first manufactured in the explosion, or that ordinary fusion continues profitably through uranium. [Kobayashi, Karakas & Lugaro's chemical-evolution calculations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04660). |
| Enriched cloud | Cooling ejecta can form dust, and later star-forming material contains contributions from earlier stellar populations. Dust in SN 1987A is observed evidence for a supernova dust-production route, not proof that all such dust survives or that this was the Sun's particular source. A narrative jump to a mixed, enriched cloud is appropriate. [Matsuura et al., SN 1987A dust observations](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20110015451). |
| Cold dust and ice | Water can form on cold grain surfaces through reactions involving deposited oxygen species and hydrogen atoms. Laboratory work used very cold substrates and traced reaction products; this is a surface-chemistry premise, not liquid droplets forming in an empty cold cloud. Keep reactive surface oxygen distinct from oxygen locked inside a silicate mineral. [Dulieu et al., laboratory water formation](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.2082), [Ioppolo et al., hydrogenation of oxygen ice](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.0129). |
| Water inherited by a disk | Isotopic measurements in the V883 Ori disk connect disk water with protostellar and cometary reservoirs and support inheritance from earlier cold material. They do not demonstrate that every terrestrial water molecule survived the entire journey unchanged. Spatial temperature differences matter: water ice, vapour and refractory solids are different materials. [Tobin et al., V883 Ori observations](https://www.eso.org/public/archives/releases/sciencepapers/eso2302/eso2302a.pdf). |
| Dust to planetesimals | Small grains can adhere, but larger collisions can bounce or fragment; perpetual pairwise sticking is not a complete formation mechanism. Concentration of solids by their interaction with disk gas, followed by gravitational collapse, is a researched route across this difficulty. Present concentration and collapse as a physical episode, not a universal, precisely known assembly line. [Zsom et al., collision model](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/200912976), [Johansen et al., planetesimal-formation simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.3890). |
| Growing, differentiated worlds | Accretion, impact heating and metal–silicate separation overlap. Metal can segregate toward cores while silicate remains above; later collisions combine bodies that are already differentiated. Avoid a cold, complete Earth that receives a separately fabricated iron ball as its core. [Rubie et al., coupled accretion and differentiation model](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.3509). |
| Moon-forming impact | A giant impact is a strong working premise, but impactor properties, mixing, debris evolution and timing remain actively constrained by simulations and samples. Some simulations form a satellite directly; others grow it from a disk. “An impact scenario that could have made the Moon” is more honest than a uniquely established recipe. [Kegerreis et al., direct satellite formation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01814), [Nimmo et al., lunar remelting and chronology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08231-0). |
| Hot planet to liquid water | A magma ocean, volatile release, atmosphere and heat loss interact. A steam-rich atmosphere can delay cooling; retained water can condense when conditions permit. A modelled cooling time is not a known historical stopwatch. Outgassing explains transfer from interior to atmosphere, not the original cosmic acquisition of hydrogen. [Lebrun et al., coupled atmosphere–magma-ocean model](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jgre.20068). |
| Early crust and sea | Ancient zircons provide evidence interpreted as crust interacting with liquid water by about 4.4 billion years ago. This establishes neither the exact first rain nor a recoverable map of the first coast. A basin receiving rain can represent local accumulation within a wider cooling world. [Wilde et al., original zircon study](https://doi.org/10.1038/35051550). |

## The transitions most likely to teach something false

**Atom-making is not nuclear burning.** Two neutral hydrogen atoms do not make a helium atom merely by touching. Nuclear reactions must conserve the relevant particles and take place under suitable conditions; chemical bonding rearranges electrons. Avoid a literal `hydrogen + hydrogen → helium` tutorial. An atom-level opening should distinguish a nucleus from the completed atom. A stellar-burning action should represent a whole process in a stellar interior rather than silently claiming that two icons are its balanced equation. Light already exists before recombination; light emitted by one result is not a reusable packet that powers all subsequent cosmic history. These are consequences of the nuclear/atomic distinction in the sources above.

**“Cold dust” is doing real work.** Grain surfaces retain reactants and enable reaction sequences that would not occur the same way in dilute gas. Name the input sufficiently clearly to distinguish hydrogen **atoms** from hydrogen **molecules**, and reactive oxygen on a surface from a rock's oxygen-bearing lattice. Lamberts et al. specifically found that direct H₂ + O is unlikely to be an important water-producing route in cold clouds; selecting the familiar formula without its mechanism would be a substantive error. The appropriate abstraction is a complete grain-surface reaction episode, not an artificial series of single-hydrogen chores. [Lamberts et al., experiments and modelling](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.3055).

**Water-rich bodies are one contribution, not a solved exclusive origin story.** Hydrogen-bearing planetary building materials need not be visibly icy. Barrett et al. found hydrogen associated with sulfur-bearing material in enstatite chondrites and proposed substantial inherited hydrogen. Separate experiments and core-formation modelling by Tsutsumi et al. require contributions from carbonaceous material as well. These results constrain reservoirs differently; neither warrants the story declaring that all oceans came from a final comet, or that asteroids were irrelevant. Carry water-bearing material into the growing Earth, allow later additions, and distinguish a body's hydrogen inventory from free liquid water. [Barrett et al., 2025 paper/version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13637), [Tsutsumi et al., 2025 experiments and models](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-65729-5).

**The inner disk is not simply “where water cannot exist.”** Water vapour has been detected in the terrestrial-planet-forming region of PDS 70. The study considers local chemistry, shielding and replenishment. This is evidence about another system, not a direct observation of the young solar system, but it rules out teaching the snow line as a boundary with no water of any kind inside it. Use the distinction between ice stability and water's existence. [Perotti et al., JWST observations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10432267/).

**Earth and Moon must both survive the depicted impact accounting.** If the collision produces a debris state, it must still describe the surviving/reworked Earth; the later surface cannot belong to an inexplicably recreated planet. Theia is the conventional name for a hypothetical impactor, not an independently observed world whose precise composition and trajectory are known. The familiar Mars-sized depiction is a selected scenario. Preserve uncertainty in the visible framing as well as author notes, since readers may skip explanatory cards. The collision sources above support this distinction.

**An early sea does not require modern continents, modern air or modern tectonics.** Separate initial solidification, later crustal reworking, weathering and the emergence of substantial land. The amount, distribution and tectonic setting of Hadean crust remain inferred from sparse evidence. A 2024 zircon study argues for meteoric water interacting with crust at or before 4.0 billion years ago; it is not a license to give the very first ocean a mature river network or a modern continental landscape. A basaltic local shore is a narrative reconstruction, not an observed historical site. [Gamaleldien et al., hydrological-cycle inference](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-024-01450-0), [Drabon et al., evidence for heterogeneous Hadean crust](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7923375/).

**Weathering changes material; it does not automatically make soil or life.** Water–rock contact, breakdown of minerals, dissolved constituents and sediment movement offer physical consequences if the chosen scene actually needs them. They must not be padded into a prerequisite ladder for liquid water to exist. A particular clay mineral, salinity, carbonate deposit or stable carbon-cycle feedback would require its own local chemical and environmental premises. Those details have not been verified by this review and should not be supplied solely to increase the combination count.

**Tides are a response of an existing world, not water-making.** Lunar forcing can affect rotation, mixing and heating. Coastal tide heights also depend on ocean geometry and dynamics; “closer Moon” does not justify a fixed towering tide everywhere. Biewald et al.'s climate simulations illustrate sensitivity to ocean and geothermal assumptions, rather than reconstructing the first shoreline. Wind-driven waves and lunar tides should remain distinct. The Moon can make the final scene richer without becoming an ingredient necessary for an ocean. [Biewald et al., early-Earth tide and climate modelling](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2024PA005016).

## Constraints on a full-length combination design

These are review recommendations, not a proposed manuscript or a claim that 25 chapters can sustain 200 good actions.

- Treat a combination as a **complete physical operation at the chosen scale**, not automatically a balanced elementary reaction. Its two visible operands must still explain the material contribution or meaningful contact.
- Give cooling, compression, rotation and radiation concrete settings and consequences. An unlimited “time” or “gravity” token used on everything would conceal the mechanism and make very different processes look interchangeable.
- Find variety in processes that change what material can do: gathering, collapse, nuclear transformation, ejection, condensation, sticking, concentration, melting, separation, impact, degassing, condensation of vapour, transport and accumulation. Merely renaming successive sizes or temperatures is not additional scientific content.
- Maintain separate rocky and volatile-bearing threads where they genuinely contribute differently. Do not force every small grain, molecule or planetesimal through the same exhaustive sequence.
- Do not imply that a later process caused an earlier one: water can precede the Sun; planets begin assembling while their star and disk evolve; core formation overlaps accretion; some water can predate the Moon-forming impact; a sea need not await weathered continents.
- Use an enriched cloud and the evolving Earth as continuous material systems. An inventory icon may represent a sample or population; a unique Earth, Moon or star needs an explicit identity and fate.
- When a mandatory action is merely elapsed time, a universal physical law or an unverified chemical detour, bundle it, frame it as an event, or remove it. Factual defensibility alone does not establish an enjoyable combination. The first manuscript still needs a recipe-level authenticity review and independent forward readings.

Source access note: papers, author repositories and agency scientific material were checked through web search/open on the review date. Several publisher pages restricted direct opening; the cited records or author-hosted versions supplied the claims used here. Newer models are cited as models, not as consensus replacements. No origin-of-life mechanism was reviewed; the first-sea ending avoids implying that water and chemicals make life through one known recipe.

## Sources checked for the full manuscript

The manuscript distinguishes helium’s earlier recombination from hydrogen’s later last-scattering era. [Rubino-Martín, Chluba and Sunyaev](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.0594) calculate the separate recombination histories. The opening treats them as processes within an era, then locates hydrogen transparency at about 380,000 years.

The forming Sun shines by accretion and contraction during the gas-disk chapters. Main-sequence solar hydrogen burning begins much later than the early gas-rich disk work: compare [Boothroyd and Sackmann’s solar evolution model](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20010111092/downloads/20010111092.pdf) with [Dent et al.’s disk observations](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140010832). Sunlight does not require an already main-sequence Sun.

The carbon-rich evolved-giant passage uses shell burning, deep mixing and envelope loss as distinct processes. [Marini et al.](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202039613) model carbon-star evolution and dredge-up; [Liljegren et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06735) examine atmospheric shocks and radiation pressure in dusty winds. The chosen giant is a representative carbon-rich case, not the fate of every lower-mass star.

The gas-rich/gas-poor giant comparison follows a core-accretion scenario. [Frelikh and Murray-Clay](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.00862) model modest envelope acquisition in a depleted nebula and discuss the timing constraints. This supports the selected comparison, not a unique reconstruction of Uranus or Neptune.

Hydrothermal water reacts with the local buried rock, rather than requiring rock previously weathered by rain. [NOAA PMEL’s circulation account](https://www.pmel.noaa.gov/eoi/chemistry/circulation.html) identifies the roles of heat, rock, fluid composition, pressure and circulation. Mineral products remain composition-dependent in the story. The earlier transition from steam to retained liquid follows coupled atmosphere/surface physics, illustrated by [Zahnle et al.’s steam-atmosphere model](https://doi.org/10.1016/0019-1035(88)90031-0); it is not an exact historical timetable.

The tidal passage separates a rocky body’s internal gravity from the Moon’s different pull on Earth’s near and far sides. [NOAA’s tide explanation](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/about/k-12-education/oceans-coasts/what-causes-tides) supports that distinction. The story also retains rotation and basin geometry and makes no numerical claim for an early coastline’s tide height.

The advanced-burning alternatives bundle contraction through successive fuels, including neon burning between carbon and oxygen and the silicon-burning network before an iron-group core. [Ray’s account of massive stars as thermonuclear reactors](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.5407) and [Laplace et al.’s stellar-evolution models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02058) support those stage connections. These are complete evolutionary episodes, not a claim that squeezing cold carbon alone makes oxygen or iron.

## Dense stellar remnants

The final compact-remnant combination pairs a collapsed core with pressure in dense neutron-rich matter, rather than adding gravitational collapse twice. [NASA Goddard’s neutron-star introduction](https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/neutron_stars1.html) describes collapse being halted in an appropriate mass range; the story leaves the exact mass boundary and uncertain dense-matter equation of state unspecified. Its pressure token includes the physical support of neutron-rich matter and does not claim a gas of noninteracting neutrons alone explains every stellar interior.
