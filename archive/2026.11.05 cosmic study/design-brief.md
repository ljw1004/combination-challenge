# Cosmic: The Lights That Remain

## Purpose and attitude

**What can a collision destroy when almost every star survives?** Follow matter through the encounter of two gas-rich spiral galaxies. An old orange star loses its former orbit; extended gas collides and makes new stars; those newly made stars later pass through a galaxy differently from the gas that made them. The ending is a shared cosmic arrangement whose constituents have different histories.

The cosmos itself supplies wonder and attachment. Seek the clarity, scale, causal curiosity and philosophical resonance of Carl Sagan’s cosmic narration. No human protagonist, domestic framing, observer, deity or inevitable march toward life is needed. The narrator is warm, exact and curious. Let surprising physical consequences carry the philosophy rather than announcing what the player should feel.

## Scope and development

The user has authorized a complete combination game aiming for approximately 200 combinations, repeated fresh reviews and fast playthroughs, detailed playtesting, and then artwork. Develop it within this project. It remains outside the released catalogue; release is a separate readiness decision. The earlier sea-and-Moon manuscript and artwork are an unreleased baseline, not the current design constraint.

Use [the active study](README.md) and [working notes](working-notes.md) for experiments, decisions and outcomes. At every substantive creative revision, develop two or three meaningfully different candidates using the preceding learning. Compare changes in structure, participation, causal work and payoff; cosmetic variants of one solution do not count. Kill weak scenes and reconsider the premise when necessary. The count is an ambition, not a reason to retain repetitive transformations.

The chosen architecture follows material through a galaxy encounter, including a substantial excursion into the smaller star's disk. Keep the made world and moon, the moon that becomes rings, the rubble world that loses its sun, and stars whose companionship changes. These give different concrete meanings to survival. The disk's departing material must enter a cold cloud and later contribute to the central stars through required combinations. Each change of scale identifies actual earlier material and returns something consequential to the larger story.

The selected game has 113 combinations across 22 playable chapters and an ending, with an 80-combination canonical completion. Tests of both a fuller material-history game and a shorter remnant-focused alternative informed that choice. Repeated preparation was cut rather than filling the approximate 200-combination ambition; keep the distinct consequences and actual ancestry when revising the count.

The orange star is a recurring landmark at wide views; the lane-born population remains the final unfinished encounter. This is a connected set of material histories, not one indestructible parcel or a single clock. Make early disk loss and later rocky impacts explicit when a scene spans both. During the long galactic separation, gas can spread, mix and gather again without losing its material ancestry. Do not preserve a shell's exact form for the whole merger interval.

## What the player handles

Use a small recurring vocabulary of material things: stars, gas clouds, dust, stones, ice and their significant arrangements. No collectible Gravity, Cooling, Time, Compression or nuclear-reaction inventory. Necessary processes belong in the physical receiving setting and concise result explanations. Distinguish unique bodies from representative portions of a reservoir; one particular star or world cannot take incompatible simultaneous journeys.

Every gesture represents a complete natural operation, with necessary physical conditions and elapsed time established in the scene or result. A cloud collision can include shock heating, energy radiated away and later collapse; it does not instantly fuse clouds into stars. Avoid long ladders of renamed states. A result should change what can happen, reveal a useful contrast, or contribute to the story’s question.

Follow the [complete-operation technique](../../docs/story-authoring.md#complete-operations-and-elapsed-time), [recurring identity technique](../../docs/story-authoring.md#recurring-objects-and-places) and [physical prerequisite rules](../../docs/story-authoring.md#physical-prerequisites-and-late-events). Fixed regions must be recognizable physical places. Later returning regions need distinct logical receiving identities where earlier availability would reveal the payoff prematurely. Preserve actual created young stars through their return crossing, and reveal their stellar behavior after the action.

## Pacing and comprehension

Prefer two purposes and roughly four to eight starting things per scene. Keep chapter openings short and oriented: where in the encounter, what has changed, and what draws attention now. Let combinations enact the important events. Goals invite a physical investigation without giving away every result. Earlier creations must do useful later work.

Assume players may skip optional readings. Names, visible circumstances and consequences should support a sensible next action without prior astrophysics expertise. Explain specialized mechanisms after a concrete reason to care. Optional discoveries should offer a different consequence, not inflate the recipe inventory or demand completion for its own sake.

## Scientific contract

This is one selected, physically plausible encounter rather than a claim about every merger. Stars are compact and widely separated; their orbits can change without stellar collisions. Gas occupies extended regions and interacts directly. Newly formed stars acquire the first behavior. Not all gas forms stars; radiation, outflows and explosions alter the surroundings. A selected rounded remnant does not imply all mergers erase disks.

Contraction already releases light before sustained hydrogen fusion. Dust can hide existing stars and also absorb or scatter radiation, with the actual material and geometry determining its effect. Planet formation, if retained, needs established dusty disks and plausible local conditions; successive collisions can accrete, erode or disperse material. No automatic life, liquid ocean or Earth copy is required. Keep established results, chosen models and uncertainty distinct. Maintain sources and bounds in [research](research.md).

## Review and playtesting

Follow [story authoring](../../docs/story-authoring.md) and [fun and evaluation](../../docs/fun-and-evaluation.md). Use fresh reviewers for each assignment and revision, with current inputs and requirements but no previous verdicts. Separate narrative necessity, scientific plausibility, forward understanding, mechanics, visual recognition and continuity. A plausible explanation does not prove an intuitive gesture, and completion does not prove engagement.

Use fast tests to develop hooks, arc and combination choices, then detailed tests to examine information seeking and meaningful decisions. Retest changed passages and verify the complete final route. Keep actual version identities, scope and evidence limits with outcomes. Human feedback overruled the earlier acceptance; AI approval alone cannot establish that the new game is enjoyable to people.

## Visual direction

Artwork follows textual development and playtest iteration. Material things use circular icons. Fixed receiving regions use a clearly different open, line-drawn treatment that belongs to the cosmic scene; processes do not masquerade as collectible objects. Compare two or three art directions on the same representative sequence before selecting a style. Preserve clear silhouettes and readable names at phone size.

The selected style uses softly blended luminous pastel and chalk-gouache forms on blue-green, plum, cream and amber fields. Portable illustrations display at 80 px; open charcoal receiving drawings display at 160 × 168 px, with transparent space separating their names from dragged-item labels. Backgrounds keep a pale, quiet working area. The 145 circular appearances include four age-adjusted returns; 21 fixed identities have 26 explicit chapter presentations, and 13 backgrounds cover the encounter. The ending inherits the final crossing's scene. Production details belong in [artwork](artwork/README.md).

Recurring stars, clouds, disks, worlds and galactic landmarks retain identifying forms through permitted changes. Show chapter-entry conditions in the backgrounds, leaving discoveries to the player. Do not paint completed results into an earlier setting or use busy cosmic texture where the player needs working space. Follow the [icon guide](../../docs/icon-guide.md), including separate recognition and continuity reviews and browser inspection of actual combinations.
