## `b-ch1` Grains against grains
background: ![The young system within the galactic encounter](../assets/backgrounds/background-03.webp)

During those same early millions of years, another descendant of the dark lane grows beside the lane-born stellar population: this young star and its disk. Within the disk, gentle encounters gather grains; a fast encounter can undo the gathering.

> NOTE: The imported young-system is the actual derived host. Warm-disk material belongs to that same system. Settled dust is a continuing reservoir; a destructive encounter does not restore an earlier unique clump. Rebuilding is optional, and both loose fragments and rebuilt clumps have useful later routes.

**Goals**
- Gather rocky pebbles in the warm disk -- `b-hard-pebbles`
- See what a hard collision leaves
  - either: `b-chipped-grains`
  - or: `b-rebuilt-clumps`

**Hotspots**
- `young-system`
- `b-warm-disk`

**Items**
- `b-falling-dust` Dust falling into the disk
  These inherited grains came with the gas that formed this particular young star. They are a small incoming portion, not a new disk.

- `b-slow-grains` Slowly meeting grains
  A neighboring supply of grains moving gently relative to one another. Several portions can take part in local encounters.

- `b-fast-grains` Fast crossing grains
  A narrow stream crossing the slower material. Its speed relative to the loose clumps makes their collisions destructive.

- `b-icy-grains` Ice-coated grains moving inward
  These grains began farther out in this same disk. Gas drag is carrying them toward its warmer region.

- `b-warm-disk` Warm inner disk
  The inner part of the disk already surrounding the followed young star. Rock grains can remain here where their ice coatings cannot.

- `b-settled-dust` Dust settled into the disk
  The falling grains join the existing disk. Drag against its gas changes their motion, and much of this material settles toward the disk's crowded middle plane. Separate portions remain available for the first gatherings and for later encounters with broken grains.

- `b-fluffy-clumps` Fluffy grain clumps
  Gentle encounters let small grains stick in open, fragile gatherings. They are not yet stones, and a harder encounter can undo them.

- `b-chipped-grains` Grains from shattered clumps
  The fast grains strike the fragile gatherings. Much of their apparent growth comes apart: the same solid material is again divided among smaller pieces.

- `b-dry-grains` Grains stripped of their ice
  As the inward-moving grains enter the warm disk, their ice evaporates into the surrounding gas. The rocky grains remain. The lost ice has changed phase, not vanished.

- `b-hard-pebbles` Rocky pebbles
  Repeated gentle encounters build these selected dry grains into compact pebbles. This operation stops well short of claiming that sticking alone can build a planet.

- `b-rebuilt-clumps` Clumps rebuilt from fragments
  Broken grains meet fresh settled dust or the slowly meeting grain supply gently enough to stick again. These are new gatherings made from the actual fragments; the earlier clumps have not been restored. Rebuilding is one route onward, not a compulsory repair.

- `b-ground-icy-grains` Shattered ice-coated grains
  A fast encounter breaks the incoming icy grains before they reach the warm region. Smaller fragments still carry ice; fragmentation and evaporation are different ways to change the same material.

## `b-ch2` One world, a moon, and the pieces
background: ![The young system within the galactic encounter](../assets/backgrounds/background-03.webp)

Within this same disk, gas-rich bands crowd the followed solids together. One world can acquire a hot interior and a moon while a smaller neighbor is broken into material that takes several different futures.

> NOTE: The formation operation bundles selected gas-mediated concentration, gravitational collapse and elapsed accretion, not limitless sticking. The growing world has one linear identity through its impact and moon. b-shattered-body is a preserved fragment reservoir; b-rubble-pile is one actual required body drawn from a portion of it and survives for the later encounter. The giant-impact moon is a selected model, not Earth or its Moon.

**Goals**
- Give the surviving rocky world a moon -- `b-moon-world`
- Gather a small world from a shattered body's pieces -- `b-rubble-pile`

**Items**
- `b-gentle-body` Small bodies approaching slowly
  A separate population of rocky bodies on gently converging paths. One member can join the larger world; another can meet a fragment cloud. These selected collisions retain much of the arriving mass.

- `b-large-impactor` Large worlds on crossing paths
  A separate population of large rocky bodies in the inner disk. One selected member strikes the growing world and melts much of its interior; another later approaches that same survivor at a grazing angle. These are distinct impactors, not one body used twice.

- `b-metal-fragments` Metal-rich rocky fragments
  A separate portion of the inherited solids. Metal already exists in this disk; the young star is not manufacturing it for these worlds.

- `b-fast-stones` Fast rocky projectiles
  A population of fast stones and smaller rocky bodies crossing the inner worlds and debris. Individual portions strike different targets; survivors remain among the planets for later encounters.

- `b-slow-orbital-rubble` Rubble already orbiting the struck world
  A small, separate supply already orbiting the tracked world. Its motion is close enough to that of the impact debris for repeated collisions to gather material.

- `b-rocky-bodies` A population of rocky bodies
  Rocky pebbles encounter either loose fragments or clumps rebuilt from those fragments in a crowded, gas-rich band. In the selected formation model, mutual drag concentrates the solids until dense patches collapse under their own gravity. The operation includes that concentration and collapse; it is not unrestricted grain-by-grain sticking. Many separate rocky bodies emerge.

- `b-growing-world` A young rocky world
  One of the newly formed rocky bodies meets a slower incoming body. Repeated accretion and the retained material of this encounter leave a growing world. This is the particular world followed through the hot interior and moon-forming impact.

- `b-molten-world` The rocky world with a molten interior
  The large impactor strikes the actual growing world. A substantial part of the impactor remains, and the deposited energy melts much of the survivor's interior. The world has changed; it has not been replaced by a fresh body.

- `b-layered-world` The rocky world with a dense core
  Metal-rich fragments join the hot world. Within its molten regions, dense metal can sink while lighter rock remains above. The operation includes settling and heat loss.

- `b-shattered-body` Fragments of a shattered small body
  Fast rocky projectiles strike a different, smaller member of the same solid population. Its weaker binding permits disruption rather than merely cratering. This is an extended fragment reservoir: one portion can gather into the rubble body while other fragments remain between the planets.

- `b-rubble-pile` A small world held together as rubble
  A portion of the shattered body's fragments meets a slowly approaching rocky body. Repeated low-speed collisions and gravity leave this one loose, bound aggregate. It is a distinct small body, separate from the continuing fragment reservoir and from the moon-bearing rocky world.

- `b-impact-world` The struck world inside an orbiting debris cloud
  The grazing impact strikes the same layered rocky world. Some of both bodies joins the survivor; some rock enters orbit around it. This single result contains the surviving world and its actual surrounding impact debris.

- `b-moon-world` The rocky world with a small moon
  Orbiting rubble meets the impact debris. Far enough from the world for loose clumps to hold together, repeated collisions and gravitational gathering leave a moon around the same struck world; other fragments fall back or escape. This is one selected moon-forming history.

- `b-metal-coated-body` A rocky body with metal-rich surface rubble
  Metal-rich fragments strike and accumulate on a separate small body that remains mostly solid. The added metal stays mixed through surface rubble instead of sinking promptly into a deep core. The material alone does not determine the interior.

**Combinations**

- `b-hard-pebbles` [consume] + `b-chipped-grains` [consume] -> `b-rocky-bodies`
- `b-rocky-bodies` [consume] + `b-gentle-body` [consume] -> `b-growing-world`
- `b-growing-world` [transform] + `b-large-impactor` [consume] -> `b-molten-world`
- `b-molten-world` [transform] + `b-metal-fragments` [consume] -> `b-layered-world`
- `b-layered-world` [transform] + `b-large-impactor` [consume] -> `b-impact-world`
- `b-impact-world` [transform] + `b-slow-orbital-rubble` [consume] -> `b-moon-world`
- `b-rocky-bodies` [consume] + `b-fast-stones` [consume] -> `b-shattered-body`
- `b-shattered-body` [preserve] + `b-gentle-body` [consume] -> `b-rubble-pile`

## `b-ch3` The world that takes the gas
background: ![The young system within the galactic encounter](../assets/backgrounds/background-03.webp)

The view returns to the first few million years of this same disk, before most of its gas escaped. Farther from the star, ice adds to a large solid gathering; the surrounding gas gives that body a future unlike the inner rocky world's.

> NOTE: The explicit return in time keeps early gas-giant formation distinct from the longer rocky-impact history. The cold-ring operation bundles a selected sufficiently massive concentration and accretion history; a ring alone does not guarantee a core. Gas capture and radiative cooling form one complete core-accretion operation. icy-survivors is a distinct selected set of bound scattered bodies, made before the giant's later retirement into ringed-giant.

**Goals**
- Let the large outer body encounter the disk's gas -- `b-giant`
- Follow what gathers around the giant and what travels beyond it
  - both: `b-loose-moon`
  - and: `icy-survivors`

**Hotspots**
- `b-cold-drift`
- `b-giant`

**Items**
- `b-frosty-dust` Frost-coated dust
  An outer-disk population of inherited grains. Their ice coating is solid in this colder region. Distinct portions can gather onto stones or coat fragments after a collision.

- `b-small-stones` Small outer-disk stones
  Rocky fragments already present among the outer grains. These are a separate material portion from the inner pebbles.

- `b-cold-drift` Cold ring where icy solids collect
  A cold pressure maximum in this existing young star's gas-rich disk. It contains enough solid material for the selected concentrated swarm to form substantial bodies. Gas drag, local crowding and subsequent gravitational gathering are part of the setting; the place is not a universal planet-making machine.

- `b-cool-disk-gas` Cold gas around the outer body
  A reservoir flowing through the outer body's neighborhood. Some portions can become part of the giant; others enter with enough angular momentum around the planet to settle into a small circling disk.

- `b-planet-dust` Dust near the outer body
  Inherited grains remain near the growing outer body. As gas settles into a disk around the giant, separate portions enter that flow or are swept up by the moons growing within it.

- `b-moon-ice` Slow ice-rich pieces around the giant
  A population of ice-rich pieces and loose rubble on gently converging paths within the giant's cold outer disk. Different portions join moonlets or meet later fragments beyond the close, disruptive region.

- `b-near-giant-bodies` Icy bodies passing near the giant
  A separate subset of the same disk's outer solid population. Their approaching paths take them through the giant's gravitational surroundings without physically striking it.

- `b-icy-pebbles` Icy pebbles
  Frost-coated dust gathers on the small stones. The resulting pebbles contain both rock and ice.

- `b-icy-core` A large ice-and-rock body
  The outer pebble population drifts into the cold gas ring and becomes strongly concentrated. In this selected model, dense patches form bodies that accrete nearby solids over an extended interval. One substantial ice-and-rock body emerges from that whole episode. Its mass matters when it encounters the gas still surrounding it.

- `b-giant` A young gas giant
  The large solid body binds some of the cold surrounding gas. The growing envelope radiates energy, permits further gas to settle, and undergoes rapid growth while the reservoir lasts. The complete accretion episode leaves this one giant, with the actual earlier solid body deep inside it.

- `b-planet-disk` Gas circling the young giant
  A separate portion of the surrounding gas enters the actual young giant's gravitational surroundings. Its angular momentum leaves a small disk around the planet rather than sending every parcel straight into it. Distinct portions of this disk can gather different incoming solids.

- `b-rock-moon-material` A dusty disk around the giant
  Dust joins part of the gas circling the actual planet. The solids are now carried through a small disk that can feed moons. Incoming larger ice-and-rock pieces can also gather in the gas disk directly; this dust contribution is not mandatory preparation.

- `b-loose-moon` A small moon made of loose rubble
  Slow ice-and-rock pieces enter the disk around the giant. Through concentration, repeated encounters and gravitational gathering, one portion becomes a weakly bound moon. Fine dust may already have joined the disk, or the incoming pieces may supply the necessary solids themselves. This moon begins just outside the region where the giant's unequal pull would disrupt it.

- `icy-survivors` Icy bodies beyond the planets
  The giant redirects the approaching icy bodies. This followed subset remains bound to the star on long orbits reaching beyond the planets; other possible encounters are outside this selected history. These are a distinct outer reservoir in the same system, not another star or a duplicate giant.

**Combinations**

- `b-icy-pebbles` [transform] @ `b-cold-drift` -> `b-icy-core`
- `b-icy-core` [transform] + `b-cool-disk-gas` [preserve] -> `b-giant`
- `b-cool-disk-gas` [preserve] @ `b-giant` -> `b-planet-disk`

## `b-ch4` Too close to remain a moon
background: ![The young system within the galactic encounter](../assets/backgrounds/background-03.webp)

Now follow that outer planet forward. A small impact changes the loose moon's close approach, while farther material has room to gather: can the same giant have both a ring and another moon?

> NOTE: The ring is a selected hypothetical tidal-disruption origin, not an established origin claim about Saturn. Impact direction, weak binding and pericenter must fit the model. The actual b-giant retires into the movable ringed-giant after its required scattering use. The separate outer moon is not made from the same unique close moon; it uses another portion of the planet's disk. Cratering it is optional.

**Goals**
- Follow the loose moon through its close approach -- `ringed-giant`
- Gather a moon farther from the giant -- `b-outer-small-moon`

**Hotspots**
- `b-giant`

**Items**
- `b-inward-impactor` A small impactor meeting the moon head-on
  A separate small body meets the rubble moon head-on. The moon already orbits just outside the region where the giant would pull it apart; this selected impact gives a modest backward push while leaving a bound remnant.

- `b-moon-stones` Stones within the giant's small disk
  A population of small rocky solids orbiting this giant. Separate portions meet the moon-making disk and later outer fragments gently enough to gather with them.

- `b-moon-projectiles` Fast stones crossing the moons
  A reservoir of distinct small projectiles. Their effects depend on the target's size and binding.

- `b-close-moon` The loose moon on a dangerously close path
  The impact changes the actual rubble moon's orbit. Some material is stripped immediately, and the surviving loose body approaches much nearer to the giant.

- `ringed-giant` Giant planet with rings
  The close-passing rubble moon enters a region where the giant's unequal pull overwhelms its weak binding. Some pieces stay on close orbits where they cannot gather into another moon; collisions spread and flatten them into rings around the actual giant. The view now follows that whole outer planet. Material farther away can remain gathered as a separate moon.

- `b-outer-small-moon` A small outer moon
  Incoming stones gather in a separate outer portion of the giant's gas disk, with any dust already mixed through that portion. The complete encounter leaves a small moon well beyond the strongly disruptive inner region. It is a distinct body from the loose moon on the close path.

- `b-scarred-rock-moon` The outer moon with fresh craters
  Fast stones strike the actual small outer moon. In this selected encounter it stays intact, with new craters and redistributed surface rubble. The close moon's loss of form is not the fate of every companion.

**Combinations**
- `b-loose-moon` [transform] + `b-inward-impactor` [consume] -> `b-close-moon`
- `b-close-moon` [consume] @ `b-giant` [transform] -> `ringed-giant`
- `b-rock-moon-material` [preserve] + `b-moon-stones` [consume] -> `b-outer-small-moon`

## `b-ch5` A system inside the encounter
background: ![The young system within the galactic encounter](../assets/backgrounds/background-03.webp)

The view widens across the same system, later in the galaxies' outward passage. Its rocky world still has the moon made by impact; its outer giant has rings, and other bodies range farther out, while some disk material is about to rejoin the surrounding galaxy.

> NOTE: The four required exports are distinct movable selections within one system: the actual host and inner worlds, one outer ringed giant, a distant icy reservoir, and between-planets debris. The earlier b-rubble-pile remains an additional unique body for the encounters strand. b-dusty-outflow is a required actual return of mixed gas and fine dust to the surrounding nursery. The inward icy body is separate from icy-survivors. The imported young-system retires into rocky-system through an actual encounter with remaining inner disk material, not through a zoom-only operation.

**Goals**
- Bring the rocky world through its disk's remaining stones -- `rocky-system`
- Follow the material left among the planets and returned outside
  - both: `collision-debris`
  - and: `b-dusty-outflow`

**Hotspots**
- `young-system`
- `b-exposed-disk`

**Items**
- `b-fast-stones` Fast rocky projectiles
  A population of fast stones and smaller rocky bodies crossing the inner worlds and debris. Individual portions strike different targets; survivors remain among the planets for later encounters.

- `b-exposed-disk` Exposed inner disk
  The inner disk of the same young star, now thinning. Starlight heats its exposed gas and arriving icy bodies; sufficiently small dust grains are also readily driven outward. The surrounding star supplies the radiation, not an inventory token.

- `b-lingering-gas` Exposed gas carrying small dust grains
  A remaining mixed portion of the original disk: tenuous gas with very small grains suspended through it. Planets have not taken all the birth material.

- `b-inward-icy-body` An icy body on an inward path
  A separate small survivor of this disk's outer population, on an eccentric path through the warm inner system. It is not taken from the icy-survivors reservoir, which remains a distinct set of distant bodies.

- `b-active-comet` An icy body shedding gas and dust
  The inward body enters the warm inner disk. Ice at its surface becomes gas and carries dust away; the solid nucleus remains. A bright tail is material leaving a body, not the disappearance of that entire body.

- `b-broken-comet` Fragments of the warming icy body
  A member of the fast rocky projectile population strikes the actual active nucleus. The impact breaks it into smaller pieces and exposes fresh ice. This is a new consequence of a real collision, not another label for the same warm passage.

- `b-dry-comet-remains` Rocky remains after the ice escapes
  The small exposed fragments continue through the warm inner region. Much of their newly exposed ice escapes as gas, leaving rocky grains and rubble. The operation follows the material made accessible by the impact.

- `rocky-system` Young star with rocky worlds
  The actual moon-bearing rocky world encounters the remaining inner stones in its own star's disk. Some strike and join it; others are dispersed. The view widens to the same young host and its inner rocky worlds, including the struck world and its small moon. The ringed giant, distant icy bodies, rubble body and between-planets debris are separate material selections within this one system.

- `collision-debris` Debris left between the planets
  The dry remains of the inward icy body meet the actual rocky fragment reservoir on crossing paths. Their collisions leave intermixed rock, rubble and smaller pieces between the planets. One contribution began in the outer cold region; the other came from the smaller body shattered beside the growing rocky world.

- `b-dusty-outflow` Disk material returned to the surroundings
  Stellar radiation heats the exposed gas, while drag and radiation alter the small grains' motion. This selected mixed portion escapes the thinning disk and enters the surrounding nursery. The star has retained compact worlds while returning some of their birth material to the larger galactic encounter.
