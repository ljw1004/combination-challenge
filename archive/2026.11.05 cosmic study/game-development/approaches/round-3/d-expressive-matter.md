# D — Matter remains usable

Fresh author candidate, 6 November 2026. Inputs: the current [brief](../../../design-brief.md), [Act I](../../act-1.md), [Act II](../../act-2.md), and [Act III](../../act-3.md). Act III was checked again after its revision to end with `c-ch10`, “The stars cross.” This is a proposed direction, not a manuscript edit or a playtest verdict.

## The direction

Make the existing repertoire behave more like matter. Broken grains should remain suitable solid ingredients. Ice should lose its ice in the warm region whether or not its previous collision followed one prescribed route. Gas expelled by the small planetary system should be able to join the gas story outside it. Familiar material should occasionally offer two plausible continuations on the same board.

The resulting shape is a network with a few memorable crossings: fragments can join a surviving body or remain in the belt; the disk can retain worlds while returning matter to the nursery; expelled gas can remain dispersed or help supply a later cold cloud. The old-gas-to-new-stars return crossing remains the last and largest change of behavior. These additions do not put a second climax after it.

**I can defend 18 additions for a first implementation candidate, with four further conditional candidates. I cannot honestly supply 30–45 worthwhile additions through this approach alone.** Even 22 new recipe declarations would not add 22 actions to a normal playthrough: alternate producers often shorten it. Treat this as increased freedom and continuity, not a way to certify a 200-action game. No new primitive supplies, new result identities, or new chapters are proposed.

The first implementation set is E01–E11, E14–E18, E21–E22. Hold E12, E13, E19 and E20 until their specific conditions below are made convincing. In particular, the two late hot-gas routes are not a licence to cool any hot cloud with any cold token.

## Exact operation ledger

The formulas use the actual current IDs. Fates distinguish unique hosts from sampled reservoirs. A reservoir marked `[consume]` contributes a portion; its reading must establish a population before the engine retains it for another required use. The same newly made result cannot be made again by its other producer. None of these formulas returns an ancestor to its earlier identity.

“Coexistence” below specifies the intended receiving chapter and material place. It is an implementation requirement, not an assertion that the untouched manuscript already carries every optional ingredient there. The later section explains the required goal and carry changes.

## Make these routes real rather than dangling recipes

Alternate result readings must retain only claims shared by their producers. Do not leave “these grains came from this one cloud” text on an output that now has another producer. The distinct input identities carry the detailed history; the result should explain their actual common outcome. Retain the explicit lineage of `lane-born-stars`, the tracked rocky world, `young-system`, and the unique giant unchanged.

- In `b-ch1`, make the two goals `b-hard-pebbles` and `b-mixed-pebbles`. Every route to the latter still uses material that actually passed through fragmentation, either directly through E07 or through rebuilt clumps. Keeping `b-rebuilt-clumps` compulsory would defeat the shortcut.
- In `b-ch2`, retain the existing cold-swarm and inner-stock goals. E08/E09 give genuine alternate routes. The extra breakdown and redusting sequence becomes a discovery, not a universal prerequisite for gathering icy solids.
- In `b-ch5`, retain the existing moon-world and belt-body goals. E11 directly supports the latter. E10 can feed the longer collision route when that is the player's chosen investigation.
- In `c-ch2`, existing `c-dust-rich-cloud` remains a goal. Its dependency paths can bring E14/E15's required earlier creations into the scene. The parent reservoir descriptions must distinguish portions; do not simply revive a retired cloud because its old ID appears in Items.
- In `c-ch6`, select an actual arrival state with both existing dust-rich material and surviving returning gas. The latter must have been reserved as a separate portion when central star formation used its earlier portion. If all returning material is narrated as one finite parcel, E18 must replace its central-star use rather than coexist with it.
- In `c-ch9`, the cloud goal should accept **either** `c-outer-cold-cloud` **or** `c-outer-dust-cloud`; retain the cluster-lit-cloud goal. This permits E22's pre-existing dust to matter. Otherwise the supposedly direct route would still force construction of the bypassed intermediate elsewhere.

Carry requirements are part of implementation. A derived Items declaration alone does not carry an object. For E10, `b-rock-fragments` is made on a mandatory earlier route; describe it from the outset as a population with a remaining portion, and ensure the later recipe dependency protects it. For E13, `b-dusty-fragments` becomes optional when E11 exists, so do not make its late use depend on optional preparation in `b-ch5`: either establish a genuine goal-supported alternate route to `b-outward-dust`, or omit E13. E03/E05 can retire their inputs after their goals have latched; their outputs have no route back to those inputs.

Recipes are global. Audit the earliest coexistence as well as these intended chapters. In particular, making a new alternate producer can pull a future primitive forward or keep an old material on screen. Any operation that becomes available early must already be physically valid there. This direction does not depend on a manuscript heading acting as a recipe gate.

The two fast-projectile substitutions remove two IDs from one reservoir family; the two slow-ice substitutions likewise remove two. This is eight removed primitive IDs, not eight new kinds of matter. No recipe should become `X + X` after these substitutions; none of the listed current operand pairs does. Check pair collisions again after any additional consolidation, because two previously distinct pairs can acquire conflicting outputs.

Do **not** consolidate `b-fast-grains` with `b-slow-grains`, or `b-fast-ice` with `b-slow-ice`; their different relative motions do causal work. Do not merge star-orbiting `b-passing-rubble` with world-orbiting `b-slow-orbital-rubble`. Do not merge `b-fast-boulder`, `b-large-impactor`, `b-grazing-impactor` and `b-inward-impactor`: size, target and encounter direction are the operation. Do not merge outer frosty dust automatically with already-captured circumplanetary grains; that would erase a material transfer.

## Why no extra ring scene, and why stop here

Ring impacts are already real work in `c-ch3`: `collision-debris + ringed-giant -> c-ring-spray`. More compatible projectile recipes could be written, but most would produce that same spray without adding a new consequence. Nor can ring spray automatically reach any outer moon: ejection speeds and the receiving moon's orbit matter. A new chapter that paints one moon with another moon's dust would need a substantial later consequence to outweigh another surface-state ladder. I would not add it for this count.

Other rejected ways to reach 30 include adding every grain type to every aggregation, treating every gas as a producer of every luminous cloud, and replaying old nuclear-light results with `c-refilled-disk`. The last is also mechanically wrong once those results have been created and retired. Additional combinations must not become a back door for recreating them.

The measure of this direction is whether a player can remember and use matter's behavior: *these are still icy pieces; that is still loose gas; the dust I broke can leave the disk*. It should increase sensible action choices while making the ingredient vocabulary smaller. If approximately 200 declarations remains important, a separate genuinely new physical payoff will be needed; this candidate should not carry that numerical burden.

## Scientific basis and first checks

The proposed operations are selected histories, not universal outcomes. Grain growth, fragmentation and radial drift interact; simply adding more collision partners does not guarantee growth. The original modeling of these competing effects provides a mechanism-level boundary for the solid routes: [Birnstiel, Dullemond and Brauer, Dust retention in protoplanetary disks](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/200912452). The warm-region routes use the existing drift and ice-loss premise supported by [NASA's Webb observations of water vapor associated with drifting icy material](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/nasas-webb-findings-support-long-proposed-process-of-planet-formation/).

For E14/E15/E20/E21, a dense cold recipient and sufficiently rapid radiation matter. Hot gas plus a cold cloud can destroy the cold phase, preserve it, or add cooled gas under different conditions. [Farber and Gronke's dusty-cloud simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07991) bound survival and dust exposure; [Gronke and Oh's cloud-growth calculations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04771) support selected growth when mixed gas cools sufficiently quickly. Applying those mechanisms to the particular fictional locations and elapsed history here is a design inference requiring a science pass, not a result established for this invented encounter.

First checks should compare a short route using E07/E09/E11 with the retained longer material history, then the disk-return path E15 and outer-gas return E21. Verify actual simultaneous inventory and both orders of unique-host transformations. Ask the player what the fragments can do before revealing a result. Record whether they remember the disk-return gas when it becomes a cold cloud, and whether that memory supports rather than distracts from the final young-star crossing. No tests have been run for this proposal; no experience claim follows from its recipe count.
