# B — Three ways to make solids worth combining

Authoring comparison for Act II, 6 November 2026. These are competing playable structures under the [active brief](../../../design-brief.md), not review verdicts.

| Approach | Concrete play | Advantage | Failure condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| One survivor, repeatedly remade | A particular rocky body grows, is shattered, reaccretes and acquires a moon. `World + Grazing impactor → Struck world with orbital debris`; `Struck world + Orbiting rubble → Same world with moon`. | Strong identity and memorable consequences. | Every encounter becomes another adjective attached to the same world; outer material exists only to feed it. |
| Parallel material histories | Warm rocky material, cold icy material and one large gas-binding body develop concurrently, then share a later stellar crossing. `Icy pebbles @ Warm gas ring → Dry rocky remains`; a separate outer icy swarm survives. | Temperature and location change what the same familiar materials can do. The player builds several consequential descendants. | Too many near-identical populations obscure continuity, or planet formation becomes a separate survey. |
| Destruction and reuse | Each constructive chain meets a destructive encounter, and its actual fragments supply the next operation. `Fragile clumps + Fast grains → Fragments`; `Fragments + Settled dust → Rebuilt clumps`. | “Combine” stops meaning automatic improvement; material identity survives a loss of form. | Repeated break-and-rebuild loops become an excuse for more clicks. |

**Chosen hypothesis:** parallel material histories, with one tracked rocky world and destructive reuse where the pieces have a later purpose. The rocky world keeps a continuous lineage through the major impact. The giant is one separately tracked body. The distant icy reservoir and between-planets debris are distinct populations in the same system. Each receives an actual derived export for Act III.

The manuscript has ten chapters and 72 authored recipes. Three are alternative producers that accept sensible material histories rather than adding mandatory steps; several further discoveries are optional material contrasts. The required path is therefore shorter than the authored count. Mechanical checking rejected two initially proposed optional operations, and gas escape was bundled with its immediate mixing to keep the final starting board within eight things. The count was allowed to fall rather than replaced with extra growth stages.

The strongest contrast is gas versus compact solids, inherited from the larger galaxy encounter. Within the disk, the narrower tests are gentle accretion versus fragmentation, ice retention versus evaporation, a sufficiently massive core taking gas, and loose material gathering outside the giant's disruptive inner region while remaining in rings closer in. Technical mechanisms belong in result readings and author notes, never as collectible forces or elapsed-time tokens.

Every two or three chapters, the opening locates this system within the still-unfinished galactic encounter. Those sentences add orientation, not mandatory orbital transformations. The final host identity includes the existing inner world, and the final escape of disk gas and small dust returns material to the surrounding nursery.

**Immediate deletion candidates after play:** repeated warm-grain processing in chapters 1–2; the sequence of fragment processing in chapter 5; the two separate moon cratering examples in chapter 7; the comet's double destruction in chapter 8. A player wanting the next cosmic consequence but feeling held up by these operations is evidence to merge or cut them. A count near 200 for the whole game does not protect them.

**Scientific limits:** the swarm-collapse, core-accretion, giant-impact moon and disrupted-moon ring histories are selected models requiring informed review. Broad disk-to-planets context comes from [NASA's planetary-system overview](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/planetary-system/); [NASA's Saturn overview](https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/facts/) describes rings as orbiting solid debris and discusses possible disrupted-body origins. Neither source validates this particular recipe graph. No claim is made that these are the origins of Earth, its Moon or Saturn.
