# C — A Thousand Ways Through

Proposed game architecture, 6 November 2026. This is an independent candidate, not an adopted revision of the [active brief](../../../design-brief.md). It develops [The Lights That Remain](../../../narrative/round-3/g-galaxies.md) and [G — What passes through the second time](../../../concepts/round-2/g-encounter.md). No manuscript, artwork or engine change is proposed by this document alone.

## Hook and participation

**Two galaxies meet. Within that one encounter, matter takes profoundly different ways into the future. Follow the paths that catch your curiosity, then bring their survivors into the same unfamiliar sky.**

The initial question remains excellent: what can a collision destroy when almost every star survives? Here it becomes an invitation to explore a place. Beyond the bright disks, a tail grows stars. Between the centres, gas collides. Beside a brilliant cluster, another cloud is being driven apart. Every widening view makes the encounter less like one catastrophe and more like a landscape of different futures.

The player has no fictional body, telescope, notebook or divine intention. Dragging brings material into an actual physical encounter. It selects a thread to follow through a natural event; it does not claim that an intelligence arranged the galaxies. A star stream contributes stars and motion. A receiving region contributes surrounding matter and a gravitational setting. The result is matter in its changed arrangement, never an observation, answer card or completed entry in an atlas.

The distinctive pleasure is **finding consequential contrasts**. After making stars in one crowded gas region, the player can pursue another cloud and discover why the encounter does not make stars everywhere. After following an outward stream, the player can find a cluster born far from either bright centre. The old orange star and the lane-born population remain compulsory anchors. They give the optional breadth a common destination.

An opening voice sample, not manuscript:

> The galaxies have begun to pass through one another. Their stars find almost nothing to strike. Their clouds find other clouds. Far beyond the bright centres, a dark knot is gathering in a stream of torn-out gas. The encounter has no single edge, and no single consequence.

## What “open atlas” means in this engine

Chapters remain ordered. There is no destination menu, unrestricted map travel or promise that twenty-six chapters can be visited in any order. The structure is spatial within three broad epochs: **the first passage, the widening interval, and the shared galaxy**. Adjacent chapters often inspect simultaneous regions; the text says when the view moves sideways rather than forward in time.

Each chapter is an atlas plate with a small working set, usually two physical receiving regions and one or two purposes. Its goal normally accepts several useful outcomes. The player chooses which material path to develop, can continue exploring after the arrow appears in the browser, and carries a few actual creations into later scenes. A different choice reveals a different part of the same encounter, rather than selecting an incompatible universe.

This is a limit as well as a distinction. The normal logical chapter test stops at completion; it does not prove that browser players will stay for optional work. The candidate succeeds only if the alternatives invite curiosity before the first goal is satisfied and some materially change later opportunities. An optional recipe that nobody has reason to try cannot justify the scale.

## The material grammar

The reusable families are **cloud gas, dusty gas, stars, stellar groups, gas-and-star streams, and material expelled by young stars**. Names introduce relevant visible distinctions: loose, compact, dusty, bright, hot. Terms such as shock, radiative cooling, tidal force, fusion and orbital energy belong in concise explanations when their mechanism matters. None becomes a portable ingredient.

The following twelve operations are concrete design examples, not checked manuscript syntax or universal astrophysical laws. Each receiving region represents a specified physical part of this selected encounter. A complete operation can span millions of years; cooling and collapse are not additional collectible steps.

| Combination | What the gesture contributes and why pursue it |
|---|---|
| Old stellar stream @ Passing stellar outskirts → Lengthened stellar stream | Moving stars encounter a changing gravitational field. The stars survive while their arrangement changes. |
| Gas from the dark lane @ Crossing cloud → Lane-born young stars | The gas reservoirs collide, compress, lose heat and form stars from part of their material. This is the required ancestry. |
| Loose bridge gas @ Fast crossing gas → Hot spread-out gas | A different collision leaves diffuse, disturbed material. It gives a concrete contrast to “collision makes stars.” |
| Tail gas @ Dense knot in the tail → Young tail cluster | The outer reservoir contributes gas to a gathering far outside the original disk. The optional cluster can survive into chapter 25. |
| Sheltered cloud gas @ Dense cloud interior → Small stars beneath dust | A different portion of an established reservoir forms a hidden population. Dust conceals the result; it does not create the stars. |
| Surrounding cloud gas @ Bright young cluster → Glowing cloud edge | The stars supply radiation; nearby gas responds. The result is a physical illuminated region, not a light token. |
| Loose dusty gas @ Exposed side of the young cluster → Dispersing dusty gas | Young stars alter another part of their surroundings. A successful nursery can lose the material from which further stars might form. |
| Outward stellar stream @ Outer reaches of the paired galaxies → Returning stellar stream | In this chosen bound stream, the surrounding gravity bends the outward journey back. A later destination now becomes possible. |
| Returning gas @ Central gas stream → Crowded central cloud | Streams meet and gather actual gas; the result supports later star formation without adding a “compression” ingredient. |
| Loose young association @ Returning stellar outskirts → Young stars on separate paths | The earlier made population passes through the stars and spreads. Stellar survival and group survival become different questions. |
| Compact young cluster @ Returning stellar outskirts → Cluster on a changed orbit | The dense group survives this selected encounter as a group. This alternative has a different final visible inhabitant. |
| Lane-born young stars @ Returning stellar outskirts → Lane-born stars on changed paths | The compulsory reversal: matter once stopped as gas now crosses as compact, widely separated stars. |

Reservoirs and individuals need different treatment. “Gas from the dark lane” can stand for many portions of a large supply; using different portions does not place one parcel on two contradictory journeys. The old orange star has exactly one evolving identity. Neither source can quietly change its physical meaning because another recipe needs it. A reservoir stays present only while its visible continued extent makes sense; preservation is not permission for duplicate unique stars or endlessly renewed clouds.

## Alternatives that the existing engine can support

Use ordinary disjunctive goals: a purpose such as “Follow a young population through the returning galaxy” can accept either a surviving compact cluster or a dispersed young stream. Both are real results, and each produces a different continuation. No exclusivity is needed for physically separate populations. The goal must describe what either accomplishment establishes without naming the hidden result.

Keep the compulsory orange-star and lane-born lineages independent of optional material. Their actual transformations and later operands establish continuity. Optional experiments cannot consume the sole required descendant. A natural shared reservoir can supply several portions, but a unique specimen follows one explicit chain. Do not depend solely on cleanup protection to make an apparently destructive choice harmless.

Recipes are global. First-passage and returning outskirts therefore require distinct fixed receiver identities, not a reused receiver plus a later chapter heading. The required results establish the material ancestry; the later chapter supplies the physical setting. Use at most two live fixed regions and avoid fixed-state retirement unless every legal order actually retires the old region.

Optional carry needs explicit work. A derived item listed in a later Items section is not supplied or automatically carried. For the tail cluster, a later disjunctive goal can accept either its descendant or an independently available required-stream descendant. If the cluster was made and survives, that goal dependency can carry it. If it was skipped, the required stream still finishes the chapter. Its original fixed nursery is absent, so the later chapter cannot magically supply a ready-made cluster. Prototype both histories and the pessimistic opportunity analysis before multiplying this pattern.

Avoid dozens of independent alternative histories carried forever. Resolve local comparisons within their plate; retain only a few important optional lineages across an epoch. There is no resource-allocation economy, failure lock, recipe-count gate or requirement to complete an encyclopedia. The engine’s continuation and opportunity checks are necessary checks, not evidence of enjoyment or a universal no-softlock proof.

## What earns the breadth, and what would reject it

The strongest optional payoff is a persistent physical consequence: the cluster made in the tail appears in the final outskirts; a loose group instead becomes part of the wider stellar population; gas left outside a nursery can encounter the returning streams. A second payoff is an immediate contrast that changes a later prediction. Producing two labels and reading why they differ is insufficient if the player had no reason to expect or care about the difference.

Begin with three small playable plates: the crowded overlap against the diffuse bridge; a tail cluster against a loose association with later carry; and the returning crossing with old stars, newly made stars and surviving gas. Together these test the architecture’s hardest promises. Do not begin by filling all 202 slots.

Run repeated fresh `--fast` tests while revising these plates, then detailed tests for inferred actions and before-result predictions. The critical prediction is whether newly made stars will now behave like stars rather than their parent cloud. Test readers who skip explanations separately from readers who use them. Browser sessions must additionally test whether people voluntarily follow optional paths before advancing, whether they recognize their later descendants, and whether peak clutter remains understandable.

After the pilot, develop two or three substantially different treatments of each weak creative passage, compare them with fresh readers, and expand the strongest treatment. Repeat fast and detailed cycles across the full manuscript before artwork. Preserve concise outcomes under the project’s normal verification policy; do not describe planned tests as passed evidence.

Reject or shorten this approach if choice repeatedly means “choose which similar adjective to reveal,” if optional payoffs are invisible without explanatory cards, or if the 73-combination route feels like the complete story while the rest feels like an appendix. A good seventy-combination encounter is preferable to padding the atlas. The 200-combination ambition survives only if the physical breadth survives those tests.

## Scientific starting points and remaining limits

The star-versus-gas distinction follows the selected narrative and the observed interaction described in [NASA’s account of NGC 3256](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/when-galaxies-collide-xnyic/). Young clusters in tidal tails and their differing possible later fates have a concrete basis in [Hubble’s tidal-tail cluster survey](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasas-hubble-traces-string-of-pearls-star-clusters-in-galaxy-collisions/). The [Antennae account](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/antennae-galaxies/) also distinguishes cluster formation from widespread later dispersal. These support the breadth worth prototyping; they do not validate every proposed recipe.

The actual cloud densities, speeds, shielding, mass loss and orbital circumstances need primary-source checking for the selected model before manuscript acceptance. Labels such as “compact” cannot stand in for those conditions. The final rounded remnant is this encounter’s outcome, not a rule for all galaxy collisions. No humans, life, domestic analogy, black-hole spectacle or planet-building detour is needed to supply an ending.
