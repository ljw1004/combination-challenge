# A — The Lights That Remain: an ensemble of matter

Working game architecture, 6 November 2026. This is a proposed expansion of [the galaxy narrative](../../../narrative/round-3/g-galaxies.md) and [encounter concept](../../../concepts/round-2/g-encounter.md), under the [active brief](../../../design-brief.md). It is neither a manuscript nor an evaluated game. No other candidate or review verdict informed this proposal.

## The wager

**An old star will survive the destruction of its galaxy. The cloud beside it will disappear by becoming other things. Which kind of survival lasts?**

Begin during the first approach. The orange star is already old; the dark lane already contains gas and inherited dust. There is no preliminary tour through the Big Bang or manufacture of chemical elements. The opening crossing establishes the central astonishment: stars pass between stars, while the much more extensive gas collides. The player then follows the consequences through four related material histories.

The proposed expansion adds a substantial **dust → disk → rocky and icy bodies** thread within the cloud's descendants. This is the principal departure from the supplied prose. It gives the player recognizable things to join, break, gather and revisit, while the encounter remains large enough to contain their entire formation. There is no Earth, ocean finale, human destination or claim that a planet matters more than the gas which never became one.

The ending returns to the scale of the opening. The former dark lane is distributed among stars, dispersed gas and solid bodies. The old orange star passes through that population, still shining. The two spirals are gone. The player has made enough of the surviving matter's history to understand why this is neither simple destruction nor simple preservation.

**This expansion is conditional.** If the dust thread makes players forget the galaxies, the answer is to shorten the game, not attach another paragraph reminding them of the premise.

## Four threads, one encounter

| Thread | Persistent identity | What it contributes to play |
|---|---|---|
| The orange light | One orange star within its named stellar stream; later views still follow that population | A recognizable return and a control case: an arrangement can be destroyed while its stars survive. Limit orbital transformations to consequential crossings and the turn home. |
| The dark lane | One established gas reservoir, followed through identified portions and descendants | Material joins material; some becomes stars, some is displaced. Later stars inherit a history the player enacted. |
| The dust within it | Inherited grains carried into one selected young stellar system | Concrete accumulation, breakage, icy versus dry material, and the survival of compact bodies amid changing surroundings. |
| The cloud's remainder | Gas left outside stars and disks, then swept outward or carried back | Prevents “cloud becomes stars” from meaning total conversion. Supplies the later gathering and makes dispersal consequential. |

The young cluster is a descendant of the dark lane, not a fifth opening protagonist. The disk is a descendant setting, not a newly delivered box of planetary ingredients. At most two threads occupy a chapter's working board. After three or four closer chapters, return to the established galactic view and show how far the encounter has progressed.

The player is following and composing natural encounters, without an in-world engineer. A drag means “bring these material histories into the stated physical encounter.” It does not mean carrying a star in a hand, choosing a galaxy's destiny, or commanding gravity. Material portions are draggable. Receiving clouds, disks, stellar regions and established worlds may be fixed. Heating, cooling, gravity, time, fusion, orbits and “survival” never become inventory tokens.

## A 26-chapter architecture

The numbers below allocate a **200-recipe design envelope**, with six to eight combinations per chapter. They are not an inventory of 200 demonstrated good actions. Each row names the different material work that might justify the allocation. Full authorship must either find that work or lower the allocation; subdivisions of an existing action do not count as evidence. In particular, the late third of this outline is under pressure.

Each chapter should contain two interdependent purposes or two worthwhile branches, normally with two to four operations per branch. One or two optional contrasts can fit; requiring every available experiment to hit the count cannot.

## Twelve combinations to test first

These are conceptual recipes, without final engine fates. Their fixed contexts are actual physical regions; each operation includes its necessary elapsed time. None is evidence that the entire chapter allocation is earned.

1. **Old stellar stream @ Other galaxy's stellar outskirts → Bent stellar stream.** The stream supplies stars; the receiving region supplies the changing gravitational surroundings. The same stars survive.
2. **Dark-lane cloud + Crossing gas cloud → Dense, cooling cloud.** Both contribute gas and relative motion. Compression heats before radiation removes energy; the result is not instant ignition.
3. **Dense, cooling cloud + Inflowing cold gas → Young stars within gas.** A complete episode of gathering, contraction and eventual sustained fusion; much material remains outside stars.
4. **Birth-cloud fringe @ Bright new stars → Expanding gas shell.** The gas is material; the new stars provide radiation and outflows. These stars must actually descend from earlier play.
5. **Dust-bearing infall + Young star's disk → Dust-rich disk.** Material joins an existing rotating system. The disk is a physical descendant setting, not a portable “rotation” ingredient.
6. **Fine dust + Porous grain clumps @ Cool disk → Larger grain clumps.** The setting establishes suitably gentle collisions. This may be the one scene where a contextual two-material recipe earns its extra interaction.
7. **Icy grains + Rocky grains @ Cold outer disk → Icy pebbles.** Both materials remain represented in the result; it does not mean cold itself has been added.
8. **Concentrated pebble swarm @ Dense disk ring → Rocky bodies.** Bundle the difficult growth transition; explain the selected formation model rather than implying unlimited grain-by-grain sticking. Research before adoption.
9. **Fast-moving rocky bodies @ Crossing inner disk → Collision debris.** A deliberately destructive result whose smaller material becomes useful in the next operation.
10. **Collision debris + Surviving rocky world → Scarred, enlarged world.** The world is the same body, and the incoming material contributes mass and impact energy.
11. **Young stellar group @ Returning stellar outskirts → Deflected young stellar group.** The critical prediction test: matter once handled as gas now behaves as compact stars.
12. **Returning cool cloud + Central gas cloud → New stellar nursery.** A familiar operation earns recurrence through its changed contributors and destination; do not replay the original seven-step formation lesson.

Reservoirs require care. “Young stars within gas” cannot simultaneously become two whole copies of the nursery. Establish distinct material portions in the earlier scene and carry their descendants; preserve a reservoir only when its portions genuinely remain available. Avoid extraction gestures whose only purpose is making the engine issue several outputs.

## Ingredient and recurrence budgets

Start with four to six material items and one or two fixed receivers. Subsequent chapters normally carry two to four earlier results and introduce no more than two fresh material supplies. Aim for four to seven movable objects at entry and no more than nine at peak. These are design limits to inspect, not measured results.

The common vocabulary stays small: **stars, gas, cloud, dust, ice, grains, stones, disk, world, stream, shell, debris**. A concrete condition can matter—cold, dense, exposed, fast-moving—but a succession of adjectives must not supply the game’s length. Technical explanations can name mechanisms after the player has a reason to understand them.

Use recurrence to change predictions: gathering can produce a cloud, a compact body or a dispersed population; collision can enlarge, shatter or barely touch its participants; a formerly dark cloud can become lights that later pass through one another's surroundings. Keep each recurring operation's novel contribution explicit. There should be fewer detailed steps the second time an already-understood process occurs.

Global recipes need actual receiving-context prerequisites. The first and returning stellar outskirts are different physical configurations, represented separately. Chapter headings and goal order cannot gate the later encounter. No compound slots are needed merely to make stars look assembled from installed parts.

## What could be enjoyable, and what could collapse

The strongest affordance is **recognition with changed expectations**: the cloud the player helped gather later crosses a galaxy as stars. The most tangible work is solids growing, breaking, and contributing again. Independent warm/icy and exposed/protected branches permit meaningful order of exploration without inventing scarce units or making cosmic survival a moral choice. The old orange light supplies anticipation through recognition, not constant maintenance chores.

The serious weakness is length. Chapters 13, 17, 21–26 could easily become “same stars, another orbital adjective.” They do not currently have eight distinct operations established. Chapters 9–12 might earn substantial making but become a separate planet game. Chapters 7, 14 and 15 risk three versions of “gas scatters and gathers.” Neither lovely narration nor scientific truth excuses these defects.

The explicit deletion version merges 2–3, 6–7, 14–15, 17–18, 21–23 and 24–26. That gives approximately **18 playable chapters and 120–150 combinations**, depending on retained solid-body work. It preserves the principal reversal and ending. This is a real rival to the 200-recipe architecture, not an emergency concession. If both versions produce the same remembered events and the longer version adds mainly renamed states, select the shorter one.

Before expansion, prototype three passages: first gas/star divergence; grain growth with an actual destructive branch; and the return crossing by newly made stars. Run fresh `--fast` players on the passages and proposed surrounding narration, then fresh `--detailed` players on the material choices. After revisions, compare the long ensemble and compressed ensemble with fresh participants; continue the numerous whole-story and focused cycles described in the [evaluation guide](../../../../../docs/fun-and-evaluation.md). Record whether players can identify the descendants, explain the different collisions, and name a consequence they wanted to reach. Completion and enthusiastic AI prose do not establish human enjoyment. Artwork waits for a viable manuscript and successful editorial/playtest iterations.

## Scientific support and limits

The encounter is a selected gas-rich merger scenario, not a forecast that every galaxy collision produces these outcomes. NASA describes young clusters in tidal tails and several possible later fates for those clusters; this supports the outer-stream branch but does not establish its precise chronology here. See [Hubble's tidal-tail cluster account](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasas-hubble-traces-string-of-pearls-star-clusters-in-galaxy-collisions/).

The proposed solid-body branch follows the broad connection between star formation, circumstellar disks, collision, and residual solids after disk gas disperses described in [NASA's planetary-system overview](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/planetary-system/). The contrast between early grain growth and externally eroded disk material has observational precedent in [Hubble's Orion disk account](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/survivor-planets-astronomers-witness-first-steps-of-planet-growth-and-destruction/). These support possibilities, not every recipe or the survival of this selected system.

Before manuscript adoption, independently verify the pebble-to-body operation, realistic relative timings, gas return, feedback effects, and the survival assumptions for the selected cluster and disk. Do not fill uncertain steps with unfamiliar physics nouns and call that explanation.
