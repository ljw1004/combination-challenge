# B — Where the Cloud Goes

## Structural experiment

Follow the material that the first burst of stars does **not** keep. The forward question is not a new survival comparison in every scene. It is a physical pursuit: the cloud makes lights, but where does the rest go? Each departure carries the player into the next setting. Planet making belongs because the same lane leaves material circling a smaller star; the route back to the galaxy belongs because that disk releases actual material. The black hole interrupts any comforting assumption that every departure eventually returns.

This is a branching material history, not the biography of one magically indestructible parcel. Keep the existing populations and reservoirs distinct. The lane-born population remains an actual earlier creation awaiting the last encounter. The orange star provides a recognizable wide-view landmark; it neither narrates nor witnesses the events.

The following roughly thousand-word narrative is the proposed literary spine. Its discoveries would be distributed among chapter openings and **post-combination** readings. It is not an opening monologue that discloses all the results before play.

## Narrative candidate

The orange star has shone for billions of years. Around it, a spiral has turned slowly through the darkness. Now another galaxy crosses its path. The star will survive. The spiral will not.

There is room between stars for almost every approaching star to pass. Their paths bend under the gravity of both galaxies. The orange light travels outward with its neighbours, leaving the curve that once seemed to contain it.

Beside that curve lies a dark lane. It has no hard edge, no single centre, no surface that could protect its shape. Another cloud enters it. Here the spaces are filled with gas. Matter strikes matter, heats, slows and gathers. The crowded lane begins a history that the orange star cannot share.

In its darkness, many centres contract. Falling gas makes them shine; continued gathering kindles sustained fire within them. Elsewhere in the same lane, a smaller knot makes a solitary star, with dusty material still circling it.

The first lights have appeared. Most of the cloud remains outside them.

Some of that gas now shines in their light. Some is struck by a narrow stream from the smaller star. Loose material is driven away; dense knots endure for longer, their shadowed interiors sheltered behind the gas that faces the stars. The nursery is dismantling itself. Follow what leaves it.

Near the base of a stretched galactic tail, surviving knots meet another supply of cold gas. They gather into a cluster beyond the old spiral. Farther out, dust carried into hot, thin surroundings is worn away. Its atoms remain, but the small solid surfaces on which other matter could gather are gone.

Other grains have taken a quieter route. They are circling the smaller star. Here the cloud's history narrows to a disk.

Grains settle and meet. Gentle encounters make loose gatherings; faster grains break them. The fragments enter further encounters. In crowded bands, stones become bodies large enough to hold other material nearby. There is no uninterrupted ascent from dust to world. Making something changes the ways it can be unmade.

One growing world melts under repeated impacts. Dense metal sinks; lighter rock remains above it. A later collision throws matter into orbit, where some gathers into a moon. A smaller body is shattered. Its pieces can make a loose rubble world, held together without becoming one solid stone.

Farther from the same sun, while gas still fills the young disk, ice adds to a large solid core. The core gathers gas and becomes a giant. Around it, another disk makes moons. One approaches too closely. The giant pulls its near side and far side differently; the moon cannot hold together. Its material spreads into rings.

The world with its moon and the giant with its rings now travel around one sun. Much of the disk that supplied them is gone. Some has joined the bodies; some has left the system altogether. Dust-bearing gas from this disk reaches a cold cloud outside. The planetary episode has an exit. Its material rejoins the galaxy.

The small survivors also have unfinished journeys. Ancient ice passes near the giant and is sent inward. Some strikes the established worlds. Some warms, loses gas and scatters dust through the spaces between them. The planets endure while their remaining surroundings continue to change.

The tail cluster, meanwhile, is crowded enough for close stellar encounters. A wide pair can be separated without destroying either star. A passing star can take another's place in a pair. One single star is flung outward.

Its path reaches the planetary system's distant outskirts. The rubble world has already been sent far from its sun by the giant. The approaching star changes its course again, leaving it unbound. A small share of the distant ice joins the passing star. The main planets remain with their original sun. There is more than one way to leave a system, and more than one thing to lose.

Inside the cluster, a different close encounter brings stellar surfaces together. Two stars merge; expelled material strikes nearby gas. Later, the cluster's most massive members return material in winds and explosions. Its smaller stars continue shining. Matter that entered stars can come out changed, carrying elements made within them.

The first passage is now far behind. Across the widening view, the orange star still shines along its displaced stream. The galaxies have slowed in their separation and turned toward one another again.

Gas swept from the tail meets returning gas. Some gathers into new stars near the shared centre. Some material that travelled through the nursery or the smaller star's surroundings arrives much farther inward, near a massive black hole.

It keeps circling. As material exchanges motion and moves inward, the external disk becomes brilliant. Nearby gas shines; dust warms. Some matter crosses the dark boundary. That part of the journey ends beyond any route the game can follow back.

Outside the boundary, a narrow jet carries material outward. It strikes clouds and inflates a much wider disturbance. Some clouds are heated and broken. Dense portions can remain cold while being carried away. Those that fail to gain enough speed to escape eventually turn inward again. Returned gas can feed the bright disk or gather into another population of stars.

Far from the centre, the surviving tail cluster illuminates dust carrying earlier material. The orange star follows its altered orbit. The moon-bearing world still circles its sun; the rubble world travels alone.

One earlier creation has yet to meet the returning galaxy: the stars made in the dark lane. Bring them to the crossing. What has becoming stars changed?

*After that combination:* Their paths bend, but their bodies pass between the other stars. The material that collided as a cloud crosses in a different form.

The winding arms are gone. Their constituents have taken many routes: into worlds, through stars, out of systems, into darkness and back toward light. None of those journeys could preserve the original spiral. Together, their remaining paths belong to a galaxy that did not exist when the cloud first met the cloud.

## Consequences for the playable structure

1. **Three encounter phases, one named material handoff at each scale change.** Label the early section consistently as the first passage and separation; state once that the disk and cluster histories unfold within that long interval. Begin the final central section with the galaxies' return. Do not suggest that rocky-world differentiation precedes all gas-giant growth. The proposed prose explicitly introduces the giant during the gas-rich disk interval.
2. **Make `b-dusty-outflow` the required bridge out of the planetary episode.** The present `b-ch5` already produces it and `c-ch1` can actually use it. Lead the latter opening with that material reaching `c-cold-neighbour`, then widen to the nearby nursery/tail flows. The alternative `a-dust-poor-gas` route stays physically legitimate, but should not silently substitute for the disk's promised contribution on the story's primary route. This needs a goal or producer decision, not merely different prose.
3. **End `b-ch5` on that departure.** Remove the earlier compulsory warm–break–warm comet preparation. Let the actual rocky debris from `b-ch2` supply `collision-debris` through a complete dispersal operation, if detailed checking supports the dependency change. Keep the later inward-comet episode in `c-ch3`, where its consequences affect the established worlds. This candidate proposes a graph edit; it does not claim the edit has been tested.
4. **Keep origin in names across later gas changes.** For the material thread, use short names such as “Disk gas in the cold cloud” or “Tail gas on inward paths” only where the actual creating recipe supports that provenance. A shared output from several histories needs an inclusive name such as “Travelled gas in the cold cloud”; a name must not lie about an alternative producer. The result text can state the actual route where separate outputs are necessary.
5. **The central flow is a genuine fork.** Preserve the external disk, jet, hot disruption, cold survival and bound return. Reduce duplicate renamed returns if their individual playtest outcomes do not justify them. Returning clouds are actual displaced outer material; the prose must never imply that matter emerges from inside the event horizon.
6. **Reserve the oldest unfinished thread for the final action.** `lane-born-stars` are produced early, used in their nursery, then retained. The closing prompt recalls their origin without stating how they will cross. The result supplies that answer. Their long absence is deliberate anticipation, but requires visual recognition and an earlier reminder that they still exist.

## Tradeoffs and questions for review

The material pursuit gives the planetary excursion a concrete entrance and exit, and links the central action to actual earlier work. It may create stronger momentum than a sequence of independent lessons about survival. Its risk is cognitive: the story contains several real reservoirs, not one parcel. Too many phrases like “some of that material” could disguise rather than solve the problem. A fresh player should be able to identify the origin of the current material at each handoff without remembering every cloud label.

This candidate also makes the original “what was lost?” question less continuously explicit. The answer emerges through vanished shapes and changed destinations, while the forward hook becomes “where does this go next?” Review whether that replacement sustains curiosity or merely turns the game into a travelogue. The close-encounter section is the weakest fit to the material-flow spine: its escaping star actually joins the planetary history, but the stellar merger is an adjacent branch. It earns retention only if that new consequence is more engaging than its interruption is costly.

This is an authored alternative, not a reviewed or playtested replacement. No canonical manuscript or mechanics were changed while writing it.
