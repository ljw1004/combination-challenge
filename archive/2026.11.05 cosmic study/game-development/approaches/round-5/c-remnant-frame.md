# Candidate C: The missing spirals

Author proposal for *Cosmic: The Lights That Remain*. This is narrative prose and a structural hypothesis, not a playable manuscript or an adopted revision. It uses the current [design brief](../../../design-brief.md) and [manuscript](../../../galaxies.md). The prose is intended to be distributed between short openings and the results of the actions described below; it is not a sequence of long chapter introductions.

## Narrative candidate

### The missing spirals

Something enormous has disappeared here.

Stars fill a broad, rounded galaxy. Across its outskirts shines an orange star, already ancient. Farther in, a thin seam of dust crosses the light. Almost every star that entered the encounter survived it.

Yet two spirals are missing. Their winding arms once stretched across tens of thousands of light-years. They contained the orange star, the dust, and much of the matter now spread through this unfamiliar shape.

What can a collision destroy when almost every star survives?

Begin before the shapes were lost.

### The first passage

The orange star travels among neighbours in one spiral arm. Nearby, a dark lane contains immense clouds of gas. Across the narrowing distance comes another galaxy.

The star enters its outskirts. Other stars pass at distances so great that their surfaces never meet. The combined pull of both galaxies bends its journey. It emerges still orange, still shining, on a path that no longer belongs to the old arm.

The dark lane meets gas.

Here the approaching matter occupies enough space to collide. Gas crowds together and heats. Some radiates its heat away; some spreads into a hot bridge between the galaxies. Farther out, an untouched cloud is drawn into a long tail.

One encounter has begun several journeys.

### Inside the dark lane

Cold gas feeds the crowded lane. Many centres gather, contract and shine. Their first light comes from falling matter; sustained fusion follows in their deepening interiors.

The lane now contains stars.

Beside this population, a separate turning knot makes one smaller star with a dusty disk. Both gatherings began in the same dark lane. Their material has already divided into different histories.

The new stars illuminate nearby gas. Their outflows sweep looser material away, exposing dense pillars behind it. Only part of the cloud has entered stars. The rest is still moving, and the stars have changed where it can go.

### What can hold together?

During these first few million years, grains settle through the smaller star's disk. Gentle meetings gather them; fast ones break the gatherings apart.

In a crowded band, solids concentrate enough for their own gravity to bind larger bodies. One small body is shattered. Its fragments remain nearby. A slow encounter gathers a portion into a loose rubble world: stones held together, with empty spaces between them.

Farther out, icy solids build a much larger body while the disk still contains abundant gas. It captures gas, loses heat and grows into a giant planet.

The rubble world and the giant now circle the same young sun. The giant's motion will matter when their paths approach.

### The tail takes the light

Beyond the spiral, the cloud drawn into the tail meets fresh cold gas. Near the tail's base, dense clumps carried from the nursery gather again and make a cluster of stars.

Within the cluster, a star passes a close pair. Their encounter tightens the pair and sends the single star outward, towards the young system's neighbourhood. None of their surfaces meet.

Gas driven out of the smaller star's disk reaches a cold cloud beside the nursery. Some of its dust survives the journey. Material that missed the growing bodies is joining another gathering.

The tail cluster's brightest stars have short lives. Within millions of years, winds and explosions carry some of their material into surrounding gas. A dense part of the swept shell cools; surviving grains acquire new material.

The cluster remains. Its smaller stars continue shining through a changed neighbourhood.

### The world without a sun

The star leaving the cluster now approaches the young system's distant outskirts.

The rubble world passes the moving giant. Without touching the planet, it gains a long orbit reaching far beyond it.

Out there, it meets the passing star.

The encounter changes its motion enough to release it from its sun. Its stones remain gathered. The galaxy still contains the little world, travelling without the star around which it formed.

A separate portion of the system's distant ice ends the encounter circling the passer. Most stays with the original sun. An orbit can be lost, or acquired, without a surface breaking.

### The long return

Hundreds of millions of years pass. The galaxies turn toward one another again. The old orange star is still travelling. Their centres draw closer, and returning gas meets gas around the emerging common centre.

Some gathers into another stellar nursery. Some reaches the surroundings of an existing massive black hole. There it forms a hot, bright disk outside the darkness.

A narrow jet from that external flow strikes distant clouds. Gas spreads into a broad disturbance; dense portions survive cold and are carried outward. Some travel too slowly to escape. They turn and return, supplying further stars or another bright episode around the same black hole.

The galaxy is changing what its matter can become.

### The crossing

The stars made in the dark lane approach the returning galaxy.

They are the actual stars whose first light appeared inside the crowded gas. The lane that formed them no longer keeps its old shape. Ahead lies the returning galaxy's stellar expanse.

Bring them into the crossing.

They pass between its stars. Their paths bend, but their bodies remain separate. The gas that made them collided here; its stellar descendants travel through.

Across the intervening chapters, the material has acquired a different form. Now the encounter reveals what that change permits.

### The lights that remain

The rounded galaxy fills the view again.

The orange star is there, and the stars made in the dark lane. The surviving tail cluster shines beyond the centre. The smaller sun carries its giant; the rubble world follows a journey of its own. Gas lies between these lights in clouds, thin streams and hot surroundings.

The spirals were arrangements: matter moving together in recognisable patterns. The encounter has undone those patterns and made another.

The two old galaxies are gone. Much of what they contained remains, along with things that could only begin when they met.

## Chapter and combination implications

- **Use one forward reconstruction after the opening glimpse.** Fold the glimpse into the first chapter's short introduction, followed by an explicit earlier setting. It supplies no future inventory, demands no inspection recipe and reveals no lane-born population. The final scene returns to that view with identities the player has earned. The framed opening is the only time reversal.
- **Keep the first crossing playable and asymmetric.** The orange-star action supplies the first surviving body; the dark-lane collision supplies the changing material. Preserve the actual `lane-born-stars` result until its final crossing. Do not give it an intermediate stellar-crossing recipe, predictive title or result text explaining its future behaviour.
- **Organise early play as one interval, then advance.** Within the first few million years: first passage, lane birth, initial disk solids and gas-giant growth, nursery clearing, tail-cluster birth, and the cluster star's ejection. Then enact massive-star deaths and the later outer-system passage. Keep brief elapsed-time cues at those actual boundaries; a change of scale does not silently advance every strand. Exact relative timing still requires a physical plausibility pass. Removing the extended rocky-planet history removes the current need to jump backward to a gas-rich disk.
- **Replace the planetary survey with a short material thread.** Retain grain sticking/breakage, concentration into small bodies, the shattered reservoir and actual `b-rubble-pile`, and the actual outer giant that scatters it. Cut the hot rocky-world/core/Moon sequence, ring-origin sequence, comet-processing ladder and later cratering/dust tour from this candidate. Those are substantial losses of making, proposed deliberately. The excursion now builds the particular world whose later lost orbit tests the story's central question. The giant remains the same body; an icy reservoir remains a separate selection in that same system.
- **Let the tail produce the passer before it produces an ageing cluster.** Retain the actual `a-tail-stars` population and the close-pair encounter that produces `e-escaping-star`. Use that derived star in the outer passage; do not replace it with a primitive visitor. Cut the separate exchanged-pair/rare stellar-merger branch here. It adds a second exceptional answer before the central answer has landed.
- **Shorten the gas journey by preserving names of origin.** Use “Dark-lane gas,” “Tail gas” and “Nursery outflow” as persistent name roots while the same material remains gas. State local conditions in short qualifiers. A mixed cloud must say which actual streams joined it; it is neither a relabelled intact parent cloud nor a fresh supply. Keep a new result only when it changes an available encounter or reveals a consequential difference. In particular, collapse the mixed-wind/stripped-edge/halo succession where its individual steps carry no independent question.
- **Keep the central passage as a return of earlier matter.** The swept tail shell or the actual cold cloud containing returned disk material supplies the central encounter. The black hole already exists. The jet's gas begins outside the horizon; its cold survivors return as their actual derived population. Compress repeated cloud-processing recipes, rather than replacing them with unexplained new reservoirs.
- **End with the player's crossing.** Keep the late receiver `c-returning-stellar-outskirts` unavailable in the earlier crossing. The concluding explanation appears only after `lane-born-stars` is used there. The opening image must not identify those stars or display a crossing outcome. A correct prediction remains satisfying because the player performs the material's changed behaviour.

## Risks to test

The frame reveals the loss of the spirals and therefore trades uncertainty about the outcome for curiosity about its cause. A reader may find that weaker than a wholly forward encounter. The planets lose much of their visual richness; this candidate needs the rubble world's particular history to create enough attachment. The central black-hole sequence can still become a detachable tour unless its arriving and returning matter remains recognisable. The full-length target remains unresolved: this proposal knowingly cuts many recipes and should not be expanded back toward a count until this shorter causal arc earns interest. No review verdict or enjoyment claim is implied.
