# A — What holds, and what can be lost

## Attitude and structural choice

Keep the forward encounter, but let each change of scale pose a more particular version of the opening question. The galaxy is an arrangement of stars; a star is a gathering of matter; a planetary system is an arrangement of worlds; a moon is a gathering of stones. These are not philosophical labels to collect. Their different responses to actual encounters supply anticipation. Each substantial episode asks what this particular gathering can keep, then lets the combination answer.

The narrator attends to relationships without treating the cosmos as a human society. There is no universal promise of preservation. Some bodies keep their material and lose their companions; others lose their form; some matter escapes entirely. Avoid repeating the aphorism after every discovery. The final action should do the clearest comparative work.

Use the orange star as a quiet recurring landmark at wide views: opening, tail boundary, the galaxies' return and the ending. It supplies continuity, not more actions. The planetary episode has its own question—how can compact bodies endure encounters?—and its own finished constructions. It need not pretend every pebble changes the merger's course.

## Opening

The old orange star will outlive the spiral that contains it.

Another galaxy is approaching. Its stars are scattered almost impossibly far apart; its gas spreads through immense clouds. Both galaxies will change. But a star, a cloud and the shape of a galaxy do not hold together in the same way.

Beside the orange star's long path, a dark lane approaches another mass of gas. Follow what meets, what passes, and what remains able to gather afterward.

This introduction promises a comparison without announcing the future behaviour of the actual newborn stars. The first goal still asks what happens to the orange light. The dark lane's first collision remains the physical beginning of the new material history.

## Actual chapter rhythm

- `a-ch1`–`a-ch3`: an old arrangement bends; the gas makes compact new bodies; those bodies change the place that made them. Avoid an extra inventory stage for each kind of first light.
- `a-ch5`–`a-ch6`: apparently displaced material has different futures. The pillar material's eventual stars are an actual consequence, while other dust loses its solid form.
- `b-ch1`–`b-ch2`: closeness changes the terms of survival. Solid grains can stick or shatter; a world can be remade by an impact and retain an orbiting companion. The question belongs to this disk, not a detached lesson about all planets.
- `b-ch3`–`b-ch5`: the large outer body can keep gas; proximity to that same body can undo a loose moon. The mature system is a recognisable gathering of the things made, while its released material supplies the next wide view.
- `c-ch1`–`c-ch3`: return to the cloud's wider surroundings and follow the actual expelled material. Make the concurrent early history explicit once, rather than treating the chapter order as a clock that repeatedly reverses.
- `e-ch1`–`e-ch3`: bindings themselves can fail. The actual escaped star reaches the actual small body from the disk; the two strands meet. The rare surface merger remains distinct from ordinary galaxy-wide overlap.
- `c-ch4`–`c-ch8`: different lifetimes and central flows alter the new galaxy's material. The bright disk, wide disturbance and returning cold clouds make a connected predicament rather than a tour of black-hole vocabulary.
- `c-ch9`–`c-ch10`: recognizable earlier material and the surviving cluster reappear. The lane-born stars carry the final unanswered comparison into the returning stellar expanse.

## Representative openings

### `b-ch1`

During the galaxies' long separation, a disk circles the smaller star made beside the dark lane. Here the distances are far smaller. Grains meet; some remain together. Before any world can endure, its first loose gatherings must survive their own collisions.

### `b-ch2`

Within that same disk, crowded solids begin to hold one another together. The galaxy's stars could pass between other stars, but these rocky bodies will meet surface to surface. What can a world keep through a collision that changes its interior?

### `b-ch5`

The view widens across the young system. Its rocky world carries the moon made by impact; farther out, the giant keeps rings and a separate moon. Between those survivors, smaller bodies still cross paths. The disk is thinning. What can leave this sun without taking its worlds along?

### `c-ch1`

While those worlds were being made, their surroundings were changing too. The orange star travelled farther along the stretched stellar stream. Near the tail's base, new stars faced gas that had never joined them, and material from the smaller star's disk entered the wider cloud. Being driven outward was the beginning of another journey.

### `c-ch5`

Much later, the galaxies turn toward one another again. The orange star is still shining along a path its old spiral never held. Below the changing stellar streams, gas meets gas once more. Some of it has already passed through a nursery or a young planetary system. Where can that travelled material gather now?

### `c-ch8`

Some of the displaced clouds never gained enough speed to leave. They slow, turn and return toward the centre that sent them outward. The bright disk and the nearby cold gas are still there. Has the outflow ended these clouds' possibilities, or changed the route by which they arrive?

## Closing treatment and cost

Keep the actual final stellar crossing and the current ending's concrete survivors. Give the old orange star and the made moon-bearing system a recognisable place in the final view. Then name the vanished arms once. The loss is a real loss of arrangement, not a revelation that nothing happened.

This treatment preserves the existing order and recipes. Its main cost is that concurrent times still need explicit orientation; thematic connections alone cannot make every planetary event precede every tail event. It also cannot rescue a repetitive sequence merely by attaching a question. The current playtests must still decide whether individual preparations earn their time.
