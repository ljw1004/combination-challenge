# Report

Play style: --detailed

**Definitions**
- Worthwhile: counts actions supported by meaningful decision-making or a valued story payoff or reason to keep playing, counting each action once. Show the average credited actions / average total gameplay actions per player, not an average percentage.
- Fun: a narrower count of meaningful decisions: worthwhile deliberation or intentional experimentation. Measured by --detailed, including inspections and unsuccessful well-formed attempts; --fast reports it as not measured. Fast menu-assisted diagnostic ratios are not comparable.
- Hooks: the players' reported desire to continue: "high" means specific eagerness, "medium" means mild or conditional interest, and "low" means indifference or reluctance

## Chapter 4: The star opens

Worthwhile: 4/4 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 1.7/4 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium/high (2 medium, 1 high)

Strongest moments: Combining the collapsing stellar core with the neutrino flood to create an outward supernova shock supplies the shared meaningful experiment. Players 2 and 3 still value the remnant explanation, and the independent graders disagree about whether the targeted inspection itself is meaningful.

## Chapter 8: A Sun with leftovers

Worthwhile: 9.3/9.7 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 7/9.7 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium/high (1 medium, 2 high)

Strongest moments: Making the planet-forming disk from the rotating core and icy dust, then heating its inner region with the young Sun: all three value inherited material becoming consequential for worlds. Disk formation is Player 1’s favorite; heating is Player 2’s favorite and supplies all three with a concrete planet-formation question. Combining the Sun’s protostar with hydrogen to make the contracting young Sun: all three revise an expectation about fusion and value the distinction between nuclear burning and accretion-powered light. Player 3 names this the most worthwhile discovery; the independent graders recognize meaningful hypothesis testing in every record.

Weakest moments: Creating sunlight from the contracting young Sun and escaping radiation after already heating the disk: the separate completion requirement feels repetitive and game-specific to Player 1 and arbitrary to Player 3. Players 2 and 3 nevertheless value the resulting clarification of which star supplies the light. The weakness concerns goal semantics and recipe intelligibility, not uniformly valueless prose. Trying the contracting young Sun with the magnetic field: Players 1 and 2 encounter state-dependent rejection without learning the field’s role, and Player 2 identifies it as the least worthwhile action. Both graders retain meaningful-test credit; the explanatory feedback is weak even though the attempts are motivated. Player 3 does not encounter this problem.

## Chapter 11: Small worlds have insides

Worthwhile: 9/9 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 6/9 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium/high (1 medium, 2 high)

Strongest moments: Liquid water inside an icy body + Silicate grains → Water-bearing asteroid rock: all three players identify hydrogen remaining in hydrated minerals after exposed ice disappears as the most worthwhile discovery. This delivers the educational making payoff even where graders classify the combination as routine execution, and feeds Players 1 and 3's curiosity about Earth's ingredients. Exposing asteroid interiors through collisions: core fragments give all three players a valued connection to iron meteorites; the later collision with a cooled differentiated asteroid provides explicitly satisfying closure as rocky fragments complement the metal. These payoffs remain distinct from the intervening cooling confusion.

Weakest moments: Obtaining rocky-layer fragments after already exposing core metal: the cooling prerequisite is not anticipated by any player. Empty inspections of core fragments and collisions, or Player 2's failed collision with undifferentiated planetesimals, lead to compatibility-assisted cooling. The shared problem is causal signaling and limited interface explanation, not command-format friction; purposeful troubleshooting retains independent decision credit despite reported low satisfaction.

## Chapter 17: Rock lets go of its water

Worthwhile: 7.3/7.7 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 4.7/7.7 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium/high (1 medium, 2 high)

Strongest moments: Dissolved volatiles + falling pressure → gas bubbles: all three players singled out understanding that outgassing releases existing material rather than creating hydrogen. This shared educational payoff survives the disagreement over whether executing that pairing constituted a new decision. Cracked volcanic crust + upwelling rock → hot lava among solid rock: all three connected the contrasting landscape to wanting to see later rain; Player 2 explicitly found the construction satisfying. The result supplied a shared onward hook despite routine execution.

Weakest moments: Getting separated bubbles into volcanic plumes: Players 1 and 2 encountered unexplained unavailability, while Player 3 found the eventual bubble–upwelling pairing unobvious. Their later appreciation of outgassing coexisted with recipe-feedback friction. Automatic diversion from volatile release into upwelling and crust formation: Player 3 explicitly found the first two redirected attempts confusing. Players 1 and 2 deliberately established and cooled upwelling rock instead, so this weakness concerns the redirection experience rather than a shared rejection of crust-making.
