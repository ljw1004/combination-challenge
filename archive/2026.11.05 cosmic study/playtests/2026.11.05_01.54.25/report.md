**Definitions**
- Worthwhile: counts actions supported by meaningful decision-making or a valued story payoff or reason to keep playing, counting each action once. Show the average credited actions / average total gameplay actions per player, not an average percentage.
- Fun: a narrower count of meaningful decisions: worthwhile deliberation or intentional experimentation. Measured by --detailed, including inspections and unsuccessful well-formed attempts; --fast reports it as not measured. Fast menu-assisted diagnostic ratios are not comparable.
- Hooks: the players' reported desire to continue: "high" means specific eagerness, "medium" means mild or conditional interest, and "low" means indifference or reluctance

## Chapter 3: Inside the furnace

Worthwhile: 8.3/9.3 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 5.7/9.3 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: high

Strongest moments: Combining carbon- and oxygen-burning layers into an onion of burning shells gave all three players a valued synthesis of sequential stages into coexisting regions. Players 1 and 2 named it most worthwhile; Player 3 explicitly called it satisfying. Player 1's assembly friction did not erase that payoff. Combining silicon-burning material with compression to form an iron-group core supplied the shared continuation hook: all three wanted to learn what happens as fusion support ends and collapse approaches. Its story value remained strong even though every grader classified the action itself as routine.

Weakest moments: Using the iron-group core or the massive star to finish the layered interior led to unhelpful compatibility dead ends. Player 1 reported mild frustration after both approaches; Players 2 and 3 named their core inspections least useful for progress. The weakness concerns discoverability of the assembly representation, while the unsuccessful investigations retain meaningful-decision credit. Repeatedly compressing burning material made the middle mechanically predictable. Player 2 explicitly identified this repetition, and all graders discounted later executions. Player 1 still valued the silicon reaction-network explanation; Players 2 and 3's corresponding reactions establish recognition of complexity more clearly than appreciation. Repetition therefore did not erase explanatory value uniformly.

## Chapter 8: A Sun with leftovers

Worthwhile: 8.3/9.3 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 5/9.3 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: high

Strongest moments: Sun’s protostar + neutral hydrogen → contracting young Sun: all three revise an expectation about sustained fusion and value the distinction between accretion-powered light and main-sequence burning. Players 2 and 3 name this their most worthwhile discovery. Rotating collapsing core + ice-mantled dust → planet-forming solar disk: inherited material becomes a concrete future-world construction. Player 1 names it the highlight; Players 2 and 3 connect it to continuation. For Players 1 and 3, subsequently heating the inner disk turns that payoff into a specific question about planets and water. Player 3’s symbolic-pairing criticism remains alongside the satisfaction.

Weakest moments: Rotating collapsing core + escaping radiation: all three plausibly transfer an earlier cooling mechanism, but receive compatibility rejection rather than a causal explanation of its absence here. Players 1 and 3 explicitly select the attempt as least rewarding. This is recipe-interface friction within a meaningful experiment, not evidence that their reasoning was arbitrary. Getting the young Sun to count as lighting its surroundings: heating the disk leaves the goal open for Players 1 and 3, while Player 2’s direct protostar–cloud attempt fails despite the protostar already being described as shining. Player 1 calls the eventual radiation pairing symbolic. Player 2’s outflow-cleared cavity provides a more satisfying causal resolution, preserving the difference between routes.
