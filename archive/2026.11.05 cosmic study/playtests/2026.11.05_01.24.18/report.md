Play style: --fast

**Definitions**
- Worthwhile: counts actions supported by meaningful decision-making or a valued story payoff or reason to keep playing, counting each action once. Show the average credited actions / average total gameplay actions per player, not an average percentage.
- Fun: a narrower count of meaningful decisions: worthwhile deliberation or intentional experimentation. Measured by --detailed, including inspections and unsuccessful well-formed attempts; --fast reports it as not measured. Fast menu-assisted diagnostic ratios are not comparable.
- Hooks: the players' reported desire to continue: "high" means specific eagerness, "medium" means mild or conditional interest, and "low" means indifference or reluctance

## Chapter 4: The star opens

Worthwhile: 4/4 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium/high (1 medium, 2 high)

Strongest moments: Outward supernova shock + burning shells → expanding stellar ejecta: all three players name this their most worthwhile action. The distinction between nuclei made during stellar life and during the explosion gives routine execution a valued discovery payoff and a bridge toward later worlds. Collapsing core + neutrinos → outward supernova shock: all three graders credit a motivated causal choice over further collapse. Players also value the qualification that this explosion is one modeled route rather than every massive star's inevitable fate.

Weakest moments: Collapsing core + unsupported gravity → neutron star: all three identify the mandatory conclusion as their weakest gameplay moment. Player 3 additionally finds the repeated gravity insufficiently differentiated from initial collapse. The problem is limited agency and, for Player 3, explanatory repetition—not interface malfunction; all three still value the alternative-remnant qualification.

## Chapter 8: A Sun with leftovers

Worthwhile: 5.3/6.3 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: high

Strongest moments: Adding neutral hydrogen to the Sun’s protostar corrected all three players’ expectations about fusion. Their explicit appreciation of contraction- and accretion-powered early light makes this a shared educational payoff, alongside independently credited hypothesis testing. Making a planet-forming disk from the rotating core and icy dust gave all three players satisfying continuity and a reason to anticipate worlds. Player 1’s additional combination of the young Sun with that disk sharpened the hook through the connection between distance, heating and which materials remain solid.

Weakest moments: Combining the contracting young Sun with escaping radiation to make sunlight repeated shining already described for the protostar. All three players identified reduced information or redundancy; Players 1 and 2 also found the recipe abstract or goal-label-driven. Player 1 still valued its source clarification. The weakness concerns presentation and goal separation, not command-interface failure. Adding hydrogen to the protostar left the exact developmental outcome unclear for Player 3, despite producing a valued correction. Players 1 and 2 shared the premature fusion expectation but did not explicitly identify this pairing as arbitrary.

## Chapter 11: Small worlds have insides

Worthwhile: 6.3/7 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium/high (1 medium, 2 high)

Strongest moments: Heating icy planetesimals and reacting their concealed liquid water with silicates: all three players valued the hidden-water revelation and selected water-bearing asteroid rock as the strongest payoff. Hydrogen travelling in rock after exposed ice disappears connected the making sequence to curiosity about Earth's ingredients. Colliding a small world with a metal core: all three players compared disruption with cooling, supporting the unanimously credited meaningful decision. The resulting iron-meteorite connection made the exposed interior tangible and helped sustain interest in what these fragments become.

Weakest moments: Cooling the differentiated asteroid and then colliding it again: Players 1 and 2 found the sequence somewhat repetitive and initially unclear; Player 3 found it predictable but purposeful. Explanations clarified the rocky exterior, and some fragment-origin value survived, but the repeated exposure sequence was the shared weak point. Compatible menus especially limited discovery for Player 3; the others' complaint also concerned recipe sequencing.

## Chapter 17: Rock lets go of its water

Worthwhile: 5/7 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: high

Strongest moments: Dissolved volatiles plus falling pressure → gas bubbles: all three valued learning that outgassing releases existing material rather than creating water’s hydrogen. This was a shared educational payoff, not a credited meaningful decision. Cracked volcanic crust plus upwelling rock → hot lava among solid rock: all three wanted to see rain encounter the contrasting surfaces. The final construction supplied a concrete continuation question, reinforced by their interest in fractures as water pathways.

Weakest moments: Cooling upwelling rock into basaltic crust and continuing through cracking to exposed lava felt repetitive to all three because the remaining combinations were prescribed. Initial crust formation had no separately evidenced valued payoff, although cracking and the final landscape still supplied story value. Young basaltic crust plus convection was initially abstract for Player 2 until fracturing explained the connection. Players 1 and 3 did not report that problem; all three valued the resulting future water pathways. This was representational friction rather than an interface malfunction.
