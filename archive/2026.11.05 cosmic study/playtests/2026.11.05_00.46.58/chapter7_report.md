Each identified this sequence as most worthwhile: adsorption explained the surface's role, hydroxyl made the chemistry intelligible, and ice supplied a payoff connected to water existing before planets. The second hydrogen addition was valued despite unanimously receiving no meaningful-decision credit. Forming Hydrogen molecules on dust through neutral-hydrogen inspection supplied a shared discovery and purposeful route-finding. All three valued the contrast with dust-free first-star chemistry; the ensuing cold molecular cloud gave the discovery a destination and supported interest in planetary-system formation.

Weakest moments: Trying to connect finished Ice-mantled dust to the dusty cloud produced the clearest friction. Players 1 and 2 found the failed combination unproductive and its restriction unclear or arbitrary; Player 1's subsequent cloud inspection prolonged the detour. Player 3's no-partner ice inspection was also least useful, but narrowed the search without reported arbitrariness. This is compatibility-legibility friction, not shared rejection of the story premise; the independently meaningful inspections retain credit.
