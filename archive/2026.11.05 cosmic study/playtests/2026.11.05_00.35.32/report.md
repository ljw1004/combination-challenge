# Report

Story: projects/cosmic-study/cosmic.md

Play style: --fast

**Definitions**
- Worthwhile: counts actions supported by meaningful decision-making or a valued story payoff or reason to keep playing, counting each action once. Show the average credited actions / average total gameplay actions per player, not an average percentage.
- Fun: a narrower count of meaningful decisions: worthwhile deliberation or intentional experimentation. Measured by --detailed, including inspections and unsuccessful well-formed attempts; --fast reports it as not measured. Fast menu-assisted diagnostic ratios are not comparable.
- Hooks: the players' reported desire to continue: "high" means specific eagerness, "medium" means mild or conditional interest, and "low" means indifference or reluctance

## Chapter 1: The light gets out

Worthwhile: 5/5 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium/high (1 medium, 2 high)

Strongest moments: Primordial gas + Trapped radiation → Freely travelling light: shared causal payoff linking neutral atoms to reduced scattering and transparency before stars. All three graders credit the prospective inference; Players 1 and 3 name this their most worthwhile moment, and Player 2 reports surprise. Primordial gas + A denser patch of the universe → A dense knot of primordial gas: all three value the connection between gravity, cooling and local binding amid expansion. Player 2 names it most worthwhile, and all three connect the knot's future development to wanting to continue.

Weakest moments: Helium formation and mixing the neutral gases are forced, predictable bridges with limited agency. Player 1 finds helium least engaging; Players 2 and 3 single out mixing. Their acknowledged explanatory benefits remain worthwhile even though these are the weakest interactions. Primordial gas + Trapped radiation feels symbolic to Player 2 and abstract to Player 3, although both say the explanation clarifies its purpose. Player 1 instead emphasizes satisfaction. This is a qualified representation weakness, not reported protocol friction.

## Chapter 18: Heat has somewhere to go

Worthwhile: 7.7/8 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium/high (2 medium, 1 high)

Strongest moments: Rain hitting volcanic ground and returning as steam, then recycling through the cooling atmosphere, gives all three players a valued explanation: water can remain on the planet without immediately forming an ocean. Choosing cooling first versus testing surface contact also supplies the clearest shared decision value. Persistent heavy rain meeting ground cool enough to retain water provides a satisfying construction payoff for all three. Recycling steam through the atmosphere and condensing it again becomes a repetitive single-option sequence for all three. Its explanatory value remains supported, but it also weakens pacing; Player 2 explicitly conditions continuation on fewer forced repetitions.

## Chapter 24: A sea beneath the Moon

Worthwhile: 4.7/8 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: low/medium (1 low, 2 medium)

Strongest moments: Returning freshwater to the shore supplied shared educational payoff: cloud plus rocky rise made rainfall's cooling mechanism concrete, and rain plus slope closed the circulation. Players 1 and 3 additionally singled out evaporation leaving minerals behind. The graders disagreed only about whether the terrain inference also earned decision credit. Reflected sunlight plus the early sea produced a valued closing image for all three players, connecting local water to ancient hydrogen and stellar oxygen. For Player 2 this satisfying closure supported stopping rather than continuing.

Weakest moments: The young Sun plus open space was unanimously identified as the weakest setup: compulsory, predictable and lacking a separately valued payoff. The problem was limited agency, not confusing combinations or interface malfunction. Cloud plus wind offered predictable transport while the only alternative postponed the water cycle for moonlight. All three described clear progression rather than a distinct valued discovery; their identical water-first reasoning supports routine execution, not a substantive choice.
