# Report

Story: projects/cosmic-study/cosmic.md

Play style: --detailed

**Definitions**
- Worthwhile: counts actions supported by meaningful decision-making or a valued story payoff or reason to keep playing, counting each action once. Show the average credited actions / average total gameplay actions per player, not an average percentage.
- Fun: a narrower count of meaningful decisions: worthwhile deliberation or intentional experimentation. Measured by --detailed, including inspections and unsuccessful well-formed attempts; --fast reports it as not measured. Fast menu-assisted diagnostic ratios are not comparable.
- Hooks: the players' reported desire to continue: "high" means specific eagerness, "medium" means mild or conditional interest, and "low" means indifference or reluctance

## Chapter 9: Fire on one side, frost on the other

Worthwhile: 7.3/8.3 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 2.7/8.3 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: high

Strongest moments: Heating a rocky aggregate and cooling its molten droplets into chondrules was the shared standout. All three selected the connection between particle textures and thermal history as most worthwhile, and separately appreciated acknowledging uncertainty about the heating mechanism. Decision credit for droplet cooling differed, but the valued construction payoff was unanimous. Preserving ice-mantled dust in cold outer orbits supplied a valued contrast with reprocessed inner-disk rock for all three. Together with assembling stone and metal without yet making a planet, it sustained their specific curiosity about how these materials become larger bodies.

Weakest moments: Establishing the hot inner disk exposed opaque recipe dependencies: the disk needed the young Sun before hot inner orbits could operate. Players 1 and 3 found the opening less satisfying or repetitive; Player 2 additionally found the direct rocky-dust rejection frustrating. Automatic correction maintained progress but did not eliminate interface friction, even where the grader credited a motivated test. Combining refractory minerals with metal redirected Players 2 and 3 to rock-forming dust instead. Player 3 explicitly found that requirement unclear, despite valuing the aggregate's mixed histories; Player 2 expressed interest rather than equivalent frustration. Player 1's direct dust combination avoided this mismatch. The evidence supports uneven recipe legibility, not a universally weak aggregate passage.

## Chapter 13: Worlds from wreckage

Worthwhile: 7.3/8.3 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 4.7/8.3 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium/high (1 medium, 2 high)

Strongest moments: Rocky proto-Earth + water-bearing asteroid rock: all three explicitly valued discovering that hydrated material does not imply an ocean or a cool planet. This straightforward construction delivered a shared educational payoff despite unanimous nonmeaningful-decision classifications. Disrupting a layered planetesimal and adding its exposed core fragments to Earth: all three valued the connection between differentiated interiors and planetary ingredients. Players 2 and 3 named this their strongest sequence and reported satisfying incorporation payoffs; Player 1 specifically found the iron-meteorite connection interesting. Different preparation approaches also supported meaningful reasoning.

Weakest moments: Feeding the strongly attracting embryo: all three questioned why the game required rocky-layer fragments instead of their proposed rocky planetesimals or hydrated material. Automatic substitution preserved progress but did not explain the exclusion, making this a shared recipe-legibility weakness rather than evidence against the valued scientific explanations. Finding the missing ingredient for hydrated proto-Earth: Player 2's attempted incorporation of an intact layered body and Player 3's no-partner inspection supplied little direction and were each named least worthwhile. Their independent meaningful classifications remain intact because the failures redirected planning. Player 1 inferred core exposure directly and did not encounter this particular blockage.

## Chapter 21: Another route through the rock

Worthwhile: 6/7 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 2.7/7 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium/high (2 medium, 1 high)

Strongest moments: Reacting heated water with buried rock, then mixing returning fluid with cooler seawater, connects dissolved material to precipitation. All three players identify this chemical progression as worthwhile; the return-versus-reaction question and shared deposition predictions also provide decision value. Forming a hydrothermal plume provides a shared explanatory payoff: all three explicitly value learning that its visible material is suspended minerals rather than smoke, even though the final operation itself earns no meaningful-decision credit.

Weakest moments: Building mineral deposits by combining precipitating minerals with open fractures is initially opaque to all three players. The modest-deposit explanation is valued, but compatibility-driven execution introduces recipe-signposting friction. The graders disagree on whether the inspection itself is meaningful. Completing the separate return-to-sea operation after mixing with cooler seawater weakens the ending most clearly for Player 3, who finds the final inspection unnecessarily indirect. Players 1 and 2 combine directly without the same complaint; all still value the plume explanation.

## Chapter 22: The shore is not still

Worthwhile: 7.3/8.7 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 3.7/8.7 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium

Strongest moments: Moving dark sand along the coast and depositing it in the sheltered bay: all three players valued discovering that transport precedes accumulation. Player 1 especially emphasized this sequence; Players 2 and 3 also reported surprise, and the completed bay supplied a satisfying payoff. Rounding angular stones and forming the coarse pebble shore: Players 2 and 3 explicitly highlighted abrasion and sorting as their most worthwhile experience. Player 1 valued the final contrast with the sandy bay. The payoff extended beyond the routine final placement.

Weakest moments: Applying waves to the fractured headland twice before obtaining stones stretched an already predicted outcome. Player 1 explicitly found it repetitive; all three graders treated the successive applications as execution rather than fresh decisions, although the freed-stone result still supplied valued explanatory detail. Finding the correct wave recipe for sand and pebbles introduced uneven friction. Player 1 found the distinction obscure, and Player 2 described wave intermediates as prescribed; Players 2 and 3 found inspecting the completed pebble shore locally unrewarding. Player 3 nevertheless reported no particularly arbitrary action, and all three valued the transport correction.
