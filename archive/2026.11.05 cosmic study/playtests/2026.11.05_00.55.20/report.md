**Definitions**
- Worthwhile: counts actions supported by meaningful decision-making or a valued story payoff or reason to keep playing, counting each action once. Show the average credited actions / average total gameplay actions per player, not an average percentage.
- Fun: a narrower count of meaningful decisions: worthwhile deliberation or intentional experimentation. Measured by --detailed, including inspections and unsuccessful well-formed attempts; --fast reports it as not measured. Fast menu-assisted diagnostic ratios are not comparable.
- Hooks: the players' reported desire to continue: "high" means specific eagerness, "medium" means mild or conditional interest, and "low" means indifference or reluctance

## Chapter 18: Heat has somewhere to go

Worthwhile: 10/10.3 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 4.7/10.3 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium/high (1 medium, 2 high)

Strongest moments: Cooling the steam-rich atmosphere with escaping infrared energy, then joining it with returning steam to make water recycled through the sky, supplied the strongest shared discovery. All three valued the connection between atmospheric cooling, surface cooling and reuse of the same water; Player 3 additionally made a meaningful, unsuccessful test of the earlier cooler-air rule. Persistent heavy rain meeting cool rock produced shared satisfaction as water finally remained on the ground. Each player connected that outcome to curiosity about accumulation, although Player 3’s independent grader judged the resulting hook medium rather than high.

Weakest moments: Trying falling rain on cool rock instead automatically sent it onto hot lava. All three found the restriction surprising or initially arbitrary despite the stated ability of cool rock to retain water. The weakness concerns prerequisite communication and involuntary redirection; Player 3 nevertheless valued the resulting evaporation as part of the larger cycle. Inspecting returning steam and cool rock produced empty partner lists for every player; Player 1 also found no partner for cooler air. These targeted investigations retained independent decision credit but were experienced as puzzling, unproductive or repetitive rather than rewarding discovery.

## Chapter 24: A sea beneath the Moon

Worthwhile: 8/9 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 4.3/9 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium

Strongest moments: Returning fresh water to the shore through evaporation, terrain-driven rain and runoff was the shared educational payoff. All three valued mineral separation and the completed circulation; combining the inland cloud with the rocky rise also elicited substantive uplift-and-cooling inference. Combining the still pool with sunlight reflected by the Moon supplied shared quiet closure. Each player appreciated the resulting reflection, showing that a predictable finishing action can carry story value without another meaningful decision.

Weakest moments: Combining reflected sunlight with the calm-night setting failed without explaining that the tidal pools first needed preparation. All three reported confusion, and inspection repaired the missing dependency. This was principally recipe-discovery and interface-feedback friction, not rejection of the final scene; the meaningful failed tests and diagnostic choices did not make the interruption enjoyable.
