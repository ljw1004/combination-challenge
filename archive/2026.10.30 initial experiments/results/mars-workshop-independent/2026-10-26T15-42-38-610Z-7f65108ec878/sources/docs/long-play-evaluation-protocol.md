# Prospective evaluation: a fun combination game of about 200 decisions

Protocol 1, 26 October 2026. **Not a play result.** Applies to a newly frozen candidate; it does not amend old protocols, reinterpret old stopping decisions, or supply missing captures.

## Question and scope

The owner's explicit target is the **logic of a combination game judged fun and lasting about 200 decisions, preferably across multiple levels/scenarios**. Their further correction is equally binding: clear progress can be comfortable; not every decision must be meaningful; learning the mechanics can supply part of an opening's meaning. [Revision 9](what-is-fun.md) and the [design contract](design-contract.md) govern this protocol.

Test a finite, declared campaign, not the ability to manufacture endless optional projects. Assigned goals may legitimately supply purpose. Recipes, legality, prerequisites and results remain inspectable; concepts are inexhaustible; the player selects and performs combinations rather than ordering finished outputs. UI work remains frozen. A remembered world is optional: knowledge and methods can transfer between reset scenarios.

This protocol offers an achievable **provisional agent-research judgment**, not a measurement of human pleasure, human minutes or population-level preference. Human validation is a later evidence category, not a reason to stop the authorized logical research now. No fun scalar or per-action meaningfulness threshold is introduced.

Historical lessons constrain the new test:

- [Confluence](confluence-play-review.md) ended at 42 constructive interventions; [Frontier](frontier-play-review.md) at 36 combines plus 12 waits. Their appreciated endings were not roughly-200 results. Automatic events and diagnostic waits must not inflate the claim.
- The [checkpoint](research-checkpoint-2026-10-26.md) reports Living Atlas's five assigned goals in 29 combines and a three-goal elective arc in 20. Shared goals are promising, but multiplying unplayed levels does not demonstrate the requested scale. Its original raw plays remain unavailable; see [recovery provenance](living-recovery-2026-10-26.md).
- The [Mars correction](mars-objects-opening-interpretation-2026-10-26.md) prohibits equating obvious actions with a poor opening or the owner's general preference with a particular player's reported comfort.

## Freeze before recruitment

Save a dated manifest containing candidate/version and source hashes, public interface/laws, exact player prompts, starting state/seed, offered scenarios and order, final campaign endpoint, checkpoint boundaries, capture schema, and proposed experience. State what the opening, familiar passages and later goals are intended to offer; do not promise a new mechanic or insight at every step.

Use the historical **180–240 decision-opportunity range** as the working interpretation of “about 200,” not a minimum turn rule. Declare any different interpretation before plays, explain it, and retain the actual counts. Do not move the interval after seeing results. No player receives the numeric target or a requested action budget.

A mechanics reviewer first verifies public disclosure, legal short solutions, deterministic replay, preview/transition agreement, and the absence of compulsory padding. Try obvious universal assembly, saturation, reusable prefab and goal-insensitive policies where applicable. A witness is feasibility evidence, not a player, an optimum, or a fun result. A found short solution remains legal and visible. If it solves the entire campaign quickly, investigate that fact before recruitment rather than banning it to protect length.

Use fresh sessions with no designer, source, solver, prior-play or evaluation-criterion exposure. A worker who has read this protocol may coordinate/review but is not fresh for the player conditions. Retain actual model metadata and exposure history; do not call different sessions independent human participants. Archive a frozen package and captures promptly so evidence does not exist only in a working directory.

## Small first cohort

| Session | Assignment | What it can establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: full campaign | Fresh public-only player pursues the frozen campaign to completion or its own earlier stop. | Experienced whole-campaign scale, pacing, reported value, problems and legitimate shortcuts. Continuing across assigned levels is coverage, not elective appetite. |
| B: replication | Another fresh player gets the same version, starts and neutral assignment, without A's record. | Whether A's result depends on one interpretation or inefficient path. Keep the default order for clean replication; if an alternate order is the hypothesis, preregister it and report the confound. |
| C: opening/free choice | Fresh player is assigned only the first finite goal. At its ending, offer the actually available next scenarios and stopping neutrally. | Opening orientation/comfort, satisfied closure, and any prospectively chosen additional scenario. Not whole-campaign coverage unless actually played. |

One independent skeptical reviewer audits all three after play; the reviewer must not be the model/protocol implementer or one of these players. Independence is of role and exposure, not a claim of statistical independence among similar models. Three players are a small qualitative cohort, not a vote estimating human enjoyment.

Run A as the first pilot. If it exposes a broken interface, disclosure problem, unmistakable padding or a whole-campaign short solution, fix the concrete problem in a **new version** before spending B and C on replication. Preserve A as that version's result. If A is viable, finish the planned cohort before declaring success. Do not recruit repeatedly until favorable reports outvote negative ones; further sessions need a named unresolved hypothesis and frozen condition.

## Ready-to-use player prompts

Replace bracketed fields with public material only. The player receives neither the analyst sections above nor any expected solution.

### A/B: full campaign

> Play this logical combination game using only the supplied public interface and rules. Your assignment is the campaign consisting of [public goal/scenario list and completion endpoint]. Pursue its stated goals as you normally would. Simple or short solutions are welcome. You do not need to visit every optional branch, demonstrate a particular method, or continue after the campaign is complete. You may stop earlier if you prefer; state why. An early stop is a valid test outcome, not a failure to obey.
>
> Available interface: [neutral commands and public observation]. You may inspect all public rules, legal actions and exact single-action previews, and make your own plans. Do not read implementation, designer notes, previous plays or hidden files, run a solver, or script future-state search. Select and submit your own game actions; do not delegate construction to an automated policy. If information is missing or inconsistent, report it rather than guessing what the author intended.
>
> Play normally rather than reviewing every move. I will ask two brief questions at natural boundaries and one final reflection. You need not praise the game or find every action interesting. Plain descriptions of what you did and experienced are most useful.

### C: opening/free choice

Use the same public-interface/access paragraphs, replacing the first paragraph with:

> Your assignment is to play the first goal, [public first goal], until completion or your own stopping point. After that you may end, inspect the available scenarios, or choose another one if you want. Ending satisfies the assignment. You do not need to sample the whole game or demonstrate a particular method. Simple or short solutions are welcome.

At the first valid ending, deliver:

> What would you like to do now: finish here, inspect what is available, or play something else? Any of these is fine. If you choose to play, say briefly what you want to attempt before your next game action.

Do not suggest which scenario should attract the player. Record an inspection as inspection, not elective construction. Choosing a supplied level is elective participation in an authored goal, not self-generated purpose. If C continues, revisit the choice at the next completed goal, not every few actions. Finish at the campaign endpoint; do not demand an epilogue or infinite appetite.

### Sparse checkpoints and final reflection

For A/B, freeze **two natural boundaries**: normally completion of the opening goal and a middle-campaign goal. For a single scenario use public milestones instead. Checkpoints follow game structure, not a player-visible counter; omit a boundary that a valid solution skips. Ask only:

> What are you trying to do now? Since the last check, what, if anything, was worth doing, and what felt routine or unnecessary?

Routine is not offered as a synonym for bad. Do not require the player to defend each obvious action or identify a dilemma. Preserve spontaneous remarks before asking follow-ups. At a requested stop or campaign endpoint, ask:

> How was the experience as a whole? What would you keep, shorten, remove or change, if anything? What led you to stop here?

Then use **at most two** unanswered, relevant follow-ups: (a) “Did familiar steps feel comfortable, neutral, or like unwanted work? An example is enough”; (b) “Did anything you learned or made earlier affect a later goal? If so, what?”; or (c) “If you could shorten one passage without losing what you valued, which would it be?” Label prompted recognition separately from spontaneous notice. These are reports, not proofs of mechanism. Do not ask “Was this fun enough for 200 decisions?” or request a numerical fun score.

## Counting actual play without counting paperwork

The core count is **individually selected gameplay interventions**, a conservative operational proxy for decision opportunities actually taken. A decision here need not be difficult, surprising, strategic, or equally balanced against another option.

- Count one committed combine when the player selects its operands and any relevant target/context. An obvious useful pair or a familiar construction may count. A prior plan can guide many such selections; keep the plan/insight episode count separate rather than calling every selection a new insight.
- Count another consequential in-world action, including an intentionally chosen wait, as one intervention when it is genuinely selected by the player. A wait of ten beats is at most **one** timing decision, never ten. Record combines, other actions and waits separately; combinations must still carry the proposed game rather than becoming a pretext for a waiting exercise.
- Do **not** count automatic reactions, actor turns, process ticks, animation beats, inferred internal recipes, or a composite's automatically executed internals. A player-selected compound placement is one action, not its dependency-tree size.
- Exclude navigation, catalogue reads, previews, status checks, checkpoint prose, annotations, `next`/`finish` confirmations, required acknowledgments and tool retries. A scenario selection may be interesting but is reported separately and cannot supply missing core length.
- A command that literally executes a supplied script, or a mandatory “confirm the only next instruction” click, is execution/overhead rather than an independently selected gameplay decision. A clear goal and an obvious best move do **not** by themselves make an action scripted. Distinguish the player choosing the construction from an instruction specifying it.
- Invalid attempts are recorded separately; they cannot pad accepted-play length. Undo/recovery can be actual play when chosen for an in-world reason; test/operator repair is excluded. Repeated exact no-ops used only to exhaust a test are excluded. Do not infer unwanted work from repeated syntax alone.

The reviewer marks disputed classifications with their trace indices and reports a conservative supported count and an inclusive disputed count. Report total accepted combines even when their decision status is disputed. Do not silently promote 200 commands into 200 decisions, or demote all simple enactment because it lacks new strategy. A count alone never resolves whether the length was welcome.

Examples: 20 selected combines plus 180 automatic ticks is **20**, not 200. A 200-combine selected construction can meet scale while failing the appreciation/pacing question. A 200-combine run governed by ten larger plans can be reported as 200 selected game actions and ten thought episodes, without claiming 200 dilemmas. A valid 20-action campaign solution is a good or bad **20-action result**, never a 200-action success because the unused catalogue is large.

## Evidence bundle

Retain raw chronological evidence before synthesizing judgments. Minimum fields:

```text
session: id, date, participant/model metadata actually known, exposure history,
         condition, exact assignment/questions, version + hashes, start/seed,
         offered order, endpoint, technical interruptions
stream:  exact public observations and previews; requested action and operands;
         accepted/rejected result; actor (player/automatic/operator);
         before/after state or deterministic replay inputs and state digests;
         source-linked event indices; no silent omission of failed attempts
counts:  accepted combines, other player actions, deliberate waits,
         automatic beats, inspections/navigation, invalid attempts,
         administrative/report/finish commands; supported and disputed decisions
phases:  actual goal/milestone boundaries, current versus historically earned
         predicates, first success, final state, skipped/unvisited goals
reports: verbatim checkpoints, spontaneous remarks, final report and follow-ups;
         intention source (assigned/player-selected/self-generated/unknown);
         elective choice recorded before any claimed elective construction
review:  cited transitions, positive and contrary experience evidence,
         plausible shortcut/padding explanation, alternative interpretations,
         mechanical findings versus player statements versus reviewer inference
outcome: completion/early stop/technical interruption; supplied reason;
         per-session scale and value judgment; one keep/change/reject hypothesis
         and the next discriminating test if the target remains unestablished
```

An interruption is not chosen closure. Resume the same frozen session with its provenance, or label it incomplete. Missing original captures stay missing; new replays are not restorations of old experience. Do not sum unrelated versions, several alternative paths through the same level, or different players' sessions to manufacture one 200-decision campaign. Multiple levels can count when one player actually experiences that declared sequence.

## Independent skeptical review

Replay accepted actions and verify source/capture integrity. Reconcile the full count to the event ledger and inspect representative classifications plus every disputed event; audit every substantive claimed reuse, later consequence or shortcut against the actual rules. Inspect the whole sequence for required setup, repeated copying and exhausted goals; preserve contradictory player statements rather than averaging them away.

The reviewer must answer:

1. Did the played campaign really contain about 200 selected gameplay interventions, rather than automated time, paperwork or inflated decomposition? What finished earlier, if anything?
2. What did each player actually appreciate, tolerate, dislike or leave unreported? Does explicit value extend into the substantial middle/end, or is the positive account confined to the opening or relief that unwanted work has ended? Appreciated achievement and satisfied closure are legitimate, distinct from escaping an unwelcome passage. Success alone proves none of these.
3. Were familiar passages welcome, neutral or unwanted for these players? Would shortening remove something they valued? Do not demand novel learning, new roles or dilemmas to justify comfortable routine.
4. Do the different scenarios change the relevant construction, goal or experienced purpose? Shared methods and explicit new goals are permitted. Cosmetic novelty cannot prove a new skill, but thematic appreciation can remain real reported value.
5. Does a simple public-information policy complete the **whole campaign** far sooner? Distinguish an expert's insight, a legitimate individual short level, and a short goal-insensitive routine undermining the claimed whole-game scale. Report the method and cost, not just that enumeration eventually succeeds.
6. Is the apparent success explained by assigned compliance or flattering prose rather than evidenced appreciation? Assigned completion is allowed; assignment alone cannot establish enjoyment. C's elective choice is additional behavior, not human motivation or a mandatory gate.

Test reviewer-only shortcuts after preserving the player evidence. Label privileged search separately from public-information reasoning; neither is a fresh play. A structural counterexample qualifies the claim without erasing a player's genuine report of value. A shorter expert solution does not automatically make a novice's appreciated play padding: state the inference required, prior expertise and intended audience. A short goal-insensitive routine is a different threat from a clever compression of substantial construction work. If the reviewer authored a repair, a subsequent verification must not be described as independent of that repair.

## Decision rule and research stopping condition

Report a profile, not a scalar: **constraint fit; actual scale; reported value/pacing; cross-goal coherence/transfer where claimed; evidence integrity; remaining counterexplanations**.

A defensible stopping point for this logical research target is:

> **Provisionally meets the about-200-decision logical-play target in replicated agent evaluation.** Two fresh full-campaign sessions on the same frozen candidate naturally reached their declared endings around the preregistered 180–240 scale. Their direct accounts support worthwhile overall play, including substantial middle/late play, rather than only successful task completion. An independent review verified the count, causal claims and project constraints and found no unresolved explanation based on compulsory padding, hidden recipes, outsourced construction or a short goal-insensitive routine that makes the claimed scale misleading. The actual reports, disagreements and limitations remain attached. This is not yet human-fun or human-duration validation.

This is not a unanimity-of-praise requirement. Particular weak levels and requests to shorten passages can coexist with overall appreciation; explain why they do or do not undermine the sustained experience. Unknown reports cannot be filled with success, doctrine or designer intention. If only one full player reaches scale, one stops much earlier, or praise concerns only the beginning, report **promising but not replicated at the target**, diagnose the discrepancy and test the specific uncertainty. Do not conceal unfavorable sessions.

C stopping satisfied after one short level does not invalidate a finite campaign; C continuing does not rescue A/B ending far short or reporting mostly unwanted work. A shorter valid solution is never a protocol violation. If it materially changes the plausible campaign scale, keep the solution, report the short-game success separately and improve the next candidate rather than imposing a minimum number of moves. Likewise, a long tedious campaign reaches length but not the joint target.

If the conjunction is not supported, continue the authorized build–play–review loop with a **new falsifiable change**: add a genuinely useful goal/capability when scope is short, remove/reframe unwanted work when pacing fails, or repair information when reasoning was blocked. Do not endlessly rerun identical prompts, hide easy solutions, or require further optional play to stretch an exhausted world. When the conjunction is supported, publish the qualified result and remaining human/UI questions; do not redefine success as infinite elective continuation.
