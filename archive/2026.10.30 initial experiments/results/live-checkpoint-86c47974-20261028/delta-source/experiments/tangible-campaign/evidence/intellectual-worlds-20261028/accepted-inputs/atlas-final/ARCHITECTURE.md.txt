# Atlas of Questions — full candidate architecture

**After corpus mining, not an episode-to-chapter map.** The research pass processed all 1,099 available full abstracts, close-read 56 and reviewed ten labelled supplements. Its 29 selected leads are in `MINING-FINDINGS.md`. The design below is a new full-length identity; the original full Atlas remains untouched.

## The experience

You work at an accumulating desk: an observed case, an inherited explanation, a crafted instrument, a quotation, a rival practice. The pleasure is recognizing that two things belong together—and discovering what that connection makes possible. Sometimes the result is a powerful theory. Sometimes it is a counterexample, a working object, an artistic invention, or a reason not to settle the argument yet.

The route is chronological with overlapping dossiers where intellectual strands develop in parallel. Dates identify the historical claims, not a magical journey in which a late argument causes an earlier invention. Long-gap returns matter more than a uniform requirement to reuse the immediately previous desk.

**Target: about 255–275 substantive combinations over 30 desks.** Desk sizes vary; these are not 30 identical eight-step assignments. Reading is optional. The default interaction is the existing partner highlighting and combination grammar. No arithmetic is required. No invisible picture must be mentally reconstructed. No one-to-one episode mapping, geographic quota or generic “make a report” final step.

## Five long threads

1. **Records, testimony, criticism:** sky archive → Socratic counterexample → manuscript variants → print → Bacon → public experiment → data/provenance → a self-questioning future laboratory.
2. **What kinds of things exist?** Plato/Aristotle → translation and universals → classification → Linnaeus/Humboldt/Darwin → structural categories → Borges and postmodern criticism → situated machine models.
3. **Representations and instruments:** measured sky → geometric model → optics → perspective → telescope/clock → photograph → relativity and media → simulations that admit their limits.
4. **How should one live and work?** Socratic examination → Cynic/Stoic/Epicurean alternatives → public reason → Mandeville/Smith/Malthus → Marx/Morris/Kropotkin → rival technological futures.
5. **What makes an authority?** omen and royal archive → argument and education → translation/faith/reason → textual authenticity → public knowledge and exclusion → genealogy/criticism → a future commons with room for dissent.

## Representative abstraction level

The examples below are candidate operations to be historically refined, not instructions displayed to the player.

### A definition meets an inconvenient life

- **“Justice means returning what is owed” + “A friend asks for his weapon while deranged” → “A debt is not enough to define justice.”** A recognisable counterexample from Plato’s *Republic*, not a worksheet about argument labels.
- **That unresolved demand for a good life + “Diogenes needs little from powerful people” → “Freedom by needing less.”** This reconstructs a Cynic position; it does not prove it universally best.
- **“Freedom by needing less” + “A neighbour still depends on our help” → “Sufficiency need not mean indifference.”** Mark as a teaching comparison unless a specific ancient text supplies the edge. Do not make the player destroy the Cynic alternative to complete a Stoic answer.

### A viewpoint, not a projection calculation

- **“Light travels from object to eye” + “A picture plane between eye and street” → “A window onto a painted street.”** Both labels state the physical relation.
- **That window + “One fixed viewing point” → “Linear perspective.”** No coordinates, ratio arithmetic or invisible diagram.
- **“Linear perspective” + “The viewer moves sideways” → “One view is not the whole room.”** A limit, not a failed model. This creation can return in photography and the discussion of situated observation without falsely claiming Renaissance painters anticipated relativity.

### What Francis Bacon does

- **“Sunlight warms” + “A flame warms” → “Heat seems tied to light.”** An attractive candidate explanation, not a proven general law.
- **“Heat seems tied to light” + “Moonlight is bright but does not warm like sunlight” → “Brightness is not sufficient for heat.”** A negative instance changes the question.
- **“Brightness is not sufficient for heat” + “Friction warms without a flame” → “Seek heat beyond luminous bodies.”** This does not prematurely award modern thermodynamics.
- **“Seek heat beyond luminous bodies” + “Deliberately vary the rubbing” → “An experiment on heat.”** A practical intervention now differs from collecting agreeable examples.

### Two times can remain two questions

- **“A signal takes time to arrive” + “Clocks exchange light signals” → “A procedure for synchronizing clocks.”**
- **That procedure + “Observers moving differently disagree about simultaneity” → “No single distant now.”** The supplied observation is explicitly a teaching reconstruction; no mental train diagram.
- **“No single distant now” + “The same laws and light speed still apply” → “Special relativity.”** Constraints survive; this is not “all opinions are true.”
- Separately, **“Clock intervals” + “A remembered melody runs through the present” → “Lived duration.”** Bergson’s question remains live, not an anti-Einstein scientific result.

### A book can become another book without changing its words

- **“The same printed passage” + “A later writer’s different circumstances” → “The same words, another work.”** An original teaching reconstruction inspired by *Pierre Menard*, with no copied Borges prose.
- **“A map meant to guide” + “Every detail reproduced at full scale” → “A map that cannot guide.”** Representation defeats its practical purpose; no “write a map report.”
- **“Invented scholarly citation” + “A trusted reference catalogue” → “Authority that the archive cannot verify.”** Keep the plausible text and the missing evidence inspectable, rather than awarding a generic reconciliation.

### Futures inherit arguments, not just powers

- **“Automated workshop” + “Morris: pleasure and agency in useful work” → “A workshop designed around its makers.”** Explicit speculation.
- **“Adaptive city controls” + “Residents can contest the model’s categories” → “A city that can be argued with.”** Feedback does not silently mean surveillance or a single correct planner.
- **“Self-questioning laboratory” + “Shared archive with traceable dissent” → “A discovery commons.”** The final result preserves conflicting evidence and competing lives; it does not erase them into a triumphant universal answer.

## Newly explicit core: Earth history

The user specifically emphasized theories of evolution, plate tectonics and the geological age of Earth at 18:00. These now receive three substantial connected desks, not optional references: deep-time evidence supplies evolution; fossil distributions and geological continuity make drift worth considering; the missing mechanism remains a real objection until later seafloor and magnetic evidence support plate tectonics. Sequence is not duration, useful fit is not proof, and Darwin/Wegener are not awarded instant universal confirmation.

**Narrative/gameplay reviewer:** Are these recognisable operations, with understandable labels and genuinely useful earlier creations? Where does the design slip into a survey, a report assembly line, or a forced reconciliation? Which scenes need competing goals to remain live?
