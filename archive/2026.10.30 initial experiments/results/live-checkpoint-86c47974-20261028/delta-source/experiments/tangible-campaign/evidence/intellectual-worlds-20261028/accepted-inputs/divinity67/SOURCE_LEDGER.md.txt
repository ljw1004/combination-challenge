## Required distinctions

- Augustine's healed wound illustrates loss; it does not prove created goodness or the inherited condition. Sick and healthy infants form an explicitly invented controlled case, not a claim that illness is personal guilt.
- The Book of Two Principles and Lyon ritual are separate witnesses, not a universal Cathar creed. Juxtaposing Aquinas and a surviving dualist text is an editorial comparison, not invented correspondence.
- Fire's effective functioning is not moral virtue or a sufficient defence of a creator. Aquinas's account of water's form is identified as medieval natural philosophy.
- A tool analogy cannot establish divine appointment. A substance/appearance account cannot prove a Eucharistic change by taste.
- The 1517 seller's guarantee is Luther's reported target, not official Catholic doctrine. The guilt/penalty distinction explains an indulgence's object; the separately visible authority premise challenges the guarantee.
- The Marburg agreement leaves bodily presence disputed. The before-God domain of Luther's works claim does not deny useful practice, service, or growth of faith. Trent's initial unearned gift and later grace-enabled increase are distinct.
- Calvin's devotional objection is not a ban on all art. Trent's category of relic veneration neither authenticates this fragment nor grants recognition. The same fictional cases survive the disagreement.
