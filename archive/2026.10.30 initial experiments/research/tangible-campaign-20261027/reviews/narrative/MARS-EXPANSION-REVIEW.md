Nursery deserves a shorter, separate challenge trial and should not be protected to retain 202.

## Concrete issues to correct or expose before the long pilot

### 1. Put the extended waiting rationale into player-facing story

The order does avoid an immediate rescue contradiction: crew reach a prepared refuge before furniture retrieval, and gardening precedes ascent preparation, not a launch countdown. However, the actual scenario only says “We can do more than wait” and later “We have time to wait for a crop.” Why these stranded people can or should wait through crop growth remains unstated. README and AUTHENTICITY assume secure camp time; the player should not have to read those.

A brief post-rescue invitation can establish a scheduled return opportunity and adequate camp stores without inventing a countdown or resource mechanic. For example, establish that the return ship's planned opportunity is weeks away and the expedition has stocked shelter, so this interval is for restoring camp, local research, and preparing the route. This is proposed fictional logistics, not a claim about real flight timing. Then the nursery is an act of leaving a useful place behind, not a chore delaying urgent escape.

Also put the **same first safe evening** wording in cargo-return itself. README has it, but the actual scene only says recover furniture. `cargo-supper` explicitly reuses the rescue soup still in its flask; avoid implying it has stayed warm through an unspecified extended stay. No new food system needed.

### 2. Do not call an old weather record recent at the depot

`depot-wind` and the `track-disagreement` item refer to “recent wind observations.” The operand is `weather-record`, recorded before rescue, camp life, and the first crop. It is not a fresh feed. The early record can legitimately remind the crew that surface markings change; it cannot establish the timing or origin of these much later dark tracks.

Small fix: current-versus-archival registered images establish the change; the old weather record supplies a known reason not to assume markings persist. The exposed survey identifier remains decisive. Do not silently introduce real-time telemetry or imply this observation proves ground safety. This is the clearest technical/narrative wording correction.

### 3. Make the claimed final nursery check explicit, without another token ladder

`nursery-photo` observes the empty shelf **before** diffuser installation. `nursery-settle` says “after checking the new light arrangement,” but no post-install measurement occurs. README acknowledges a narrated check and fictional compatible fixture. State that brief visual check in the installation/result text, or say the frame is positioned using the observed shadow layout. Do not turn the photo into a quantitative demonstration of adequate plant light or add calibration operations just to defend the scene.

### 4. Use human objectives, not packaging labels

New goal labels are currently “Crew portrait,” “Camp nursery,” “Sleeved comparison,” and “Depot cargo shuttle.” These name exact outputs and are not unsolvable, but `Sleeved comparison` makes the climax sound like document filing. Prefer concise outcomes: photograph the reunited crew at their table; settle the saved seedlings in the nursery; preserve the corrected basin account and separately identified specimens; restore the hauler and bring it along the marked service approach. Keep concise narrator invitations and let combinations remain discoverable.

## What each episode earns

### Cargo / table / portrait — strong emotional candidate

The rescued crew now contribute memory rather than just being passengers. Their remembered route, earlier bearing map, recovery rope, camera, and toolkit have tangible reuse. The table is familiar and worth having; personal photographs and shared supper are valid play, not lesser work because they do not produce another engineering capability. The resulting portrait later enters `family-record` and the homeward archive.

Risk: camera view → remembered route → pickup map can feel like three names for one answer if nothing visually changes. Keep if discovery descriptions or images actually distinguish what the crew recognize and what the earlier map resolves. Otherwise merge a step, without replacing the lost count. Goal motivation should mention taking a portrait as well as retrieving pictures.

### Seedling nursery — worthwhile sentiment, weakest current implementation

Revisiting the **original landing tray** after the potato harvest is an excellent long-range emotional connection. Improving the same greenhouse via a slot preserves identity, and a handover beacon mentions the nursery. A useful nursery is itself a payoff; it need not become fuel for the launch to matter.

The eight actions mostly frame, position, label, support and install. They may be pleasant careful work, but a shadow grid and diffuser could feel like a miniature repeat of the earlier thermal test without its evidentiary comparison. The author correctly identifies this as first cut if the scene drags. Treat it explicitly as another bay of the existing camp greenhouse, not packing a greenhouse and carrying it to a new place. No need to manufacture another crop or a novel measuring instrument.

### Sediment comparison — strongest new intellectual purpose, UI-dependent

This does more than append confirmation: the player refines an earlier basin story when a similar-looking ripple may have a different origin. Camera, scale, terrain model, basin record, and specimen log all return. The two-cell case keeps provenance explicit. The corrected board and specimen case actually enter the return capsule through `expansion-return-comparison-record` and `expansion-return-comparison`; they are not abandoned completion certificates.

However, the engine supplies the inference through authored recipes, not player evaluation of competing hypotheses. Do not describe this as tested scientific reasoning. With only generic tiles,12 actions risk being prose processing. The decisive views should eventually be perceptually different. Board→sleeve packaging is the most readily removable operation if attention ebbs; retain provenance, not every label.

### Wind-obscured depot / cargo — strong functional payoff

Protected controller from Weather finally has a specific compatible use; the recovered hauler replaces a newly granted departure cart and carries the real sample capsule. Cleaning an actual survey plate and erecting a sign are physical work, not an acceptance certificate. Correctly, the permanent identifier—not atmospheric inference—resolves the route.

The current three image/inference states need perceptible distinctions. The standard controller compatibility is explicitly authored fiction, not evidence that any controller fits any machine. The map/hauler result compresses narrated travel; this is within the existing grammar and does not need a new navigation minigame.

## Authenticity challenge and checked support

- The comparison's broad claim is supported: [USGS on large Martian wind ripples](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/large-wind-ripples-mars-a-record-atmospheric-evolution) describes resemblance to water-worked forms. This supports rejecting a silhouette-only conclusion, not claiming every ripple is wind-made or uncertain.
- [JPL's streambed evidence](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia17063-evidence-about-a-martian-streambed-stereo/) identifies rounded embedded pebbles as evidence of ancient flowing water. The game's contextual, qualified interpretation is appropriate; no life detection or potable-water inference follows.
- [NASA's growing-plants overview](https://www.nasa.gov/exploration-research-and-technology/growing-plants-in-space/) supports engineered growing and psychological value. It does not validate this diffuser design, shadow-based adequacy, or a self-sustaining unattended Mars greenhouse. Keep those explicitly fictional and do not promise survival until the next crew without maintenance context.
- The author appropriately distinguishes wind-altered marks from a safe driveable route, and supplies ready compatible equipment rather than dangerous improvised systems. I did not independently verify every linked source or real mission hardware detail. Technical review here is bounded to the new claims above, not certification of the whole Mars campaign.
- `contrast-scale` says the scale reference was in-scene and capture/annotation compressed. Preserve that: adding a scale card to an arbitrary existing photograph cannot by itself recover physical dimensions.
