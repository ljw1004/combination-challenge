## The episodes and their reasons to exist

### 1. The things we left behind —10 operations, after camp

**Invitation:** the rescued people remember where they abandoned their tagged furniture sled. They are collaborators who know the place, not anonymous rescue cargo. Their first safe evening can end around their own table.

Camera view → remembered approach using the crew recording → locate it on the earlier bearing map. Existing recovery rope+one padded handling kit → secured sled → narrated pickup along that route. Existing toolkit unfolds the recovered dual-purpose mess crate. Personal photos go under its clear cover; the original insulated soup flask goes on the table; the old message camera makes a crew portrait.

**Outputs:** useful mess table, shared meal scene, personal photograph subsequently stored in the homeward archive. **Old things reused:** field camera, bearing map, crew message, recovery rope, toolkit, soup flask, message camera. No spare motor, cabin or survival miracle appears in the recovered crate.

**Why interesting:** rescue turns into belonging; a crew memory helps solve a small terrain problem; a formerly ordinary piece of furniture becomes emotionally valuable. Comfort and obvious making are intended, not disguised as strategy.

**Adversarial critique:** the three view/memory/map steps could feel like a document ladder. They require visibly different image information to work. Recovering the sled is one narrated abstract operation, not a physics puzzle. If players read the three maps as synonyms, collapse one and accept201 rather than replace it. The first-evening timing must be explicit so the soup's warmth does not imply days of storage. Asking people to collect furniture before essentials is only plausible because the rescue refuge and life support are already secure.

### 2. Something for the next crew —8 operations, after first greenhouse harvest

**Invitation:** potatoes have fed the crew; the little living tray protected at landing still exists. Make room for that original plant rather than magically spawning another seed packet.

Existing camera observes a *different nursery bay* where a frame shadows the light. A cultivation grid makes the obstruction concrete. Fit the original greenhouse's compatible diffuser to its stored frame, position it from the shadow layout and clip it onto the **same greenhouse**. Label and support the original sprouted tray, then move it into the nursery bay.

**Output:** a living camp nursery and a physically improved greenhouse. **Old things:** field camera, sprouted tray, greenhouse, its already fitted light. One coherent cultivation kit provides the five new pieces. The handover beacon explicitly mentions the nursery.

**Why interesting:** a very early small success survives into a generous long-term choice. A changed use of the greenhouse makes a second project plausible without rebuilding the greenhouse. Gardening is allowed to be comfortable.

**Adversarial critique:** this is the weakest technical/design module. A camera photo diagnoses an obstruction, not plant-light sufficiency or the effectiveness of a diffuser. The fictional fixture must be known-compatible and its final check stays narrated; no quantitative horticultural claim is warranted. Light comparison may feel like a repeat of the thermal experiment, though it currently has no paired scientific measurements. Eight steps could be too much for a nursery improvement. The whole-greenhouse portability abstraction is inherited and worsened by making another scene; preferably render this as another bay of the same camp, not carrying a greenhouse across Mars. Do not add extra meters/calibration to rescue a weak premise.

### 3. Not every ripple is a river —12 operations, after buried-story

**Invitation:** a visually tempting nearby ripple pattern should challenge the first basin interpretation rather than merely confirm it. Compare distinct source contexts.

Photograph ripples and gravel in matrix; keep a scale reference; pair the pictures and locate them in the existing terrain model. Compare with the basin record: **ripples alone are ambiguous**, whereas the embedded rounded gravel plus context supports an ancient-water interpretation. A small dry impression and an already detached gravel chip remain separately sourced. A ready two-cell case retains identity as they are packed. A physical board and sleeve hold the corrected interpretation. Case and sleeve are actually packed into the return capsule later.

**Outputs:** two distinct specimens, annotated comparison, corrected story. **Old things:** field camera, scale card, terrain model, basin record, specimen log, return capsule. No new microscope, spectrometer or sensor preparation. One field comparison kit accounts for the modest new supplies.

**Why interesting:** players discover why a resemblance is not a conclusion. Their earlier scientific success is refined, not erased. The evidence goes home rather than ending as a completion certificate.

**Adversarial critique:** this engine cannot let the player genuinely choose or argue between hypotheses. Correct inference is authored into a combination description. If both pictures are text tiles, it risks being12 prose operations; actual distinct imagery is central. The dry mould sheet is fictional and not a NASA sampling procedure. A paired diagram plus context board plus sleeve may overdo presentation packaging; remove a step if the UI already displays source context clearly. One case expands the return capsule to five slots—still test real UI height. Ancient water is not evidence of life, contemporary liquid water or safe drinking water.

### 4. The tracks that were not ours —10 operations, approaching the ascent depot

**Invitation:** the old service route should be there, but recent dark marks disagree with the stored engineering survey. Don't follow the prettiest line. Find permanent markers, then recover the depot's cargo capability.

Current photo+old depot survey register fixed rocks/sockets. Existing weather record explains why transient tracks are unreliable, without proving their origin or route safety. A ready cleaning pad exposes the survey plate; the actual identifier resolves the route. Assemble the stored sign with its matching foot and point it along that route. Fit the **existing protected controller** and stored platform to an otherwise intact, explicitly model-compatible depot hauler. Bring it along the identified service approach. It replaces the newly supplied departure transfer cart and actually carries the return capsule.

**Outputs:** visible route sign, functioning cargo hauler, recovered depot approach. **Old things:** field camera, weather record, protected controller; later specimen cart/capsule. One navigation/maintenance locker plus adjacent parked hauler supplies the new pieces.

**Why interesting:** Mars changes its surface markings; an old map is useful but not sufficient. The small weather-station protection task finally pays off as the same standard controller serves a specifically matched piece of hardware. The final journey benefits from earlier care.

**Adversarial critique:** the three photographic/map inference products could again feel like document shuffling. The permanent marker must be visibly decisive; weather cannot certify ground-bearing safety. The route is the pre-engineered depot service approach, not an unexplored dune crossing. Standardized controller compatibility is a plausible explicit fiction, still convenient; do not pretend any generic controller can drive anything. A stronger physical sign placement might replace its current combination, but then reduce the combination count honestly. The hauler's move into a new output kind is ordinary existing grammar, less identity-clean than its two slot installations.

## Timeline and architecture

- Rescue/first refuge are completed before cargo recovery and camp comforts. No delay while an endangered crew waits.
- Nursery occurs after narrated first-crop growth, during established camp life, before departing toward the depot. It does not promise food before an imminent rescue deadline.
- Comparison exposures are adjacent to the already reached geology site. Depot navigation follows along the existing ascent-depot approach, before plant/cabin servicing; no side expedition during launch countdown.
- The original rescue repair/tow and signal alternatives survive. No choice2of4 objective, resource accounting, new UI gesture, implicit dangerous chemistry or magical delivery.
- `modules.json` documents insertion points. This is a **separate candidate merge**, not a generic plug-in system: each episode can be reviewed independently, but removing it requires reversing its departure hooks. `depot-way` can move directly after `buried-story` if `contrast` is rejected.
- Only grandfathered v5 locations use catalysts; new places use location operands. The new greenhouse/hauler/case slots use the existing engine contract.

## Timeline clarification after independent audit

The candidate explicitly states that refuge restoration lets both crews resume their original multi-month surface assignment before the planned return, with the existing mission food and certified life support covering that interval. Greenhouse crops are supplemental, not emergency starvation arithmetic. This is a new explicit story premise requiring editorial acceptance; if an urgent rescue-and-immediate-return tone is preferred, omit the growth/nursery arc instead. The depot uses the **earlier** weather record as evidence that surface marks change, not as a recent measurement after crop growth. A post-diffuser visual check is narrated and does not certify plant-light sufficiency.
