# Independent source spot checks

Read 28 October 2026. These checks test the disputed/less-obvious mechanisms, not all 197 recipes. No source-derived cooking temperatures, preservation schedules or complete recipes are reproduced.

- **Vinegar:** [Aspall's production account](https://www.aspall.co.uk/blogs/our-story/vinegar-production) supports managed alcohol-to-acid conversion and oxygen supply. The candidate's `cider-vinegar` context is sound. It supplies no evidence for herb toast or compote; their shared source assignment is contextual metadata, not method support.
- **Roux and filled pasta:** [Escoffier, sections 28 and 2296–2297](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/71395/pg71395-images.html) supports adding milk to prepared roux with further cooking, and shaping/filling/cooking ravioli. The candidate's sauce description includes continuing heat. There is no basis here for demanding a second saucepan gesture. Root-and-strained-yogurt filling is correctly declared an authored variation, not Escoffier's formula.
- **Marzipan:** [Goethe-Institut's recipe](https://www.goethe.de/resources/files/pdf341/marzipan_englisch.pdf) supports finely ground almond, fine sugar and a little water as a workable paste. No egg requirement should be invented.
- **Macarons:** [King Arthur's class recipe, page 2](https://shop.kingarthurbaking.com/content/class-pdfs/FrenchMacaron_August2025.pdf) confirms an almond/confectioner's-sugar dry mixture folded into meringue. Sugar in both branches is not accidental double-counting. The game's prior dry-blend reuse has real culinary meaning.
- **Choux and pastry cream:** [King Arthur choux](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/pate-a-choux-recipe) and [pastry cream](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/pastry-cream-creme-patissiere-recipe) support the relevant distinctions, not every omitted handling detail. The candidate does not treat raw starch added to finished pouring custard as instant pastry cream.
- **Chocolate:** [European Cocoa Association processing](https://www.eurococoa.com/cocoa-story/cocoa-story-the-production-process-from-cocoa-beans-to-semi-finished-products/) does contain the paste/sugar/cocoa-butter, conching and tempering section. The initial web extract displayed only the earlier section; reopening confirmed the later section. No source defect should be reported from that incomplete extraction. [ICCO](https://www.icco.org/processing-cocoa/) also supports the broad manufacture sequence. The prepared nib supply is legitimate compression.
- **Twinkie:** [Hostess's current product page](https://www.hostesscakes.com/everyday-snacks/twinkies) supports the golden sponge, sweet filling and individual wrapper identity, but not the game's ingredient formula. The live ingredients are much more complex. [Animas Museum](https://www.animasmuseum.org/online_exhibits/PetersonHouse/foods.html) states a banana-filled origin and later vanilla change. The game uses only that restrained optional flavor comparison; it does not reconstruct an original proprietary recipe or make the more specific causal story a mechanic.
- **Sugar history:** [UK Parliament](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/tradeindustry/slavetrade/overview/trade-routes-and-the-slave-trade/) supports the enslaved-labor context. It is not preservation-method evidence; the author correctly says so.

No substantive contradiction was established in these spot checks. That conclusion does not mean that every per-recipe citation is method evidence, that every food has one universal formula, or that the sequence establishes historical transmission.
