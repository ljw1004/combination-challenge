# Independent verification of the corrected fun evaluator

This review checks `fun-evidence-3` against [doctrine revision 6](history/what-is-fun/revision-6.md), the [first audit](fun-evaluator-audit-1.md), and all six actual `fun-*` captures. I read the raw reports and observations, the generated profiles, `player-experience.ts`, `fun-evaluator.ts`, `fun-reviews.ts`, `fun-evaluate.ts`, the relevant protocol and tests. I am a retrospective critic, not a new player or a human enjoyment judge. The first audit's author implemented the original report normalizer; I did not implement that normalizer or the evaluator. Implementation corrections made in response to this verification belong to the root implementer. I changed only this document.

**Verdict: the corrected evaluator substantially embodies the revised theory and now supports a useful empirical design loop.** It preserves the original stories' appreciation and satisfied completion, changes failed renewal hypotheses instead of endlessly requesting the same test, and correctly corroborates two elective combines in each revised story. Those are real improvements in the evidence and in the observed agent experience. They warrant retaining and developing the particular narrative mechanisms. They do not establish human enjoyment, isolate a causal treatment effect, or achieve the new long-game target.

Two consequential extraction defects surfaced during this verification: the new players' closure/appreciation vocabulary again escaped the intended categories, and embedded hypothetical passages were incorrectly listed as enacted. They were reported promptly and corrected during the review. The corrected behavior and remaining limits are described below; a passing coverage label alone was not my basis for the verdict.

## The four high-priority corrections

### Experience survives extraction, including completion

The original Bell and Mneme failures are repaired. Source-linked sequences, noticed responses, particular passages, explicit null renewal, optional-action zeroes and structured closure reach the experience account. Council's repetition dictionary is preserved without treating the heading `valued: "None observed"` as affirmative pleasure. Unknown fields remain available, and prose is not converted into invented booleans. These are appropriate distinctions.

Testing only the original four reports was insufficient. Bell2 reports its end through `report.actualStoppingReason`; Mneme2 supplies `report.renewedIntentionOrClosure` as a string. Before the verification correction, both generated `experienceAcrossTime.closure` arrays were empty. Their `report.appreciation` fields were also unclassified, although other passage fields retained some narrative value. This repeated the first audit's central failure on new live data.

The current adapter now retains those stopping and appreciation accounts with their source paths. A free-text combined closure/renewal account remains a topic for review, without manufacturing an explicit satisfied-closure flag. In these particular reports the words plainly describe completion, and the recorded critic interpretation says so. This is enough for an honest reviewed account; a sentiment classifier is unnecessary. Future report formats should either use the canonical structure or be checked against actual new captures before their profiles guide selection.

### Evidence now changes the next experiment

`fun-reviews.ts` supplies reviewed hypothesis ledgers with keep/change/reject/untested decisions, support, counterevidence and falsifiable next experiments. `evaluateFun` exposes those separately from player words and mechanical findings, and retains the prior designer proposal. This repairs the static copying of already-performed tests.

The changes are concrete: preserve Bell1's quiet-memory value while changing the assumption that an exposed object invites transformation; preserve Mneme1's meaningful character response while changing the assumption that known grief answers create a live question. Progression's reuse seed is retained without promoting worksheets, and constitution remains a control rather than a reason to build a larger civic optimization task. The new versions test those revisions and produce a different, modestly positive recommendation: carry the adopted question or relationship into a world that can support further purposes.

This is a **reviewed empirical loop**, not automatic interpretation of arbitrary prose. The distinction is explicitly permitted by the doctrine and is appropriate at this stage. The Bell2/Mneme2 reviews identify the root retrospective critic; they are not independent of the designer. This second audit provides independent verification of those interpretations. Coverage strings do not replace that role separation.

### The actual elective constructions have valid provenance

Both revised sessions stop their assigned exposure at genuine valid endpoints: Bell2 has a safe orchard and an available finish; Mneme2 has `baselineAchieved:true` and `safeStoppingPoint:true`. Each records an intention before continuing. The two combines lie inside the corresponding elective action interval. Bell2's later `finish` is counted separately, rather than inflating construction to three. Mneme2's private-life combine both enacts a purpose and completes the story; its constructive value is not erased because it supplies closure.

Chronological continuation intervals prevent an old elective action from corroborating a later unacted intention. A pre-existing unrelated linked session is rejected; a prospective linked run requires explicit parent provenance and a compatible start time. During verification, two narrower boundaries were also tightened: the linked origin must match the complete run/index/time tuple, and inspections at a shared decision boundary are assigned chronologically to one intention rather than counted twice. The focused regressions exercise both negative and positive examples. The same-run observations in these six captures do not depend on a linked-session inference.

Bell2 has a prospective continuation intention but no individual combine forecasts. Mneme2 also records intentions and expectations before Voice and Distance. Neither fact should be silently promoted into checked assertions. The protocol demonstrates optional construction under an agent task, not intrinsic human motivation.

### Prior disclosure and valued enactment are distinct

The quiet-bell passage is correctly traced to `initialObservation.previews[0].events[0]` before its committed result. Bell1 explicitly reports reading the public laws and previews. The passage can be appreciated in enactment without being newly revealed then.

Mneme1 requires a semantic reading: `initialObservation.recipes[0].immediateConsequence` already tells the player that the keeper uses History to preserve every version. The result expresses it differently. Exact matching therefore marks a new *verbatim passage*, while the player's report and reviewed ledger correctly deny a newly discovered tactic or behavior. The disclosure function explicitly limits its claim to exact availability; it is not a semantic novelty detector. This distinction is working only when the reviewed account is read with it.

The verification found another real error: the old path filter matched `.events` inside Bell1's embedded forecasts. Unperformed Air, Stone and Seed results then appeared with an `enactedAt` path under `observation.previews`. The corrected extractor takes enacted passages from the actual top-level response/lastResponse/events, while retaining other observation text as available information. The quiet-bell prior exposure survives that correction. New Bell2 story dialogue and Mneme2's refusal are available in committed results; the transparent recipe functions do not disclose their exact content. Earlier-session experience remains separately recorded by condition and report, rather than being reconstructed by current-run text matching.

## The generative theory and the owner's preferences

The evaluator now gives the intention–construction–response–renewal account useful evidence to work with. It distinguishes a printed possibility from an adopted inquiry, a known answer worth enacting from discovery, and eager pursuit from a concern that has been satisfactorily closed. The profiles remain partly assembled lists; their temporal and causal interpretation still comes from the specific player sequence, prospective intention and critic review. They do not automatically explain every episode.

Narrative, beauty and philosophical resonance receive a legitimate place. The quiet names, paired ferry voices, irreversible petal and private blank page are concrete reported objects of appreciation. Their value is preserved despite easy recipes or unchanged parts of the algebra. Likewise, the evaluator does not force continuation to validate an ending. Mneme2 is especially useful: respecting the unseen act can finish the adopted relationship without collecting the hidden answer. This is aligned with the owner's stated motivations and the “gods, not grocers” constraint. It is an agent's taste, not the owner's own response; future owner feedback takes precedence.

A material design limit remains: Bell2's `report.automaticResolutionLoss` explicitly says much of the pursued value could be delivered as a written story. Its easy hear-then-seal routine does not establish that combination is indispensable sustained play. Mneme2 provides stronger evidence that the actual response can change the purpose of the next familiar combination, but only within a small authored tree. The correct design move is to retain those local strengths and test how inventions and persistent actors create further meaningful purposes. Replacing them with more required dialogue tickets would not answer the question.

The proposed 30–40 minute game, roughly **180–240 decision opportunities**, remains in development. These short encounters contain neither that sweep nor human pacing evidence. Mandated long coverage must remain separate from elective continuation, and intervention counts must remain separate from player decision episodes. None of this is a reason to discard the demonstrated short pleasures or delay the next inexpensive logical experiment until certainty exists.

## Verification and remaining scope

The initial focused run passed all 30 evaluator, normalizer and decision-space tests, including actual Bell/Mneme fixtures. The two extraction defects above were discovered by inspecting raw reports and generated paths despite that passing suite. After the corrections, **all 35 focused tests pass**, including the new actual-report and provenance/disclosure regressions. I independently recomputed all six profiles without writing them: every capture replays successfully and every recomputed profile exactly matches its regenerated saved artifact. Both new closure accounts and their appreciation fields appear in the intended categories; no embedded preview passage appears as an enacted result; Bell1's quiet passage retains its initial-preview exposure; the two positive runs each still count exactly two elective combines. The final judgment relies on that concrete evidence as well as tests.

Some principle coverage remains broad: the presence of a critic review assigns several reviewed labels without proving that review addressed every F1–F10 obligation. The exact graphs remain useful mechanical evidence; their Pareto counts do not measure narrative pleasure. Progression's missing exact domain adapter limits enumeration, not the knowledge available from its actual construction and report. These are reasons to inspect a few relevant decisions and cite the reviewed examples, not reasons to turn F1–F10 into a universal score or withhold a clear verdict.

Retain the corrected evaluator and the specific narrative/relationship mechanisms. Test a continuing world in which an earlier invention or boundary changes another actor's response and supports a new player-adopted purpose, while leaving completion valid. That follows from this evidence; adding length, chores or arbitrary uncertainty does not.
