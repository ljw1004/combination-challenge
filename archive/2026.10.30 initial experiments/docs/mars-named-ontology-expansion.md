# Beyond the grammar checkpoint: a larger named Mars ontology

27 October 2026. **Design artifact only. Do not merge this into the current UI build.** The bounded checkpoint remains `src/mars-named-content.ts`: its purpose is to test the latest human interaction grammar. This larger proposal has no engine, fresh play, route-length promise or delivery claim.

## Governing grammar

Everything is an ordinary named thing. A binary combination produces one recognizable named thing; not an anonymous bundle, component tree, network state or concatenated label. Inputs remain, and a result is created only if that kind does not already exist anywhere on the board. There is no copy/design distinction and no requirement for two instances of one kind.

A location is only (a) the actual target B's meaningful catalyst setting for A+B, (b) the second operand in A+place producing a portable named result, or (c) a freely movable goal placement. No accumulated-location crafting, sticky items, source-consuming stocks, or free context override. Hover can reveal compatible novel-result partners without revealing the result name, as the owner requested. New supplies and places appear when an introduced objective needs them, not as an overwhelming full map.

The larger ontology should be **improvisation with recognizable salvage**. Starting with a heater, motor, radio or filter is appropriate. It must not become an ore-to-industry manufacturing grind merely to lengthen play.

## Proposed shape: shared things, different missions

Rather than one mandatory recipe ladder, offer several finite objective sequences sharing the same named vocabulary:

1. **Contact and shelter:** reconnect a radio, make useful power, bring a signal to a visible ridge, patch a shelter.
2. **Water and cultivation:** obtain portable water, choose a useful treatment/container, start protected growth, make watering easier.
3. **A field expedition:** prepare a rover or a simple cart, a portable view/survey tool, and the gear a particular route needs.
4. **A useful work area:** build tools for inspection, holding, lifting and small repairs rather than repeat another supply network.
5. **A dependable home:** use earlier growing, lighting, repair and thermal inventions to complete a small set of current placements, with a satisfying finite ending.

These are candidate objective sequences, not separate demo counts to add. Some may be alternative scenarios rather than required chapters. A future full-campaign assignment must declare exactly which are offered and actually play the combined experience. The user's roughly200 goal remains open; no number of recipe rows proves that duration or appeal.

## Candidate recipe families

The following is a concrete larger content pool. It includes the checkpoint's useful spine plus proposed branches. It is **not a requirement to discover every item**. Normal pairs are commutative in result; which item is dragged still determines movement and the target's context. Equivalent routes to an already existing output are suppressed by global novelty, not used to manufacture duplicates.

Some rows need semantic/content review before implementation, especially where an item such as a cover or frame represents a conventional ready-to-use component. No new physical law is smuggled into these names; this is the new named-combination game, not the superseded geometric simulator.

## Discipline on the larger list

This is intentionally broader than the checkpoint, but **it is not a catalogue to mandate**. Several close variants may be unnecessary. In particular:

- Do not require lantern, solar lamp, signal lamp, inspection lamp and illuminated glovebox just to count every branch. Choose objectives with different purposes, and let alternatives remain optional.
- Do not call a loose collection a kit simply to give a forbidden generic bag a name. A hoist, watering hose, solar charger or propagator has a conventional integrated use. “Base kit” made from a radio and a greenhouse would not meet that test.
- Food/water treatment and growing are game abstractions; labels are not real survival instructions or claims of immediate physical processes.
- Recovered complete equipment remains usable. A scenario may begin with a filter pump or a telescope. Do not force it to be disassembled and rebuilt for discovery credit.
- A single location slot also cannot simultaneously hold two different required kinds. Check both kind uniqueness and slot compatibility for the whole current endpoint, not just one objective at a time.
- Context lookup precedes novelty filtering: if the contextual result already exists, do not silently give an unmade ordinary result while B remains in that setting. Moving B out makes the ordinary pair available again. Avoid mandatory variant-collection objectives that make this sensible rule feel like a puzzle about interface state.
- The tabletop propagator/cold-frame/greenhouse scale distinction needs a clear named-object convention before expanding those close variants. Either supply an explicitly full-size/folding greenhouse frame or retain a smaller cold-frame ending; do not demand successive nearly synonymous covers merely as upgrades. The checkpoint itself stays unchanged during its current build.
- One thing per kind means a simultaneous goal set cannot demand the same kind in two slots. Either use a genuine distinct invention, make earlier success an explicitly historical milestone, or allow moving the thing and accept current-goal regression. Never silently spawn a copy.
- Several recipes can reach the same output, but only the first creates it. Alternate recipes are ways to choose an invention, not repeatable production.

## Candidate objective sequences and different endings

These are prospective alternatives using the shared pool, not prescribed solution scripts:

### A. Rescue contact to useful home

Reconnect a radio → make contact from the ridge → bring water home → establish protected growth → repair the habitat → leave a lit workbench. This preserves the checkpoint's staged motivation and understandable progress. More content should not bury this simple opening.

### B. An expedition prepared, not an archive toured

Prepare a suitable vehicle or cart → provide expedition lighting/power → obtain a useful route view if that objective needs it → supply a field water or lifting task → establish a reachable contact point. The endpoint should be a working expedition arrangement of named things, not a required photo of every location. Simple cart/lantern answers remain possible when the objective permits them.

### C. Cold-site cultivation

Choose a protected growing approach → make a useful watering tool → adapt shelter or warmth for the named cold-site objective → provide a portable food/cultivation outcome. This should use a few genuinely different inventions, not require a chain of five nearly synonymous greenhouses. No finite potato stock or food meter.

### D. Repair and inspection workshop

Establish a basic work area → choose an inspection or protected-handling device → make a lifting/holding tool useful for the current objective → finish a sealed repair. The repair bench may act as a catalyst where its actual tool setting matters. A lit workbench is not automatically insufficient merely because an elaborate glovebox exists.

### E. A compact dependable base

Select a finite set of distinct current placements that together express contact, water, growing, shelter and useful work. Previously made things remain available. Reuse may make the ending short and satisfying; do not demand they all be recombined or demonstrated again.

All places are introduced only when an objective needs their role. A new location is not itself a reward worth overwhelming the board with. Each objective must also declare the few supplies it introduces and the existing things it reuses. Exact current/history semantics belong in the eventual content sheet, not in an evaluator's retrospective interpretation.

## What to do after the grammar checkpoint is playtested

Use the human observations to select a coherent subset and revise weak names/recipes. Then write one complete objective sequence plus meaningful alternative goals using this larger shared pool. Preserve the simple opening and easy familiar progress. Ask whether intermediate inventions are recognizable and worth making, not whether each recipe remains a difficult puzzle after perfect knowledge.

Only then implement an expanded finite game in a new freeze, mechanically verify actual gestures/global novelty/location revelation, and observe a complete fresh experience. Count actual chosen combines and other meaningful selections; do not count revealed recipes, automatic supplies, hidden internal parts, hover states or repeated blocked creations as duration.

A substantially larger ontology gives more material for curiosity, useful intermediate creation and alternative goals. It does not, by itself, establish roughly200 valued decisions. That remains a whole-experience empirical question, not a number this design artifact promises.
