# Field revision: route, water and ice microscopy

Fresh consultation, 4 November 2026. Scope: the current v12 manuscript, design brief, scene artwork and the user's new requests 11–14 and 20. Earlier reviews and outcomes were not consulted. These are proposed story and visual decisions; the manuscript and artwork are unchanged by this report. The aim follows the [project philosophy](../../../../../README.md#philosophy): make the physical cause of each action understandable.

## A crossing that earns its winch

**Replace the broad shallow ramp with a confined wash and a short, steep, broken exit.** The present picture has a clear central lane and little visible change in elevation. More scattered stones will not solve that.

Earth references checked:

- The National Park Service describes Elephant Hill's steep slopes, loose rock, rock steps and awkward turns. I visually inspected its [side view of a Jeep descending](https://www.nps.gov/cany/planyourvisit/images/9408947397_2a7b29f2a3_k.jpg?autorotate=false&maxwidth=1000) and [approach to a broken rock ascent](https://www.nps.gov/cany/planyourvisit/images/20231027_155104.jpg?autorotate=false&maxwidth=1000). The useful cues are the vehicle pitched against the horizon, interrupted wheel paths, and rock faces beside the vehicle that establish scale. These photographs demonstrate difficult terrain, not that every vehicle needs a winch there. [NPS route description](https://www.nps.gov/cany/planyourvisit/needlesroads.htm).
- For terrain explicitly requiring recovery equipment, Montrose's official account of Dry Creek trails specifies winches and describes narrow washes, boulder approaches and steep exit ledges. Borrow the constrained exit and loss of easy wheel placement; do not copy its extreme vehicle-sized holes or undercut obstacles. [City of Montrose](https://visitmontrose.com/play/outdoors/activities/rock-crawling/).

**Composition proposal:** the wash crosses the picture diagonally in the nearer half of the working scene. The rover is roughly one fifth of the picture's width, at the bottom of the climb, with its wheels visibly engaged in unequal ruts. A short ascent gains about one rover-body height over several rover lengths; make this a compositional scale cue, not a certified slope. Show two low broken ledges, loose angular scree between them and a rounded upper exit. Large rocks constrain alternate lines. The approach curves into the wash, but the actual pulling pitch and cable are straight. Keep every wheel supported; the cable assists traction rather than suspending the vehicle.

A continuous bedrock foot route skirts the obstacle along its side and climbs gently to a broad shoulder behind the exit. It needs room for a suited person carrying the winch and turning around, but insufficient width for the rover. Show the whole connection to a substantial bedrock horn, with the sling low around its base. Avoid a narrow balancing ledge, a loose boulder anchor, a cable rubbing on the crest, or a pull that would drag the rover sideways. Qualified anchor, recovery point, winch rating, vehicle stability and controlled return remain author assumptions. [WARN's manufacturer guidance](https://www.warn.com/pages/basic-guide-to-winching) supports sound anchors, straight pulls and grounded recovery; it does not qualify this fictional rover.

Put a low layered ice exposure beyond the exit on the same visible terrace. The combined action chain is **rig winch → bring rover onto the terrace → take the remote core**. The finished rover remains identifiable near the sampling bank. Preserve the foot route and winch for the return; collection can include refrigerated stowage and the journey home. The reachable sampling receiver must depend on crossing: [`next:` only changes goal display](../../../../../docs/story-format-spec.md), so it cannot by itself prevent sampling before the rover crosses.

## Water approximately 15 metres from the habitat

**Use buried massive ice beneath a dry, dusty bench.** Shallow subsurface ice and layered buried deposits are observed on Mars; they are not a promise that any red plain contains usable ice. Select an invented, previously surveyed midlatitude site with sufficiently thick ice beneath its regolith cover. The habitat stands on a separate sound foundation, outside the modeled heated cavity and ground deformation. Fifteen metres is the requested scene separation, not a universal safe setback. [NASA's shallow-ice mapping](https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/mars/nasa-is-locating-ice-on-mars-with-this-new-map/); [USGS research on exposed buried ice](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/exposed-subsurface-ice-sheets-martian-mid-latitudes).

Show a small capped casing protruding from dusty ground, a modest collar and drill spoil. Once installed, the extraction head has a short insulated neck, compact controls and a real outlet. The melted cavity is underground. Do not depict an open blue pond, steaming crater or ice cubes surrounding the bore. A cased bore must penetrate far enough into competent ice to retain a roof; shallow ice access does not mean the working cavity is immediately below the surface. NASA's Rodwell work supplies the physical precedent for drilling, casing, heating and pumping. Its later laboratory report still identifies design and scaling uncertainties; it does not demonstrate a tiny complete flight installation. [2018 system study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180007948); [2020 experiments and limits](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20205011353).

**Composition and connections:** place the well in the near left, the distinct water processor a short distance to its right, and the familiar hab about 15 m behind them. Keep the same deployed station array left of the door, air scrubber right, and mounted antenna. The processor is an additional machine near the well, not a replacement for the scrubber. Perspective and a long visible ground run establish distance.

1. Fit extraction head to casing.
2. Lay the short raw-water hose continuously from head outlet to processor inlet.
3. Lay the separate longer treated-water hose continuously from processor outlet to a habitat water service port. Route it around the array and doorway, never into the scrubber.
4. Wrap the two installed runs along exactly their existing paths; the supplied trace heating and controls protect them against freezing.

Each hose operation includes both ends. Insulation can be one bundled operation over both runs. Keep the raw-water strainer upstream of treatment for the later discovery. In author notes, retain controlled well pressure, treatment for dissolved contaminants as well as particles, heat input, leak checks and isolation. A two-hose illustration shows the delivery path; any downhole heat circulation belongs to the extraction assembly and is not a third hose to the hab.

For the remote bank, show pale grey-white, dusty ice under a thin red-brown cap, with subdued rusty layers and small fractures. Use a surveyed low stable exposure without an overhang. Bright blue in the famous HiRISE scarps is enhanced colour; it is not a mandate to paint a neon-blue glacier. Real observed scarps can be very tall and actively retreating, so the convenient low sampling exposure is a declared local story premise. [JPL image and explanation](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia22077-underground-martian-ice-deposit-exposed-at-scarp/).

## A small preparation step and an open microscope

**Use a small cold-cut viewing slab, followed by microscopy.** Cutting and smoothing ice for microscopy are real operations. Modern ice microscopy research uses sawing, a microtome and low-temperature surface preparation; debris-rich ice can require a diamond wire rather than a plain blade. These are procedural precedents, not evidence for an existing miniature Mars kit. [Low-temperature microscopy methods](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/annals-of-glaciology/article/development-of-a-lowtemperature-immersion-microscopy-technique-for-ice-research/B74DDE0F4A51B49D29F4D37A80C8DB70); [debris-bearing ice sectioning](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-glaciology/article/instruments-and-methods-diamond-wiresaw-cutting-technique-for-investigating-textures-and-fabrics-of-debrisladen-ice-and-brittle-ice/D65D2CF9903ADCF23D49D1BA5C31AADB).

The visible setup can be one rigid lightweight tabletop on simple legs, a small cold preparation cradle with a guided fine saw, and one standalone optical microscope with LED illumination and image recording. Leave the space below open. The microscope's optics and stand need no enclosing cabinet. A small windowed specimen cassette sits on its cooled stage; the compact cold stage has real terrestrial precedent. Do not imply that the cassette itself refrigerates anything, or hide an entire unspecified laboratory inside the table. Cooling connects to the established sample-storage service. [Example of a microscope stage with local temperature control](https://www.linkam.co.uk/thms600).

Two actions:

1. **Ice core at cold preparation cradle → Prepared ice section.** The integrated operation records orientation and the cut location, takes a thin longitudinal viewing slab beside the visible band, smooths its optical faces and closes it in a flat holder. Keep the slab thicker than the complete inclusion. This is a thin slice relative to the core, not a prescribed micrometre-thick histology section through the body. The remainder stays in its original cold liner.
2. **Prepared ice section at microscope → Examined remote specimen.** The instrument reveals the articulated outline and its enclosing ice. The section and remainder return to cooling together as one registered remote-core package.

Quiet author notes should provide a small cooled contained preparation cassette, clean cutting surfaces, a retained preparation blank, dry internal conditions to avoid frost, below-freezing handling, and an optical window compatible with the required image. This allows an unsealed microscope stand without exposing the specimen to habitat air. A completely open warm slide would undermine both ice preservation and the contamination-sensitive investigation. No stain, immersion oil, glue or melt/refreeze step is needed for the proposed first observation; keep the main return material available for later chemistry.

Preserve three specimen identities: **source core; remote core plus its registered viewing section; original membrane**. Update downstream “whole core” and “never opened” descriptions accordingly, and prefer “unmelted” to “untouched” after preparation. The source core remains a separate reference outside the well's disturbed zone. Shape alone still establishes neither age, composition, life, taxon nor Martian origin. The fictional inclusion's size, optical visibility and successful preservation remain explicit assumptions. This is a compact visual proposal, not a demonstrated minimum mass or qualified mission design.
