# Mars design brief

This records the story’s lasting visual intent. The manuscript owns equipment, observations and combinations; [UX](../../docs/ux.md) owns interaction, and the [icon guide](../../docs/icon-guide.md) owns the production and review workflow. Sources, generation requests and registration records belong to the completed [Mars artwork study](<../../projects/mars-study/README.md>).

Use the [inspiration verdicts](<../../projects/mars-study/inspiration/README.txt>) and the preferred [multiple-accent equipment reference](<../../projects/mars-study/inspiration/02-multiple-accents.png>).

## Purpose and physical character

Make the expedition’s equipment feel like physical things the player can pick up, repair and fit together. Preserve the story’s progression from contact and shelter through rescue, shared habitation, field evidence and return. Useful work and the people it serves should remain visible in the finished constructions.

Use restrained retro industrial forms, believable cream-painted housings, fabric, exposed metal and rubber. Clear silhouettes and identifying structures take priority over tiny fasteners and surface decoration. Keep modest dust, handling marks and wear, with neither pristine showroom gloss nor heavy scrapyard distress. Use consistent elevated three-quarter views and broadly neutral upper-left illumination so carried equipment belongs in both exterior and interior scenes. Avoid large ground shadows attached to movable objects.

## Physical size and shape

Use a physically informed size hierarchy at the 390 × 844 reference viewport. The longest visible edge of a small standalone object is at least 60px, excluding transparent padding. Small fittings, seeds and sample containers stay near that floor; hand tools and records are larger where their structure needs it, and long instruments retain their long, narrow shape. Portable apparatus, panels, blankets and frames are larger again. Related objects and repeated components should agree in scale.

Rovers and crawlers should feel substantial, normally about 190–220px across. Deliberately compress buildings and spacecraft to a maximum longest visible edge near 240px so they remain manipulable within a busy scene. These bounds compress the extremes of real size; they are not a requirement to fill every object’s rectangular tile. Preserve natural aspect ratios and crop transparent margins closely. Export artwork at four times its intended display dimensions.

Fitted components use their host’s physical scale independently of their standalone minimum. Design compound bases and fittings together. Keep the base’s camera, silhouette and registration fixed across installation states; add each component where it would attach, without resizing the whole assembly or concealing other installed parts. A large finished machine should still reveal the contributions that made it useful.

## Equipment states and evidence

Keep object identity through repair and preparation: the repaired radio is the original radio, a covered seed tray remains the same tray, and restrained or insulated shelters retain their frames. Show meaningful structural additions and visible changes rather than relying on colour alone. The solar charger includes its power storage; the solar panel by itself does not.

Represent information as tangible expedition records: photographs of recognizable subjects, specimen labels, field boards, diagrams, instrument records and navigation displays. Distinguish observation, comparison and interpretation through their visible content and arrangement. Avoid making every finding an interchangeable tablet or status light. Ordinary item names and readings supply exact findings, preparation history and qualifications that a small picture cannot communicate. Preserve the distinction between specimens and their records, and between recorded evidence and an established conclusion.

## Landscapes and working places

Compose fixed hotspots as actual parts of their landscapes and rooms, following Settlement’s integrated treatment. Use transparent registered hit areas over painted terrain, equipment or architecture where appropriate. Fixed targets should have a clear physical identity and room to receive the relevant object; their ordinary names and interaction feedback remain supplied by the game.

Keep generous quiet working areas for large vehicles and differently shaped tools. Place stronger scenery around the working field, with coherent landmarks and architecture across recurring refuge, greenhouse and depot scenes. Give exposed terrain, sheltered repair, habitation, cultivation and orbital arrival distinct settings while retaining a common world. Do not paint future movable constructions into the background before the player makes them.

## Color design language

Use color to give equipment families their own character while retaining a coherent industrial collection. Warm cream housings, ivory fabric, exposed metal and charcoal rubber provide the shared base. Apply color to panels, bands, caps, linings and fittings, with one principal accent family per object.

| Accent | Equipment family | Examples in the preferred render |
|---|---|---|
| Petrol teal (`#237C80`) | Shelter and repair | Habitat-fabric lining, sealant band, seam roller, hatch latches, patch supplies and vestibule lining/fittings. |
| Muted mustard yellow (`#C4A039`) | Mobility | Rover panels and mobility fittings. |
| Cobalt blue (`#3765A6`) | Science, sampling and analysis | Sample-jar lid, core-tube band and glovebox hardware. |
| Restrained brick red (`#A84037`) | Tie-downs and small safety fittings | Tie-down cord and selected safety hardware. This is a limited equipment accent, not a claim of rescue visibility on Mars. |

The hex values are approximate paint-color targets in neutral light; sunlight, shadows, dust and wear should affect their rendered appearance. Keep the colored areas restrained and the physical materials believable. Preserve the heater's orange glow, warm headlights and glovebox illumination, and red/amber/green signal lamps according to their function. Natural terrain, rock samples, ice and photovoltaic cells retain their material colors.

Orange rescue clothing is an Earth/ocean reference; it does not establish visibility against Martian terrain. The cool accents explore separation from the warm ground. The equipment-family colors are an art direction, not a new gameplay rule.
