# Report

Story: projects/creation-study/creation.md

Play style: --detailed

**Definitions**
- Worthwhile: counts actions supported by meaningful decision-making or a valued story payoff or reason to keep playing, counting each action once. Show the average credited actions / average total gameplay actions per player, not an average percentage.
- Fun: a narrower count of meaningful decisions: worthwhile deliberation or intentional experimentation. Measured by --detailed, including inspections and unsuccessful well-formed attempts; --fast reports it as not measured. Fast menu-assisted diagnostic ratios are not comparable.
- Hooks: the players' reported desire to continue: "high" means specific eagerness, "medium" means mild or conditional interest, and "low" means indifference or reluctance

## Chapter 1: Create the heavens and the earth

Worthwhile: 6/8.3 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 4/8.3 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium/high (1 medium, 2 high)

Strongest moments: Asteroids + Rocky world → Moon is the shared peak: all three infer a lunar impact from the collision hint, then identify the explanation linking debris, tilt and reflected moonlight as especially worthwhile. Moon + Rocky world → Steadied world adds value without a difficult choice. All three specifically appreciate that the Moon limits changes in tilt rather than fixing it forever, and connect this sequence to their willingness to continue.

Weakest moments: Finding the asteroid route exposes limited recipe legibility. Players 1 and 3 receive an unchosen recipe after their Earth + Void Moon hypothesis fails; Player 3 calls that attempt least worthwhile. Player 2's preliminary Rocky world inspection is their least worthwhile moment, despite meaningfully narrowing the search. The weakness concerns feedback and discoverability, not the later impact payoff. Sun + Rocky world → Light adds little evidenced payoff beyond completing the daylight requirement. All three execute the obvious pairing, and Player 1 explicitly calls Sun creation and illumination predictable; Players 2 and 3 do not report comparable frustration.

## Chapter 2: Rain, oceans and tides

Worthwhile: 9/10 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 5.7/10 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium/high (2 medium, 1 high)

Strongest moments: Water + Stone crust → Steam cloud, followed by Steam cloud + Air → First rain, was the shared explanatory high point. All three valued the vapour/droplet distinction and rain mechanism; inspection or a motivated failed receiver test supplied decision value without requiring independent discovery of the successful recipe. Tides + Ocean → Tidal shore delivered a satisfying wet/dry habitat payoff for all three despite recipe reservations. For Players 2 and 3, its connection to coastal life supplied a concrete reason to consider continuing.

Weakest moments: Forming a coast through Stone crust repeatedly conflicted with players' spatial expectations. All tried Tides + Stone crust, and Player 1 also tried Ocean + Stone crust. These meaningful tests nevertheless exposed unintuitive recipe boundaries; Player 1 explicitly found the repetition weak. Air + Water offered Players 2 and 3 little explanatory feedback and was their stated least worthwhile attempt. Player 1's reversed Water + Air command revealed useful partners immediately, indicating operand-sensitive interface friction rather than a substantive difference in the weather hypothesis.

## Chapter 3: The water cycle

Worthwhile: 7.7/8 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 5/8 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium

Strongest moments: Rivers + Earth → Sediment, followed by Sediment + Rivers → Delta, was the shared strongest sequence. Players valued transported material becoming new land through slowing flow and branching channels; all named delta formation most worthwhile. The intermediate sediment explanation also received its own clarification or usefulness testimony. Rivers + Sun → Water cycle supplied a valued synthesis for all three players despite being independently classified as routine execution. Players 1 and 3 explicitly found the integrated explanation satisfying; Player 2 valued how it brought the stages together. Player 3's abstract-recipe criticism remains compatible with this payoff.

Weakest moments: Rivers + Ocean failed despite all three players expecting river-mouth deposition. Each named it a least-worthwhile moment, with Player 1 explicitly reporting frustration. The weakness lies in the recipe boundary resisting their process model, even though each independent grader credited the motivated test. Rainfall required Clouds + Water after all three tried another plausible partner. Players 1 and 2 called that requirement somewhat arbitrary; Player 3 reported surprise rather than the same explicit criticism. Automatic redirection kept progress moving but supplied the recipe instead of allowing players to resolve the distinction themselves.

## Chapter 4: Moving plates and continents

Worthwhile: 9.3/10 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 7.7/10 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium/high (2 medium, 1 high)

Strongest moments: Volcano + Water → Hydrothermal vent is the shared standout: all three players name the connection between volcanic heat, seawater chemistry, and life without sunlight as most worthwhile. It supplies a concrete reason to explore life's development even where the independent grader regards the combination itself as routine. Weathered rock + Rivers → Mineral-rich sea gives Players 1 and 3 explicitly satisfying closure to the weathering-and-transport sequence. Player 2 reports expected completion instead, so this payoff is not treated as universal or as fresh decision value.

Weakest moments: Direct Rivers–Continent transport is rejected despite all three players independently expecting it from the goal and erosion explanation. Later weathering restores causal clarity, but the initial recipe prerequisite creates confusion. Operand-sensitive feedback compounds the friction: Player 2 receives alternatives, while Players 1 and 3 receive a bare rejection. Trench + Fire becomes an unproductive dead end for Players 2 and 3, both of whom identify it as their weakest detour. Their motivated experiments retain independent meaningful credit, and the trench's subduction explanation still has value. Player 1 does not attempt this combination, so its frustration is not shared by all three.
