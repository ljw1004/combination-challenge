**Definitions**
- Worthwhile: counts actions supported by meaningful decision-making or a valued story payoff or reason to keep playing, counting each action once. Show the average credited actions / average total gameplay actions per player, not an average percentage.
- Fun: a narrower count of meaningful decisions: worthwhile deliberation or intentional experimentation. Measured by --detailed, including inspections and unsuccessful well-formed attempts; --fast reports it as not measured. Fast menu-assisted diagnostic ratios are not comparable.
- Hooks: the players' reported desire to continue: "high" means specific eagerness, "medium" means mild or conditional interest, and "low" means indifference or reluctance

## Chapter 1: Create the heavens and the earth

Worthwhile: 5.7/8 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium/high (2 medium, 1 high)

Strongest moments: Asteroids striking the rocky world to form the Moon is the shared peak: all three players infer a Moon-forming impact and select the resulting connection among debris, tilt and reflected light as most worthwhile. Combining the Moon with the rocky world to steady its tilt supplies a separate shared explanatory payoff. All three value the qualification that the angle can still vary and cite the Moon explanations when expressing interest in continuing.

Weakest moments: Creating the Sun and applying it to the rocky world produces two predictable actions with little separately valued payoff. Players 2 and 3 identify the whole sequence as least engaging; Player 1 specifically singles out illumination. Putting Earth into the surrounding deep initially leaves all three unsure why that receiving place helps make a Moon. The asteroid explanation repairs the connection, with stronger discovery testimony from Players 2 and 3; Player 2's grader nevertheless credits the initial choice as meaningful, unlike the other graders.

## Chapter 2: Rain, oceans and tides

Worthwhile: 6/7 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium

Strongest moments: Making clouds and first rain: Water + Stone crust supplies all three players with an appreciated vapour/droplet distinction, with Players 1 and 3 reporting an unexpected result. Players 1 and 2 select it as most worthwhile; Player 3 instead favors Steam cloud + Air for explaining rainfall. Ocean + Tides → Tidal shore: all three explicitly find the wet-and-dry habitat conclusion satisfying. Ocean + Tides → Tidal shore: Players 1 and 2 find the coast's origin insufficiently established; Player 3 finds the extra action somewhat redundant. The habitat payoff does not remove this shared recipe-level weakness.

## Chapter 3: The water cycle

Worthwhile: 5.7/7 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium

Strongest moments: Sediment + Rivers → Delta is the shared standout: all three players value slowing flow depositing sediment into branching new land. Routine execution delivers a concrete construction payoff and a reason to continue. Ocean + Sun → Evaporation supplies a separately valued distinction for all three players: water leaves as invisible vapour while salts remain. Player 1 also identifies it as a revelation in the final report.

Weakest moments: Clouds + Water → Rainfall is predictably routine for Players 1 and 2 and mechanical for Players 2 and 3. Player 3 also finds the cloud-to-rain sequence repetitive. The weakness concerns recipe representation and repetition, not command handling. Rivers + Sun → Water cycle has a recipe-to-explanation mismatch for Players 2 and 3: they expect river evaporation but receive a whole-cycle summary. This is not unanimously weak; Players 1 and 3 value the closing synthesis, and Player 3 explicitly finds it satisfying.

## Chapter 4: Moving plates and continents

Worthwhile: 6.7/7 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium/high (1 medium, 2 high)

Strongest moments: Volcano + Water → Hydrothermal vent was the shared strongest payoff: all three valued the connection between volcanic heat, seawater chemistry and life without sunlight, even though the combination itself executed an established plan. Continent + Rainfall → Weathered rock combined a meaningful weathering inference with interest in mineral pigments across all three players. For Player 2 specifically, the pigment detail became an unresolved question motivating continuation.

Weakest moments: Moving plates + Water → Trench repeatedly disappointed the intended hot-spring route. All three found the explanation useful, but the ingredient mapping remained indirect or difficult to anticipate; this was recipe legibility, not protocol friction. Weathered rock + Rivers → Mineral-rich sea offered predictable follow-through rather than a fresh decision. Players 1 and 3 identified limited novelty, but Players 1 and 2 also explicitly valued its satisfying closure.
