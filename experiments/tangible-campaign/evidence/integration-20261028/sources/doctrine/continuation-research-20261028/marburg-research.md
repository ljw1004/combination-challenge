# Marburg desk: agreement without sameness

Research only, 2026-10-28. No candidate edit, publication, UI work, or fresh-player claim.

## Finding

A Marburg continuation is justified if it lets the player identify a particular unresolved question while retaining genuinely shared commitments. Do not award a theological verdict. Six constructions can use two actual earlier creations rather than treating old manuscripts as keys.

## Checked primary sources

1. **Zwingli, On the Lord’s Supper (1526)**, Second and Third Articles; English translation Geoffrey W. Bromiley, *Zwingli and Bullinger*, Library of Christian Classics24 (1953), pp185–238. [Full text](https://www.uecf.net/books/zwingli-and-bullinger/chapter5.html). The primary text starts at **THE TEXT**, after the translator’s introduction. The latter is distinctly partisan and must not be quoted as Zwingli. This is an OCR-derived transcription, not a facsimile checked against the German. Its relevant paragraphs are coherent, but new long quotations require edition checking.
   - Third Article, closing explanation of the commemorative rite: the widow/ring analogy uses **“This is my late husband”** (6 words). It makes naming by representation intelligible, not compulsory.
   - Second Article, discussion beginning with three articles of the creed: bodily ascension and continued divine presence are distinguished; do not turn his position into absence of Christ or absence of spiritual reception.
   - Recommended use: paraphrase the argument, preserve attribution, avoid importing the translator’s judgments or Zwingli’s caricature that opponents literally tear Christ with their teeth.
2. **Luther, Large Catechism, Part V (1529), §§8–18,33–37.** [Primary text](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/sacrament-of-the-altar/). [Translation provenance](https://bookofconcord.org/about-the-translation/): F. Bente/W. H. T. Dau, *Concordia Triglotta*1921, public domain. Bread and wine joined to Christ’s words are distinguished from ordinary food; validity is distinguished from the communicant’s faithful reception of benefit. Luther’s positive ground is the instituting promise, not a sensory experiment.
3. **Marburg Articles, 3 October1529**, Articles6–10 and15. [GHI primary document](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/from-the-reformations-to-the-thirty-years-war-1500-1648/ghdi:document-4311). Ellen Yutzy Glebe translation; paraphrase. Article15 records both spiritual-reception agreement and unresolved corporal presence. Its commitment to Christian love is qualified by conscience. It is not evidence that reconciliation occurred, that every participant meant exactly the same thing, or that modern religious toleration had been adopted.
4. **Zwingli to Joachim Vadian, 20 October1529.** [GHI primary letter](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/from-the-reformations-to-the-thirty-years-war-1500-1648/the-marburg-colloquy-ulrich-zwinglis-report-october-20-1529). Jackson1901 translation edited by GHI. Own eyewitness account, intensely partisan; valuable for contested reports and self-presentation, not a neutral transcript or verdict that Zwingli won.
5. **Andreas Osiander’s report (1529).** [GHI primary testimony](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/from-the-reformations-to-the-thirty-years-war-1500-1648/the-marburg-colloquy-report-by-a-lutheran-eyewitness-1529). Osiander arrived Saturday afternoon and explicitly learned the morning discussion from others. His report is primary testimony but the opening session is secondhand. Use as corroboration and provenance challenge, not sole evidence for Zwingli’s reasoning.

## Recommended six constructions

These are editorial reconstructions, not historical events or assertions that these sheets literally lay together.

| Inputs | Created distinction | Why the pair matters |
|---|---|---|
| Widow showing ring + institution wording | A sign can bear its referent’s name | Analogy opens an interpretation without pretending to establish it. |
| That interpretation + ascension/return leaf (with human/divine distinction) | Zwingli’s reading of the meal | Interpretation is constrained by his wider account, not a word-matching rule. |
| **Earlier `substance-and-appearance`** + Luther’s bread-and-Word account | Bodily presence is not one single theory of bread | Do not equate Luther with Aquinas’s substantial conversion. Explicitly label this an editorial comparison. |
| The two later readings | The unresolved bodily-presence question | Shared bread or shared scriptural wording does not resolve this disagreement. |
| **Earlier `free-service`** + Article10/15 conduct commitment | Disagreement does not cancel the stated claim of love | Applies an earlier made distinction to a new, uncomfortable case; does not claim actual conduct fulfilled it. |
| The unresolved question + Article15 shared spiritual reception | A statement they can sign without merging accounts | Final outcome has precise limits; no victory badge. |

Possible stronger local side branch, only if the desk needs it: Luther’s Word principle + a clearly fictional unworthy-minister case → the minister’s personal virtue does not make the sacrament valid. Distinguish this from the benefit received through faith. Do not add merely to increase recipe count.

## Authenticity and gameplay objections to retain

- The ring analogy alone does not demonstrate the Eucharistic conclusion. The intermediate must say **may**, not **therefore must**.
- Do not combine a generic “Catholic” card with a generic “Protestant” card. Aquinas, Luther, Zwingli and the shared document make distinct claims.
- Do not use Zwingli’s1530 *Account of Faith* or1531 *Exposition* as sheets available at the1529 meeting. They are not needed here.
- Keep short readings optional and attributed. Pair success should reconstruct relation, application, or constraint, not reward recognition of identical words.
- Neither the shared text nor a successful recipe is empirical proof of presence, grace, or doctrinal truth.
- Earlier creations must actually be earned and carried forward. Runtime lifecycle checks must demonstrate this; the research alone does not establish it.
