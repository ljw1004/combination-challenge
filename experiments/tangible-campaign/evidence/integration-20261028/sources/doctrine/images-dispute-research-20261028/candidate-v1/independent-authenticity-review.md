## Primary-text checks

### Calvin on images

The seven-word art quotation matches I.11 §12. His distinction allows visible historical scenes without licensing their devotional use or church installation. §§9–11 address honour mediated through an image, not merely someone thinking paint literally is God. §13 contrasts humanly devised church images with word-consecrated signs. Thus the fictional rescue sketch and the panel refusal are compatible, and the petitioner's stated intention does not automatically answer his objection. The candidate does not repeat his polemical claims about early church history as independent findings. [Calvin, *Institutes* I.11 §§9–13, Beveridge translation](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.iii.xii.html).

### Calvin's actual relic treatise

The work begins at printed p.217, after the translator's dissertation. The relevant catalogue reports are in Calvin's text at p.255: a face at Amiens and a whole head at St Sylvester, Rome. His admission about having no visiting commissioners appears at pp.276–277. The four-word genuine-or-false quotation is in his concluding objection at p.278. No finding here depends on the introduction or a translator's numbered note. The candidate rightly presents one reporter, not two independent witnesses; comparison asks for reconciliation rather than proving fraud. Its grant of authenticity remains hypothetical through `images-genuine-refusal` and the ending. [Calvin, *Treatise on Relics*, Krasinski translation, 1870 edition, pp.217, 254–256, 276–278](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/32136/pg32136-images.html).

### Trent's positive teaching and limiting procedure

The decree affirms veneration of saints' bodies and benefits bestowed by God through them. Its separate image paragraph locates honour in the represented person rather than resident divinity. The candidate does not collapse those accounts into a universal denial of effects through material things. Its positive relic teaching is abbreviated, but explicitly retained in the review-rule description, rather than replaced by general scepticism.

The same decree requires episcopal approval for unusual images and recognition of new relics, with consultation and further procedures for difficult matters. The panel is explicitly unusual from its initial petition. Referral and withholding recognition are justified hypothetical applications, not fabricated historical decisions. General image teaching is never made sufficient approval for this particular installation. [Trent, Session XXV, decree on saints, relics and images, Waterworth translation, pp.233–236](https://history.hanover.edu/texts/trent/ct25.html).

### Aquinas: exact causal role and limits of the older input

III q.62 a.1 distinguishes principal and instrumental efficient causality and rejects reducing sacraments to accompanying signs. Divine institution matters to the instrumental account. The old token preserves that stronger argument. Its first new use makes a material-agency objection insufficient without establishing this panel's appointment; its second distinguishes baptismal causality from the council's account of image-honour. Neither use turns Aquinas into Calvin, proves a panel is a sacrament, or disproves all spiritual effects of images. [Aquinas, *Summa theologiae* III q.62 a.1](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/4062.htm).
