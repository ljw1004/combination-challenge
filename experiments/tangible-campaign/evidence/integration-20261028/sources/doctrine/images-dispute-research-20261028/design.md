# Proposed case combinations — not executable content

Source IDs refer to [source-ledger.md](source-ledger.md). Everything in the combination tables is an **editorial game-design proposal**, not a claim that the authors performed these combinations. The phrases in the output column are working labels, not final copy.

## The physical case-file

Use a few recurring cases, with readable labels and attributed source leaves. The object can remain visible as its annotations change. No new UI mechanic is required: ordinary binary recipes and normal highlighting suffice.

1. **A painted martyr panel proposed for a church.** Fictional teaching case. Its accompanying note asks whether it can serve as the font does; it does not state that Catholic teaching regards painted wood as a deity.
2. **A worshipper kneels before that panel.** The fictional worshipper says the honour concerns the saint. The game should take that intention seriously without deciding the gesture is thereby authorised.
3. **A sketch of a river rescue.** Fictional depiction of a visible human event, without worship or a claimed likeness of divine essence. This controls the “Calvin bans every picture” misreading.
4. **Two slips from Calvin's relic catalogue.** One records his report of a whole head in Rome, the other his report of a face at Amiens. Source boundaries remain attached. Neither slip is a verified museum record.
5. **A sealed new relic petition, with no provenance attached.** Fictional diocesan case, introduced on the Trent desk. It is not one of the named objects in Calvin's catalogue. A separate assumption card can grant authenticity for an argument without certifying any real object.

Do not use an anonymous “magic statue” as the sole Catholic case. It would make the opposition trivially agree against a position neither text endorses.

## Desk A: What belongs in this church?

The player's work is to annotate what changes when a thing is **pictured**, **used devotionally**, or **claimed authentic**. C1 and C2 supply the historical positions; the scenarios supply applications.

| “Suppose this relic is genuine” + C2's concluding objection | **Authenticity would not answer Calvin** | Separates the two questions without requiring the player to disprove a miracle. |

**Candidate goals, not seven mandatory objectives:** explain the font/panel boundary; account for the worshipper's intention without losing Calvin's objection; separate authenticity from permitted veneration. The rescue case may be a short lateral discovery. The catalogue case should not become a collection puzzle demanding arbitrary anatomical totals.

One original creation is an assured physical carry-in; a second is proposed conditionally. Preserve their content: neither earlier argument is consumed as a disproved belief. The two first outputs are partial case annotations, not new general axioms.

**Actual checkpoint constraint:** the existing completed route has incorporated `later-sign-language` into `later-bounded-agreement`; there is no loose ring-derived token. The proposed callback cannot simply be added after that state. A new future recipe would keep the token alive during a full replay through the engine's `needed()` rule, altering earlier retirement. Either explicitly accept and test that change, or omit the two-step ring route and use the direct kneeling-case/C1 combination. Do not substitute the whole Marburg manuscript as a generic key just to preserve a continuity count. The assured `instrument-of-grace` route already meets the per-chapter continuity requirement.

## Desk B: What should the bishop permit?

Reopen the same panel and objections. Introduce the fictional petition only when the player has already distinguished an identity question from a permission question. T1 supplies both the positive doctrine and the limiting procedure.

| Proposed combination | Working output | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|---|
| Calvin's panel-objection annotation + T1's image paragraph | **The council reads the same gesture differently** | An attributed reply, not a proof that every painting or gesture is acceptable. The contested practice remains visible. |
| That revised panel file + **`instrument-of-grace`** | **This panel has not become another baptism** | A required use of an original earned creation. Keep reference, honour, and sacramental efficient causality distinct. Do not assert that images can have no spiritual effects whatsoever. |
| The questioned catalogue + the new petition | **This petition still lacks its own evidence** | Neither a confident label nor the polemic authenticates this newly submitted object. The two real reported objects are not silently identified with the fictional one. |
| That deficient petition + T1's recognition procedure | **Hold recognition; request examination** | The practical outcome is limited to the hypothetical new claim. It does not announce a historical bishop's decision. |
| That hold decision + the earlier “authenticity would not answer Calvin” result | **The present refusal conceals different next steps** | A potentially surprising ending: more evidence could change the clerk's disposition, not Calvin's rule. The shared present action is not doctrinal agreement. |

**Candidate goals:** qualify the panel's role; decide what to do with the unsupported new petition; explain whether new provenance would remove the disagreement. No need for a final all-arguments-into-one manuscript recipe.

The T1 source leaf must retain the positive account of holy bodies as well as the recognition rule. Otherwise the practical hold would misleadingly suggest a generally sceptical or anti-relic council. The image paragraph and body paragraph must not be collapsed into one “no power in matter” card.

## Alternative branch: Who may be asked to pray?

Use this **instead of the relic branch** if saint invocation is the desired next serious issue. Images remain the other branch. These four conceptual combinations show the shape; wording still needs a human read.

### Concrete material

- A neighbour's request for prayer (fictional ordinary case).
- A prayer addressed to Peter in heaven asking him to pray, not to act as an independent saviour (editorial specimen; the short Latin form is also discussed by Calvin in III.20.22).
- The adjacent petitions and mediator passages of 1 Timothy 2; C3 §§19, 24; T1's invocation paragraph.

### Possible path

1. **`limits-1517` + the neighbour's request** → *Does asking another abolish the principal?* The earlier argument makes the question precise, rather than proving a position on heavenly saints.
2. **That question + C3 §19 / the 1 Timothy passage** → *Mutual prayer is not a second redeemer.* The living case is allowed on Calvin's own terms.
3. **That result + the Peter petition, read with C3 §24** → *The living case does not establish this address.* This needs two binary steps or a source-attached case, not a silently three-input recipe. The remaining issue is warrant and communication, not a simple count of intercessors.
4. On the Trent desk, **that unresolved petition + T1** → *The council authorises the request under Christ.* Retain **`limits-1517`** as a second step to explain dependent help without inferring that Trent and Luther's earlier ministry coincide.

This branch's merit is that it resists both caricatures: “Catholics install extra saviours” and “Calvin forbids praying for one another.” Its weakness is the amount of theological scope that short labels may conceal. It is not ready to implement from these four lines alone.

## Optional evidence, not automatic extra content

Gregory's broken-pictures letter supplies a third position on teaching and adoration. It could replace the rescue sketch if a human reader wants more actual historical texture. That replacement changes the argument and needs its own recipe review; do not append it solely for breadth.

The bronze serpent offers a good same-object/different-use problem. It would be preferable to another generic commandment card, but a Calvin-specific commentary citation remains unchecked in this pass. Keep it out of an attributed Calvin recipe until that gap is closed.

## Logical review requirements before any implementation

These are **proposed acceptance questions**, not passed tests. Only the existing checkpoint's token audit has actually run:

- Can a reader explain why the early baptism result is relevant without assuming it settles the new case?
- Does the ring-derived result distinguish *what is meant* from *what is permitted*?
- Does the player retain a real art-permitting example, rather than leaving with “Calvin hates art”?
- Do reported catalogue claims retain their reporter and uncertainty after combination?
- Does a hypothetical grant of authenticity remain a grant, never transform into an authenticated real relic?
- Does the final Catholic hold remain open to a changed evidential state while the Calvin objection is not thereby answered?
- Can each desk be completed with the actual final ten-desks checkpoint's creations, without manufacturing an earlier raw source that an alternative valid path may have used differently?
- Under normal highlighting, do multiple productive next actions remain intelligible? Do not call it success because an automated reader guesses every pair quickly or because it struggles with obscure labels.
- Is the second desk experienced as a change in the same case, rather than a repetitive opposite-label exercise?

If the last answer is no, prefer one well-focused mixed dossier or stop at the ten-desk ending. More source material is not itself a reason to add a chapter.
