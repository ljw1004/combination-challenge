# Source review: optional Sugar note

Consulted **Richard Ligon’s actual 1657 text**, not the automatically generated ebook landing-page summary:

[Richard Ligon, *A True & Exact History of the Island of Barbados*](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70489/pg70489-images.html), printed for Humphrey Moseley, London, 1657; online transcription checked 2026-10-28.

- **Printed pp. 22–23:** the completed purchase of half of Major Hilliard’s sugar plantation includes enslaved people in its enumerated property. Ligon describes assisting its management. This supports the first sentence and identifies the author’s interested colonial standpoint.
- **Printed pp. 113–116:** Ligon presents projected annual plantation expenses, including provisions and clothing for enslaved workers and their treatment as replaceable property. This is an authentic historical *published projection*, **not an estate ledger or proof that its hypothetical figures were actually paid**. Accordingly the note says “projected plantation budget.” No figures are reproduced in the game.
- **Scope:** seventeenth-century Barbados, a particular early-modern Atlantic plantation sugar economy. The text explicitly refuses to stand for every region or period of sugar history.
- **Voice:** contemporary game-author paraphrase and contextualization, not a quotation and not enslaved workers’ own testimony. Ligon’s account is evidence about the colonial system, not an endorsement of his attitudes.
