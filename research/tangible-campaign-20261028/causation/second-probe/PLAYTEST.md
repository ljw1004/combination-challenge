# Second probe: test plan

## Hypothesis and scope

The first probe successfully communicated end-relative quality, but its only physical progression was prescribed. This probe tests **multiple fitting means and the possibility of reusing an earlier artifact unchanged**. It does not require hard strategic choice at every action; obvious, comfortable crafting counts as a positive experience.

The assignment is `FRESH-PLAYER-ASSIGNMENT.txt`. Give it unchanged to a new source-blind controller. Do not disclose the first probe's lesson, the source discussion, available routes, or why the crate and basket were selected. One player's route may leave the comparison unobserved; report that as inconclusive, not success.

## Protocol correctness

- All object gestures use `drop`, resolved by the unmodified runtime's `M.dropAction`.
- No raw place-at-location command substitutes for a browser drop.
- Automatic advancement, terse acceptance feedback, current views and partner help mirror the relevant logic.
- The logical `playerView` exposes descriptions more fully than the browser; do not generalize accessibility/discoverability findings to the visual UI.
- Baseline and every command record source hashes. Resuming after source changes is rejected.
- The full transcript includes requested gesture, resolved action, before/after view, hint output, and all engine events including automatic advances.

## Observe before asking

1. Which carrier gets made first, and why? Does the player independently discover another?
2. Does ordinary rope-making or carrier-making feel comfortable, satisfying, or merely administrative?
3. Does the second end guide a prediction from properties, or only a search for a highlighted pair?
4. If a basket exists, does the player recognize it can work unchanged?
5. If a closed crate exists, does the player adapt it, make/use a basket, or try another plausible method?
6. If both exist, is preserving the closed crate meaningful to the player, or irrelevant clutter?
7. Does the short quarry collection convention make sense, or feel like arbitrary parking?
8. Does an unimplemented but plausible action become the main interest again? Keep that negative evidence.

## After the unprompted report

Only after the assignment's six questions are answered, ask a few relevant counterfactuals:

- “Could you have supplied a different carrier for the first job?”
- “For the second job, did a successful first object necessarily need changing?”
- “If fine sand, rather than coarse stones, were the next cargo, which of the things you have would work?”
- “Did having the tools determine what you should make, or did something else matter too?”
- “Could a different arrangement meet the mason's need? What would it have to do?”

Answers to these are prompted diagnostic evidence, separate from spontaneous play. They do not turn the game into a quiz or prove a uniquely Aristotelian insight.

## Decision

**Continue investigation** if the player enjoys comfortable making and notices/uses legitimate alternatives or unchanged reuse. Still do not promote a full causality campaign from one controller.

**Keep only the craft result** if it is pleasant but feels like another recipe chain. End-relative usefulness is not uniquely Aristotle, and a recipe success does not establish causal pluralism.

**Rethink the content model** if the alternatives seem nominal, the rope step feels like paperwork, or the collection trip is unreadable. Do not lengthen the chain, add an arbitrary budget, or invent a compulsory choice quota to rescue the hypothesis.

No human or source-blind session is included in the author validation. Exact route and semantic-search results are correctness evidence only.
