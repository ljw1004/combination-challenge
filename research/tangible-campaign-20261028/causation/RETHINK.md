# Rethinking “Why This Boat?”

Research and design proposal, 28 October 2026. Not an endorsed replacement, human playtest, or published game. Existing v4 and live source remain unchanged.

## The problem, before a solution

The old boat pilot makes a sound introductory contrast, but its play is two assemble–shape–seal–fit chains. The scene tells the player that the ferry is too wide, then supplies the second chain. Its end-directedness mostly resides in descriptions. Removing the philosophical wording would leave almost the same task. Reusing the pitch brush is technically continuity, but the previous **boat** does not become the next problem's material.

More recipes, four cause-labelled slots, or a “choose two explanations” panel would not fix this. Four causes are not four competing ingredients. Moreover, **every intentional crafting game already weakly enacts final causation**. A different game needs a sharper experiential contrast, not merely the word “purpose.”

## Sources checked and what they support

### Aristotle, *Physics* II.2–3, II.8–9

The four explanatory roles include persistent material, defining form, the source of change, and the end for which something is done. The same artifact can have several causes without competition. II.2 distinguishes a user's knowledge of a rudder's appropriate form from a maker's knowledge of wood and operations. II.9 reasons from an end to its required conditions: material properties alone do not explain why a wall is organized as shelter. II.8 also allows purposive attempts to fail. A later successful result is not what reaches backwards and physically makes the earlier artifact. [Primary text, Hardie/Gaye translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/physics.2.ii.html).

### Andrea Falcon, “Aristotle on Causality,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*

The article treats causes as irreducible explanatory roles, with metaphysical as well as explanatory significance. Formal cause is not simply an external drawing. Efficient cause may involve the artisan's craft, not just a tool striking matter. It distinguishes Aristotle's argument for natural teleology from a proof and distinguishes regular function from merely beneficial coincidence. Our fictional workshop therefore cannot demonstrate Aristotle's claims about nature. [Academic interpretation, substantive revision 2023](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-causality/).

### Aristotle, *Metaphysics* VII.7

Craft reasoning begins with an understood end/form and works back to a practicable intervention; making proceeds forward from that intervention. This is a particularly productive distinction for play: the player's reasoning can go **job → needed property → available operation**, while the executed combinations remain physical **A + B → C**. The game does not need a purpose-token that magically changes matter. [Primary text, W. D. Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.7.vii.html).

### Aristotle, *Parts of Animals* I.1

The text uses conditional necessity in both craft and natural explanation and attends to organized wholes, rather than just lists of material constituents. This suggests a possible later organism episode, but not permission to portray conscious planning inside a seed or to present historical teleology as a demonstrated modern biological mechanism. [Primary text, University of Chicago edition](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/aristotle/parts1.html).

The following proposals are **design inferences**, not examples asserted to be Aristotle's own. No boatbuilding, conservation, food, horticulture, or industrial instructions are intended.

## What a defensible pilot must make happen

1. A practical end precedes the choice/action it explains. It is in the scene, not an ingredient.
2. Actual object properties change, not only goals or names.
3. Something well made for one end must be inappropriate for another without becoming badly made in general.
4. Material and skilled action still matter. An intention does not perform the transformation.
5. Every later chapter materially depends on an earlier creation.
6. The player can do ordinary satisfying combinations. No compulsory vocabulary quiz.
7. We do not infer philosophical understanding, agency, or fun from mere completion.

## Sharply different approaches—and objections

| Approach | What the player actually does | What it could reveal | Strongest objection / falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Commission backwards** | A customer presents a load and route; choose among buildable forms, then make and deploy one. At least two designs must genuinely work for a job; later circumstances separate them. | The end constrains choice of matter/form instead of being an extra push. | Still ordinary engineering if the one highlighted recipe tells the answer. A broad/skiff noun match is not enough. With reusable stock and no cost, making everything may dominate. |
| **B. A good hole** | First seal a created bucket so it holds water. Later deliberately perforate that same bucket so water and fine grit pass through while gravel remains. | A property can be a defect or a virtue relative to an end. “Upgrade” is not one universal ordering. | Still a linear recipe if drilling is the only legal next action. It tests criterion reversal, **not compelling choice**. Simply changing “catch” to “wash” in text would fail; water-holding capability must actually disappear. |
| **C. Repair the right thing** | Encounter a failed job with several plausible faults: sound materials in the wrong arrangement; right arrangement badly joined; suitable artifact sent to the wrong job. Repair through concrete operations, not labels. | Reasons for failure need not all be deficient material or deficient physical workmanship. | Diagnosis becomes a disguised multiple-choice lesson unless evidence is perceptible and repairs have credible consequences. Current engine has no physics or test-result observation system; do not invent one quietly. |
| **D. Re-purpose without re-making** | Use an earlier created vessel as a weight, resonator, or shade; then intentionally alter it for that newly adopted use. | Current usefulness and original end of manufacture are not identical. Re-purposing is a new purposive action. | Mere use of a pot as a doorstop does **not** show it was made for holding a door. This can teach the wrong account of final cause unless the distinction is handled carefully. A label swap is insufficient. |
| **E. Ends without a planner** | Tend an organism whose parts develop in an organized relation to life activities, with historically attributed explanations available after play. | Aristotle's account is broader than human intention. | Highest conceptual risk: “feed seed to obtain tree” is just a growth chain; engineered deterministic outcomes would not establish natural teleology. Do not build this first. |

**Selection: B, only as a small falsification probe.** It puts a physical reversal into the smallest number of existing actions. A would be the stronger eventual choice-driven game, but we do not yet have a convincing low-complexity version. C requires an observation contract to be proposed. D is useful as a later challenge to an over-simple account. E needs a separate historical/natural-philosophy treatment.

## Executable probe: “A Good Hole”

Two scenes, six authored combinations, one unchanged fork runtime. No new UI, purpose slots, labels to classify, arbitrary quota, timer, or outcome token.

### 1. Keep the water in

Invitation: **“Rain is dripping through the roof. Make something that will catch it.”**

Goal: **“Catch the roof leak.”**

- Staves + hoops → bucket shell.
- Pitch + rag → sealing pad.
- Bucket shell + sealing pad → watertight bucket.
- Put the bucket beneath the leak.

### 2. Let the water through

Invitation: **“The roof is mended. The mason needs a washing sieve: fine grit and water must pass through, but the gravel must stay.”**

Goals: **“Make a washing sieve.” → “Wash out the grit.”**

- Previously made bucket + hand drill → pierced bucket.
- Pierced bucket + gritty gravel → loaded bucket.
- Loaded bucket + spring → washed gravel in pierced bucket.

The deliberately small count is not padded with a polishing step.

The physical lineage is visible in each label. The water-holding capability is present on the watertight bucket, then absent from the perforated versions. The drill and seal pad remain reusable tools. The bucket is not a newly conjured second vessel. Gravel is a non-food material, avoiding a pitch-contaminated cooking example.

The final transformation compresses washing. The runtime verifies recipe/capability rules, not hydrodynamics or actual cleanliness. A sealed bucket can of course wash gravel by other methods; **the scene specifically requests a through-flow washing sieve**. We do not claim holes are uniquely necessary for every imaginable washing task.

### How the four roles are present, without four inventory tokens

- **Material:** the same stave-and-hoop vessel persists through the lineage.
- **Form:** closed seams versus a perforated arrangement determine different affordances.
- **Source of change:** the fictional craftsperson, enacted by the player, uses a sealing pad or drill. Tools alone are not the whole explanation of skilled making.
- **End:** catching runoff versus separating fine grit from gravel explains which arrangement is wanted. It does not drill or seal anything.

This mapping is a design interpretation. Success would be a player spontaneously noticing “a good water bucket is the wrong sieve,” and grounding that judgment in the changed object, not reciting these labels.

## Existing-runtime limits; no invented fixes

The fork deliberately retains an input while unused branches need it. Thus authoring two different finite transformations of one bucket can leave multiple available versions until remaining uses are exhausted. That behavior is part of the current reusable grammar, not a defect this research is authorized to change. A mutually exclusive design experiment needs explicit design evaluation first.

Replacing a fitted slot also cannot quietly erase its earlier component. Reversible refitting or disassembly must have deliberately authored, visible results. The pilot therefore uses a single physical lineage and no slots. It **cannot establish meaningful design choice**. Do not promote it as the final replacement merely because tests pass.

## Decision after the probe

- If players see only “first seal, then drill” and not the reversal in what counts as good: reject or radically rethink B; do not add explanatory toasts.
- If they see the reversal but find it dull: keep the conceptual result, reject this game loop. Explore A or C with genuinely inspectable alternatives.
- If they enjoy making and can explain the counterfactual without prompts: investigate a small choice-bearing extension. This still does not establish learning of Aristotle's natural teleology or of all four causes.

No human observations are available at this point.
