**Verdict: accept both as narrow authored simplifications.** This check was completed before generating the scenario candidate by the separate reviewer `check_salad_authenticity`. ## Roasted roots + vinaigrette → existing roasted-root salad

The current motivation asks for dressed roast roots, and the existing output is generic rather than mustard-specific. The existing oil-and-cider-vinegar dressing is therefore a reasonable direct preparation. This is a design inference from the stated goal, not a requirement that every plausible pair work.

[Giada De Laurentiis’s recipe](https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/giada-de-laurentiis/roasted-root-vegetable-salad-recipe-2095657) supports the pairing, not this exact simplified dish. No quantities or complete recipe are copied. The game’s description of a warm salad assumes serving while the roots remain warm; the action does not create heat, and its caveat explicitly compresses serving temperature.

## Dry ground mustard + vinaigrette → existing mustard dressing

[Colman’s own foodservice guidance](https://colmansusa.com/foodservice) explicitly lists mustard powder for vinaigrettes. The producer guidance supports the ingredient use, not an exact dose, time, or order. The independent reviewer also checked [chef-authored dressing advice](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/features/chefs-favourite-easy-salad-dressings): Stuart Collins uses English mustard powder with acidic ingredients before the oils, without a standalone mustard-paste preparation. This corroborates that a mandatory separately discovered paste is not universally necessary.

Adding dry mustard to an *already-made* vinaigrette is the game’s sequencing abstraction. The new action says a little dry mustard is thoroughly whisked in, and its caveat retains quantity, hydration, mixing-order and resting limitations. It does not describe dry powder as prepared mustard, promise Dijon-equivalent flavor or claim a complete formula. The old paste-first route remains fully available.
