## 1. A sky worth recording — c. 700–500 BCE

A cloudy night has left a gap. Make an account another scribe can check, then mark when to watch the Moon again.

**Objectives:** Make a watching calendar; Separate report and interpretation.

**Claim scope:** Numeracy, written observation and divinatory interpretation coexist; this is not modern controlled science.

1. Clay tablet + Reed stylus → **Marked tablet**
2. Marked tablet + 12 + 18 measures → **Number account**
3. Number account + Grain, not jars → **Reckoning table**
4. Seen / clouded / missed + Date signs → **Dated sky record**
5. Dated sky record + Reckoning table → **Interval table**
6. Interval table + Earlier eclipse intervals → **Cycle table**
7. Cycle table + Dated sky record → **Watching calendar**
8. Dated sky record + Omen interpretation → **Record and interpretation**

**Sources:** [Francesca Rochberg, Science and Ancient Mesopotamia, Cambridge History of Science](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-history-of-science/science-and-ancient-mesopotamia/C48D6E70188ED938863F479B692D465B), [Eleanor Robson: Digital Corpus of Cuneiform Mathematical Texts](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/dccmt/)

## 2. What does a prediction prove? — c. 400–330 BCE

Two speakers predict the same shadow but give different causes. Show what follows from each claim—and what the prediction cannot decide.

**Objectives:** Frame the unresolved question.

**Claim scope:** The reconstructed dispute reuses earlier evidence; no claim is made that Aristotle read this fictional tablet.

1. Every dimming = cover + Dated sky record → **Stated premises**
2. Stated premises + Inference form → **Valid argument**
3. Valid argument + Dimming without cover → **Counterexample**
4. Same result, rival cause + Watching calendar → **Rival prediction**
5. Rival prediction + Valid argument → **Unsettled cause**
6. Dark ≠ covered + Record and interpretation → **Distinct claims**
7. Counterexample + Distinct claims → **Qualified claim**
8. Qualified claim + Unsettled cause → **Open question**

**Sources:** [Aristotle: Prior Analytics, translated primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/prior.html), [Mark Schiefsky, The Creation of Second-Order Knowledge in Ancient Greek Science](https://www.mprl-series.mpg.de/studies/1/12/index.html)

## 3. A diagram is not yet a proof — c. 300–150 BCE

A copied diagram has lost a line, and two manuscripts disagree. Restore a checkable proof without pretending the surviving copies agree.

**Objectives:** Complete the proof roll.

**Claim scope:** Greek deductive practice and Alexandrian textual comparison are reconstructed together; modern editorial notation is identified as such.

1. Line segment + Compass → **Two intersecting circles**
2. Two intersecting circles + Straightedge → **Triangle diagram**
3. Triangle diagram + Valid argument → **Geometrical proof**
4. Geometrical proof + Counterexample → **Proof conditions**
5. Copy A: missing reason + Copy B: wrong label → **Variant table**
6. Variant table + Triangle diagram → **Restored diagram**
7. Restored diagram + Editorial signs → **Marked reading**
8. Marked reading + Proof conditions → **Critical text**
9. Critical text + Variant table → **Proof roll**

**Sources:** [Francesca Schironi: Textual Scholarship in Alexandria and Beyond](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/schironi/wp-content/uploads/sites/259/2024/10/43_Textual-Scholarship-in-Alexandria-.pdf), [Euclid: Elements, David Joyce edition](https://mathcs.clarku.edu/~djoyce/elements/)

## 4. Three mathematical languages — c. 800–850 CE

A land problem reaches the workshop in different notations. Make a usable calculation and a geometric justification—not merely a fluent translation.

**Objectives:** Calculate with positional digits; Make a checked algebra method.

**Claim scope:** Greek works circulated through Syriac and Arabic; Indian arithmetic was another substantive input. Do not attribute every algorithm to a single institution or source.

1. Proof roll + Syriac witness → **Compared witness**
2. Compared witness + Arabic terminology → **Arabic proof**
3. Indian numeral treatise + Reckoning table → **Decimal procedure**
4. Unknown + 5 = 12 + Decimal procedure → **Written equation**
5. Written equation + Arabic proof → **Balanced operation**
6. Balanced operation + Unknown + 5 = 12 → **Checked solution**
7. x² + 6x diagram + Arabic proof → **Square construction**
8. Square construction + 3 × 3 missing corner → **Completed square**
9. Completed square + Checked solution → **Algebra method**

**Sources:** [Oxford History of Science Museum: mathematics in Islamic cultures](https://www.mhs.ox.ac.uk/scienceislam/mathematics.html), [Greek Sources in Arabic and Islamic Philosophy, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/arabic-islamic-greek/), [Maths in the Early Islamic World, BBC synopsis reproduced by participant Peter Pormann / University of Manchester](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/clippings/bbc-radio-4-in-our-time-maths-in-the-early-islamic-world/)

## 5. Where does the light come from? — c. 1020 CE

The bright doorway appears upside down inside the dark room. Make an image and a ray account that can survive moving the lamp.

**Objectives:** Form the courtyard image; Explain the image; Keep the remaining question open.

**Claim scope:** Ibn al-Haytham integrates mathematical reasoning with investigations of light and sight; this is not the invention of a universal scientific method.

1. Opaque board + Small aperture → **Aperture board**

4. Lamp + Arabic proof → **Ray diagram**
5. Ray diagram + Camera obscura → **Crossed rays**
6. Two lamp positions + Ray diagram → **Predicted spots**
7. Predicted spots + Lamp → **Comparison record**
8. Comparison record + Crossed rays → **Optical model**
9. Optical model + Open question → **Optical question**

**Sources:** [A. I. Sabra and Jan Hogendijk, The Optics of Ibn al-Haytham Books IV–V, Warburg Institute / University of Chicago Press](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/distributed/O/bo156866715.html), [Cambridge Peterhouse MS209: Alhacen De Aspectibus](https://medieval.lib.cam.ac.uk/catalogue/Peterhouse/MS-PETERHOUSE-00209)
