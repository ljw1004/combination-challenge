# Concrete opening recipe plan

Author-visible design evidence. Do not provide this to a fresh discovery tester.

## The next eclipse watch — c. 700–500 BCE

The king wants the eclipse report. The watchers need a date to return. A clouded night and an unattended night must not become “nothing happened.”

Goals: Mark the next eclipse watch; Prepare the king’s report

- **Blank tablet for a dated sky report** + **Reed stylus** → **Ruled tablet for a dated sky report**. The writing surface is ruled for entries.
- **Ruled tablet for a dated sky report** + **Month 13: eclipse seen; 14: clouded; 15: missed** → **Dated eclipse tablet**. The tablet carries the particular observation and the two gaps.
- **Lunar month counts: 1, 7, 13** + **Reed stylus** → **Month reckoning: 7−1=6; 13−7=6**. A numerical reference for the two intervals. The reference survives its astronomical use.
- **Dated eclipse tablet** + **Earlier eclipses: months 1 and 7** → **Seen eclipses: months 1, 7 and 13**. The present tablet is placed alongside the two earlier reports.
- **Seen eclipses: months 1, 7 and 13** + **Month reckoning: 7−1=6; 13−7=6** → **Eclipse watches recur six months apart**. The three dated reports support a six-month watching pattern in this schematic case.
- **Earlier eclipses: months 1 and 7** + **Month reckoning: 7−1=6; 13−7=6** → **Earlier pair: six months between eclipses**. The subtraction is first applied to the two old reports.
- **Earlier pair: six months between eclipses** + **Dated eclipse tablet** → **Eclipse watches recur six months apart**. The present month-13 eclipse adds another six-month interval to the older pair.
- **Eclipse watches recur six months apart** + **Future full Moons: months 16, 17, 18, 19** → **Watch the full Moon in month 19**. The next six-month opportunity is marked on the future calendar.
- **Dated eclipse tablet** + **For the king: an eclipse threatens his reign** → **King’s report: the eclipse and its omen**. The report brings the observed event and its proposed significance to the king; neither is silently substituted for the other.

## A dark Moon, two explanations — c. 400–330 BCE

One speaker says every darkening comes from a cover. Another points to a dying lamp. Meanwhile two accounts of the Moon predict the same next eclipse.

Goals: Reconstruct the covering argument; Break the “every darkening” claim; Compare the eclipse predictions

- **Every darkening is caused by a cover** + **Dated eclipse tablet** → **The Moon darkened; every darkening supposedly has a cover**. The observed particular and the speaker’s universal premise are now side by side.
- **The Moon darkened; every darkening supposedly has a cover** + **Every A is B; this is A; so this is B** → **If those premises hold, the Moon had a cover**. The inference is valid; its universal premise remains an assertive, challengeable claim.
- **Every darkening is caused by a cover** + **Every A is B; this is A; so this is B** → **Under that premise, any darkening implies a cover**. The rule is arranged before a particular recorded case is supplied.
- **Under that premise, any darkening implies a cover** + **Dated eclipse tablet** → **If those premises hold, the Moon had a cover**. The recorded lunar darkening instantiates the arranged rule; the conclusion has the same conditional standing.
- **Every darkening is caused by a cover** + **An uncovered lamp darkens as its oil runs out** → **One uncovered lamp breaks the “every darkening” claim**. The single case defeats this universal premise, not all accounts of eclipses.
- **Earth screens the light—or the Moon itself changes** + **Watch the full Moon in month 19** → **Both accounts predict a dark Moon in month 19**. The rival accounts make the same prediction at the calendar’s appointed watch.
- **Both accounts predict a dark Moon in month 19** + **Month 19: the Moon darkens; no cause is seen** → **One observed dark Moon fits both rival accounts**. This observation answers the shared prediction; the accounts are still distinct.

## Make three equal sides — c. 300–150 BCE

The triangle must have equal sides, not merely look convincing. Two copies disagree over its vertex, and a second sketch claims equality despite lengths 5, 5 and 6.

Goals: Expose the unequal sketch; Complete the triangle proof

- **Segment AB: construct three equal sides** + **Compass set to length AB** → **Circles centred at A and B, both with radius AB**. The two circles provide an intersection C in the Euclidean plane.
- **Circles centred at A and B, both with radius AB** + **Straightedge** → **Triangle ABC: join A and B to the circle intersection**. The sides have been drawn from the actual construction.
- **Triangle ABC: join A and B to the circle intersection** + **Every radius of a circle has the same length** → **AC=AB in one circle; BC=AB in the other**. The construction, not the sketch’s apparent proportions, warrants these equalities.
- **AC=AB in one circle; BC=AB in the other** + **Lengths equal to AB are equal to each other** → **AC=AB and BC=AB; equal to one length means equal**. The equalities and the equality premise are assembled.
- **AC=AB and BC=AB; equal to one length means equal** + **If those premises hold, the Moon had a cover** → **AC=AB=BC: the constructed triangle has three equal sides**. The mathematical conclusion follows from the specified premises.
- **“Every equal-looking triangle is equilateral”: sides 5, 5, 6** + **One uncovered lamp breaks the “every darkening” claim** → **The 5–5–6 triangle defeats “equal-looking means equilateral”**. A genuine contrary case overturns the sketch’s universal claim, not the circle construction.
- **Copy A: circles meet at C; join AC and BC** + **Copy B: circles meet at D; join AC and BC** → **One copy says C; the other leaves C undefined**. The exact disagreement remains visible.
- **One copy says C; the other leaves C undefined** + **Triangle ABC: join A and B to the circle intersection** → **C restored at the intersection; the D reading is recorded**. The construction supplies the missing referent without concealing the rival reading.
- **C restored at the intersection; the D reading is recorded** + **AC=AB=BC: the constructed triangle has three equal sides** → **Triangle proof: three equal sides, vertex C restored**. The completed roll contains the construction, its proof, and the particular repaired reading.
