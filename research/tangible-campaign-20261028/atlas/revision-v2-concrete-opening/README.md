# Atlas: concrete opening revision

**The failed opening was asking for the author’s nouns after the player had already understood the ideas.** This revision makes the particular record, claim, contrary case and diagram do the work, and lets completed arguments remain completed. It is a new research candidate, not a replacement or a claimed improvement in enjoyment.

## What changed

Only chapters 1–3 are redesigned. The 29-chapter structure and speculative future remain. There are still 258 recipe definitions, but legitimate alternatives make a full route **254–256 combinations**, not 258 compulsory operations. The first three chapters take **21–23 combinations**, down from 25 compulsory operations.

1. **The next eclipse watch — 7–8 combinations.** Put the dated observations on the tablet. Compare particular eclipse months, calculate their gaps, and mark month 19 for another watch. The other finished object is a report to the king containing the observed eclipse and its omen—not the backwards action “combine these to separate them.” Clouded and missed observations remain distinct.
2. **A dark Moon, two explanations — 5–6 combinations.** Reconstruct the covering argument; defeat its unrestricted premise with an uncovered dying lamp; compare two accounts against their shared prediction. These are three finished results. There is no compulsory `Distinct claims → Qualified claim → Open question` closure. A counterexample with no local partner is now correctly finished/gold, not a stranded intermediate.
3. **Make three equal sides — 9 combinations.** Construct the two circles and their triangle; derive the equalities; expose the supposedly equilateral 5–5–6 sketch; resolve the particular C/D copy error. The proof roll makes a definite mathematical claim. It does not end in a disclaimer recipe.

The alternatives are two small, meaningful evidence-order choices, not a collection of guesses made valid after the fact:

- Compare the current and earlier eclipse records before calculating their intervals, **or** calculate the interval in the older pair before adding the current record.
- Assemble the universal premise and observed case before arranging the inference, **or** arrange the universal rule before supplying the observed case.

Both alternatives use the same necessary evidence and produce the same result. Their intermediate labels say what has actually been done. Pairing arbitrary objects is still not an accepted operation.

### Scope and downstream contracts

The earlier-created inputs are mandatory on **every** legitimate route: the observation tablet and watching calendar in chapter 2; the created argument and counterexample in chapter 3. The proof is alternative-aware, not the old unique-producer shortcut. Every subsequent chapter still mandatorily uses at least two earlier-created outputs.

Removing `open-question` requires precisely three downstream operand changes: `make-optical-question`, `make-situated-image`, and `make-open-atlas` now use the already-created case in which one observed eclipse fits rival accounts. These are pedagogical reuses, not claimed historical transmissions. **230 later recipes, every later item, and all later stage narratives/objectives are unchanged.** `downstream-remaps.json` records the exception; tests compare the rest structurally against the original.

This is not a claim that the later campaign has escaped its own document-making abstractions. In particular those three handoffs are still broad analogies and need observation during later play. They have been kept narrow so this trial tests the opening rather than an unbounded rewrite.

## Diagnosis from the preserved negative trial

The source-blind trial `fresh-opening-v1/sessions/fresh_atlas_a` finished chapter 1 and stopped in chapter 2. Its useful successes should not be discarded: seen versus clouded/missed, numerical intervals and recurrence, valid inference versus true premises, and rival accounts matching one prediction all registered.

Its failures were actionable:

- Writing observations on the marked tablet failed because the tablet secretly belonged to a grain-accounting chain. The new tablet is genuinely for the sky report.
- Comparing earlier eclipse reports with the dated record failed despite being a sound operation. That comparison is now substantive, not merely an alias.
- A calendar object had to be guessed as a particular report, rather than marked using actual future dates. The future calendar strip now has explicit month numbers.
- The omen goal described separation but required joining. The report now does what its title says.
- `Counterexample`, `Unsettled cause` and `Distinct claims` had no visible subject binding their required joins. The new contrary case is terminal; the two remaining finished results retain their particular claims and observations.

The trial’s empty counterexample highlight was not itself a faulty engine relation: its required partner had not been created. It was a bad content dependency. No runtime hint, toast, acceptance rule or highlight rule was changed here.

The old 29-chapter `scenario.json`, old trial scenario, and both stopped session files remain byte-identical. `preserved-evidence-manifest.json` protects them. Do not erase this negative result after a better trial.

## Historical grounding

The Babylonian dates are invented consecutive lunar-month counts in a deliberately small recurrence exercise. They are not an authentic prediction algorithm or a claim that every sixth full Moon is eclipsed. The scene preserves observational and divinatory activity together; it is not modern science defeating religion. The original cuneiform and Rochberg sources remain attached.

Aristotle’s discussion explicitly distinguishes knowing an eclipse from knowing its cause and considers Earth screening, rotation and extinction. This is a better primary-text anchor than anonymous “rival mechanism” language. The particular speakers, lamp case and matched forecasts remain authored teaching cases, not quotations or historical observations. [Posterior Analytics II.2 and II.8](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/posterior.2.ii.html)

The geometric construction and equality argument follow the core of Euclid I.1. The 5–5–6 sketch and inconsistent C/D copies are invented. Merely renaming C to D consistently would not be a mathematical error; the faulty copy instead names D and then refers to an undefined C. [Elements I.1](https://mathcs.clarku.edu/~djoyce/elements/bookI/propI1.html)

The Babylon-to-Greek reuse is a gameplay comparison, not a claim that Aristotle read this fictional tablet. Likewise a created contrary-case argument is being reapplied to the sketch; no documentary lineage is invented.
