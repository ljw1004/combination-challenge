## Added evidence

- `two-principles-full`: [online primary-text transcription](https://gnosis.org/library/cathar-two-principles.htm), based on Wakefield/Evans with editorial changes. **I.9–10** actually gives the watcher analogy and the reply that a creator supplies the walker’s being and conditions. **III.1–2** discusses terms such as “all”; **V.1–6** displays disagreement among dualist groups and argues for an evil maker. This corrects the original candidate’s overly narrow CUHK attribution. It does not license the archive introduction’s claim of a doctrinal/popular “winner”.
- `summa-evil`: [Summa I q.49](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1049.htm), aa.1–3. The good-tree objection and explicit Augustine citations make the return to earlier manuscripts substantive. There is no demonstrated direct Aquinas–Book of Two Principles exchange.
- `aristotle`: [Perseus/Scaife, Nicomachean Ethics I.1](https://scaife.perseus.org/library/urn%3Acts%3AgreekLit%3Atlg0086.tlg010.perseus-eng2/), 1094a, Rackham translation. A short line on the good and ends supports a philosophical inquiry card. This is a comparison, not a claim that these six quoted words caused Aquinas’s theology.
- `genesis`, `romans`, `matthew-tree`: KJV passages verified at [Genesis 1:31](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%201%3A31&version=KJV), [Romans 7:19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%207%3A19&version=KJV), [Matthew 7:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%207%3A18&version=KJV). The host identifies KJV as public domain. The English translation is a reader-facing rendering, not a claim that Augustine or medieval authors read KJV.
- `ritual-catalogue`: [CNRS IRHT Initiale, PA 36](https://initiale.irht.cnrs.fr/codex/2627), gives a mid-thirteenth-century/southeastern-France possibility and notes Cathar/Waldensian attribution questions.
- `ritual-study`: [Harris/Peytavie, 2006](https://www.persee.fr/doc/heres_0758-3737_2006_num_44_1_2088), addresses the Occitan New Testament’s attribution. The article’s existence/title were checked; the full study was **not** read. Do not cite it as establishing a specific conclusion here.

## Difficult evidence, not concealed

Harris’s 2005 foreword suggests early-fourteenth-century northern Italy for the manuscript, whereas the IRHT catalogue reports another possible date/place. Manuscript copying, the New Testament’s translation, the appended ritual and underlying traditions need not share one origin. The game therefore says “a separate medieval witness,” not a precise uncontested date.

Direct fetches of Gnosis and the Rialto body page returned 502/timeout errors. The university index and critical notes were readable; indexed primary-text excerpts were readable for the operative passages (including the watcher and rival-dualist exchange). The old Gnosis mirror supplied Harris’s foreword/numbered ritual text via the search index. No access restriction was bypassed; no full modern edition was downloaded or reproduced. Full critical-edition review remains desirable before calling the research scholarly-complete.

## Quote audit across the whole candidate

The direct modern Luther quotation is eight distinct words, 24 words counting the same two snippets repeated in label, description and reading; no other Luther passages are copied. Bible labels/readings use KJV, not the earlier modern Knox draft. Aquinas uses the older English Dominican translation; Aristotle uses Rackham’s older translation. No modern editorial introduction is quoted. Quotation marks distinguish actual excerpts from evocative **unquoted paraphrase** labels. Terminal punctuation and initial capitalization are adapted for card display without changing words.

This is a conservative excerpt/provenance record, not a legal opinion about every jurisdiction. Reusing the longer modern editions would require a new licensing check; this candidate does not do that.
