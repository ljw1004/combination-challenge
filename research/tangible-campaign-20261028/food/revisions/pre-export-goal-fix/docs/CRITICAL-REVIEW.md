# Design review after the first executable graph

## What earns the length

The goal route has 185 combinations; the catalogue has 197. The other 12 are optional discoveries, not a fabricated total. Twenty chapters each ask for useful food for a changing human situation: caravan breakfast, harvest lunch, rain shelter, crowded market, stale bread, a birthday, early train, heat and provisions for travel. This is still a fairly long cooking journey; count alone does not prove that it earns a player's time.

The strongest connections are material and causal:

- Hearth flour/dough become risen bread and later pasta, pastry, sauce and sponge.
- Apple juice becomes alcoholic cider, then vinegar, then a lunch dressing. The oxygen-bearing vat changes the work; it is not a generic “fermentation” stamp.
- Butter/flour on the cool bench produce crumbs; water binds shortcrust. At the saucepan the same familiar ingredients cook into roux. This is the retained pilot's strongest experiment.
- Broth, bread, roast roots and cultured spread cross between soup, salad, filled pasta and pie.
- Pouring custard and starch-thickened pastry cream have different dependencies and later uses. Choux, sponge, jam, whipped cream and chocolate reconnect instead of resetting every chapter's pantry.
- Yolks first, whites later; cooked fruit and syrup later become frozen or commercially preserved food. No claim that an oven or a wrapper alone preserves everything.
- The final cake needs earlier-created sweet egg foam, sifted flour and vanilla creme. Loading the injector fits the original object’s visible slot; it stays the same tool while dispensing filling.

## Changes made during review, not counted as fresh-player findings

1. The first version had unrelated three-dish commissions. Motivation now follows the same caravan and its changing needs; no second abstract research storyline was added.
2. Two soup-stage prep quotas were poor local goals. Roasted roots now enter the actual bean filling; breadcrumbs are made at the noodle bench that immediately uses them.
3. A vague quick-batter chain became a recognisable wet-mixture → enriched mixture → flour batter sequence. There are still ordinary preparation steps, not magical “prepared food” tokens.
4. The loaded injector was initially a renamed replacement. It is now a slot installation with a capability check and visible retained contents.
5. Generic fruit juice was too imprecise for “cider.” The starting fruit is apples; citrus, apricots and pitted dates are separate justified supplies.
6. The macaron branch now reuses the made almond-and-sugar blend; marzipan and cocoa manufacture have actual supporting sources instead of an overbroad historical cookbook citation.

## Remaining risks—not passed by logical tests

- **Dessert concentration.** The middle and later game has many filled or topped desserts. Real differences in structure exist, but an uncoached player may still experience repetitive assembly. Remove a weak dessert episode rather than adding a novelty flag or requiring unrelated research papers.
- **Catalogue-size pantry.** Current scene arrivals are bounded, not the full 254-item catalogue. Nevertheless the largest logical scene has 19 visible item tokens, and several arrivals have 10–11. Those are not browser/touch readability results. The dairy breakfast is the first clutter stress case.
- **Repeated ovens.** There are 31 stationary locations over 20 chapters, generally one or two per chapter and never more than three. Most are ordinary heat sources. They do not require construction, but repeated oven drops may still become routine. Preserve the meaningful cool-bench/hot-pan contrast; test whether other cooking destinations earn their footprint.
- **Retained portions.** Shared flour, butter, dough and prepared batches remain visible by explicit authored choice, not automatic graph exhaustion. This enables experimentation but is not a realistic mass/stock economy. Some retained preparations may look like duplicate food.
- **Narrative is not chronology.** The moving caravan is a framing fiction for historical techniques. It must not be advertised as an accurate chronological history or a documented chain from a particular hearth culture to Hostess.
- **Minor formula compression.** The game names real products but is not a recipe. Full sponge, marzipan, quick-bread and commercial-cake formulations are more constrained than the graph. Never market the logical test as food-science or safety validation.
- **Potential familiarity plateau.** Repeating “mix, fill, bake, top” for 185 combinations could be tiring despite truthful food. A fresh pilot is required before treating the long candidate as a replacement for the short starter.

## Decision

Keep the candidate isolated and source-testable. Retain the archived four-scene starter and saucepan pilot. Do not deploy or replace them on the strength of solvability. Focus the next independent playtest on pastry→sauce and precision→packaged cake; use the shorter orchard→soup segment if the question is whether old food actually feels valuable. The player must not receive this graph, the 185-count target, or the authored route.

## Explicit goal-presentation tradeoff

Three source-stage export preparations—fruit syrup, vanilla custard and sifted flour—must be earned before later chapters can use them. They currently appear as intermediate goals that reveal local dish goals. This represents a preparation/service order, **not** a literal ingredient dependency of those local dishes. Independent final preparation goals would be a cleaner alternative if the reveal feels artificial; removing the requirement and conjuring the missing ingredient later would not. This issue was sent to the parent for review rather than concealed by the passing objective validator.

The final adversarial route has a slightly larger scene than the ordinary witnesses: up to 12 arrival items and 20 live items after deliberately retrieving extra milk/sugar. Normal canonical/exhaustive routes remain at 11/19. A browser test, not these counts, must judge the practical footprint.
