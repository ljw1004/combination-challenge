# A larger continuous Elements world: measured graph and contextual benches

> **Historical policy candidate (15:00 UTC update):** This freeze predates the user’s new instruction that finished objects remain tangible where made, with removal only for upgrade/incorporation and explicit travel to physical areas. Its hiding/bench behavior does **not** satisfy that new instruction. The already-running source-blind trial must complete unchanged and be interpreted only as evidence about the prior policy. No favorable count or trial result here validates the new persistent-object design.

## Findings first

This isolated experiment adds a substantial reusable craft web, not another tiny fourth-chapter epilogue: **152 discoverable outputs, 179 binary recipes, 156 total concepts, 19 invitations**. Materials, tools, wheels, paper, dye, books, optics, boats, buildings and gardens recur across branches. The source is runnable through a captured text trial interface; it is not integrated into the live browser UI.

Correctly honoring obvious shortcuts materially reduces duration. Final frozen V2 has an **exact minimum of 44 successful creations from four elements**, or **24 more from the existing authored 22-creation checkpoint**. A deliberate set of richer alternative endings has a minimal authored route of **70 creations from four elements**. These are solver results legally replayed through the model—not observed human decisions. The proposed 80–130-successful-creation hypothesis is **not met**, and no step was inserted just to cross 80. The optional ontology is larger than any required story route. Comfortable crafting counts as meaningful progress; nothing here demands that each action be a strategic dilemma.

The old global retention rule fails badly at this size: **83 active items** in 300 shuffled full-world orders and **89** on an adversarial order. Merely dropping terminal goal contributors or hiding things without an immediately known partner does not solve it: adversarial peaks remain **74** and **71**, respectively.

A separate, non-gating contextual view is promising. Across three goal routes and six adversarial schedules (1,059 states), **a natural-place bench peaks at 33 things; selecting an item and bringing every known partner peaks at 37**. The current-invitation bench peaks at 20. Every currently legal recipe has a verified owning-place witness with both operands visible. This is logical discoverability and count evidence, not visual/mobile usability or enjoyment evidence.

## What the larger graph actually adds

- **Materials and moving things:** Ore + Kiln → Metal; Wood + Stone → Axe; Axe + Wood → Planks; sharpening and woodworking lead to Saw, Hammer, Nails, Crate, Wheel, Cart, Pulley and Crane. The direct Axe route to Planks stays; the longer Saw route is optional, not compulsory.
- **Using moving water:** Wheel + Water or Wood + Water → Waterwheel; Waterwheel + Stone or House → Mill; Waterwheel + Saw → Sawmill. Mill + Grain can produce Flour, but Grain + Stone still works. Machinery does not invent stock or throughput administration.
- **Civil uses:** Planks + Rope → Footbridge; Arch + Planks → Bridge; Dock + either existing boat → Ferry. Courtyard, Tower, Bench, Bookshelf and Display case become reusable assemblies. The walking-crossing invitation asks for walking, not arbitrary replacement of a working raft.
- **Stories and signals:** Charcoal + Water → Ink; Ink + Paper → Letter; a letter or greeting card with either boat yields Mailboat. Paper + Wood → Puppet; Puppet + Curtains → Puppet theatre; it can be used with a Courtyard. Books, pictures, shelves and public rooms have parallel uses.
- **Observation and records:** Telescope + Paper → Star chart; Plant + Book → Herbarium; these can support Star atlas or Botanical display. Rain gauge and Windsock are simpler observation alternatives. The inherited simple telescope is not retroactively made harder.
- **Cultivation and household making:** Flowers branch into Seeds and Dye; Seeds branch into Oil, Sprouts and a Seedbed. Oil/lye/soap, colored cloth/paper, cold frames, trellises and climbing gardens form connected branches. These are storybook abstractions, not chemical instructions or a gardening maintenance simulation.
- **Welcoming a visitor:** Covered landing can be made with Dock + Awning, Tent or Houseboat. There is no mandatory oilcloth→awning ladder. Lighthouse has House/Lantern, Tower/Lantern and House/Oil lamp alternatives at the shore. A boat discovery naturally reveals the shore; no shore resource is manufactured.

Of the 152 outputs, 105 are reused in another recipe and 47 are terminal. Some ends are deliberately satisfying final objects. Others feed later branches. Terminal count is not a verdict on quality; the issue is whether the overall web invites continued use rather than attaching many badges to unrelated recipes.

## Invitation structure and remaining depth problems

The added sequence asks for a walkable crossing, useful water power, moving bulky things, helping people find the place, a place worth returning to, carrying work or hospitality, observation, cultivation, household comfort, a dry landing, a shared story place and a record. Most accept recognizably different outputs; older accomplishments count wherever they serve the stated purpose.

This creates more varied purposes than three repeated choose-two lists. It does **not** prove nineteen satisfying chunks of play: several invitations can already be satisfied on arrival. The minimal path exploits this legitimately. Some requests remain close to naming a product, especially the dry landing; its three material routes do not become three different dramatic purposes just because they are alternatives. The observation invitation says “record,” while a Windsock or Rain gauge supports observing rather than recording; this wording mismatch is retained as a known issue in the frozen trial, not silently edited mid-trial.

The richer authored ending choices are explicitly: Bridge, Sawmill, Cargo ferry, Signal tower, Gallery, Mailboat, Star chart, Climbing garden, Soap, Covered landing, Children's library and Botanical display. This is a chosen tour through alternatives, not a mandatory checklist imposed by the game and not a claim that an ordinary player will choose it. See `results/authored-richer-choices.json` and the replay in `results/stress.json`.

## Bench grammar: why this is not deleting options

Six freely selectable work areas—hearth, garden, workshop, shore, study, courtyard—organize intentions. There is also a current-invitation view. Changing place changes only a projection, never recipes, discoveries, capabilities or legality. No ingredient is consumed or manually packed/unpacked.

Each unfinished local output contributes its **known prerequisite frontier**, across every alternate recipe, stopping at already-known subassemblies. This automatically brings over useful familiar materials. Selecting an on-bench item additionally brings **all currently known legal partners**, regardless of work area. Unknown result names are not revealed. Locations such as cellar/shore still supply the existing binary target context; placing a target there is separate from switching a work-area view.

This differs from true retirement: an unfinished tool absent from one bench remains canonical and appears automatically when a relevant place, goal or partner is selected. Truly finished terminal objects can leave the working surface while their achievement remains scored. The transcript always announces a new discovery before it leaves the projection.

The current-invitation view alone is insufficient: up to **66 legal recipes have both ingredients hidden there** in the tested states. Every one is available on its owning place's bench, and every one-hidden pair is surfaced by selecting its visible operand. This is a verifiable retrieval rule, not proof that a player will know where to look. Free place navigation must be discoverable, and must not become another inventory-management chore. The source-blind trial is needed specifically to challenge that tradeoff.

No current browser controller has been changed. Spatial continuity, drag anchors, scrolling, birth animations and gesture cancellation remain untested with these projections. The CLI does not establish those properties.

## Tests and exact route method

- `node --test tests/logical.test.ts`: 3 tests pass, including 300 seeded full-discovery legal runs, retention safety, 358 directed recipe-alternative checks, and preserved-input equality. All 152 outputs reachable; final canonical count 156 and active zero.
- `python tests/solve.py`: binary MILP minimized discovered outputs with recipe/input constraints and capability goal support. The combined dependency graph is checked acyclic, preventing self-supported recipe cycles. Shore recipes also require known Raft or Sailboat. HiGHS returned exact optimality with zero gap for all three cases (44, 24, 70). Every selected solution is subsequently replayed by legal model actions.
- `node tests/stress.ts`: shortest and authored routes, greedy peak maximization, intermediates-first, delayed textiles, delayed food, delayed metal and delayed goal terminals. All stress runs complete. Full chronological recipes and projected sizes are retained.
- `node tests/benches.ts`: checks every legal pair has a place witness, and one-hidden partners surface upon selection, across those 1,059 states. Full per-state counts and hidden-pair counts retained. These observed maxima are not universal mathematical bounds.
- Isolated TypeScript noEmit for the model and bench module passed.
- Harness smoke exercises checkpoint initialization, next invitation, place switching, automatic partner surfacing, Houseboat creation and Undo. Capture smoke verifies chronological byte capture and terminal-state save. No native/browser/controller integration was run.

## Preserved failures and revisions

`freeze/candidate-v1.ts` and `results/v1/` retain the first larger graph: 177 recipes, 53/32/79 route counts, randomized peak 73 and adversarial global peak 85. Independent review found commonsense capability omissions—Salad not counted as food, Oil lamp not light, Tent not shelter, Dyed cloth not fabric, Crate not carrier. V2 corrects these and similar omissions; Fruit legitimately counts as ready-to-eat, Torch as light, Bucket as a vessel, and an existing Library as a place for shared stories. Those corrections shorten the graph rather than being hidden to protect duration numbers. Covered landing gains Tent and Houseboat alternatives. Raw V1 evidence remains valid for V1, not for V2.

Branch-author rejections remain in `docs/elements-civil-reviewed.json` and `docs/authored-pool.json`: Bowl was not a credible construction ingredient for Table; Table→Banquet was a weak relabeling; Garden seat and Bell cart mostly relocated objects; Art display duplicated Gallery; Land yacht lacked a current purpose. Banner→Flag was excluded as gratuitous extra staging; the Banner instead has a later Signal tower use.

Still questionable: Earth + Stone → Ore, Planks + Stone → Wheel, Pulley + Wood → Crane, Glass + Water → Lens, and oil/lye chemistry compress substantial real processes. That is consistent with the established storybook genre, but player plausibility is not established by source-aware agreement. The graph is not marketed as a chemistry or engineering lesson.

## Pending source-blind evidence and publication boundary

A fresh player must receive only `docs/PLAYER-INSTRUCTIONS.txt`, a fresh session name and the wrapper—not minima, output totals, desired conclusions or these reports. Full wrapper records go to `trials/SESSION/chronological-capture.jsonl` with raw stdout/stderr bytes and exit codes; `finish` or `stop` saves terminal state. The player may stop voluntarily. Their qualitative report and raw input history must be preserved even if unfavorable.

This research currently establishes a larger coherent executable hypothesis and a plausible bounded workspace rule, **not ~200 enjoyable decisions**. The next empirical question is whether goals, optional exploration and clear progress make players want to continue without managing a catalog. Distinguish meaningful attempted combinations, successful discoveries, navigation, hints and repeated failures. Do not count interface actions or failed guesses toward the target merely to improve a number.
