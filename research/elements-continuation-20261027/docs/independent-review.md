# Independent source-aware continuation review

Reviewed frozen `freeze/candidate-v2.ts` and `results/shortest-routes.json`, 2026-10-27. This is source-aware design criticism, not a fresh-player trial. I did not independently rerun the parent's 1,000-order test or the route enumerator.

## Verdict

**Useful small experiment; not yet demonstrated release-ready continuation or a solution to the 200-decision goal.** The model preserves honest shortcuts, reusable inputs and alternative outcomes. Its reported eight-creation minimum from the authored checkpoint is appropriately modest. Do not lengthen that route merely to improve a number. However, most additions are one-shot endpoints, and the three invitations repeat the same choose-two structure.

The evidence supports testing a next slice, not asserting enjoyment, mobile usability at 36 active items, or 200 meaningful decisions. Native/controller regression and fresh-player continuation evidence are still needed before a quality claim.

## Strongest findings

1. **21 of 26 new outputs are terminal.** Only Net, Paper, Book, Lens and Telescope are ingredients in another recipe. Terminal outcomes can be satisfying, but this is primarily a wider set of endings, not yet a much deeper reusable ontology. The cleanup mechanism makes such endpoints disappear, potentially reducing the sense that the settlement changed.
2. **Shared food still lacks continuation.** Existing Feast and Fruit tart have no downstream use. River picnic proceeds through Bread and Picnic basket; the promise that the existing boat, house and food become a beginning is only partly fulfilled. Consider direct parallel picnic routes from the existing shared foods and either boat. Do not require re-making another food lineage.
3. **House + Rope → Swing is the least convincing required-route recipe.** It resembles a workaround for the already occupied Wood + Rope → Raft pair. Orchard + Rope → Swing would offer a recognizable tree context and reuse Orchard, though it must remain an optional branch, not a mandatory intermediate inserted to add length.
4. **Glass + Water → Lens is underspecified.** A water-filled glass can magnify, but the result reads as a manufactured optical lens. Label or feedback would need to make the intended water lens legible. Lens + Wood → Telescope is acceptable storybook compression once a lens exists; it should not be marketed as realistic optics.
5. **The invitations are choices, but their rhythm is repetitive.** Three consecutive choose-two capability checklists are better than exact recipes, yet risk feeling like renamed recipe shopping lists. The `making` category has only Pottery workshop. Images/books and learning can both be met by Paper → Book → Library: that legitimate shortcut is not two independent creative decisions.
6. **The active-item result is encouraging, not sufficient.** A reported 36-item shuffled peak is within the target budget, but 1,000 orders do not prove a global worst-case bound. The exact checkpoint starts at 24 active versus 18 in the original model, so future recipes increase clutter before the new goals arrive. Real mobile inspection and earlier-chapter regression matter.

## What works

- Boats have parallel practical and recreational applications; Sailboat is not mandatory over Raft.
- Houseboat, Tent, Paper, Drawing, Flute, Drum, Book and Hourglass are recognizable enough for this established storybook language.
- Growing accepts existing Greenhouse. Outing accepts existing Picnic basket. No hidden demand for newly created objects overrides prior accomplishments.
- No supply depletion, collect-all gate, invented geography chain or mandatory cellar work was added.
- The shortest-route report separates successful creations from human decisions and does not pretend the continuation alone provides substantial playtime.
- Different categories can be completed through different branches; do not collapse these back to one canonical route.

## Invitation improvements

Keep intent prominent and category labels secondary. For example: “What would make you come back here?” can include growing, making or play; “What would you like to share?” can include music, pictures or a place to learn. These still need understandable completion feedback, but should not imply that an exact prescribed output is the purpose.

At least one later invitation should change the decision pattern rather than simply requesting another two badges. This should come from a changed setting or purpose, not from deleting familiar recipes or withholding supplies. A separate shoreline, winter or hilltop scenario is a better next test than extending the same board indefinitely.

## Raw review rationale and rejected fixes

- Rejected manufacturing a marsh or river by combining a boat with water: travel does not create geography.
- Rejected adding stages between Net and fishing craft just to lengthen the route.
- Rejected treating 792 nondominated dependency routes as 792 player decisions or evidence of enjoyment.
- Rejected forcing both music and library: the Book → Library shortcut should remain valid.
- Rejected automatically promoting all terminal outputs into intermediate assembly steps. That would turn decoration into compulsory padding.
- Tent from Wood + Cloth is simpler and more legible than House + Cloth → Tent; the frozen candidate uses the stronger form.
- Paper from Fiber + Water is stronger than the earlier proposed Wood + Water and is a useful correction.
- The existing shared-food omission is more important to repair than adding another entirely new craft branch.

## Suggested next evidence

Freeze any revision separately, preserve v2 and its raw counts, then test whether players voluntarily continue after satisfying one of the new invitations. Record what they expect from House/Rope, Glass/Water and the already-made Feast/Fruit tart; do not coach the intended answers. Include old completed saves and new-game paths. Report successful creations, failed attempts, movement/curation and goal advances separately.
