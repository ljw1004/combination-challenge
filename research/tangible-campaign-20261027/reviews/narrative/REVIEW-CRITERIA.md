# Narrative and local-challenge review

This review tests whether content supports the proven interaction grammar. It does not establish human enjoyment, historical accuracy, or a scientifically realistic simulator.

## Release gates

1. A player can state the immediate purpose in one sentence, without decoding a metaphor or knowing unrevealed recipe names.
2. Every required ingredient is already present, discoverable from present things, or introduced through a named, plausible cache. Supplies are not magically tailored to the next recipe.
3. A place has a durable identity. Travel does not move old locations. If returning is necessary, the narrative says why and the engine lets the player bring the needed actual tools without a cross-board drag chore.
4. Gold marks the current completed outcome, including required placement, never a mere precursor. All listed objectives are required; no arbitrary choose-N quotas.
5. Every created result is a recognizable named thing, practice or idea. It is not an inventory bundle disguised as a noun or an extra adjective added merely to increase depth.
6. Each stage changes the situation in a way the player can understand: safer shelter, a reachable destination, a new witness, a workable practice, a newly usable instrument, or a meaningful social capability.
7. Later stages use earlier achievements or answer earlier questions. Repeated structure is allowed when it provides comfortable progress; repeated objectives without new context or consequences are padding.
8. The campaign has a visible end state. A total of 200 recipes is not evidence of 200 worthwhile decisions. Count the shortest credible completion route, an exploratory route, and optional discoveries separately.

## Tone

One brief in-character invitation plus explicit objective text is normally enough. Do not narrate every combination. Avoid constantly manufacturing emergencies, withholding ordinary facts, or making the player fail to understand a poetic instruction. The player should be curious about the next situation, not worried they missed a hidden checklist.

## Authenticity boundary

Mars is grounded adventure fiction, not survival advice. Ideas recipes are conceptual relationships, not historical claims that two isolated causes produced an invention. Food should distinguish stylized food logic from real safe cooking instructions. Theology must name its perspective and avoid presenting contested doctrinal interpretations as universal facts. Source verification is a separate review.

## Evidence labels

- Verified in source/trace: a specific contract, transition, reachable objective or data count checked directly.
- Narrative assessment: a reasoned judgment about coherence, tone, recognizability or likely repetition.
- Human result: only actual user playtest feedback, never an automated solver's enthusiasm.
