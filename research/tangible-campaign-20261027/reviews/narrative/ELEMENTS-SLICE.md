# Elements: Crossing → Mill → Bakery

Source read around 19:19 UTC; the author is still revising the full campaign. This is a narrative/data review, not a blind player trace.

## Why this slice is worth testing

The passage from an individual shelter to helping neighbors gives the next constructions a social purpose. A bridge and cart, a mill, and bread are recognizable results. Planks, rope, cloth, grain and tools return in understandable roles. The cellar is introduced at the bakery rather than requiring an accidental trip to an old stage.

Comfortable sequential making is appropriate here. Axing wood into planks, shaping a wheel, assembling mill gearing, filling a flour sack and baking bread can provide satisfying progress without each click representing a strategic dilemma.

## Changes before interpreting a playtest

- A mill does not mechanically create the already-discovered flour in this unique-discovery model. The miller's invitation should say that the player has already learned to grind a handful and is now building equipment for the community. Do not imply that the next loaf is impossible without it, or invent a new flour adjective merely to force that dependency.
- Similarly, the handcart can be a meaningful thing made for neighbors, but automatic chapter transport is not evidence that the cart's capacity was simulated.
- Wheel + wood produces a named Axle in the current draft. A wheel set or wheeled axle may better name the recognizable assembly if both components remain conceptually present. This is a terminology question, not a claim of a game blocker.
- Keep the bridge and mill at their original areas. Later map-making must use observations recorded there, not drag the structures themselves downstream.
- The repeated generic travel sentence should become a short specific arrival: the mill bend, then the bakehouse. One sentence is enough; do not narrate every recipe.

## Desired human evidence

Can a player understand the local request, notice each new result, enjoy familiar materials gaining a larger purpose, and leave the stage without curating a large inventory? Observe confusion or boredom directly. Completion counts alone cannot answer those questions.
