# A needle, a ring, and a lamp

## Decision

A familiar missing phenomenon earns this chapter: electrical current affects a compass; changing magnetic linkage can induce a current; a generator needs energy input. This is not ten vocabulary modifiers to reach a numerical quota. The chapter makes the zero reading and the stationary ring do explanatory work.

**10 recipes / 10 indispensable combinations / 18 new kinds / 1 exact imported kind / no locations / one explicit goal.** The minimum is ten because every unique output lies on the sole goal's prerequisite graph; each single-recipe omission fails the goal. This says nothing about whether ten is the best human-paced length.

The opening compass and bar magnet are familiar objects. The prepared coil and ring exhibits supply observations rather than invite electrical construction. Above/below compass directions rule out a naive straight pull; approach/rest/withdrawal distinguish change from presence; the ring prevents the mistaken generalization that mechanical motion is always essential. The hand-generator recording gives the curator a meaningful public outcome. Field lines are representations, not literal threads. The needle readings are qualitative, not calibrated current measurements.

The insertion is thematic, not strictly chronological. It connects physical electrical effects with the subsequent information/transmission chapter without pretending Shannon follows inevitably from Faraday. The curator is fictional; no historical person speaks invented lines. The museum apparatus and modern comparison diagrams have different evidentiary roles.

## Sources and claim boundaries

All seven source pages were opened through web research on 2026-10-27. URLs and per-source notes are embedded in `sources`.

- Science Museum Group's **Ørsted compass needle, 1828** supplies surviving material provenance, not an accidental-discovery anecdote.
- IOP **Oersted's experiment**, teaching notes, supports the opposite needle orientations above/below the wire. Its electrical setup instructions are deliberately not reproduced.
- Royal Institution **Faraday ring-coil apparatus** dates and describes the 1831 object and brief secondary current. It alone does not document every timing comparison in the teaching reconstruction.
- OpenStax **Induced Emf and Magnetic Flux**, figures 23.3–23.4, supports connection/disconnection transients, the unchanged-current null, magnet approach/withdrawal reversal, and the stationary null.
- OpenStax **Magnetic Fields and Lines** supports compass direction as field-line direction. The chapter does not infer field strength from these qualitative maps.
- IOP **Electromagnetism** supports field representation and the comparison of mechanical motion with a changing field in a stationary apparatus.
- IOP **Generators** supports changing magnetic linkage and the mechanical energy pathway. The lamp recording is an explicitly authored educational example, not a claim about the original 1831 ring.

The acquired comparisons are condensed teaching abstractions. They are not reconstructed quotations, original notebooks, experimental measurements made in this session, or proof of an exclusive inventor. The module does not cover Maxwell's equations, competing nineteenth-century theories in depth, the full international research network, electrical infrastructure, or the actual development chronology of generators. It is a small evidence case, not a complete history of electromagnetism.
