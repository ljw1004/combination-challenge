# Tangible campaign evaluation protocol

## Human evidence and hypothesis

On 27 October 2026 the owner reported playing Mars, Ideas and Elements to completion, enjoying all three and especially Mars's small unfolding stages. This establishes positive feedback for those short human-played versions, not for the new longer campaigns. Listening room was judged contrived; arbitrary choose-two objectives did not add interesting choice.

Hypothesis: a longer sequence of small, motivated local projects can sustain curiosity and comfortable progress when discoveries remain tangible, useful pieces travel with the player, goals are explicit, and results appear beside their ingredients. Historical or technical richness may improve Ideas only when combinations feel grounded rather than like a vocabulary exercise.

## Freeze before fresh play

Preserve scenario JSON, engine, player CLI, player instructions, source hashes and the exact starting state. Mark authored replay and solver results separately. A fresh player may read only the player-facing contract and use the CLI; do not give source, recipe outputs, optimal routes, success quotas or desired findings. This is instruction-level blinding, not operating-system isolation.

The player sees current area, tangible things and their optional inspect descriptions, explicit current objectives, in-character motivation, current known places and travel possibilities. Selection reveals compatible partners, not undiscovered results. Old areas are revisitable through explicit travel carrying actual tools, not a hidden inventory search. State mutations must go through ordinary combine/place/move/advance/visit actions.

## Evidence capture

Capture every command, stdout/stderr, exit status, resulting event, timestamp and state hash. Preserve unsuccessful actions, feedback, stops and shortcuts. Record distinct successful output combinations and slot installations separately; also count attempted combinations, placements, moves, navigation, hints and stage advances. Do not call all clicks combinations. A large ontology or a forced all-recipes sweep does not establish campaign duration.

Ask for sparse open reports: what the player was trying to accomplish, any confusing or arbitrary relation, something satisfying, and whether they would freely continue. Do not require stopping only at the end or prescribe a number of actions. Reported enjoyment from an agent is a diagnostic hypothesis, not human-fun proof.

## Independent reviews

A narrative review checks continuity, economical motivation, durable place, broad plausible caches, local problem variety and later payoff. An authenticity review checks named real things, historical/scientific attribution and fictional abstractions. A mechanical review checks reachable objectives, alternative paths, genuinely supported enhancements, canonical uniqueness, optional reuse, retirement safety and stage carry. None can substitute for the others.

After a trial, an independent reviewer replays captured actions exactly, checks hashes and counts, and challenges claims of fun, choice and length. Revise the smallest relevant cause, freeze a new version and run another fresh trial. Keep earlier failures unchanged.

## Length target and honesty

The current target is 200–300 combinations per full Mars, Ideas and Elements game. Report recipe catalogue size, reachable distinct outputs, authored successful route length and fresh-play route length separately. Preserve valid shorter routes. If the actual goals can be completed in 40 combinations, do not call a 240-recipe catalogue a full-length game. Do not pad the route with repeated naming upgrades, compulsory side collections, arbitrary choose-N quotas or busywork. Comfortable obvious progress can be worthwhile; not every action must be a strategic dilemma.

Small new-world prototypes deliberately have shorter scopes. Their purpose is for the owner to try and discard themes before any large expansion. No small prototype is represented as a completed full-length game.
