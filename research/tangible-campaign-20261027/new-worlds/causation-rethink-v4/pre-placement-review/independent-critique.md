# Independent critique: Why This Boat? rethink v4

Scope: source-aware inspection of `causation.json`, including the revised location-based rescue, on 2026-10-27. This is **not source-blind playtesting**, runtime verification, or independent verification of the cited Aristotle passages. No claim about actual player comprehension follows from this review.

## Strengths

- The job precedes measurement, measurement determines form, construction realizes the form, and success requires using the boat rather than merely owning it. This is materially better than collecting causal labels.
- Keeping the kind of timber and tools constant while changing the job supplies an intelligible contrast: material and competent workmanship alone do not explain why this particular shape was selected.
- Fictional artifact framing, explicit builder agency, and the disclaimer about natural planning avoid obvious historical overclaims. The narrow artifact illustration is defensible on its own terms; it does not claim to reproduce Aristotle's whole natural teleology.

## Original representational issue: fixed in the inspected revision

The earlier candidate acquired a stranded fisher as an inventory item before rescue, then asked the player to bring that person onto a boat at the reed mouth. That contradicted the narrative location of the person beyond the passage.

The current JSON has no fisher item or fisher cache. The person remains in the scene's motivation and location description. `skiff + @reed-mouth -> fisher-safe` describes an outward and return journey. This fixes the specific inventory contradiction at the authored-data level. The end still precedes design, and the trip realizes it; the change introduces no apparent new philosophical overclaim. Whether the UI makes this compressed journey legible remains untested.

## Remaining limitations

1. **Purpose mostly directs a prescribed crafting chain.** Instructions specify what to measure and build; there is no substantive design choice or required diagnosis of mismatch. A player may succeed by matching nouns. The likely lesson is “different tasks require different boat shapes,” a useful starting point but not demonstrated understanding of final causation.
2. **The decisive counterfactual is narrated rather than experienced.** The ferry stays at the landing and the second scene states it cannot fit. An unsupported wrong-boat combination would not itself explain physical unsuitability. This is an authored affordance contrast, not simulated evidence of grounding or insufficient capacity.
3. **Purpose changes form more visibly than making.** Both boats use the same assemble–shape–seal–fit sequence. End-directed construction is explained in prose more strongly than enacted through different player decisions. Repetition may teach the recipe more strongly than the explanation.
4. **“Same making” needs a qualified reading.** Same materials, tools, and general procedure are not literally identical skilled activity: different forms require differently directed work. The text about a different form rather than a different kind of physical push is useful contrast but compresses this distinction. Successful eventual use must not be mistaken for a later event retroactively producing the boat; the preceding intention is what directs the fictional builder.
5. **Interaction conventions may distract.** Ferry use needs placement and then cart combination; rescue instead combines a finished boat with a location and narrates the whole journey. The descriptions explain both, but their consistency and discoverability need testing. Automatic rescue on bringing the skiff to the location could still feel like parking rather than use if feedback is missed.

## Source-blind playtest questions

Ask after play without first teaching the causal vocabulary:

1. Why did you make the second boat narrow? What would still be wrong with using the first boat even if it were perfectly built?
2. Suppose the fisher were waiting across the open creek instead of beyond the reeds. Would you need the second boat? Why?
3. What did the goal change about your actions, and what stayed the same? What, if anything, was this game trying to explain beyond boatbuilding?

Treat accurate answers as evidence to investigate, not proof that players have learned Aristotle's broader theory. Watch whether answers refer to decisions and outcomes or merely repeat instructional phrases.
