# Creation

## Intent

Play a creator beginning with Earth, Air, Fire and Water. The scale is stars, oceans, continents, living worlds and minds. The pleasure is making something astonishing and discovering what it does next. The dramatic question is **Can you make a world that has a future of its own?**

Creation is an original elemental myth informed by natural processes, not a retelling of a religious text or a literal account of cosmology. Its four elements are powers: matter, motion, energy and change. They are the only primitive supplies. A combination can compress immense distances, many generations or a whole natural process. Its reading must distinguish that imaginative act from any factual claim it teaches. Life's origin and consciousness must not be presented as solved recipes.

The audience is a curious older child or adult. Offer useful ideas about stellar recycling, water and rock cycles, ecosystems, disturbance, adaptation, shared knowledge and independence. Keep the causal contribution of both ingredients visible. No construction chores, resource accounting or settlement progression. Human activity matters at the end as evidence of an independent world; it does not become a technology tree.

## Voice and arc

Address the creator directly, with concrete, spare language and moments of wonder. Let the creations surprise the player. Avoid a reverential narrator applauding every action, a punitive deity, doctrinal claims, and a foreordained march toward humans. Extinction and suffering matter; restoration cannot undo a lost lineage. Cooperation and resilience should be enacted through combinations, not announced as morals.

Move from light and stellar matter to a restless planet and ocean, then life that changes its own conditions, ecosystems with competing needs, catastrophe and renewal, and finally creatures that remember, question and create. The ending fulfills rather than cancels the player's creative power: a world carries that power onward.

Aim for about 200 substantial combinations. Follow a few recurring threads across chapters: light, water, stone, life, memory. Chapters should usually bring 4–8 symbols into focus, with at most two visible goal chains. Preserve optional discoveries when they supply real wonder or a useful contrast. Wonders accompany discoveries that need interpretation or change the story; simple intermediate actions need not interrupt play.

Treat the icons as representations of phenomena, populations and capabilities. Reusing an ancestral population does not recreate a particular dead individual. Major changes still have real consequences: the great browsing lineage ends, its traces remain, and later communities are different. Establish small mammal ancestors before the impact; keep insects, birds and mammals on distinct branches. Introduce inherited variation with early reproduction and reuse it in later divergence.

Use meaningful fixed contexts at major changes of scale: a star, a nursery, a planet, a vent, a living coast, a forest, an inhabited world, a community and a sea cave. These are places to work upon, not invented keys. They prevent life, catastrophe and art from all being completed in the opening. Earlier discoveries within an established era are welcome when their dependencies make sense. Keep later appearances of an established fixed object explicit and preserve it until its actual transformation. The cave is hollowed by waves long before its later visitors make pictures and music there; preserve its geography and dry upper walls across those appearances.

The final acts are concrete: a community tests a prediction against the shore, paints an impossible beast, changes an inherited song, and lives beside a forest whose seeds spread beyond the creator's view. The ending needs no abstract certificate of perfection or independence.

## Visual intent

Develop artwork only after the story and combinations survive repeated logical reviews. Use circular symbolic icons throughout; they are windows onto phenomena, not literal objects on a workbench. Choose and pilot a coherent visual language suited to cosmic and ecological scale. Backgrounds should show the world's progression while leaving calm working space for the symbols. The same planet, moon and principal coastline must remain recognizable through later states. No buildings or settlements as the dominant image.

The [authoring guide](../../docs/story-authoring.md), [evaluation guide](../../docs/fun-and-evaluation.md) and [icon guide](../../docs/icon-guide.md) govern development. This single active brief governs the manuscript and any subsequent study iterations.

### Adopted visual language: the painted celestial atlas

Use luminous opaque gouache with selective fine ink contours: lapis-blue grounds, jade and turquoise living forms, ochre minerals, restrained coral energy and warm ivory light. Subjects have strong silhouettes and a few identifying structures; fine paint texture supplies pleasure at larger sizes without carrying essential distinctions. Narrow ivory-gold rims unify the circular windows. Avoid glossy game coins, generic flat glyphs, photorealism, modern laboratory props and literal religious personifications.

Every inventory image is a symbolic illustrated token, including populations, places and mental capacities. Backgrounds show a world at the current stage; dragging a galaxy or a song is a creative gesture, not a physical object installation. Fixed contexts use the same visual language at a larger size. Standard exports are 256 square pixels (64px at the 390px reference viewport); fixed-context exports are 384 pixels (96px). Allow room above fixed targets for their ordinary name badge as well as room for a 64px ingredient on the target. Fixed context tokens sit at 50%, 20%, above ordinary inventory, so both their images and name badges remain visible on short phone browsers. Their position is symbolic rather than a registration with the painted landscape. Do not add a permanent second legend or labels inside pictures.

Related states must differ in structure: dividing cells have a constricted waist, partnered cells contain distinct smaller organisms; a first fern forest, fallen trunks and open pioneer woodland are different communities, not recolours. Celestial pictures use different mass/arrangement, and biological wings retain insect, bird and mammal anatomy. Songs use a human voice and a painted sound ribbon: inheritance adds another generation, and a changed song branches only at its ending. These abstract conventions depend partly on the game's names and readings. Depict people in warm varied earth tones with simplified faces; do not make one real-world culture the destination of creation.

The recurring coast has a hooked ochre headland, one arched wave-cut cave, teal water and a pale dry shelf and upper wall inside. The paired-crater pale Moon and non-Earth world geography remain recognizable. Preserve those landmarks through forest, loss and recovery. Great browsers are long-necked sauropod-like animals; their tracks pair broad oval hind prints with smaller D-shaped foreprints. Their lineage never returns alive after the impact. The cave's later art shows extant animals, imagined hybrids and invented skies rather than human memories of extinct giants.

Paint dramatic landmarks mainly in the upper part of portrait backgrounds and leave a calm, medium-light working field below. Calm space must be part of the artwork, not a CSS tint. Use 780×1688 runtime backgrounds, preserving painted proportions when extracting atlases. Images can compress scale poetically; anatomy, lineage, dry painting surfaces and the story's before/after states remain coherent.
