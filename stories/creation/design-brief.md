# Creation

## Intent

Play a creator beginning with Earth, Air, Fire and Water. The scale is stars, oceans, continents, living worlds and minds. The pleasure is making something astonishing and discovering what it does next. The dramatic question is **Can you make a world that has a future of its own?**

Creation is an original elemental myth informed by natural processes, not a retelling of a religious text or a literal account of cosmology. Earth is stone, Air is wind, Fire is flame and Water is water: their pictures and ordinary descriptions must support the combinations. They are the four elemental sources; technical item identities may distinguish their presentations. The elements may already exist when play begins. The opening is one creation scene with all four elements present: boundless Waters of the deep is a receiving landscape, while Earth, Air and Fire are movable. Air opens a Void in the waters. Within that opening, Earth gathers into a hot rocky world and Fire into a Sun. In the deep, Earth and Fire scatter into wandering bodies and stars; a great wandering body striking the world makes its Moon. The Sun remains movable, and shining it upon the world makes Light. Subsequent water cools the world, raises a steam cloud and returns as rain. This is an invented setting, not an interpretation or adaptation of a particular scripture. Invent a mythology that follows from those tangible elements; do not disguise an atomic or stellar history by relabelling them as matter, motion, energy and change. A combination can compress immense distances, many generations or a whole natural process. Its reading must distinguish that imaginative act from any factual claim it teaches. Life's origin and consciousness must not be presented as solved recipes.

The audience is a curious older child or adult. After the invented cosmogony reaches a concrete planet, preserve recognizable relationships and approximate historical realism through the ages of the Earth. That transition lets familiar natural history carry the later combinations; the myth need not keep inventing its own rules. Offer useful ideas about water and rock cycles, ecosystems, disturbance, adaptation, shared knowledge and independence. Keep the causal contribution of both ingredients visible. No construction chores, resource accounting or settlement progression. Human activity matters at the end as evidence of an independent world; it does not become a technology tree.

## Voice and arc

Address the creator in plain, clear English. The game is figuring out combinations, not deciphering enigmatic goals or poetic hints. State what the player is trying to make or change; explain what each result is and how it arose. Let scale, physical consequences, pictures and discoveries supply wonder. Remove gnomic pronouncements, vague promises, personified natural processes and praise from the narrator. Do not hide necessary information to manufacture mystery. Avoid a punitive deity, doctrinal claims and a foreordained march toward humans. Extinction and suffering matter; restoration cannot undo a lost lineage. Cooperation and resilience should be enacted through combinations and explained directly.

Move from unruly elements to a sky full of lights, a restless world and ocean, then life that changes its own conditions, ecosystems with competing needs, catastrophe and renewal, and finally creatures that remember, question and create. The ending fulfills rather than cancels the player's creative power: a world carries that power onward.

Establish the world's axial tilt during the initial creation, so the later seasons have an earlier cause. The glancing impact tips this world's rotation; its new Moon then steadies the world. Represent this as a changed landscape world in chapter one, keeping that introductory scene grounded in things. The Moon moderates changes in an existing tilt rather than creating it or fixing its angle forever. Introduce the separate Steady axial tilt symbol when the later seasons chapter uses it with the Sun. As with the movable world and water, this later technical primitive represents something already established in the fiction. Use the concrete names Stars and Asteroids throughout. In the water cycle, let sediment and the river's transport build the delta, with slowing flow and deposition stated in the result. Strong wind with rainfall makes Storm; Storm, rather than ordinary Air, brings down wood from the First forest. Volcano with Ocean makes a Hydrothermal vent. Mountain range with Ocean makes Islands: the sea floods lower ground, separating the remaining higher land. Islands then combine with Seasonal journeys to make Island life. Use rocky islands separated by visible water channels, without an erupting volcano. Do not introduce a loose Earth-and-Fire recipe that distracts from the opening landscape operations.

Fire entering the waters of the deep makes stars. This is an accepted mythic gesture whose grandeur and material character should be expressed through the artwork. The primordial waters can suggest a dense cosmic nebula without claiming literal astrophysics. Keep human prose spare once the images communicate the setting. Supply text-only evaluations with separate visual context when needed, identifying provisional imagery as an authored premise; do not make the human story carry extra explanation solely for readers who cannot see its pictures.

Aim for about 200 substantial combinations. Long, connected ancestry is a defining pleasure: an early creation can matter again far into the story. Preserve that scale and cumulative wonder. Comparisons with other stories describe structure; shorter chains are not a quality target. Remove an action only when its own contribution or payoff is weak. Follow a few recurring threads across chapters: light, water, stone, life, memory. Chapters should usually bring 4–8 symbols into focus, with at most two visible goal chains. The cave chapter deliberately has three independent strands—painting, song and teaching—visible from arrival, so no strand implies a prerequisite for another. Preserve optional discoveries when they supply real wonder or a useful contrast. Wonders accompany discoveries that need interpretation or change the story; simple intermediate actions need not interrupt play.

Treat the icons as representations of phenomena, populations and capabilities. Reusing an ancestral population does not recreate a particular dead individual. Major changes still have real consequences: the great browsing lineage ends, its traces remain, and later communities are different. Establish small mammal ancestors before the impact; keep insects, birds and mammals on distinct branches. Introduce inherited variation with early reproduction and reuse it in later divergence.

Let the player race ahead by combining. Chapter order is a narrative invitation, not an era lock. Use actual causal dependencies where an event needs something to exist, and brief `[If skipped]` passages where skipping a chapter would otherwise lose an essential transition. Large circles signify pivotal creations and remain movable. Fix an item only when its landscape or apparatus supplies a meaningful receiving context. Such places may naturally gate an operation; they must first be created from the four elements through actual recipes. Island life can receive Variation where separated birds live; Advancing ice can receive already varied survivors where cold changes which descendants persist. Keep these places present after their result appears. The sea cave is hollowed by waves long before its later visitors make pictures and music there; preserve its geography and dry upper walls across those appearances. Its later receiving surface belongs to the actual painted landscape.

Move postglacial forest recovery before the learning, language and music chapters. Name its result **New forests**: these provide food, shelter and living space for the later communities. Spreading trees are no longer the final achievement. The final acts are a community testing a prediction against the shore, painting a remembered animal and adding stars, learning a song from one another, and bringing their shared singing together with wonder. **Shared song + Wonder → Song of creation** is the culmination, as an ordinary pair of movable icons. Keep each musical step distinct: recalling a sound, joining voices, and expressing wonder at the world. Learning from others happens within Shared song. Keep **Shared discovery + Story → Teaching** as a goal of the preceding chapter, alongside painting and making a song: passing on understanding is a substantial achievement in its own right. Do not make it another prerequisite token for the final song, and remove New question and Remembered song as separate preparation steps.

The last playable chapter is **A day of rest**. Painting, making a song and teaching belong to the preceding chapter, where the work is done. The already-created **Social group** becomes the gathered tribe in the final landscape. Drag Song onto the people to make Shared song; then combine the Shared song and Wonder icons anywhere on the board. The second action has no hotspot requirement. These are two simple gestures, not a difficult final puzzle. The gathering holds the creation of Shared song until the last chapter, so the culmination still happens there. The completed paintings remain part of the tribe's surroundings, while the final artwork evokes the scale of Song of creation.

The song embodies the singers' understanding of creation and their place within it. That understanding is limited and can develop as they learn more; the player was present at the beginning and knows what happened. Leave that difference implicit in the ending. Do not describe the singers as inventing an account, add a separate creation-story ingredient, unpack their beliefs or certify an answer. Their version of a song of creation is enough. Keep the final reading short and let the final artwork carry the scale.

Use Memory directly once in the song branch: Echo plus Memory makes a repeatable song. Shared song also inherits Memory through Social group's learned warnings. The investigation and paintings have their own remembered experience; they remain worthwhile earlier creations without every one becoming a literal ingredient in the final song. The ending needs no abstract certificate of perfection or independence.

## Visual intent

The story and combinations are accepted for the artwork pass. Develop and review backgrounds, changing hotspots and the concept treatment of Variation against the current manuscript. Use circular symbolic icons throughout; they are windows onto phenomena, not literal objects on a workbench. Choose and pilot a coherent visual language suited to cosmic and ecological scale. Backgrounds should show the world's progression while leaving calm working space for the symbols. The same planet, moon and principal coastline must remain recognizable through later states. No buildings or settlements as the dominant image.

The [authoring guide](../../docs/story-authoring.md), [evaluation guide](../../docs/fun-and-evaluation.md) and [icon guide](../../docs/icon-guide.md) govern development. This single active brief governs the manuscript and any subsequent study iterations.

### Adopted visual language: Ravilious-inspired painted landscapes

Use the supplied Ravilious landscape and four-scene study as the main background references: sculpted landforms, clear planes, directional pencil and dry-brush marks, translucent watercolour and a restrained range of related colours. Preserve atmospheric depth and distinct silhouettes. Volcanoes, glaciation and primordial waters need elemental force, using scale, displacement, weather and light. Calm scenes may be spacious without becoming domestic or uniformly pastoral. Keep the existing circular gouache icons legible against the new backgrounds; they can carry richer colour than the scenery.

The opening uses the supplied Pillars of Creation study as its direct visual reference. The waters of the deep are a dense painted nebula, with fiery, energetic illuminated dust and less prominent paper grain than the later quiet landscapes. The outer nebula must stay pixel-identical when the cavity opens; register a local changing region over one shared outer painting. Its large Waters receiver remains available around the smaller, nested Void receiver after creation. Leave a bottom area for the movable starting elements. Air opens an irregular cavity through it: dust sweeps aside, its centre fades toward near-black, and compressed rims and trailing filaments reveal depth, and the void remains part of the same volume rather than a flat black circle pasted over the painting. A small magnified rocky sphere forms at the centre of the cavity, mostly dark iron-brown rock with a few glowing cracks. Leave ample open void around it. The asteroid impact that creates the movable Moon changes this same Rocky world hotspot to a hotter, visibly distorted and oblate molten mass shifted from the normal world centre by about half the normal sphere’s diameter to the right and a quarter diameter down. Keep it almost in its normal position and clearly visible, rather than buried behind the lower-right cloud tendril. Use the chapter-local `icon after creating Moon:` directive; this is an appearance change, not another item or recipe. Applying Moon then replaces it with the centered spherical Steadied world, still molten for the cooling chapter that follows. All three states share one registered artwork anchor. Keep the nebula unchanged; any slight overlap with foreground dust should remain natural without obscuring the impact silhouette. The molten surface has fine irregular incandescent mottling across dark rock, without external glow, sweeping bands, continents or developed relief. No opening world state has an axis line, loop, orbital ring or painted Moon. The later Steady axial tilt icon may keep its axis and small top loop on the evolved ocean-and-continent world. Keep the movable Moon and Light near the world receiver’s periphery, clear of all three visible world states. The exaggerated displacement and return are mythic visual shorthand, not a claim that lunar gravity instantly rounds or relocates Earth. The Moon belongs exclusively to its movable icon and must not appear in any background, including the opening, observation scene and finale. Chapter two still starts with molten rock; adding Water creates Stone crust. Its landscape shows a near-level magma ocean with thin transient cooling skins, irregular incandescent seams and localized spattering, informed by lava-lake photographs. Heat and radiant light supply the energy; avoid rolling lava hills or pre-existing mature relief. Scale is deliberately mythic and can combine vastly different magnifications. Keep the setting and its material continuity beautiful and readable across all states; no literal scientific scale or explanatory diagram is required.

Chapter five takes place completely underwater in a shallow primordial mineral pool, with a softly refracted rippling surface overhead and quiet submerged ground for icons. There are no visible organisms before the player creates life. This setting illustrates the story's invented transition, not an established account of life's origin.

The opening nebula and final Song of creation are full-bleed compositions with no reserved blank playspace. Every other background needs an uncluttered area for movable icons, formed naturally by ground, water, shadow or atmosphere. The final scene is abstract, anchored closely in the supplied Pluscarden reference: interwoven vertical fields, braided bands, fragmented colour and ambiguous forms in a Ravilious-inspired watercolour medium. Let colour, rhythm and structure evoke the song. Do not recap achievements in literal vignettes or clearly depict people, landscapes, animals or celestial objects. No copied lettering or doctrinal figures are needed.

Every inventory image is a symbolic illustrated token, including populations, places and mental capacities. Backgrounds show a world at the current stage; dragging a Moon or a song is a creative gesture, not a physical object installation. Pivotal creations use the same visual language at a larger size without becoming fixed. Standard exports are 256 square pixels (64px at the 390px reference viewport); larger exports are 384 pixels (96px). Size follows the represented subject and a small number of major milestones. Keep the Sun, the whole-world states (including impact winter and recovery), and whole-forest communities at 96px across their changes. First forest, renewing and flowering forest, fallen forest, pioneer woodland and new forests share that scale; a seed or young grove remains a smaller part or stage. The other large milestone tokens are the first living cell, photosynthesis, multicellular life, language and the final Song of creation. Regional habitats, flowers, animal lineages and ordinary processes use 64px: a hydrothermal vent, a living coast or tree dwellers do not acquire extra emphasis merely because an earlier draft fixed them in place. Recipe `[transform]` alone does not transfer size: emitted Light is not another Sun, and rivers or weathered fragments are not another whole world. The primordial waters, opened void and molten ground are actual landscape receivers during creation. All three remain in that scene. Ordinary Water and a large movable Rocky world are separate primitive identities introduced in chapter two: the player understands them as the same water and world, now represented at a different scale. Both world identities display the name Rocky world and retain recognizable geography. No conversion operation or engine unfixing feature is needed. Cooling the movable world transforms it into Stone crust, shown at the standard 64px size by the user’s preference. Rocky world remains large at 96px. Center the molten ball geometrically inside its circular frame. The cave's fixed receiving surface is registered to its painted dry inner wall, with room for two ingredients and their ordinary name badges on short phone browsers. Dark cave, Cave, Painted cave and Painted cave with stars are successive states of the entire cave, with one landscape hotspot replacing its predecessor at the same centre. None is a small fixed picture icon. Pigment plus Memory at the lit cave is the story's only two-ingredient contextual operation. Adding Stars to the animal painting is a single-ingredient hotspot action; preserve the animal as the stars appear. Remove the imagined beast and the separate sky-painting branch. The completed wall remains inside the same cave during the day of rest. Do not add a permanent second legend or labels inside pictures.

Variation first appears as an ordinary-size movable circular icon, with a large compact horizontal pencil-outline DNA double helix against cream. This retains the inventory's circular language while distinguishing a concept from painted physical subjects. Later environmental hotspots use a smaller unframed horizontal pencil helix in the same graphite language, borrowing Cookbook's distinction between outlined hotspots and painted objects. The habitat itself remains painted scenery and receives the item; the emblem identifies it as an appearance of Variation. Use the actual marine shallows, woodland margin, separated coastal habitat and vegetated foothills, all made from earlier creations. The helix is a modern visual shorthand for inherited differences, not a claim that the chemistry of life's origin is solved. Preserve the distinction between inherited differences and the environmental conditions that affect their survival; do not imply that an environment deliberately produces a needed mutation. Keep the first concept movable and later natural receivers fixed through existing story semantics.

Recover existing paintings where possible. Use exact, centered circular frames and opaque interiors; remove neighboring atlas fragments and uneven hand-painted perimeter artifacts. New paintings use full-bleed square sources with their essential subjects inside the eventual circle, followed by deterministic cropping and framing. The production record states any accepted source-aspect correction or edge coverage.

Related states must differ in structure: dividing cells have a constricted waist, partnered cells contain distinct smaller organisms; a first fern forest, fallen trunks and open pioneer woodland are different communities, not recolours. Celestial pictures use different mass/arrangement, and biological wings retain insect, bird and mammal anatomy. Songs use a human voice and a painted sound ribbon; shared singing joins several voices. The final Song of creation icon uses a circular crop of the abstract ending painting, carrying its woven colour and rhythm into the inventory rather than depicting a singer or literal earlier creations. These abstract conventions depend partly on the game's names and readings. Depict people in warm varied earth tones with simplified faces; do not make one real-world culture the destination of creation.

The final Song of creation painting should stay close to Pluscarden’s interwoven colour and vertical structure while remaining an original abstract composition. Its forms should not resolve into a collection of recognizable things. The preceding social scene supplies the human singers; the final image expresses their song without illustrating its subjects.

The recurring coast has a hooked ochre headland, an arched wave-cut cave, teal water and a pale dry shelf and upper wall inside. Preserve those landscape landmarks through forest, loss and recovery. The paired-crater pale Moon remains recognizable within its icon alone. Great browsers are long-necked sauropod-like animals; their tracks pair broad oval hind prints with smaller D-shaped foreprints. Their lineage never returns alive after the impact. The cave's later art shows an extant animal and stars, not a hybrid beast or human memories of extinct giants.

Chapter 24 takes place deep inside an enclosed cave chamber, with no outdoor view, visible entrance or daylight. Before Fire, the chamber is very dark with faintly discernible wall and floor shapes and difficult to make out. Firelight reveals the dry painting wall; its warm pool of light and surrounding darkness give the pictures their setting. Use the rock's contours as part of the drawing surface. The animal painting itself draws on Paleolithic parietal art: economical, confident mineral-pigment contours, broad partial ochre fields, tapering legs and expressive antlers, with bare stone showing through. Keep the surrounding Ravilious-style cave, but give the marks on its wall this distinct material language. Avoid smooth modern wildlife illustration, uniform outlines, modeled fur or decorative celestial glyphs. The later stars are irregular pigment dots, retaining the same animal and wall. Their celestial meaning belongs to this invented scene, not an asserted interpretation of archaeological signs. The earlier cave-making history remains. The earlier wave-made Sea cave is displayed as Dark cave here. Bring Fire into Dark cave to replace it with Cave, then replace that with Painted cave and finally Painted cave with stars. Every state can receive a Call to make Echo, so painting and song remain independent. Exactly one cave hotspot is present at a time; do not retain a separate acoustic cave alongside the changing painting. On the day of rest, follow the supplied quiet gathering study: five small, simply painted early Homo sapiens adults, including two women, around a campfire near the vertical centre of the full scene, farther from a less prominent cave in the background. Use varied ages, brown skin tones and individual weathered faces informed by museum reconstructions, without contemporary beauty-model styling or indiscriminately borrowing Neanderthal anatomy. A broad dark starry sky and faint Milky Way above them recall the opening dust clouds and make the people feel small within the world. Preserve deep blue night and localized welcoming firelight. With only two combinations, this scene does not need a large blank playing field; modest low-contrast space for the few tokens is sufficient. The cave mouth is dark and unevenly lit, with only a faint torch deeper inside. Match its rock character to the deep chamber, without moving its paintings to the entrance merely to make them visible. The cave holds their shared culture even when its interior pictures are out of sight. Social group is the actual gathering in this landscape, not a fixed circular token. Review the registered scene states at their actual playing size.

In ordinary playable landscapes, concentrate dramatic landmarks in the upper part of portrait backgrounds and leave a calm working field naturally connected to the setting below. The opening nebula and final Song of creation deliberately use the whole page. Calm space, where needed, must be part of the artwork, not a CSS tint. Use 780×1688 runtime backgrounds, preserving painted proportions when extracting atlases. Images can compress scale poetically; anatomy, lineage, dry painting surfaces and the story's before/after states remain coherent.

Generate evolving hotspot states from a complete scene and use that scene as the edit reference for each successive state. Register the changed artwork to one shared canvas and camera. Transparent receiver-only PNGs suit unchanged scenery; they cannot reveal fire or paintings that do not exist in the background. For the cave, compose chapter 24 deep inside, add fire, then an animal painting, then stars; chapter 25 looks toward the established coastal cave from outside, with the social circle in front. Preserve the cave's material continuity through the change of view.
