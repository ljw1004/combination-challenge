The [live feedback revision](../../projects/mars-study/production/live-feedback/README.md) tracks the current changes; the earlier [emergency revision](../../projects/mars-study/production/v12/emergency-revision/story-plan.md) records the adopted sequence; its [water/garden consultation](../../projects/mars-study/production/v12/emergency-revision/science-water-garden.md) and [metal/fuel consultation](../../projects/mars-study/production/v12/emergency-revision/science-metal-fuel.md) retain supporting research and evidence limits. ## Survival priorities and useful work

Use the user's “rule of 4s” as a narrative ordering mnemonic: four seconds for wits, four minutes for air, four hours for shelter, four days for water, four weeks for food and four months for company. These are story-priority labels, not literal deadlines or a simulated survival clock. Begin with immediate stabilization and breathable shelter, secure power and water, and establish the slow-growing farm before optional investigation or return-fuel construction. Mara's delayed messages supply human contact throughout the solo mission.

The first two chapters require no fabricated metal or workshop tools. A found rock stabilizes the lander; a small hand-applied patch repairs the starter habitat. Recover the primitive steel pin in chapter 3 while still scavenging outside for water, after the cable work, then save it for the later electromagnet. The original delivery carried compact science, habitat and repair equipment; it did not anticipate manufacturing return fuel. Every subsequent part must earn its payload, donor or local fabrication history. Let difficult conditions and useful discoveries drive events; the astronaut responds sensibly, without contrived mistakes or irrational conflict.

Use ceramics where they do the necessary job without expensive metal: the later berth deck, modest bed walls, growing granules, furnace containment and a few useful supports. They still cost work. Do not add brick walls, paving, furniture or platforms merely to make a place look finished. A supply icon represents usable stock; it is not a claim of inexhaustible cargo. Group routine making, maintenance and repeated batches into complete operations instead of adding inventory bookkeeping.

## Story, voice and sequence

The cover shows the intact, inflated but unpowered delivered starter habitat alone, without a spacecraft or puncture. The opening “coming in hot” text leads into the crash scene: a solo astronaut has landed badly beside that now-collapsed habitat. Its original upper cabin can return to orbit; the grounded frame needs a repair. Two independent opening goals stabilize the habitat and lander. Air, electrical power, communications and manually collected water come first. Chapter 4 establishes the insulated pressure hab in the cave: shovel the footprint, unfold the hab directly on the prepared ground, inflate it, then add lights. Chapter 5 builds the outdoor solar furnace and makes bricks and porous growing granules for the later berth and three-crop farm. Shelter therefore precedes local ceramic production. **Establish the farm and harvest the first lettuce before the discovery.**

The next meltwater refill clogs the cloth prefilter. The residue contains tiny ambiguous brown chips damaged by melting, pouring and straining. The first microscope view shows wet filter sludge on glass, without ice: mostly mineral grit, with only a few suggestive irregular edges. No recognizable body parts or dominant plate should establish life at this stage. Both microscope views retain the prepared slide’s rusty-brown material colour. Earth asks for fresh ice: first an untouched reference from the same visible bank, then a remote layered sample across a difficult gully. A cold shaving from the remote core goes on an ordinary microscope slide; the microscope reveals an articulated outline. The glass slide carries no visible enlarged organism. Preserve the uncertainty about its age, chemistry, life and origin.

The discovery gives NASA a reason to request an earlier return. Iron comes from the red ground through concentration and molten-oxide electrolysis in the adapted solar furnace. Only then does water electrolysis produce hydrogen and a separate oxygen supply in locally made cylinders. The fuel chapter adapts one of the previously made empty tanks as its reaction chamber during site preparation, with hydrogen and atmospheric carbon dioxide fitted in either order. The original electrical supply powers it. Methane is collected separately; oxygen never enters this plant.

A dust storm interrupts production and threatens the home and garden. Chapter 16 has only the reserve-power and water-service selectors; pausing the manual gas batches is bundled storm preparation, without a fuel-plant switch. Their backing plate fits the two controls closely, with no unused third-width panel. Existing reserve heat and power keep essential services running. The astronaut screens the entrance wall and covers the plants, then cleans the original solar array. Repair proceeds through ceramic blocking and a central footing, removal of the temporary rock, and a substantial iron support. The same oxygen and methane cylinders deliver gas to the lander's separate flight-tank systems.

A rendezvous-alignment hold motivates supper. Three independent crop targets yield potatoes, soybeans and lettuce in the same lined harvest basket, in any order. The clean galley pot receives potatoes and soybeans in either order, then lettuce completes the meal. Washing, soaking and thorough cooking are implicit complete operations. The two original cores remain on existing cooling until their separate flight-stow operations; slides stay behind.

From the pilot's seat, the arming plug means fitting the plug, checking ARMED and pressing START. The original cabin departs while the lower stage and repaired support stay behind. The final picture looks down toward the completed base from roughly 30–60 metres above it, or shows cabin and base together. End at this departure, without an orbital docking picture, Earth arrival or closeout chores.

Preserve the substantive human reactions: first leaves against the barren landscape, Mara repeatedly watching the fragments, the long pause at the microscope, rain outside Mara's window during the storm, supper worth recording, and the astronaut's own launch command. Mara's delayed recordings supply purpose and response rather than recipes. Short player prose explains physical work and feeling. Research qualifications belong in author notes, not compulsory inspection puzzles.

## Physical continuity and fixed objects

Use plain functional forms, exposed fabric and metal where they do useful work, modest wear, readable silhouettes and elevated three-quarter views. **No decorative flourishes.** Every visible component must be original compact mission equipment, a finite repurposed packing/service part or the minimum locally made part required by the action. Hand markings and repairs give the equipment character. The earlier [equipment reference](../../projects/mars-study/inspiration/02-multiple-accents.png) supplies scale and colour context only; any retro-industrial ornament, decorative trim or implied resource abundance is superseded. Do not introduce furniture, cabinets, elaborate feet or duplicate instruments to make a machine look complete.

The cave hab is one elongated enclosure with a single entrance/service end. Inside views toward that end show the small window left, sink centre-left, then vent and door right. Its end-on exterior shows the same wall reversed: door left, small window right. Preserve longitudinal ribs, the rear-left berth and the same three crop zones. The original surface starter dome remains a different building and houses the laboratory and storm controls.

Construction preserves camera, scale, feet, floor contact and installed components. The furnace feet and receiver axis do not move when petals, receiver or electrodes are added. Lander repair keeps the same rock, cross-member, ground, ceramic footer and cabin height. Do not paint future constructions into earlier states. Inspect actual gameplay at phone size.

Air has three live fixed objects at most: the array/charging connection, scrubber and antenna mounting. The powered array remains the solar-panel receiver for the reserve battery. Keep its receiving rectangle on the panels, independently of the full lead artwork; preserve a complete separate cable from the habitat service outlet to the scrubber. Both connections must remain visible in either installation order. The antenna gets a small independent target above the dome; the scrubber has its own connection on the right. Keep the array left of the starter dome in Air and storm recovery.

Each of the three farm beds is an independent fixed construction: ceramic walls, Fabric, growing medium, a drip branch and its own planting. Call the filled but still dry soybean bed “Unwatered soybean bed”; after its drip branch is fitted, call it “Soybean bed”. Use an unfinished-state adjective to suggest useful work, then remove it when that condition is resolved. Only the potato bed takes the opaque foil cover. Three distinct crops justify three simultaneous targets in construction, tending and final harvest. The sink remains visible throughout but is not a target for these operations. Preserve chapter-only late receivers for storm cleaning and support repair, and IDs `cave-shelter`, `first-harvest`, `water-problem`, `lander-repair` and `specimen-stow`.

## Manual water, filter and visible ice

The water source is a low, dusty, stable ice exposure about fifteen metres from the starter habitat. Its rusty band, cap and neighbouring rock identify it again during source-core collection. Exposed midlatitude ice is real; this convenient local geometry and foundation setback are explicit fictional site choices.

A fixed battery-powered galley heater and a separate small pot perform melting. Shovel loose chunks from the visible ice face; either put them in the pot before placing it on the heater, or place the empty pot first and add ice there. Both paths yield the same murky meltwater pot, and the heater stays in place. Its own battery is charged from the established station supply. Closing the suitable pressure-capable lid for outdoor heating, carrying inside and opening are bundled. No wired heater is dragged onto the ice.

Fabric around a reclaimed drinking-water cartridge makes the filter. Cloth strains grit; the cartridge treats the selected source's dissolved contamination. The user accepts the modest fiction of a suitable reused cartridge and favourable local water. It comes from drinking-water service, not the air scrubber. Clean water stays in the same recognizable pot, filled near the brim and subtly blue. Routine repeated trips replenish supplies; one icon does not mean one pot sustains the farm indefinitely.

The later brown clog sits in that same filter directly above the same pot, without an invented stand or jar. An empty microscope slide samples the sludge; the microscope supplies magnification. Enlarge the slides as readable icons rather than literal scale. They must read as thin glass microscope slides, with a frosted label end and grey-white panes backed at 20% opacity so noisy scenery remains visible through them. The blank has no coverslip or sample. Both prepared slides share a clear coverslip over a large, unmistakably brown sample patch, visibly distinct from the blank; their water/ice identities and readings remain separate. Depict no enlarged microscopic organism on the slide itself. Eyepieces face the seated viewer; the rear control stays behind. The untouched reference core comes from the same visible bank away from the harvested edge. Its receiving area follows the actual ice face rather than a wide empty strip.

Combining the granules with clean water includes grading, rinsing and checking at the existing sink; it never crushes finished bricks. Qualified firing and rinsing deal with the selected batch's contaminants and soluble residues. This is porous mineral root support, not instant fertile Earth soil. Crop-specific nutrient delivery includes soybean nitrogen. Keep finite cycles, salinity monitoring and ordinary substrate maintenance, without inventing perpetual closed-loop beds.

Each bed receives its own drip branch connected to the existing hab service header. Gravity-fed drippers meter only the water the crops and evaporation need. ## Iron, water electrolysis and fuel

**Iron is recovered from iron-bearing red regolith.** The small steel pin first appears in Chapter 3 while scavenging outside for water, after the Air chapter’s cable work. Introducing it beside the power cable in Chapter 2 would invite electromagnet construction before its purpose is established. It remains a genuine piece of salvaged steel used with a cable as an electromagnet. Magnetic concentration enriches selected iron-bearing minerals; it does not make metal. A high-temperature laboratory electrode assembly adapts the existing solar furnace, and a power cable supplies controlled current. Solar heat melts the selected mineral bath; electric current separates iron from oxygen. No preceding hydrogen supply, carbon/flux token, crop-trimming char, alloying step, meteorite or bulk salvaged-metal substitute is used.

The two bounded advances are compact equipment that can sustain molten-oxide electrolysis and a selected feed/process yielding workable iron. For this game, locally formed iron canisters and the support are strong enough. Do not burden play with alloy qualification, tests or inspection chores. General repair tools cover modest forming and joining. Process oxygen at this early furnace is not collected; later divided water electrolysis deliberately supplies both hydrogen and clean oxygen.

The finite precision-part allocation follows the [materials consultation](../../projects/mars-study/production/v12/emergency-revision/science-metal-fuel.md#finite-precision-parts-explicit-donor-assignments):

- The furnace's mineral-analysis experiment donates its specialist high-temperature electrode insert and insulated feedthroughs. The receiver is plain refractory containment with necessary insulation and contacts, never a decorated appliance.
- General electrical repair equipment supplies **one shared controller and service readout**, with temperature and pressure sensing. Move it between scheduled metal, water and fuel batches. Dedicated gas-contact fittings stay separate; no machine acquires another ornamental control box.
- The small regenerative-power experiment has one unused electrolyzer service insert with a compatible membrane, electrodes and gas-separating internals. It supplies the new water cell; it is distinct from the furnace electrodes and does not disable active life support.
- The gas analyzer's nonessential carbon-analysis channel donates its nickel methanizer catalyst, compact heater/sensor and sample-drying fittings. Its original measurements stop. The microscope and cold specimen storage remain intact.
- The isolated atmospheric-service branch supplies CO₂ capture, separation and release at useful pressure while breathing continues.
- Allocated gas-service repair spares provide the four short cylinder valves/connectors, seals and dedicated hoses; oxygen-contact pieces remain separately clean. No active regulator, emergency oxygen or live safety valve is cannibalized. Pausing the manual fuel batches requires no separate fuel-plant selector.

Iron makes the new divided water-cell body. Two successive clean-water deliveries commission its two sides; the power connection is assumed. The first creates the H₂-ready cell, the second adds a separate O₂ target on that same vessel while preserving H₂. After the first fill, H₂ receives drops over the full vessel. After the second fill, the O₂ receiver covers the right half and takes priority there by its later paint order; H₂ remains available over the left. These receiving areas are independent of the small painted spigots. Both accept the common Empty tank. Iron can make that tank before or after water is added. The cell rests on firm cleared ground, a flat rock or its packed laboratory tray; there is no manufactured table, rack or permanent feed-bag stand. Each gas requires its commissioned outlet. The water-cell insert supplies separated gases at useful delivery pressure, the atmospheric branch supplies pressurized CO₂, the reaction circuit keeps a pressure difference to its receiver, and the lander provides final conditioning. These integrated capabilities and sufficient batch throughput are bounded mission premises, not properties of a cable or empty bottle.

The common Empty tank and all four labelled gas canisters share **plain round-ended matte Iron bodies, simple formed seams, one short grey reused valve each, and hand-marked gas identities**. No handles, brass, individual gauges, riveted nameplates, decorative fasteners or foot rings. Necessary closures and joins remain simple. Show tanks vertically by the accepted game convention, with shallow hollows or found stones sufficient to steady them. A water pot is smaller than the cell, and a tank is taller still. The existing rover handles longer transport. Preserve the same bodies, fittings and markings through fabrication, filling and transfer.

The methane installation receives hydrogen, CO₂ and existing-station power in any order. Site preparation adapts one of the previously made iron tanks as the central chamber, bundling the allocated catalyst and process internals. Do not introduce new Iron, special gas-fitting tokens, a catalyst-fetch puzzle or a supplied processor-core token here. The plain chamber carries a clear hand-marked CH₄ on its main body, matching the water cell’s H₂/O₂ lettering; the mark remains visible through every fitting state. It rests on the existing few ceramic pieces or found rocks. No elaborate legs, manufactured table, masonry-platform sequence or second solar field is needed.

The methane reaction uses hydrogen and CO₂, produces methane and water, and releases heat. Oxygen has no feed or store in this scene. Small water recovery, drying, gas handling and controls are complete-equipment functions. Recovering process water reduces net demand rather than eliminating it. The original array has an explicit capacity/time margin for scheduled processing while essential services remain powered. Repeated batches and the storm interruption provide elapsed production time.

A separate locally made methane cylinder receives the dry fuel only after hydrogen, CO₂ and power are installed at the prepared chamber. The same oxygen cylinder from water electrolysis and methane cylinder from this plant are dragged directly onto matching O₂ and CH₄ labels at the lander ports. These are permanent original service markings, visible from the crash onward, through repair, transfer and departure. The resulting picture includes the appropriate dedicated hose; no hose-crafting action is needed. Their gas services never mix. The lander's existing active conditioning accepts gas and liquefies it in separate flight tanks; that is an explicit future spacecraft premise. Repeated deliveries with these same cylinders fill the tanks, not two small bottles holding an entire ascent load. No reactor, extra array or factory pads appear in the transfer view. Hoses disconnect before launch.
