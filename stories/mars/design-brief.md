# Mission to Mars — design brief

This brief owns the live story's lasting narrative, physical premises and visual direction. [The manuscript](mars.md) has 24 chapters. The [emergency revision](../../projects/mars-study/production/v12/emergency-revision/story-plan.md) maps the user's feedback to the new sequence; its [water/garden consultation](../../projects/mars-study/production/v12/emergency-revision/science-water-garden.md) and [metal/fuel consultation](../../projects/mars-study/production/v12/emergency-revision/science-metal-fuel.md) retain supporting research and evidence limits. The adopted material is **Iron**, without alloying, char or a carbon supply. Earlier steel, well, cryogenic-store and heat-buffer proposals are superseded.

Shared rules remain in the [authoring guide](../../docs/story-authoring.md), [format specification](../../docs/story-format-spec.md), [icon guide](../../docs/icon-guide.md) and [evaluation method](../../docs/fun-and-evaluation.md). Artwork and checks for an earlier revision do not verify this manuscript.

This is the active authority for accepted Mars intent, including iterations and production work in `projects/mars-study`. Follow [one active design brief](../../docs/story-authoring.md#keep-one-active-design-brief): update this file when a decision is adopted, keep unaccepted departures in the study's working notes, and preserve the accepted [v12 reference](../../projects/mars-study/mars-v12/mars.md) and its artwork unchanged. Prefer an established object, found support, simpler operation or omission where it serves the same purpose. Fixing a canister affects fabrication, gas collection, reactor fitting and lander transfer; changing the tent affects every exterior, interior, planted and storm view. ## Survival priorities and useful work

Use the user's “rule of 4s” as a narrative ordering mnemonic: four seconds for wits, four minutes for air, four hours for shelter, four days for water, four weeks for food and four months for company. These are story-priority labels, not literal deadlines or a simulated survival clock. Begin with immediate stabilization and breathable shelter, secure power and water, and establish the slow-growing farm before optional investigation or return-fuel construction. Mara's delayed messages supply human contact throughout the solo mission.

The first two chapters require no fabricated metal or workshop tools. A found rock stabilizes the lander; a small hand-applied patch repairs the starter habitat. Recover the primitive steel pin in chapter 2 while still scavenging outside, then save it for the later electromagnet. The original delivery carried compact science, habitat and repair equipment; it did not anticipate manufacturing return fuel. Every subsequent part must earn its payload, donor or local fabrication history. Let difficult conditions and useful discoveries drive events; the astronaut responds sensibly, without contrived mistakes or irrational conflict.

Use ceramics where they do the necessary job without expensive metal: the smooth protected footprint, modest bed walls, growing granules, furnace containment and a few useful supports. They still cost work. Do not add brick walls, paving, furniture or platforms merely to make a place look finished. A supply icon represents usable stock; it is not a claim of inexhaustible cargo. Group routine making, maintenance and repeated batches into complete operations instead of adding inventory bookkeeping.

## Story, voice and sequence

A solo astronaut lands badly beside a delivered starter habitat. Its original upper cabin can return to orbit; the grounded frame needs a repair. Two independent opening goals stabilize the habitat and lander. Air, electrical power, communications and manually collected water come first. The outdoor solar furnace makes bricks and porous growing granules. A small cave then shelters a snug pressure tent, berth and substantial three-crop farm. **Establish the farm and harvest the first lettuce before the discovery.**

The next meltwater refill clogs the cloth prefilter. The residue contains patterned fragments damaged by melting, pouring and straining. Earth asks for fresh ice: first an untouched reference from the same visible bank, then a remote layered sample across a difficult gully. A cold preparation produces a standalone thin ice slab; the microscope reveals an articulated outline. Preserve the uncertainty about its age, chemistry, life and origin.

The discovery gives NASA a reason to request an earlier return. Iron comes from the red ground through concentration and molten-oxide electrolysis in the adapted solar furnace. Only then does water electrolysis produce hydrogen and a separate oxygen supply in locally made cylinders. The merged fuel chapter uses the iron to make a central reaction chamber, with hydrogen and atmospheric carbon dioxide fitted in either order. The original electrical supply powers it. Methane is collected separately; oxygen never enters this plant.

A dust storm interrupts production and threatens the home and garden. Existing reserve heat and power keep essential services running. The astronaut screens the entrance wall and covers the plants, then cleans the original solar array. Repair proceeds through ceramic blocking and a central footing, removal of the temporary rock, and a substantial iron support. The same oxygen and methane cylinders deliver gas to the lander's separate flight-tank systems.

A rendezvous-alignment hold motivates supper. The merged garden/supper chapter gathers three successive loads in the same basket: potatoes, lettuce, then soybeans. Beans go to the sink, then combine with potatoes for a thoroughly cooked stew; fresh lettuce joins last. The source core, examined remote slab and original membrane remain on laboratory cooling until their three separate flight-stow operations. The remote parent-core remainder stays protected in the laboratory.

From the pilot's seat, the arming plug means fitting the plug, checking ARMED and pressing START. The original cabin departs while the lower stage and repaired support stay behind. The final picture looks down toward the completed base from roughly 30–60 metres above it, or shows cabin and base together. End at this departure, without an orbital docking picture, Earth arrival or closeout chores.

Preserve the substantive human reactions: first leaves against the barren landscape, Mara repeatedly watching the fragments, the long pause at the microscope, rain outside Mara's window during the storm, supper worth recording, and the astronaut's own launch command. Mara's delayed recordings supply purpose and response rather than recipes. Short player prose explains physical work and feeling. Research qualifications belong in author notes, not compulsory inspection puzzles.

## Physical continuity and fixed objects

Use plain functional forms, exposed fabric and metal where they do useful work, modest wear, readable silhouettes and elevated three-quarter views. **No decorative flourishes.** Every visible component must be original compact mission equipment, a finite repurposed packing/service part or the minimum locally made part required by the action. Hand markings and repairs give the equipment character. The earlier [equipment reference](../../projects/mars-study/inspiration/02-multiple-accents.png) supplies scale and colour context only; any retro-industrial ornament, decorative trim or implied resource abundance is superseded. Do not introduce furniture, cabinets, elaborate feet or duplicate instruments to make a machine look complete.

The cave hab is one elongated enclosure with a single entrance/service end. Inside views toward that end show the small window left, sink centre-left, then vent and door right. Its end-on exterior shows the same wall reversed: door left, small window right. Preserve longitudinal ribs, the rear-left berth and the same three crop zones. The original surface starter dome remains a different building and houses the laboratory and storm controls.

Construction preserves camera, scale, feet, floor contact and installed components. The furnace feet and receiver axis do not move when petals, receiver or electrodes are added. Lander repair keeps the same rock, cross-member, ground, ceramic footer and cabin height. Do not paint future constructions into earlier states. Inspect actual gameplay at phone size.

Air has three live fixed objects at most: the array/charging connection, scrubber and antenna mounting. The array becomes the cable's charging receiver after connection, while its physical panels remain in place. The antenna gets a small independent target above the dome; the scrubber has its own connection on the right. Keep the array left of the starter dome in Air and storm recovery.

The farm construction is one compound bed assembly plus the sink. After medium and drippers, three seed slots and the opaque potato-cover slot are independent. Fitting a cover must preserve any planted crop. Later tending has the three actual crop zones. Final harvest uses the whole mature assembly and sink, with its deliberately sequential basket loads. Preserve chapter-only late receivers for storm cleaning and support repair, and IDs `cave-shelter`, `first-harvest`, `water-problem`, `lander-repair` and `specimen-stow`.

## Manual water, filter and visible ice

The water source is a low, dusty, stable ice exposure about fifteen metres from the starter habitat. Its rusty band, cap and neighbouring rock identify it again during source-core collection. Exposed midlatitude ice is real; this convenient local geometry and foundation setback are explicit fictional site choices.

The heater includes a small existing galley pot with a suitable latched pressure-capable lid. Collect loose ice, close and heat the pot, carry it inside, then open and filter. A source action shows the visible ice, not a bore, extraction head, underground cavity or open melt pond. The small heater and pot later cook supper after cleaning.

Generic Fabric around a reclaimed drinking-water cartridge makes the filter. Cloth strains grit; the cartridge treats the selected source's dissolved contamination. The user accepts the modest fiction of a suitable reused cartridge and favourable local water. It comes from drinking-water service, not the air scrubber. A small portable collapsible tank with a tap receives the clean water and is carried inside. Routine repeated trips sustain the finite growing cycles; the game does not claim one pot supplies an entire farm indefinitely.

The later problem is brown material clogging that same cloth prefilter while refilling. It interrupts filling, not a tap already supplied by a full clean tank. A fresh piece of Fabric restores flow while retaining residue for the membrane. The reference core penetrates untouched ice beside the hand-harvested edge, away from heater contact and surface disturbance. Delete all former heated-cavity boundaries, well piping and industrial-water machinery.

## Cave, berth and farm

The cave mouth is smaller than the deep chamber, irregular and partly obscuring the tent. The rough initial floor contains substantially more hand-clearable rubble and grit; clearing makes a real patch and modest spoil pile without undermining rock or removing giant boulders. Laid brick courses make a smooth pad only under the tent footprint. There is no exterior apron, entrance baffle or perimeter wall. The folded envelope looks compact but substantial enough to unfold across the final footprint. It includes its own thin floor protection, with no separate padded-underlay payload.

Before air, the unfolded tent is deeply collapsed, roughly one quarter of its full height: there is physically no room to install the lights. The inflated tent fits tightly behind the rock edges. Lights fit directly to the inflated room and warm its small window; **Bring in light** is the final construction goal. The cave supplies shielding; the rated envelope supplies pressure, airlock and service functions. The existing air handling and heater make it usable before the storm. There is no later process-heat buffer.

Keep the sheltered berth and its smooth supported ceramic deck. Light reflective insulation and the camping mattress may fit in either order. The berth remains part of the home even when a camera does not show it.

Sheet means generic flexible waterproof stock for liners. Fabric means washable permeable cloth for a grit prefilter, basket lining, foil backing, plant cover and cleaning. Use clean pieces appropriate to food/water contact; dirty industrial cloth never returns to food. Do not equate a waterproof sheet with a permeable strainer.

The furnace makes a separate loose granular ceramic batch: clean water and screened regolith form a granule charge, which is fired in a tray. The sink grades, rinses and checks those granules as one operation; it never crushes finished bricks. Qualified firing and rinsing deal with the selected batch's contaminants and soluble residues. This is porous mineral root support, not instant fertile Earth soil. Crop-specific nutrient delivery includes soybean nitrogen. Keep finite cycles, salinity monitoring and ordinary substrate maintenance, without inventing perpetual closed-loop beds.

Gravity-fed drippers meter only the water the crops and evaporation need. The adopted small-bed fiction needs no recirculating irrigation, drainage collection, return hose or pump. Configuring the pulses and replenishing the carried reservoir are included in ordinary tending; the storm reduces watering with demand.

The potato cover spans the open medium with loose overlapping shoot/planting flaps. It does not merely wrap an already opaque brick wall or shade the leaves. Soybeans and lettuce may be sown before it, and even potato planting can precede the loose cover. The initial medium leaves headroom below the original rim for later hilling. Adding more medium directly to the growing potato bed includes lifting and replacing its existing cover; there is no collar-building step. The original ceramic walls carry the load.

**Cargo net** (`cargo-mesh`) is strong light synthetic restraint net salvaged from finite cargo packing, allocated to three jobs. One panel supports the chosen soybean cultivar from the pressure hab's original attachment tabs. Another folds and ties into a sling basket with a clean Fabric lining; the same basket gathers every harvest. A third reserved panel supports the storm canopy above the leaves; tensioning it is bundled with fitting the Fabric cover. No rods, rails, rings, wooden trellis or rigid basket frame are added. These crops and their controls are mission premises, not a complete-diet guarantee.

During the storm, both the entrance-wall thermal screen and Fabric canopy use existing pressure-hab attachment tabs. The canopy hangs above the foliage and descends to bed edges while retaining circulation. Lower it during dark periods and roll it back for lamp hours; that routine is included in the operation. It retains warmth but creates no heat. Existing heaters, stored energy and reduced drip pulses carry the temporary interruption. The old semantic ID `growing-room-service-rail` now identifies the entrance attachment tabs; it does not authorize a new rail.

## Iron, water electrolysis and fuel

**Iron is recovered from iron-bearing red regolith.** The saved steel pin remains a small genuine piece of salvaged steel used with a cable as an electromagnet. Magnetic concentration enriches selected iron-bearing minerals; it does not make metal. A high-temperature laboratory electrode assembly adapts the existing solar furnace, and a power cable supplies controlled current. Solar heat melts the selected mineral bath; electric current separates iron from oxygen. No preceding hydrogen supply, carbon/flux token, crop-trimming char, alloying step, meteorite or bulk salvaged-metal substitute is used.

The two bounded advances are compact equipment that can sustain molten-oxide electrolysis and a selected feed/process yielding workable iron. For this game, locally formed iron canisters and the support are strong enough. Do not burden play with alloy qualification, tests or inspection chores. General repair tools cover modest forming and joining. Process oxygen at this early furnace is not collected; later divided water electrolysis deliberately supplies both hydrogen and clean oxygen.

The finite precision-part allocation follows the [materials consultation](../../projects/mars-study/production/v12/emergency-revision/science-metal-fuel.md#finite-precision-parts-explicit-donor-assignments):

- The furnace's mineral-analysis experiment donates its specialist high-temperature electrode insert and insulated feedthroughs. The receiver is plain refractory containment with necessary insulation and contacts, never a decorated appliance.
- General electrical repair equipment supplies **one shared controller and service readout**, with temperature and pressure sensing. Move it between scheduled metal, water and fuel batches. Dedicated gas-contact fittings stay separate; no machine acquires another ornamental control box.
- The small regenerative-power experiment has one unused electrolyzer service insert with a compatible membrane, electrodes and gas-separating internals. It supplies the new water cell; it is distinct from the furnace electrodes and does not disable active life support.
- The gas analyzer's nonessential carbon-analysis channel donates its nickel methanizer catalyst, compact heater/sensor and sample-drying fittings. Its original measurements stop. The microscope and cold specimen storage remain intact.
- The isolated atmospheric-service branch supplies CO₂ capture, separation and release at useful pressure while breathing continues.
- Allocated gas-service repair spares provide the four short cylinder valves/connectors, seals and dedicated hoses; oxygen-contact pieces remain separately clean. No active regulator, emergency oxygen or live safety valve is cannibalized. A general spare service selector becomes the fuel-circuit control with a new hand-marked label.

Iron makes the new divided water-cell body. Clean water and the existing power cable commission it. The cell rests on firm cleared ground, a flat rock or its packed laboratory tray; there is no manufactured table, rack or permanent feed-bag stand. Filling either cylinder requires the completed cell. The water-cell insert supplies separated gases at useful delivery pressure, the atmospheric branch supplies pressurized CO₂, the reaction circuit keeps a pressure difference to its receiver, and the lander provides final conditioning. These integrated capabilities and sufficient batch throughput are bounded mission premises, not properties of a cable or empty bottle.

All four gas canisters have **plain round-ended matte Iron bodies, simple formed seams, one short grey reused valve each, and hand-marked gas identities**. No handles, brass, individual gauges, riveted nameplates, decorative fasteners or foot rings. Necessary closures and joins remain simple. Found stones or shallow hollows steady them, and the existing rover handles longer transport. Preserve the same bodies, fittings and markings through fabrication, filling and transfer.

The merged methane installation accepts the central iron chamber, hydrogen cylinder and CO₂ cylinder in any order, followed or preceded by its existing-station power connection. The chamber-making operation includes the specifically allocated catalyst and process internals; do not add a catalyst-fetch puzzle or supplied processor-core token. The plain chamber rests on small ceramic pieces or found rocks that keep its hot body clear of the ground. No elaborate mounting legs, manufactured table, masonry-platform sequence or second solar field is needed.

The methane reaction uses hydrogen and CO₂, produces methane and water, and releases heat. Oxygen has no feed or store in this scene. Small water recovery, drying, gas handling and controls are complete-equipment functions. Recovering process water reduces net demand rather than eliminating it. The original array has an explicit capacity/time margin for scheduled processing while essential services remain powered. Repeated batches and the storm interruption provide elapsed production time.

A separate locally made methane cylinder receives the dry fuel only after chamber, hydrogen, CO₂ and power are actually installed. The same oxygen cylinder from water electrolysis and methane cylinder from this plant later acquire their dedicated transfer lines and feed matching lander ports. Their gas services never mix. The lander's existing active conditioning accepts gas and liquefies it in separate flight tanks; that is an explicit future spacecraft premise. Repeated deliveries with these same cylinders fill the tanks, not two small bottles holding an entire ascent load. No reactor, extra array or factory pads appear in the transfer view. Hoses disconnect before launch.

## Field specimens, repair and supper

The gully offers a continuous steep rutted wheel route with loose rubble; the winch adds traction, not flight across a gap. A difficult qualified foot scramble over rocks permits rigging and recovery without a painted easy path. Sound anchoring, rover stability and winch rating remain scene premises. The rover pulls straight into the winch line and ends below the anchor. Recompose the nearby ice face large enough to be an obvious sampling target within this same chapter.

The reached-ice receiver is derived from bringing the rover across. No primitive distant target permits early core collection. Sampling includes a cold sealed liner, the existing rover cold cradle, controlled return and winch recovery. At the lab, a compact cold fixture cuts and contains a viewing slab thicker than the inclusion. Show the slab alone at a readable enlarged scale. The remaining core stays protected in the laboratory, while the slab, source core and membrane become the three separate flight specimens. Preserve cold handling and evidentiary uncertainty without adding routine-assay puzzles.

For lander repair, the original legs remain stable and the rock remains an unloaded safety catch. Two broad ceramic side stacks and a central footer secure the area; they are blocking, not lifting jacks. Only then does the player roll out the stone. Only the clear gap accepts the substantial short iron strut with saddle and broad foot, comparable in thickness to the original legs. The vehicle never changes height. Keep the exact background texture, footing and contact registration across all states. The lower stage and support stay behind.

Final harvest deliberately uses three sequential loads in the same cloth-lined, folded and tied cargo-net sling basket in the merged garden/supper scene. The initial basket gathers potatoes; taking that load back to the beds includes putting the potatoes on the clean counter and gathering lettuce; the next visit sets lettuce aside and gathers soybeans. Retained crop tokens represent the set-aside loads, while the same basket is reused. Use its identical recognizable design in every image. This avoids four simultaneous crop/sink targets while preserving the user's single-basket and merged-scene choices.

Soybeans come last and have exactly one downstream operation: wash, soak and drain at the actual sink. That transformation must retire raw harvested beans in every legal order. Prepared beans and potatoes then cook thoroughly in the cleaned galley pot/heater; fresh lettuce accompanies the stew last. There is no raw-bean bypass, cooker-building chain or new utensil puzzle.

## Time and verification

Growth, water trips, material batches, gas production, soaking, cooking and repeated deliveries are complete-operation abstractions. Do not add timers, waiting tokens, quantity accounting or a new inventory economy. Local canister strength is an accepted game simplification, not a prompt to introduce testing penalties. Count portable/fixed identities honestly; combination counts do not establish enjoyment.

Use shared lint, checkpoint replay and focused checks of changed branches to check retirement, alternate construction orders and actual gates. Inspect integrated phone-scale pictures and targets separately. Preserve concise verification outcomes under the [engineering retention policy](../../docs/engineering.md#verification-records). Update this brief when intent changes; shared UX changes belong in its owning document. This story revision requires no engine change.
