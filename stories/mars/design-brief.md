## Color design language

Use color to give equipment families their own character while retaining a coherent industrial collection. Warm cream housings, ivory fabric, exposed metal and charcoal rubber provide the shared base. Apply color to panels, bands, caps, linings and fittings, with one principal accent family per object.

| Accent | Equipment family | Examples in the preferred render |
|---|---|---|
| Petrol teal (`#237C80`) | Shelter and repair | Habitat-fabric lining, sealant band, seam roller, hatch latches, patch supplies and vestibule lining/fittings. |
| Muted mustard yellow (`#C4A039`) | Mobility | Rover panels and mobility fittings. |
| Cobalt blue (`#3765A6`) | Science, sampling and analysis | Sample-jar lid, core-tube band and glovebox hardware. |
| Restrained brick red (`#A84037`) | Tie-downs and small safety fittings | Tie-down cord and selected safety hardware. This is a limited equipment accent, not a claim of rescue visibility on Mars. |

The hex values are approximate paint-color targets in neutral light; sunlight, shadows, dust and wear should affect their rendered appearance. Keep the colored areas restrained and the physical materials believable. Preserve the heater's orange glow, warm headlights and glovebox illumination, and red/amber/green signal lamps according to their function. Natural terrain, rock samples, ice and photovoltaic cells retain their material colors.

Orange rescue clothing is an Earth/ocean reference; it does not establish visibility against Martian terrain. The cool accents explore separation from the warm ground. The equipment-family colors are an art direction, not a new gameplay rule.
