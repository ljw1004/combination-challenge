# History of ideas: design brief

## Purpose and scope

Let curious high-school and adult readers trace how questions, arguments, instruments and institutions change from ancient times to the modern world. Follow the game's [educational purpose](../../README.md#philosophy): combining should leave the player with understanding they can use beyond the game.

Keep the historical breadth while making the journey selective. A scene earns its place by connecting substantial threads or by offering a discovery worth experiencing in its own right. Prefer fewer substantial encounters to repeated setup, summary and qualification chapters. The current length target is roughly two-thirds of the former 85 playable chapters and 272-combination completion route; counts guide editing, not judgments of quality or estimates of human playing time.

## Connections worth carrying

- Records, predictions and explanations: ancient sky archives, geometrical models, observations that discriminate between models, reproducible experiments, and the limits of modern prediction.
- Interpretation and trust: manuscripts and translations, visible variants, dating and attribution, intact transmission, and the difference between a reliable message and a justified claim.
- Authority and inclusion: the common good and those excluded from it, legal status and emancipation, control of work, institutional categories, and contestable proposals for living together.
- Life, Earth and time: reconstructed histories, evolution's unresolved inheritance question and its later answer, changing accounts of clocks and lived time, and environmental consequences.

These threads can cross and return after a gap. A carried item should contribute evidence, an argument or a method to its next use. Shared subject matter alone does not justify a combination. Connections are not automatically claims of direct historical influence.

## Voice and action

Put the player in successive investigators' positions. Give each scene a particular question and let the combinations do the explanatory work. A small calculation, an assessable objection, a contrasting observation or a disputed rule can make an abstraction intelligible without requiring an essay or a new input mechanic.

Desk clearing is implicit. Do not narrate a cleared table, cleared worktable or reset workspace to justify a chapter transition. A thinker may return the next day when that passage of time matters to the story.

Keep valuable qualifications where they change what can be concluded. Incorporate repeated caveats into the result they qualify. Supply a promised trial outcome or discrepancy; do not complete an experimental goal with a statement that experiments are useful.

Attribute historical positions, distinguish reconstruction from quotation, and label invented teaching cases. The journey must allow disagreement and coexistence rather than imply an inevitable ascent toward the latest thinker. Follow the [authoring guidance](../../docs/story-authoring.md#keep-intellectual-and-factual-claims-honest) for claims and sources.

The speculative observatory and civic ending should apply the earlier methods to concrete proposals. Let a recorded objection change a bounded arrangement, including who does the work and who can challenge a decision. A useful accomplishment need not settle politics or eliminate plural good lives. Handle records and proposals rather than people as inventory objects, and do not add a resource-allocation economy.

## Presentation and evaluation

Use the existing illustrated settings and readable text items during revision. Keep abstract working sets small. The user permits this story's revision and evaluation entirely in the logical game space; image study is unnecessary. The [UX document](../../docs/ux.md) owns interface behavior.

Use the [Ideas study](../../projects/ideas-study/README.md) for current hypotheses, iteration outcomes and playtest reports. Follow the shared [evaluation method](../../docs/fun-and-evaluation.md): fresh participants, separate factual review, and explicit limits on what chapter-level `--fast` trials establish. Human pacing and enjoyment remain questions for human play.
