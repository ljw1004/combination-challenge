# History of ideas: design brief

## Purpose and scope

Let curious high-school and adult readers trace the interesting interplay of ideas across human intellectual thought, from ancient times to the modern world. Questions, arguments, instruments, arts and institutions meet, change one another and open new possibilities. Follow the game's [educational purpose](../../README.md#philosophy): combining should leave the player with understanding they can use beyond the game. The experience should be interesting, educational and inviting to a smart, curious person who need not already know the subject.

Keep the historical breadth while making the journey selective. A scene earns its place by connecting substantial threads or by offering an interesting fruit of thought worth experiencing in its own right, even when it contributes nothing to a later development. Friendship, melancholy, lived time and artistic experiment need no technological descendant to justify attention. This is a selective journey, not an exhaustive or proportionally representative history of every intellectual tradition.

Length is settled. Current priorities are playability, enjoyment, intelligibility, hooks and adequate teaching of the ideas included. Give an explanation enough room to work while removing needless repetition and obscurity; neither cutting words nor adding information is an end in itself.

## Connections worth carrying

- Records, predictions and explanations: ancient sky archives, geometrical models, observations that discriminate between models, reproducible experiments, and the limits of modern prediction.
- Interpretation and trust: manuscripts and translations, visible variants, dating and attribution, intact transmission, and the difference between a reliable message and a justified claim.
- Authority and inclusion: the common good and those excluded from it, legal status and emancipation, control of work, institutional categories, and contestable proposals for living together.
- Life, Earth and time: reconstructed histories, evolution's unresolved inheritance question and its later answer, changing accounts of clocks and lived time, and environmental consequences.

These threads can cross and return after a gap. A carried item should contribute evidence, an argument or a method to its next use. Shared subject matter alone does not justify a combination. Connections are not automatically claims of direct historical influence.

## Voice and action

Put the player in successive investigators' positions. Give each scene a particular question and let the combinations do the explanatory work. A small calculation, an objection the player can judge, a contrasting observation or a disputed rule can explain an abstract idea without requiring an essay or a new way to enter answers.

Introduce a new perspective through its people or community, place, approximate date and motivating problem. Continue an established inquiry without repeating its biography. Comparative settings can span writers, places and dates; identify that comparison rather than inventing a meeting. Speculative scenes and teaching examples remain explicitly invented.

Let an idea earn its appeal through the problem it addresses, its premises and what it explains or enables. A reply should expose a particular difficulty or offer a distinct account, without presenting history as a procession of foolish mistakes. A worthwhile endpoint may remain an open philosophical question or a new way of noticing something.

Desk clearing is implicit. Do not narrate a cleared table, cleared worktable or reset workspace to justify a chapter transition. A thinker may return the next day when that passage of time matters to the story.

Keep valuable qualifications where they change what can be concluded. Incorporate repeated caveats into the result they qualify. Supply a promised trial outcome or discrepancy; do not complete an experimental goal with a statement that experiments are useful.

Attribute historical positions, distinguish reconstruction from quotation, and label invented teaching cases. The journey must allow disagreement and coexistence rather than imply an inevitable ascent toward the latest thinker. Follow the [authoring guidance](../../docs/story-authoring.md#keep-intellectual-and-factual-claims-honest) for claims and sources.

The imagined observatory and community should apply the earlier methods to concrete proposals. Let an objection change a particular rule, including who does the work and who can challenge a decision. Solving one problem need not settle every political question or imply that everyone should live the same way. Handle records and proposals rather than people as inventory objects, and do not add an economy for allocating resources.

## Presentation and evaluation

Apply the shared [iteration process](../../docs/story-authoring.md#develop-a-story) and [intellectual synthesis and changing perspectives](../../docs/story-authoring.md#intellectual-synthesis-and-changing-perspectives) technique. This brief records the story's particular voice, scope and educational aims; the shared guide owns the reusable review procedure.

Follow the [shared wonder guidance](../../docs/story-authoring.md#write-wonders-and-attribution): a motivating question, the essential contribution of the ingredients, the reasoning and result, then a separate italic restatement using the item's local title where appropriate. Result cards must explain themselves without ingredient readings; titles must communicate to players who skip the cards. Primitive readings explain their own premise or example and need no artificial synthesis.

Write clear, plain English. Avoid AI-style shorthand that piles abstract labels together: say who does what, what changes and why it matters. A shorter phrase is not simpler if the reader must unpack it. Use a technical term when the idea needs it, and explain it in ordinary language; familiarity with the subject is not a prerequisite.

Use direct reasoning rather than autobiographical narration. Wonders should rarely say “I read,” “I argue” or “I extend.” First person can naturally voice a hypothetical person in an example; it should not routinely impersonate the historical thinker. Keep names where a comparison or borrowed claim needs attribution. Identify the essential work of each paragraph, prefer concrete words, and remove framing that merely announces reasoning. Preserve qualifications that change what can be concluded.

Audit every substantive idea for its motivating question, intelligible explanation, concrete case, limits and role in the journey. Ask what the player could explain or apply afterward. Some ideas connect later work; others earn a satisfying encounter in their own right. Mentioning a name or a technical term does not establish teaching coverage, and a thematic association does not make a good recipe ingredient.

Use the existing illustrated settings and readable text items during revision. Keep abstract working sets small. The user permits this story's revision and evaluation entirely in the logical game space; image study is unnecessary. The [UX document](../../docs/ux.md) owns interface behavior.

Use the [Ideas study](../../projects/ideas-study/README.md) for current hypotheses, iteration outcomes and playtest reports. Follow the shared [evaluation method](../../docs/fun-and-evaluation.md): fresh participants, separate factual review, and explicit limits on what chapter-level `--fast` trials establish. Test title clarity and standalone card clarity separately. Ask learners to explain a distinction and apply it to a new case; successful completion or repetition of a label does not establish learning. Human retention, pacing and enjoyment remain questions for human play.
