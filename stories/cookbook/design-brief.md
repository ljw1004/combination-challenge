# Cookbook design brief

This is the lasting editorial and visual intent for Cookbook. Read it before revising the story, its recipes or its artwork. Keep it current when that intent changes. The manuscript owns the actual ingredients, recipes and goals; [UX](../../docs/ux.md) owns interface behaviour, and the [icon guide](../../docs/icon-guide.md) owns reusable illustration methods. Research, construction history, production sources and verification belong in the [Cookbook study](../../projects/cookbook-study/README.md), which can be archived without retiring this brief.

## Purpose and voice

Write a practical cookbook. Make doughs, sauces, fillings, pastries and cakes. Treat fundamentals done well as worthwhile in themselves. The cognitive style is a clear, dependable cookbook such as Julia Child or Le Cordon Bleu Kitchen Essentials, with occasional culinary judgment in the spirit of the Flavor Bible. Avoid mysterious narration, food-blog reminiscence, ornamental adjectives and novelty for its own sake.

Keep the cover wording: “Write a cookbook. Make doughs, sauces, fillings, pastries and cakes.” Chapter introductions are brief captions. A useful title names the subject or marks the occasion and normally occupies its own italic paragraph, without a full stop. A title alone can be enough. Do not follow it with a redundant list of dishes: the goals already provide the task. Breakfast’s “Juice, porridge, bread.” and Home-made bread’s expression of appetite earn their place.

Occasionally add a separate paragraph offering culinary judgment beyond the immediate recipe: “Acidity balances richness.” “Add variety through texture.” “Balance rich with plain.” One or two easy sentences are enough, and each must add value. Do not explain combinations or narrate their mechanics. Roughly one principle every five to ten chapters is a check against overuse, not a quota. Keep this knowledge in chapter prose, without adding creation popups.

## Cooking and progression

Breakfast, afternoon tea and dinner are distinct serving beats. Tea comes about a third of the way through, giving an early accomplishment before the longer sequence toward dinner. The two later serving chapters assemble previously prepared foods; their preparation belongs in earlier chapter goals. Avoid a preassembled tea plate that merely duplicates the following stand assembly.

Keep plain porridge and fruit yoghurt in the opening. Reuse ingredients and preparations meaningfully. Brown butter is welcome; repeated herbed-butter embellishments are not a substitute for good fundamentals. Mushroom filling connects ravioli with Beef Wellington. Preserve excess beans earlier without immediately reopening the can; reopen the earlier fruit preserve later with a tool suited to its jar.

Use a chef’s knife consistently. Name hob and oven uses clearly. Heat matters where its omission would confuse the preparation, especially milk added to hot roux; do not add a heating gesture to every warm dish. Keep successive sauce states in the saucepan. Toss ravioli with herb brown butter in the frying pan. Assemble the meringue-coated Unbaked Alaska before baking it. Finish and ice the ganache-filled chocolate cake in its preparation chapter, not at dinner. Call the sugar-and-water mixture “Icing.”

## Serving occasions

Afternoon tea has two independent goals: fill the stand and brew a cup of tea. A kettle on the parlour sideboard supplies a cup of boiling water; add tea bags. The cup remains separate, with no delivery target or hob in the room.

The stand holds cucumber finger sandwiches, scones, brown-butter biscuits, jam roll, whipped cream and jam. Prepare the cream in Dairy, sandwiches in Home-made bread, and jam roll before tea. Arrange the biscuits around the post on the top tier. Keep one whole scone, cream and jam together on the middle tier, with the cream pot behind the spoon-free jam jar. Put a generously sized jam roll and its slice on the bottom tier. Finger sandwiches sit directly on the tier, without another plate: three slim crustless fingers with small gaps, using the accepted arrangement in the production composition. Leave visible china around the food and keep everything securely inside the rims and clear of the central post.

Call the final occasion Dinner. Its domestic dining table receives finished Wellington, a bean dish, salad, juice and desserts, with room for either cake or both. Preserve generous dessert variety. Completion comes from arranging the food itself, without a trolley, serving hatch or delivery step. Keep the completed table visible in the closing scene.

## Visual identity

Preserve Cook’s Pen: observed food structure, fine irregular pen contours and restrained colour washes, recalling older cookbooks of roughly 1900–1950. Ordinary names and recipe context can supply distinctions appearance alone cannot establish, such as exact powders, fermentation or preparation history.

Keep a physically informed size hierarchy. At the 390×844 reference viewport, small standalone drawings have a 60px minimum longest visible edge; transparent padding does not count. Bowls, jugs, plates and long tools should be larger than small ingredients. Compare components within drawings too: an apple beside a jug should match the ingredient apples, and the juice jug should be comparable to the water carafe. Deliberately compress furniture and equipment; fitted servings use the scale of their compound, independently of standalone icons. Production dimensions and sources are recorded in the [art collection](../../projects/cookbook-study/rework-art/README.md).

## Cookbook pages and rooms

Each cooking chapter has a faint background associated with its culinary subject. Loose thematic border art runs along the top; a shallow, connected culinary spread occupies the bottom, leaving a broad quiet working field. Avoid isolated decorative objects that resemble draggable ingredients. Use shared dishes, utensils, overlapping forms and food traces. Let substantial foreground forms continue beyond the bottom edge rather than leaving a blank strip below a complete still life. Keep the sides open and allow cropping. Vary pale work surfaces, including cool marble for pastry; use tablecloths sparingly.

Afternoon tea and Dinner are places rather than cookbook pages: a home parlour and a home dining room. Furnish the full scene while retaining quiet working surfaces. The tea stand starts safely on its painted table, with room around its feet. The kettle rests on the sideboard at the room’s scale. The dining table is the movable compound, so the background leaves its floor space open. Use exposed wooden flooring beneath the dining table. Keep the floor continuous with the surrounding room and preserve its perspective. These places should feel hospitable and provide a reward for the cooking.

Fixed cooking stations use spare monochrome pencil drawings, distinct from the coloured movable food. Keep each chapter to at most two hotspots. The fruit press and churns are fixed apparatus; breakfast uses a simple cookstove. Centre a single station above the working area and separate pairs. Where one stove drawing represents hob and oven, saucepan work belongs toward the top and baking toward the lower part. Do not print station names into the painting; normal game feedback supplies them. Give receiving targets comfortable room around the equipment and register room furniture to the actual supporting surface.
