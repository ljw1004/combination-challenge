# Cosmic: A Sea Beneath the Moon

## Purpose and attitude

Follow matter from a cooling universe to an uninhabited shore. The player brings physical ingredients and circumstances together, learning why some arrangements become stars, minerals, worlds and water. There is no builder, cosmic intention or inevitable march toward people. Destruction supplies later creation; a place worth looking at can be an ending in itself.

The voice is clear, spare and warmly curious. Concrete results carry the wonder: light escaping, iron sinking, molten droplets gathering, a river finding a basin. Neither an omniscient deity nor a visiting astronaut speaks. Use second person sparingly. Avoid grand claims about destiny, consciousness or the universe wanting an observer.

## Scope and structure

The accepted story contains 198 authored combinations across 24 playable chapters and a text-only ending. Follow a few connected material threads across substantial changes of scale: primordial gas; stellar enrichment and dust; a solar disk with rocky and icy branches; growing and differentiating worlds; the Earth–Moon encounter; cooling rock, atmosphere, rain and a sea. Life belongs to a possible continuation.

Chapters focus a physical setting or event with two understandable purposes and usually 4–8 starting objects. Independent branches should offer a real order of exploration, then meet. Cross-chapter objects must do useful work. Optional transformations should reveal a distinction or a striking consequence, not add bookkeeping. Cut steps whose only contribution is a renamed state or a higher recipe count.

## Scientific contract

This is an educational model, not a balanced chemical-equation simulator. Each combination represents a complete natural process, including elapsed time, surrounding material and energy transfer where the result explains them. A nucleus is not an atom; fusion is not ordinary chemistry. Preserve the distinction between primordial material and elements made in later stars. The first stars have no inherited rock dust.

Small icons represent portions of material, processes or views at different scales, not equally sized handheld objects. Recurring Earth and Moon states follow the same bodies. When an event has multiple products, represent a physical reservoir that can subsequently yield them; do not imply matter vanishes or create duplicate planets.

Reenact a leading Moon-formation model with its uncertainty stated. Earth’s water has more than one plausible source and is present during accretion; late impacts are not its sole source. The growing Earth accepts hydrogen-bearing rock and inherited metal in either order. Cold inherited disk ice need not melt before it counts as a water store. Different views of the same sea can both supply circulating water, and the fractured headland itself supplies eroded stones. Cooling, outgassing and condensation overlap. The Moon changes tides; it is not a prerequisite for liquid water. Early landscapes have no plants, animals, familiar continents or breathable air. Primary-source research and specific limits belong in [scientific research](<../../archive/2026.11.05 cosmic study/research.md>).

## Visual intent

Use the selected astronomical chromolithograph treatment: chalk and dry gouache grain, midnight indigo fields, feathered ivory light, amber heat, charcoal and copper rock, and teal water. The 245 circular medallions have transparent exteriors and thin pale rims; 288 px exports display at 72 px. Their scale is symbolic. Names and readings identify exact chemical species and invisible mechanisms; the artwork distinguishes broad families and physical arrangements without pretending to encode balanced equations.

Sixteen portrait backgrounds establish chapter-entry conditions without painting in the discoveries ahead. The early Moon has pale feldspathic crust, without modern dark maria. Molten Earth remains the same dark basalt-and-orange world; cutaways depict its contents rather than duplicate planets. The final coast retains a blunt stepped headland on the left, a low ridge on the right and three principal hollows: largest foreground-right, another middle-left and a small farther basin. Rising water can hide those boundaries; later exposed pools retain them. The completed Moon over the sea belongs to the cover and ending.

No invented instruments, hands, labels, modern vegetation, familiar continents or decorative machinery. Keep circular boundaries and normal item names readable against the scenery on a phone. Preserve the scientific direction of contraction, pressure, radiative cooling and reflected light, including the geometry of the Moon’s unequal pull across Earth.
