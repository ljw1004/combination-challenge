## Bottom line

The visible material does support **broad, connected intellectual history rather than mathematical minutiae**. Concrete cases permit real interpretation: a weapon returned to a dangerous friend, fossils inside rock, breeders retaining inherited traits, workers losing control, and cooperation in survival. Returning creations sometimes change the question in a meaningful way, especially geological time entering selection and scarcity being separated from moral burden.

The recurring weakness is **prescribed intermediate-summary assembly**. Several obvious, reasonable comparisons receive only “No visible change,” while a near-paraphrase manufactured one step earlier accepts the same evidence. Ordinary highlighting makes these paths discoverable; its use is not a failure. The issue is whether the accepted path teaches a distinction that explains the rejected one. Sometimes it does; often the visible text does not.

These are logical/readability findings, not human enjoyment, retention, historical-accuracy certification, or crowded-desk visual findings.

## Accessibility and variation

**Different subjects, similar construction grammar.** Greek justice uses a counterexample; natural explanation supplies conditions; geology reconstructs past events; evolution combines accumulation and descent; industry raises agency and moral inference. These genuinely differ. Yet many routes still follow “general inherited concept + concrete premise → summary; summary + next premise → prescribed conclusion.”

**Desk 4 offers distinct schools but not witnessed deliberation between them.** The five required outcomes concern independence, convention, fellowship, sufficient pleasure, and suspended judgment. Their premises are distinguishable and everyday. In my actions, however, rival schools did not answer one another; I assembled all their summaries rather than choosing or testing competing ways of life. This is a limitation of observed play, not a hidden-graph claim.

**Read sometimes adds needed content, sometimes only attribution/meta-commentary.**
- Read for tilted beds names deposition, deformation, erosion, renewed deposition: useful missing context. [Earth/life 15]
- Read establishes that changeable beauty is Plato's problem and Aristotle offers a rival account of form. The bare labels require that context to distinguish the operation reliably. [Greek 23–31]
- The cave Read says it is Plato's cave image and no diagram is needed, but does not explain escape/turning. The output **Education as a turning of attention** draws on background knowledge beyond the visible shadows sentence. [Greek 42–45]
- **Population–subsistence pressure** Read names “Malthus's problem” without explaining it; **Lifelike motion does not settle what life is** Read merely says another question remains open. These are weak re-entry explanations. Their original creation scenes were prepared, not played by me, so this does not establish a full-game onboarding failure. [Earth/life 27–31, 67–72]
