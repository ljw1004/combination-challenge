# Mars whole-world logical play

Play this combination prototype through the working-home and remote-observation tasks, using only the supplied player interface. Do not inspect implementation files, recipe tables, authored routes, tests, prior reviews or other player records. Give your own account of the experience, not what you think the designer expects.

This is one continuously expanding world. All your starting raw supplies are already present: new objectives do not grant more items. New places become available as the tasks unfold. Old things remain where you left them, and useful things may be moved onward. There is no packing or world reset between home and field tasks.

Inspect things, places and objectives; ask which partners currently admit a new result or an installation; combine two things or a thing with a place; place or move a thing, including back to loose workspace. Hints show legal partners, not the name of an undiscovered result. Recipes are not secret passwords; use hints whenever helpful.

Ordinary ingredients are inexhaustible capabilities, not finite stock. Only one outside thing of each kind exists across the whole world. A successful combination returns surviving source A to its origin; target B stays where it was. A target's actual location can change the result. Combining with a place makes a portable thing. Putting something at an occupied place moves the previous occupant loose. The obsolete broken radio is explicitly replaced by its repaired successor.

Some things are typed assemblies rather than a sequence of renamed upgrade objects. A Rover shows blank spaces without part labels until they are filled; optional installed parts appear when present. Adding a part updates that same Rover. A contained part is not separately movable. If the part has another authored use, its outside representative remains available to reuse; otherwise installation moves it inside. This does not make arbitrary ordinary ingredients disappear. The assembled thing provides its stated capabilities, not every capability of its contents.

Goals ask for useful functions at their stated places. Relevant enhanced things can satisfy those functions. A current placement requirement can become false when you deliberately move its thing; a historical achievement remains achieved. The interface reports when all final requirements are satisfied.

This logical interface lists the world rather than rendering a scrolling spatial view. It does not simulate physical drag distance, viewport density or search travel. You may comment on the list/hint experience, but do not infer what a graphical scrolling board would feel like.

Play naturally. Simple solutions, optional construction, exploration and comfortable progress are welcome. There is no action quota or need to create every possible thing. If stuck or something seems broken, record the attempt, expectation and result and investigate through the player interface rather than source inspection.

Briefly note changes in understanding or enjoyment when they occur, without narrating every gesture. At completion or stopping, report:

- Which tasks you actually completed; why you stopped if incomplete.
- Concrete satisfying constructions, useful choices or moments of comfortable progress.
- Concrete confusion, guessing, repetitive work or object-search burden.
- Whether old equipment gaining new uses and enhanced things satisfying goals felt coherent.
- Whether assembly slots, part retention and returning a combined source to its origin were understandable.
- Whether home-building and observations felt meaningfully different, and what consequences drove choices.
- Any interface defect with exact command and observed result where possible.

A critical or uneventful report is useful. Distinguish your actual experience from hypotheses about humans. Do not estimate human play duration.
