# Whole-collection Mars play assignment

You are playing a logical prototype of six independent combination puzzles. Complete the collection if you can, using only the supplied player interface. Do not inspect implementation files, recipe tables, authored routes, tests, prior reviews, or other players' records. Your report should reflect what you actually did, not what you think the designer wants.

You can inspect your current things, places and objectives, ask which partners currently admit a new combination, combine two things or a thing with a place, and place or move things. Partner hints do not name an undiscovered output. New results are named after a successful combination. Recipes are not secret passwords: use the hints whenever helpful.

Things are inexhaustible capabilities, not finite stock. Ordinary inputs persist; a repaired radio replaces its explicitly obsolete broken predecessor. Each board allows only one thing of a kind. A dragged source becomes loose, while a target stays where it is. A target's actual place can change the result. Combining with a place creates a portable thing. Putting something at an occupied place moves its old occupant loose, rather than consuming or attaching it.

Each level is a separate saved puzzle with explicitly declared starting equipment. The next level is not travel with the same possessions; you do not need to pack or retrieve anything. Learned recipes carry forward as knowledge, but new boards start with their own stated things. Current placement requirements may become false when you move things; historical achievements stay completed. The interface will say when the level is complete and allow an explicit transition.

Please play naturally. Simple valid solutions, exploration, optional construction, and comfortable progress are all welcome. There is no target number of actions and no need to construct everything. If you become stuck or an interaction seems broken, record what you attempted and what you expected, and use the available interface to investigate. Do not substitute source inspection for play.

During play, briefly note genuine changes in your understanding or enjoyment without narrating every gesture. At the end (or if you stop), report:

- Which levels you actually completed and why you stopped if incomplete.
- A few concrete moments of satisfying construction, useful choice, or comfortable progress.
- Any moments of guessing, confusion, repetitive work or excessive object searching.
- Whether familiar things serving later purposes felt useful or merely repetitive.
- Whether different levels felt meaningfully different; which visible consequences drove any choices.
- Any interface defect, with the exact command and observed result if available.

Do not estimate a human play duration or claim other people will enjoy this. Distinguish your experience from hypotheses. A critical or uneventful report is as useful as a positive one.
