# What is fun for Combination Challenge?

Working model, revision 9, revised on 26 October 2026 after the owner's 05:40 UTC correction: clear progress can be comfortable, and learning the mechanics can supply part of an opening's meaning without every decision being meaningful. Different explicit goals within a shared ontology remain a central route; any narrative still belongs in the game's primitives rather than authored prose scenes. This document is a design hypothesis to improve through play, not a definition of every kind of fun or a validated measure of human enjoyment. UI work remains frozen. The [unchanged revision 8](history/what-is-fun/revision-8.md), [revision 9 review](history/what-is-fun/review-9.md), earlier revisions and the [source review](interesting-decisions-research.md#independent-review-of-what-is-funmd-revision-1-before-application) preserve the changes. The [dated Mars postplay addendum](mars-objects-opening-interpretation-2026-10-26.md) separates this correction from the frozen plays.

## What makes combining worth the player's attention?

> The useful question is not how many combinations occurred, but what made performing them worth the player's attention.

The owner has identified **narrative sweep, beauty, philosophical resonance and the hook of discovering what comes next** as central personal motivations, citing their experience of Psychonauts 2. An earlier clarification specified the narrative vehicle: “I don't really want much text narrative at all. Any narrative we find must be emergent in the primitives of the game.” These are compatible preferences. A history of what the player made, what it changed and what answered it can have sweep and meaning without dialogue, chapters or a supplied character mystery carrying the experience. This is an option for creating value, not a requirement for a narrative-heavy level.

The owner specifically values combinations applied to the map: **a place in the terrain is itself a thing that can be combined with other things**. A character's avatar, a foe indicator or a Tetris block can express an event through the game's building blocks. Spatial worlds, recognizable actors and opposition remain valid design materials, not mandatory ingredients in every level. The correction does not request an unthemed algebra or the removal of characters. Logical models should establish the state, relations and changes that could eventually be visible; this is not authorization to resume UI work.

The current generative hypothesis is: **fun can arise when the player learns how the game works, makes clear progress, or adopts and realizes an intention through combining.** Devising a construction and valuing its consequences remain important possibilities; they are not the only ones. Comfortable progress need not hide a strategic dilemma, a new insight or a new purpose in every action. Wanting to act again is a further hypothesis, not a universal condition of a successful level. The next purpose may be generated by the world, chosen by the player, or explicitly supplied by another level. Intelligent action and victory alone establish neither enjoyment nor appetite.

A game can offer a developing relationship between a player and a world, rather than a series of individually defensible decisions. Anticipating and repairing disorder, pursuing an ambition against opposition, and growing from a small world into astonishing powers remain experience engines to investigate. If a developing relationship is claimed, it must be enacted by the system rather than principally supplied by its account of what a character supposedly feels. A bounded construction challenge need not make that claim.

### Different goals in a shared sea of combinations

The owner's shared-ontology correction is that a level may be **ontology-heavy rather than narrative-heavy**: a huge tree of combinations can be reused between levels, with a different goal in each. This is a legitimate central design route, not a fallback for a sandbox that failed to renew itself. A concise explicit objective can make a portion of that shared possibility space relevant. Completing it can be the successful end of a level; the next level may supply the next purpose without a character arc or emergent emergency.

The ontology is the reusable structure of concepts, combinations and public effects. A tree or more interconnected graph can contain substantial authored content. Its size is neither proof of fun nor a reason to reject it. Recipe revelation and inexhaustible concepts still apply: the player works out and performs a worthwhile construction with known possibilities, rather than guessing which pairs the author accepts. Reuse can mean carrying knowledge and methods into another goal; it need not mean preserving every physical object or world state between levels. State persistence is a separate design choice to test.

Before building a large catalogue, test a representative connected slice with several materially different goals. Ask what each goal makes relevant, what the player must work out, what knowledge transfers, and where execution becomes satisfying enactment or unwanted routine. A later level need not introduce a new operator or a wholly new skill to be worthwhile. It should offer the claimed experience, not receive novelty credit merely for changing names.

A finite ontology may eventually be exhausted, and exhaustive construction may eventually solve a level. Neither fact alone invalidates it. Distinguish the cost and experience of that method from a short, goal-insensitive routine that removes the proposed work or leaves only unwanted clicking. Conversely, adding thousands of recipes or making enumeration expensive does not establish meaningful play. Keep the quality of a level, the value of varied goals, and the scope of the whole experience separate.

### History made from primitives

For this project, **an emergent history is a sequence of consequential state changes produced by the player's combinations and reusable world laws**. The player can recognize how a prior creation, arrangement or actor response made a later event possible. Its course may be surprising without relying on a concealed recipe or an unrevealed eligibility condition. Deterministic rules can produce such histories; randomness and chaos are neither required nor sufficient.

Primitives may include places, terrain forms, objects, routes, living processes, records, purposes, actors and relations. Start a cheap experiment with a coherent representative slice and investigate what its combinations can do; this does not impose a small final ontology. A place is an operand, not just a coordinate beside an unrelated recipe menu: combining it with something may change what grows there, what passes through it, what it remembers or whom it protects. Position and connection can then give the same concept different consequences elsewhere.

One promising tentative substrate is a **place as catalyst or context for A + B at P**: the persistent place enables a combination, and the result can affect that place or the connected map. Directly combining a concept with a place is another possibility. Neither is a universal axiom; keep or discard each according to the three givens of combination, interactive fun and empirical testing. Do not keep a three-way action solely because it creates more legal cases.

Place, time and other simple backdrop conditions can contribute to an unfolding history. Time may be a causal context or process: an earlier construction can change what a later phase permits, and a process can leave a visible trace that affects the next intervention. Public timing, growth, recurrence, sequencing or an opponent's continuing action are permissible design materials. Do not conflate them with an arbitrary action quota, a finite resource renamed time, or a deadline added solely to manufacture a tradeoff. Not every prototype must omit time, and time pressure need not be the only source of challenge. The history should be readable in changed state, processes and relationships rather than a sequence of dated paragraphs.

Carry the praised availability logic into terrain operands. Hovering a concept could reveal applicable places and results; hovering a place could reveal combinations supported by its current state. In logical testing, expose the equivalent legal-action predicate, result and relevant conditions. Actual eligibility rules remain public. Searching for arbitrary place-specific recipes or a hidden catalyst condition must not become the challenge. This records a discoverability requirement for the substrate, not a new UI implementation task.

All game laws are authored. For an emergent-history claim, distinguish reusable rules whose interaction generates events from dialogue or a custom scene trigger supplying the development. Authored level goals and initial conditions are legitimate ways to organize the shared ontology; they are not disallowed scene writing. Concise goals, names and compact explanations are welcome. A character can have an explicit persistent policy, change position or allegiance, refuse an interaction, transform a place or repurpose a creation. Those actual acts can carry a relationship. A name attached to a fixed sequence of replies cannot establish one.

When a candidate claims emergent history, describe its primitive kinds, public interaction laws and one causal chain it might generate. Ask:

- If dialogue, chapter openings and atmospheric prose were removed, what consequential sequence would still occur? Keep labels and explanations needed to understand actual state.
- Which earlier arrangement or event makes the present situation different? Could the relevant effect arise in another configuration through the same law?
- What must the player work out or skillfully construct, and what worthwhile ambition or opposition gives that work a purpose?
- What would the player's changed world, actor or map show about the sequence so far? A log may clarify history, but the experience should not depend on reading an authored account in place of seeing its consequences.

This is a requirement for any narrative the project uses, not a demand that every level generate a narrative, and not a claim that authored stories cannot be enjoyable. Bell and Mneme appreciation remains historical evidence of what those players valued. Their few elective actions do not validate a prose-led campaign as the desired vehicle. The Far Bank campaign is **rejected as the current direction** before a completed playtest. Its artifacts remain history; useful primitive mechanics may be reconsidered in a different design.

### The intention–construction–response loop: completion or continuation

1. **Intention.** What is this level asking the player to accomplish, or what do they choose to make happen? An assigned goal can remain the purpose throughout a successful level. Record the supplied objective and the player's actual intention separately; mark adoption unobserved if necessary.
2. **Tension and devising.** What gap, uncertainty, opposition or opportunity makes a combination worth imagining? All recipes may be known while their larger implications still invite thought.
3. **Consequential response.** What does the world do, and what does the player notice? The result should honor a coherent law, resolve or revise an expectation, and preserve a recognizable connection to their action.
4. **Completion or continuation.** Has the player reached a valued ending, or does something else now seem worth attempting? A useful result can open a new power, reveal a consequence, change an adversary's behavior or make a self-chosen project imaginable. A different level can also supply the next explicit goal. Distinguish these sources; neither another legal move nor another assigned objective proves self-generated renewal.

Not every action must traverse this entire loop. Several combines can enact one intention; a satisfying known action can be part of a larger ambition. Nor must every success create another emergency or voluntary project. Relief, appreciation, closure and choosing to stop satisfied are legitimate experiences. Distinguish eager continuation, satisfied completion, boredom, confusion and required compliance. Finishing a goal-directed level without further appetite is not by itself a failure.

### Clear progress, learning and pacing

An opening can be worthwhile because the player is getting oriented: finding out how to combine, what an object does, and how an action advances a clear goal. Public recipes and exact previews can support this learning rather than spoil it. Reading a rule, recognizing its result and becoming familiar with using it are different from guessing a concealed recipe. None requires a dilemma at every step.

There can also be comfort in doing clear things and seeing progress, including after the mechanics are understood. Do not make this value conditional on measurable learning, a changed role, a later payoff or a special claim that each routine act is intrinsically meaningful. Familiar enactment can keep the same purpose. This is the owner's design preference and a prospective hypothesis, not evidence that the existing agents or a hypothetical human enjoyed a particular sequence.

Judge simplicity in relation to the stage and proposed experience. A short orientation, a comfortable passage and a later construction problem can reasonably ask different things of the player. Pacing concerns how these passages feel together, not maximizing strategic dilemmas per combine. Later goals may give familiar actions further purposes; this is one possibility to investigate, not a debt that every early action must repay. Nor must every later passage become harder or novel.

Keep the distinctions light: clear progress is an observable change; understanding needs evidence beyond information being available; comfort is an experience report; strategic depth is a separate structural claim. A few representative events and a short retrospective can inform the relevant question. Do not interrupt every combine with a grading form or require players to justify enjoying it. Missing subjective evidence remains unknown, not either comfort or boredom.

### Experience engines to invent with

The designer should choose a mechanism that might make pursuing the declared goal or developing world worthwhile, not merely assert that a state space is large. These are alternatives to investigate, not obligations for every level or established laws of enjoyment.

| Engine | Possible sequence | Prediction worth trying | How it could fail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Goal-directed construction | Understand an explicit goal → select and compose relevant parts of the shared ontology → realize a working answer → finish | The player can explain the construction inference and values the achievement or enactment; another goal changes what is useful | The goal reduces to an unwanted prerequisite checklist or the same short goal-insensitive routine |
| Anticipation and restored order | Notice a developing problem → invent an intervention → see order return → appreciate closure or a new opportunity | The player can name an anticipated problem and values the restored state or an intended next use | The same emergency repeats forever, or restoration leaves nothing the player values |
| Ambition, opposition and adaptation | Adopt a project → construct a strategy → encounter understandable resistance → revise while retaining an ambition | A player changes an approach in response to this opponent or world, whether the objective was assigned or self-chosen | Random obstruction replaces a legible opponent, or every response is a prescribed counter-recipe |
| Expanding agency | Begin with a small conceptual world → invent an intermediate → discover a previously unavailable kind of action → imagine something larger | A prior result becomes a whole ingredient in a new, player-articulated possibility | Unlocks merely add nouns or postpone an obvious prerequisite chain |
| Consequences of one's ingenuity | Solve a problem → the solution changes a connected part of the world → recognize ownership of that change → choose whether and how to respond | The player names a new intention tied to a specific consequence of their own construction | Success is always punished with mandatory repairs, or the new problem could follow any action unchanged |
| A developing relationship | Act on a persistent actor/world → observe a characteristic state-changing response → revise expectations → try a new way of dealing with it | The next plan refers to something learned about this particular actor or system and what it actually did | A personified name hides a fixed menu, dialogue sequence or an endlessly resetting situation |
| Clear progress and orientation | Take a clear step → see it work → become familiar with the mechanics or simply appreciate progress | The player reports orientation, comfort or satisfaction in progressing; these need not all occur | Completion is treated as proof of comfort, or the evaluator demands a dilemma from a teaching step |
| Familiar enactment | Understand a relation → realize it → perform familiar actions toward the same or another purpose | The player values doing it; no changed role or new insight is required | The evaluator supplies a poetic meaning the player did not experience |

Discovery can also be an engine: a combination reveals an interaction, a prior construction becomes useful in a different role, or an actor repurposes something under its known policy. The next intention may be “I want to understand what this place is becoming,” rather than “I need to optimize the next move.” The discovery should be an event or possibility in the game, rather than the next paragraph being withheld. An evocative concept can have value through meaning and association without introducing a new algorithm; that value does not by itself establish a primitive-generated history or a new kind of challenge.

Persistent places, actors, capabilities and consequences are candidate anchors for caring. So are a clear construction goal and growing mastery of a reusable ontology. A score or another level does not automatically supply value, but a new explicit challenge is a legitimate source of the next purpose. Continuity may lie in the world's causal structure, in a player's developing project, or in learned concepts and methods applied across distinct goals; do not conflate these forms.

### Sweep, beauty and conceptual choice

For a candidate, the designer should name **the goal, world or construction to care about; the concepts worth combining; and the public laws or relations that make working toward it worthwhile**. State whether the candidate tests a bounded goal, cross-level reuse, or a developing world. Build a representative playable slice before extending its scope. A written encounter by itself does not test the intended vehicle.

Combining a living process with a preserved form, a record with a place, or a purpose with a persistent actor can suggest different kinds of creation. The concept should have an understandable role in what happens. Expose its local rules; thematic association must not become another hidden password. Philosophical meaning can arise from preserving a memory while changing its audience, or protecting a place at the expense of contact, because the player actually constructed those relations. A speech about memory or freedom cannot establish the corresponding consequence.

Beauty may be found in an elegant construction, a transformed landscape, a simple law producing an unexpected pattern, or a recognizable earlier creation acquiring a larger role. These are hypotheses to test through the owner's and players' responses, not guaranteed rewards for symmetry, complexity or a poetic name. Event labels, icons, actor indicators and compact state explanations can make such developments legible. Visual and tactile execution remain outside the present logical test.

Keep reported conceptual or aesthetic appreciation even when the underlying reasoning is familiar. Do not infer a new skill from new terminology, and do not erase a player's appreciation just because it concerns an isomorphic structure. Separate that finding from whether a candidate fits this project's current direction and achieves its declared level or whole-experience scope.

### Locate the live question

A designer's proposed question, a question printed by the game, a player-adopted question, a known answer worth enacting, and a satisfactorily closed concern are different things. Record who posed the question, what was already disclosed, whether this player adopted it, and what appreciation, action or stopping followed. A supplied goal can be sincerely desired; self-chosen does not require inventing something outside the offered possibilities. A question about arranging terrain or responding to a foe is as eligible as a question about a person.

For a claimed discovery, distinguish available public laws, disclosed forecasts, a noticed event, a changed interpretation and an appreciated enactment. Exact text matching detects some prior disclosure; it cannot establish semantic novelty or understanding. Do not conceal functional conditions to create a story reveal. Earlier Bell/Mneme reports about passages and character questions remain valid reports under their original conditions, but opening another authored passage is not evidence that primitives generated a new history.

The first audit showed why this needs an implementation contract. Different report vocabularies hid the Bell player's quiet-memory appreciation and Mneme player's satisfied closure from the evaluator's summary. Preserve source paths, explicit absence, negative reports and unknown fields. A summary must not turn unrecognized language into missing experience.

### Separate designer and critic

The **designer** proposes an experience engine, a declared level or sequence scope, an intended experience, and a small coherent mechanism or representative ontology slice. Speculation is welcome. The designer need not prove fun before building a cheap logical experiment.

The **critic** asks whether the mechanism produced that sequence in play, checks alternatives and rival explanations, and proposes a falsifying next test. The critic must not demand statistical proof before every invention, nor treat uncertainty as a reason to stop designing. A negative finding should narrow or change a mechanism; it should not become an endlessly expanding warning checklist.

Roles and access should be recorded. An independent critic can audit the implementation and traces without inheriting the designer's desired verdict. Several agents agreeing is still correlated AI evidence.

### Test the declared goal; diagnose appetite when relevant

For a goal-directed level, declare the objective, public rules, intended experience, stage in the game and valid ending before play. The experience may be orientation, clear progress, comfortable enactment, construction or insight. Test the relevant claim without requiring all of them: what the player understands or works out, what they actually do, and how they report it. Assess progress and failure as well as success. If testing several levels over one ontology, investigate transfer and pacing where relevant, not mandatory novelty on each board. Completing an assigned objective is legitimate goal-directed play; it is not evidence of voluntary continuation or comfort.

Use an elective-interest diagnostic when the hypothesis concerns self-generated projects, continuing-world renewal, or desire to take another level. It is not a universal pass gate for every bounded level. Before such a diagnostic, specify a short required exposure and a genuine stopping point. After the first meaningful response, ask what, if anything, the player wants to attempt next **before suggesting a desirable answer**. Offer stopping as a fully valid way to finish the test. If they choose continuation, record the actual optional action and the intention expressed before it. A promise to play again is weaker evidence than doing so.

Do not call instructed completion voluntary. Report separately: an expressed future intention, an optional action actually begun, a concrete self-generated possibility, a satisfied stopping reason, and continued play required by the task. Agents can comply with an optional-play prompt without feeling absorbed; none of this establishes human motivation.

The experience account must identify the intended goal or pleasure, the work it required, the resulting construction or response, and the observed completion, continuation or stopping. For a renewal claim, additionally identify the proposed invitation and what the player made of it. If a player performs a clever construction and stops satisfied, that may support a successful finite level; do not call it sustained absorption or reject it for lacking another project. When continuity is the question, a closed-ending/continuing-consequence comparison can help. If several mechanics change at once, do not claim the comparison isolates continuity.

## The experience we are trying to create

The player can learn the mechanics, make clear progress, or understand a goal or possibility and combine things to make it real. They can also enjoy enacting or expressing a relationship they already understand. They may feel comfortable, oriented, inventive, curious, capable or expressive. New possibilities are one source of value, not a requirement for every action. The useful question is not how many combinations occurred, but what made the passage of play worth the player's attention.

For this project, combining must be central to the activity. Its inputs, their relationship or their order should explain the result. This does not require labeling every individual combine meaningful: clear, familiar execution can be part of worthwhile play without a new reason attached to each step. A recipe catalogue attached to an unrelated ability menu is weak evidence. All legal combinations and their rules may be known. We are not trying to make guessing the author's vocabulary enjoyable.

The player has inexhaustible access to established concepts. Do not manufacture tradeoffs with finite ingredient stocks, action credits or production chores. Time pressure is a possible game form, not the default cure for a dull construction problem.

A large ontology or emergent simulation does not remove the need for a game. A sandbox that merely adds nouns has not yet established a worthwhile challenge. A clear goal can give otherwise available combinations purpose; new opposition or narrative is not automatically required. When proposing a construction challenge, look for an ambition, obstacle, opponent or structural tension that makes devising worthwhile. An actor can provide opposition through an inspectable policy and actual world actions; a terrain relation can constrain several desired outcomes at once. Learning the basic mechanics or making comfortable progress need not already exercise a skill above them. No single kind of challenge, constant emergency or opponent is mandatory.

## Interesting decisions are one route to fun

The owner's Sid Meier proposal remains a decision-making hypothesis, not a requirement governing every passage: an interesting decision is informed, consequential, and presents a worthwhile choice without an obvious universal winner. This is our operational synthesis, not a verbatim Meier definition. His talk allows situational tradeoffs, consequences, understandable information and different play styles, while explicitly acknowledging limits of the decision lens for some puzzles and rhythm games. [Talk transcript, third-party transcription](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dklassic/GDC-transcript/main/static/src/transcript/WggIdtrqgKg.txt), [contemporary report](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-sid-meier-on-how-to-see-games-as-sets-of-interesting-decisions).

- **Informed:** the player can understand the relevant options and explain a plausible reason for choosing one. This does not require knowing the optimal multi-step plan.
- **Consequential:** alternatives change a relevant possibility, property, commitment or world response. Different names or animation do not suffice.
- **A real choice:** more than one option deserves consideration in the current situation. An obviously worse option is not a tradeoff merely because it exists.
- **A situated choice:** what is useful can depend on the world, goals or chosen approach. A short common winning routine can expose a solved problem.

Publicly available information establishes an opportunity to choose intelligently. It does not establish that the player understood it. A correct prediction, a relevant adaptation or a concrete player account can provide further evidence. These remain separate findings.

Not every action needs to be a strategic decision, or meaningful in its own right. A puzzle can offer one excellent insight followed by a short construction. An opening can teach the mechanics through clear actions; a familiar passage can offer comfortable progress without an earlier insight having to justify it. A player can form a plan before the first move and still enjoy making it work. Count a plan as a possible decision episode where relevant; do not demand a new dilemma from every combine command.

A generic algorithm that eventually solves every finite puzzle is not the same as a short routine that makes these particular encounters uninteresting. This includes building every reachable concept in a finite ontology. Report its cost, goal sensitivity and actual player experience before judging it. Eventual exhaustive solvability is not a rejection criterion; a cheap universal routine can be counterevidence to a claimed construction challenge. Equally, overwhelming a solver with more possibilities does not establish depth.

## What a player might be doing

These are different activities to observe, not a hierarchy that awards points for the grandest label:

| Activity | What to look for | Common false inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategy | Choosing a lasting approach, relationship or commitment that shapes later possibilities | A long plan or large number of states automatically means strategy |
| Tactics | Responding to this situation using current capabilities | Every local choice is trivial because a long-term plan exists |
| Optimization | Improving an already working construction along a valued dimension | Smaller or faster automatically means more enjoyable |
| Construction/execution | Realizing an understood composition | A fully understood action must be busywork |
| Exploration/learning | Testing a relation or applying it in a new context | More discoveries prove curiosity or learning |
| Upkeep/overhead | Repeating actions to maintain a settled arrangement | Every upkeep action is either fun or tedious for everyone |
| Ritual/expression | Choosing an action for its felt significance, pattern or authorship | No measurable tactical improvement means no value |

The last two rows matter to the owner's hunch: combining may be familiar and repetitive while its meaning changes. The chores paper provides evidence about social board-game activities, not a general validation of solo repetition. Our solo-combining hypothesis needs its own evidence. Ask what repetition is doing for this player and preserve disagreement.

### Decision episodes

Where a decision or insight claim is being tested, name the expected unit of thought. An episode starts with an unresolved question or choice of approach and ends when that question is settled, replaced, or abandoned. It may span a plan plus several combines; one irreversible combine can itself be an episode. This lens need not describe an orientation or comfortable passage, and missing decision episodes do not invalidate those experiences.

Use a player's brief annotation or a concrete report tied to actions to identify experienced episodes. If neither establishes the boundary or unresolved question, mark it unknown. Model analysis can separately identify opportunities for a consequential choice. Do not retrospectively split one plan into many episodes to raise a density score. Counts at different episode definitions are not comparable.

## Nonresource tradeoffs to investigate

Examples include flexibility versus permanence, independence versus coordination, breadth versus specificity, retaining a living property versus transforming it, and exposing information versus preserving privacy. These are candidate design materials, not automatic interesting decisions.

A real game must make both sides arise from the same coherent operation. If the player can harmlessly acquire and apply every safeguard first, the supposed tradeoff has collapsed. That falsifies the tradeoff claim, not automatically a separate insight, construction or expression claim. If one arrangement achieves every valued outcome better, the alternatives may be dominated. If every first move can still reach exactly the same outcomes, the consequential commitment may occur later. Find it rather than declaring every setup action strategic.

Test future joint achievability, not only immediate numbers. Two currently incomparable outputs can be merely a choice of order if a safe continuation obtains and applies both. Keep exact enumeration, bounded searches and unknown feasibility distinct. Reversible setup may be useful exploration even when it makes no binding commitment yet.

Distinct viable outcomes are evidence of a choice structure. They do not show that anyone cares about the difference. Compare alternatives from the same saved state under the same disclosed rules. A truncated search is not proof an alternative is impossible. Conversely, a unique-solution insight puzzle can be worthwhile without a Pareto frontier. A clear answer after the insight does not invalidate the earlier insight. The evaluator must identify which kind of pleasure the candidate claims to offer.

## Repetition, mastery and meaning

Classify repeated actions by their use and context, not their syntax. Combining the same pair to maintain the same settled result differs from reusing an established construction in a new role. Both differ from a familiar action performed because the player enjoys its rhythm or significance.

At a useful checkpoint, ask what the player made of the repetition. It may teach a mechanic, support comfortable progress, enact an understood intention or become unwanted work. A new role or unresolved question is not required. Use a concrete report or example to support an experience claim, not as a demand to justify every repeated action. An absent report means unknown, not boredom. Likewise, an eloquent description does not establish enjoyment or correct understanding.

Do not rescue unwanted routine with an invented account of its possible significance. A player reporting that an action has become work is counterevidence for that player's experience, even though another player might value it. Preserve both rather than averaging away a disagreement.

An actionable counterfactual is: if this settled chain resolved automatically while preserving its logical result, what would this player lose? A new use, a chance to reconsider, authorship, a meaningful enactment or an explicitly valued moment may matter. Same final state does not prove that execution has no value. In a solo logical test, social/tactile reasons remain untested unless the relevant mechanism is actually present.

Logical play can test changing roles, inference, authorship and consequences. It cannot establish tactile pleasure, a pleasing rhythm of hand movements, or human emotional response. Keep those out of the current verdict rather than filling the gap with speculative UI work.

## The evaluator's job

For a game and a saved play, first state the **declared scope, stage and experience account**: the assigned or adopted goal, the work and noticed response, and the observed completion, continuation or stopping reason. Distinguish orientation, comfortable progress, challenge, transfer across goals and self-generated renewal; do not require evidence of all of them. The following are reviewer prompts, not a form attached to each action. Mark inapplicable claims as such rather than inventing evidence. Then produce:

1. **The proposed pleasure:** what kind of experience this candidate claims to offer, and which observations could contradict it.
2. **A decision account, where claimed:** a few concrete decisions with available information, a plausible alternative and the resulting difference. Include where a meaningful commitment occurs; no dilemma is required to validate an opening or comfortable passage.
3. **A learning/reuse account, where claimed:** what mechanics become understood or familiar, or what a construction, concept, rule or method enables later. Distinguish public disclosure from demonstrated understanding, and retained objects from transferred knowledge. No new learning or later use is required from every clear step.
4. **An attention and pacing account:** distinguish inference, orientation and familiar execution from unwanted repetition using the player's evidence and stage of play. A changed role is not a prerequisite for valued execution; a clear result is not proof of comfort.
5. **An adversarial account:** attempt an obvious dominant policy, a short universal routine, all-safeguards preparation and a cosmetic-variety explanation where applicable.
6. **A recommendation:** keep, change or reject one concrete hypothesis, with counterevidence and a next test.

For each key claimed consequence, record the relevant combination, prior state or history, public law and actual resulting change. Separate those facts from the player's interpretation and any authored scene, stage trigger or explanatory text. For a narrative-development claim, check whether primitives generated it, prose supplied it, the vehicle was mixed, or it is unknown. A level's explicit objective is a legitimate supplied purpose, not a falsely emergent event. Check project alignment separately from reported enjoyment; an appreciated passage can remain appreciated while a prose-led candidate is rejected as the current vehicle.

Functional disclosure must include the state and history needed to reason about those laws. If audience attaches to a voice origin, reveal that origin; if a stored record can restore exactness, show the record; if an intermediate event leaves lasting exposure or a changed actor policy, include that consequence in the functional forecast. A compact avatar or indicator may later display such facts, but brevity cannot justify obscuring them. Mystery about an undocumented condition is not the desired curiosity.

Do not collapse these into a supposedly measured fun total. Report mechanical findings, player reports and evaluator interpretations separately. A numerical estimate can be a prediction to validate, but cannot become evidence for its own correctness.

The density idea can be useful for a decision-heavy passage: worthwhile decisions can be diluted by unwanted work. It is not an overall pacing target, and low dilemma density cannot by itself reject an opening or comfortable passage. Commands are not time, and an AI's tool latency or deliberation time is not human playtime. Report the numerator, denominator and access condition. Do not rename actions-per-game as interesting-decisions-per-minute. Bundling a solved chain into one command must not automatically improve the attention verdict. Track the claimed experience and valued execution separately.

## Tests that should catch a bad evaluator

- Many labels lead to the same world: no consequential-choice credit.
- One option dominates every other: do not count the menu as a dilemma.
- A player correctly builds an elegant three-step answer: do not reject it just for brevity or an early insight.
- A player repeats a known action in a new functional role: do not automatically call it upkeep.
- A player explicitly enjoys familiar repetition: preserve that report even if no new skill is learned.
- Clear opening actions introduce the mechanics: do not penalize them solely for low strategic dilemma density or infer learning merely from successful completion.
- A player values simply making progress: do not require a new insight, later purpose or separately meaningful decision to validate that report.
- An agent reports routine answer selection: keep that report; do not convert the owner's general preference for comfort into this agent's or a human's enjoyment.
- A player reports satisfaction while predicting the rules incorrectly: keep pleasure and understanding separate.
- All player reports are absent: subjective findings remain unknown.
- A game is solved by exhaustive enumeration or eventual construction of every concept: report the cost, method and experienced work before deciding whether it is a shortcut.
- A finite level ends with appreciated completion and no optional continuation: do not fail it merely for lacking self-generated renewal.
- Several explicit goals reuse the same ontology: do not demand new prose, a new operator or persistent world state from every level; test goal relevance, transfer and the claimed experience.
- A large catalogue receives praise without a played goal or construction: do not turn size or future potential into evidence of fun.
- Several nondominated endings exist but differ in arbitrary points: structural variety alone does not establish meaningful tradeoffs.
- A familiar expert solves the whole puzzle immediately: investigate prior skill before concluding the puzzle is universally trivial.
- Text says an actor chose custody, but only a policy label changed: do not credit an institution or blocked transmission without checking the actual state and effects.
- A character's fixed question motivates two elective scene-unlocking actions: preserve that behavior without crediting a long primitive-generated history.
- All prose is removed, leaving an effortless catalogue of harmless unlocks: do not equate reduced text with a worthwhile emergent game.
- A named place hides a unique eligibility rule: do not count ontology search as a spatial challenge.

## Different ways of combining

A level may reuse the same combination laws for a different explicit goal; it may also change the local meaning of combination if its rules are explicit. A concept's meaning can also change what the player cares about, even when part of the algebra transfers. We need not force geometry, processes, ideas and institutions into the same operator merely to reuse an implementation. A place as an operand or catalyst is a promising way to let context matter; it remains a hypothesis rather than a required wrapper for every experiment.

Compare three things: **semantics** (what the combination does), **skill** (what the player must work out), and **consequences** (what changes afterward). Joining a shape, transforming a living material and binding independent councils are candidate differences. Renaming shapes as ideas is not sufficient to establish a new inference, but the ideas may still contribute conceptual value; test that claim separately. Different truth tables can still invite the same familiar construction method. Conversely, the same familiar method can acquire a new significance through a different level goal or the history of a developing world. Ask what transferred and what required a new inference; do not equate new vocabulary or an authored chapter with a new kind of play.

## Safeguards supporting the experience theory

F1–F10 are useful critics of false positives. They support the generative account above; passing them does not demonstrate absorption or invent the next compelling mechanism. Their cosmetic-variety warning rejects a false claim of strategic novelty, not the possibility that theme or beauty gives the player real value. The current project direction requires combination to carry the activity and any narrative to arise through primitives; it does not require each level to generate another purpose. Keep alignment, declared scope and pleasure findings separate.

### Principle-to-evidence contract

These IDs are an audit contract, not a checklist that every game must score positively on.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | State the proposed pleasure, stage and scope | Declare the relevant experience, including clear progress or orientation; specify opening, familiar passage or later challenge and bounded level, sequence or continuing-world scope | Treating every level as a self-renewing sandbox or every opening as a strategic-depth test |

The post-play reviewer should mark coverage, cite a concrete trace or fixture, and identify the most consequential mismatch. It should not simply agree that the document sounds reasonable.

## Test level quality, then the whole experience

The owner's earlier target was about 30–40 minutes at roughly one decision every 10 seconds: approximately 180–240 decision opportunities. Keep that historical ambition separate from the current stage-sensitive evaluation: it is not a cadence requirement for an opening or every comfortable passage, nor a quota for each level. The experience may span multiple goal-led levels over a shared ontology without endlessly renewing one world. Commands are not decision opportunities, and AI tool time is not human playtime. Do not silently revise earlier protocols that used the original target.

Test a bounded level against its declared purpose first. A good short construction, a satisfied ending and a limitation in whole-game scope can coexist. Next test a small sequence of distinct goals using the same ontology: identify which concepts become relevant, what methods transfer, where another inference or valued enactment occurs, and how much repeated setup becomes burden. Do not estimate a worthwhile 30–40-minute experience by multiplying an untested level count or recipe count. A sequence of nominally different objectives may still repeat the same unwanted work.

For a candidate that specifically claims a long developing world, test whether interacting laws, retained creations, opposition or processes sustain worthwhile play over that declared scope. A large map, scene list or menu does not establish it. For a goal-led sequence, explicit new objectives may supply purpose; they need not emerge from the previous world's history. Either route must earn the attention it asks for. Do not add compulsory repetition merely to inflate length, or confuse a player's appreciated familiar enactment with padding.

Use mandated diagnostics for coverage and optional checkpoints for the appetite question when relevant. Declare their scope prospectively. Forced completion is not appetite; choosing another assigned level is not the same as inventing an unprompted project. Record meaningful episodes separately from combines, waits and automatic actions. When a level naturally resolves, keep its ending. If a test claimed a longer scope and runs out of meaningful work, retain that scope failure without treating it as proof that the finite level is invalid. Earlier 180–240-opportunity protocols and their outcomes remain historical conditions, not retroactively successful multi-level tests.

## Audit and revision contract

For each played candidate, keep a reviewed ledger of which experience hypotheses to retain, change, reject or leave untested, with supporting and opposing trace references and a new falsifiable experiment. Copying the designer's pre-play next-test sentence is not closing the loop.

Verify elective construction against a valid earlier checkpoint and recorded intention. Keep a chronological choice log; distinguish continued construction, optional inspection, choosing closure and required action. A linked new session must have prospective provenance, not merely an arbitrary matching filename. These checks establish protocol behavior, never intrinsic human motivation.

The independent [first audit](fun-evaluator-audit-1.md) evaluated revision5's implementation against four actual plays. Revision6 incorporates its report-preservation, live-question, disclosure, chronology and evidence-linked revision findings. A correction by the audit author should be identified as such; subsequent verification should not be mislabeled independent of that implementation.
