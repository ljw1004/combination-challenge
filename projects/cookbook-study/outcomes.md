## Practical cookbook rework and serving chapters

The later rework replaces the notebook narration with direct technique and flavour explanations. Breakfast now ends with plain porridge, flatbread and juice; fruit yogurt remains. A tiered afternoon tea gathers earlier baking before the later chocolate, frozen-dessert and Wellington work. Dinner serves Wellington, bean pie, salad, juice and several desserts. Both cake alternatives can be made and loaded independently. The canned beans remain sealed earlier in the book. The earlier fruit preserve is reopened much later with a jar opener; the mismatched can-opener image was replaced with a labeled placeholder after the user caught it. Original paintings are unchanged; 47 reproducible placeholder assets cover new dishes, changed equipment and serving furniture.

Four separate, replay-verified logical-agent sessions supplied bounded evidence:

- The original opening reader made 66 accepted combinations through early pastry, with one rejected placement-dependent attempt. Earlier preparations supported continuity, but the knife-to-breadcrumbs shortcut and later assembly repetition were concerns.
- The revised opening reader made 71 accepted combinations through cheese sauce, with no rejected game actions. They reported useful understanding of yogurt straining, vinegar aeration, duxelles, brown butter, lamination and off-heat cheese. The unexpected beans-to-stew intermediary prompted a clearer introduction. Both opening readers expected the knife to make slices; the final revision instead crushes dry bread with the rolling pin and explains this in the introduction. The knife now serves mushroom chopping. This trial preceded the final plain-porridge edit.
- The Wellington/feast reader began after 255 setup events, then made 14 combinations and two placements without rejection. Cooling the filling before wrapping and the final dining-room delivery were clear. Eight cake/dish slots for seven required servings caused hesitation; the introduction now explicitly allows both cakes and their overlays occupy distinct places.
- The scones/tea reader began after 198 setup events and completed through serving and the next introduction. Handling instructions helped, and gathering soft bread, tender scones and crisp biscuits gave the stand a purpose. Fitting five servings added little new decision-making; a pot fitted into the stand was understood as grouping the service but felt slightly abstract. Two failed moves were CLI-schema errors, not cooking mistakes. An explicit brew-tea goal was added after the frozen trial to prevent leaving the saucepan before preparing the next chapter's drink.

The hypothesis that direct introductions improve prediction has local support from these reports; no controlled comparison or human learning-retention result is claimed. Tea and dinner give distinct construction payoffs, but sustained pacing and native-phone comfort still need human trials. New artwork remains deferred.
