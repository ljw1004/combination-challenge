# Fixed working stations

Coloured equipment looked like draggable food. The bread chapter compared five ways to make apparatus belong to the page. [Browse the candidates](index.html); the [comparison PNG](proofs/comparison-stations.png), transparent source drawings and original [generation briefs](prompts/) retain each artistic statement.

| Treatment | Statement | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A · Pencil outline | Sparse graphite schematic, open interiors, a lightly drawn fixed elevation. | **Clear user winner.** Remove printed names, separate the pair slightly and fit each receiving rectangle to its drawing. |
| Dotted outline | Crisp separated dots define the apparatus without filled surfaces. | Explored at the user’s request; not selected. |
| Technical plate | More detailed graphite elevation with construction hatching and datum lines. | Retained alternative; not selected. |
| Built-in kitchen | Continuous architectural wall/worktop joins the proofing cabinet and oven. | Retained alternative; not selected. |
| Cookbook diagram | Muted blue-green panels and white-line sections suggest printed method diagrams. | Retained alternative; not selected. |

The [old coloured atlas](../cookbook/production/artwork/equipment-hotspots.png) preserves the preceding equipment style. It is no longer an input to production. Names now come exclusively from game feedback. Single stations are centred at the top; pairs are separated. The proofing shelf’s rectangle has roughly 7px padding, the oven 5px, and most other apparatus 7px. No chapter needs three stations.

The [production collection](production/README.md) retains the completed rollout. Selection and user feedback establish this direction; native-device comfort and fresh human recognition remain open in the [backlog](../../backlog.md#icon-recognition-awaiting-human-trials).
