# Kitchen cookbook adaptation

This manuscript turns Kitchen into the development of a cookbook at a kitchen table. The working title is **Cookbook**. Each session asks what a technique does, where a preparation can lead, or how earlier preparations become a dish worth eating. The closing supper brings those connections together before the collection is committed to the book.

The current draft is maintained in [stories/cookbook/cookbook.md](../../../../../stories/cookbook/cookbook.md). The [original travelling manuscript](../baseline/kitchen-caravan.md) preserves the former premise. This document records editorial choices; it is not a standing authoring guide.

## Editorial approach

The narrator is an original, companionable cook: observant about texture, direct about technique, interested in eating the result. First-person observations occasionally give the page a personal appetite; “we” invites the player into the work. The named writers were points of reference for the setting and values, not voices to reproduce. No passage is written as, attributed to, or quoted from them.

The sequence now develops through seven connected groups:

1. Breakfast, dairy, bread, cultured preparations and sharpness.
2. Broth, roasting, salads and the contrast between soft and crisp.
3. Pasta, pastry, sauces and ordinary savoury meals.
4. Fruit, almonds, caramel and the distinction between pouring custard and a filling.
5. Choux, whipped cream, sponge, chocolate and useful finishes.
6. Quick breads, frozen desserts and egg-white preparations.
7. The limits of preservation, a complete supper and a book worth sharing.

The chapter order already supports these relationships, so it remains intact. The narrative structure changes from a sequence of people needing service at different destinations to a cook following related questions through the notebook. There are no invented journaling combinations or administrative goals. Cooking remains the action.

Most goals now use concrete cooking verbs—bake, press, churn, fill, dress, finish—while retaining the exact target dishes and alternatives. Introductory text connects those actions to a reader’s future use of the book. It does not require players to compare tastes, record results, choose a recipe for publication or perform any other action the game cannot represent.

The pantry and saved preparations belong to many sessions under the cooks’ care, with appropriate storage and usable portions. The fiction does not imply that one batch of milk, cream or custard sits out through the entire manuscript. Specialist visits explain the cider, chocolate and commercial preserving equipment without making the narrator travel.

The Service hatch is the kitchen’s opening to the tasting table. Supper trolley carries the meal, chosen cake and juice; the sealed stew tin accompanies the discussion of preserving. Its five slots are preserved, including the separate alternative cake slots. The tin is an example of specialist work, not an unexplained second serving of supper or a claim that the game teaches safe canning.

The Evelyn quotation remains verbatim in its existing item reading, with its historical-health qualification and attribution. The chapter introduces it as a copied passage from an old salad book in the margin. The sugar reading and its geographically and historically bounded account of enslavement also remain intact.

## Research used

Sources were consulted on 1 November 2026 for framing and editorial values. No new recipes were derived from them.

- [Molly Wizenberg, About Me, Orangette](https://orangette.net/about/): the author describes a home-cooking background and the connection between food and writing. This supports the domestic, personal notebook premise; it is not a prose template.
- [Smithsonian, Julia Child’s Kitchen](https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/exhibitions/food/online/julia-childs-kitchen): the museum presents the home kitchen as a place for cooking, tools, learning and company. [Its account of collecting the kitchen](https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/stories/behind-scenes-twenty-years-julia-childs-kitchen) identifies recipe testing alongside cooking for friends and family. The adaptation takes that combination of serious work and hospitality as a useful setting.
- [Penguin Random House, Marcella’s Italian Kitchen](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/77518/marcellas-italian-kitchen-by-marcella-hazan-foreword-by-molly-baz/): the publisher describes precise home cooking and regional recipes. This is a different title from the user’s *Marcella Cucina*, so it supplies only broad context; the contents of *Marcella Cucina* were not available for direct examination in this pass.
- The user-provided `/Users/ljw/code/health/flavor-bible.md` introduction and the `/Users/ljw/code/health/flavor-dictionary.md` index were consulted for their broad starting points: appetite, ingredient, technique and flavour relationships. They support attention to variation and contrast. Their text was not copied into the manuscript, and the existing ingredient catalogue was retained for this first full visual conversion.

The Julia Child Foundation site could not be accessed during this pass. Smithsonian material provided the directly available institutional source instead. The draft makes no claim to reproduce any named writer’s recipes or complete method.

## Outcome and limits

The adaptation preserved the chapter order, 230 item identities, recipes, goal conditions, five trolley slots and existing item readings. It changed the title, introductions and goal wording to express cookbook development. Semantic checks passed with the existing warnings.
