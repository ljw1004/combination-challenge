## Comparisons across players

### Testing whether the cord travels with the wave

All three directly combine the **paper rider with the travelling cord** at their respective local step 1, explicitly to investigate Jo’s question. Each subsequently reports satisfaction with the distinction. Their agreement supports all three graders’ meaningful classifications: an identical, strongly suggested pairing can still be a purposeful observational test. It does not establish a choice among many strategic alternatives.

### Watching more waves versus preparing an instrument

After the rider, Player 1 attempts the **movable bridge on the rider-equipped cord** at local step 2, preferring a defined vibrating length to immediate plucking. Players 2 and 3 instead directly use the **tension-adjusting key on the rider-equipped cord** at their local step 2, expecting changed waves. This is a reasoned difference in immediate priority—instrument preparation versus further wave observation—not random guessing. It supports the grader’s sequencing credit for Player 1, without supplying the specific tension hypothesis absent from Players 2 and 3’s notes.

Player 1’s bridge rejection then changes the information situation. **Inspecting the rider-equipped cord** at local step 3 diagnoses available preparation and automatically produces the same two-loop construction reached directly by Players 2 and 3. Thus the different meaningfulness classifications concern different choices: diagnostic search for Player 1, generic tool selection for the other two. The shared resulting discovery is valued by all three, but that does not make the underlying decisions equivalent.

### Fitting the bridge: inference, routine assembly and caution

With two broad loops visible, Player 1 **inspects the cord and automatically fits the bridge** at local step 4; Players 2 and 3 **combine the bridge directly with the two-loop cord** at local step 3. Player 1 explicitly cites the earlier rejection as a reason to check availability, whereas the others have not encountered that rejection. This is not a fair test of an intrinsic preference for inspection versus direct combination: prior feedback differs.

The graders preserve a narrow distinction between Player 2’s non-meaningful “next part” selection and Player 3’s meaningful inference about defining vibrating length. Their expectations overlap substantially, so the comparison exposes a borderline judgment rather than a robust difference in puzzle depth. Both classifications remain unchanged. All three report interest in the bridge’s immediate explanation, independently supporting story/result value even where the decision itself receives no credit.

### Plucking now versus fitting soft feet first

Once the bridge is installed, Player 2 **tries the plectrum** at local step 4 while explicitly considering pads first. Player 3 **fits the pads** at local step 4 while considering immediate plucking, tentatively predicting support or isolation. This is a genuine sequencing divergence between plausible approaches, but not proof that both are meaningful: Player 2’s grader credits a borderline readiness test; Player 3’s grader finds the pads prediction too general. Player 3 avoids the rejection but still identifies fitting the feet as least engaging.

Player 1 also **tries the plectrum on the bridged cord**, at local step 5, but had already articulated the playing plan before the failed bridge attempt. Its grader therefore classifies this as routine execution rather than a new experiment. The difference from Player 2’s positive classification is retained, not standardized merely because the commands and results match.

Following rejection, Players 1 and 2 **fit the pads** at local steps 6 and 5 respectively by elimination. Both find their purpose clearer, but Player 2 explicitly calls that explanation useful; Player 1 mainly acknowledges clarification, and Player 3 remains negative about engagement. Finally, all three **pluck the machine on soft feet**—Player 1 local step 7, Player 2 local step 6, Player 3 local step 5—and receive the automatic length-and-pitch comparison.
