# Mars v2 design brief

This brief belongs to the preserved, unaccepted v2 experiment. Use the parent [story-design guidance](../story-design.md) for the user’s current requirements and open questions.

**Mars: What the Ice Kept** is a compact science-fiction expedition about making a place to work, noticing something extraordinary and bringing the actual samples back. The [game's philosophy](../../../README.md#philosophy) guides its combination of useful understanding, construction and discovery. The manuscript owns the playable events; [UX](../../../docs/ux.md) owns interaction, and the [icon guide](../../../docs/icon-guide.md) owns artwork production.

## Narrative and emotional intent

The player is a solo surface surveyor with a functioning field station, one rover and an expedition ship in orbit. A survey of exposed ice leads to the preserved body of a small, long-dead, bug-like animal. It never revives or becomes a companion. The discovery is the central fictional premise, not an ambiguous reward withheld at the ending.

Keep two connected threads: investigating the ice and growing a small crop during the field season. Establish the sampling question before the discovery. Collect contrasting material on one field trip, compare it under shared light, then let microscopy resolve the unfamiliar outline. The original purpose of the survey makes the discovery possible. The nearby clearer ice remains useful comparison material without becoming a certificate of the animal's age or origin.

The opening's lettuce pays off in an ordinary meal after explicit weeks of growth. The protagonist communicates with the ship in prose and returns on the planned flight. Shared observation closes the solitude of the opening. Use specific perception and small reactions rather than explanatory speeches. Introductions establish a situation and purpose; the player performs the operation, and its result supplies the new observation.

Keep the geography legible: station, one accessible ice exposure, station again. The orbital handoff is an ending, not a spacecraft assembly puzzle. Rescue crews, photography, report objects, extra vehicles and repeated shelter repairs do not belong in this arc. A new accident would need a causal and experiential purpose of its own; it must not exist merely to lengthen the game.

## Compositions and continuity

Each combination must perform recognizable work with what its inputs supply. Favor complete tools and coarse physical operations: sowing and watering, fitting a sample barrel, extracting an intact core, arranging two samples, illuminating them, examining an inclusion, harvesting food and packing the actual samples. Retain useful tools across work. Keep primitive arrivals to coherent preparations; an item declaration should not become a new supply merely because a later chapter mentions it.

The two cores remain two physical objects through paired and observed states. Pairing represents supported side-by-side arrangement, not fusion. Illumination changes what can be seen, not what the ice contains. Microscopy examines the band-bearing member of the pair; its neighbor remains intact. Both finally occupy the carrier. Do not replace specimens with pictures, reports, success tokens or a list of instrument readings.

The corer includes power, controls, seals and supplied liners. The bench and microscope provide cold working environments. The galley already contains the prepared ration. Picking and rinsing a handful of outer leaves can be one ordinary action; the growing center remains. These premises should be clear without turning seals, switches, utensils and individual gestures into extra puzzles.

A small, understandable working set matters more than a recipe quota. Keep comfortable progress, useful construction and wonder; do not manufacture resource dilemmas or add biological finds just to rescue a weak chapter. Use the [evaluation process](../../../docs/fun-and-evaluation.md) to test whether the physical work earns attention, distinguishing fresh-player evidence from source-aware review.

## Scientific grounding and fictional boundaries

Exposed, layered Martian water ice provides a real starting point for the setting. The particular accessible face, its contents, the future expedition and its equipment are fictional. A clear-ice sample and a dust-bearing sample can support a visible comparison; two samples cannot establish a whole region's distribution or history. [USGS: exposed subsurface ice sheets](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/exposed-subsurface-ice-sheets-martian-mid-latitudes).

Cold storage and preserving surrounding material are grounded practices. The transparent liners and viewing windows are stated capabilities of this fictional mission, not a claim that every natural ice core can be examined intact with equal optical clarity. Keep the ice frozen and the body embedded. The story's recognizable anatomy establishes its fictional discovery; it does not teach that morphology alone resolves native origin, contamination, absolute age, chemistry or a past ecosystem. [USGS: physical sample preservation](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/national-geological-and-geophysical-data-preservation-program/physical-sample-preservation), [AWI: ice-core laboratories](https://www.awi.de/en/science/geosciences/glaciology/tools/ice-core-laboratories.html).

Conditioned blocks buffer short transfers. Active refrigeration serves the rover case, station apparatus, isolated flight sample bay and receiving laboratory. Insulation does not create cold. Complete containment and flight hardware are supplied mission infrastructure; this game does not design or certify them. Food preparation remains separate from the sealed scientific samples. Planetary-protection concerns inform that separation without becoming a sequence of approvals or paperwork objects. [JPL: planetary-protection research](https://planetaryprotection.jpl.nasa.gov/research).

Lettuce grows in prepared Earth-supplied medium with nutrients, light, water, air and a pressurized cabin. Allow several weeks before picking outer leaves and leaving the center growing. The tray adds fresh food to supplied meals; it does not replace life support or feed an expedition by itself. NASA's Veggie work supports supplied growing media and repeated harvests, with reported grow-outs around a month; it does not validate this particular fictional Mars installation. [NASA: growing plants in space](https://www.nasa.gov/exploration-research-and-technology/growing-plants-in-space/), [NASA: sustained crop production](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20205009642).

## Artwork direction

The current story deliberately uses text items and existing backgrounds while the narrative and compositions are evaluated. The earlier [Mars artwork study](<../README.md>) preserves the complete collection for the previous manuscript. Its equipment and scene catalogue is historical, not a requirement to restore those story beats.

For future artwork, retain the selected restrained industrial character: cream housings, exposed metal, rubber and modest handling wear, with clear structures and silhouettes. Use the preferred [multiple-accent reference](<../inspiration/02-multiple-accents.png>). Cobalt blue identifies science equipment, petrol teal station equipment and muted mustard mobility; natural rock, dust, ice and plants retain their material colors. These are visual families, not gameplay laws.

Use physical proportions with capped extremes. A small object's longest visible edge should normally be at least 60px at the 390 × 844 reference viewport; long barrels remain narrow, trays remain broad, and a carrier feels substantially larger than a seed packet. Preserve aspect ratios and crop transparent margins. Export at four times the display dimensions. The one rover can remain substantial in the scenery; the story does not require draggable buildings or spacecraft.

Preserve identity through growth, pairing and observation. The same tray gains leaves; the two lined cores remain distinguishable by their contents, then sit together in the carrier. The tiny animal's detail belongs in the discovery reading or a deliberate magnified view, not an implausibly enormous standalone creature tile.

Integrate fixed sampling locations, the growing shelf, cold bench, microscope and galley into their landscapes and rooms following Settlement's approach. Leave sufficient quiet receiving space for the actual objects. Return to recognizable views of the same station and exposure; do not paint movable future results into a scene before the player creates them.
