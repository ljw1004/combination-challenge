### Construction a lone astronaut can perform

Review every locally made object against its actual material inputs, available tools and the work of one person. Local construction should look cut, folded, drilled, bolted, clamped or wrapped from the specified stock: straight lengths, flat plates, modest sheet bends, accessible fasteners, mesh, cloth and insulation. Show workable joints and supports. Prefer several manageable parts assembled in place over one massive piece that the astronaut could not lift or form. A broad bed structure may be substantial without requiring a single elaborate fabricated chassis.

Reserve factory precision for the imported machinery and qualified boundaries. A locally made berth, bed frame, hopper, cover or protective case should not acquire seamless molded corners, deep-drawn shells, complex machined fittings or an automotive finish. Keep the original manufactured component distinct from the simple support or enclosure added around it. The press and furnace permit only their established forming and material operations; their presence does not supply every industrial manufacturing process.

Check the intended picture before rendering, then inspect the actual result: could this assembly be made with these supplies and tools, in this place, by this astronaut? Simplify its depicted shape when the answer is doubtful, preserving the existing combinations and physical function. Art continuity includes the construction method as well as the object's identity. Retain a short causal explanation when an operation uses established supplies that its visible ingredients or final picture cannot account for.

## Dramatic promise and order

A solo astronaut lands badly beside a delivered starter habitat. The same arrival cabin can return to orbit, but its grounded lower support needs repair. The player restores shelter and water, constructs the cavern farm, plants potatoes, soybeans and lettuce, tastes the first fresh leaves, and tends the developing staple crops in one continuous farm passage. **The substantial farm must be established before the discovery.** A later change to an immediate-return mission would remove the motive to start that construction. The discovery need not occur in the first quarter; a midpoint discovery is acceptable if the preceding making earns its space.

Brown material in the raw-water intake reveals only tantalizing patterned fragments. The melted, heated, pumped and filtered sample cannot show whether they belonged to a living thing. Earth requests untouched ice and redirects the mission toward returning it. The local core preserves a reference to the water source; the farther exposed face permits choosing interior ice within a visible layer, away from station disturbance. The remote expedition immediately follows the reference core. Its second core reveals a complete body in its surroundings before the outdoor machinery and local materials become a fuel installation. A storm tests the established farm. The astronaut then completes the known support repair and loads fuel. The actual specimens remain secure in laboratory cooling through the celebratory meal, then board immediately before departure.

**The celebration meal is two chapters, after engineering and fueling:** build a cooker using the furnace's separate clean heating connection; then harvest, prepare and serve **potato and soybean stew with fresh lettuce**. A departure hold for rendezvous alignment gives the prepared astronaut a reason to pause. It is a logical mission condition, not a countdown or calendar. After the meal, the astronaut secures the actual membrane and each core in its assigned place inside the cabin. From the pilot’s seat in that same cabin, a deliberately simple final operation uses a carried **Arming plug** at the **Flight console**. The player chooses the complete launch procedure: fit the plug, confirm ARMED, then deliberately press the existing START control. Plug insertion alone does not start the engine or power the already operating cabin. Removable spacecraft arming plugs have real precedent, but this crew-accessible Mars-ascent enable socket is an explicit fictional adaptation. It is not an actual NASA ignition key. Ascent is the observed result; rendezvous and handoff follow aboard the orbiter, still in space. There is no flight meal box, station winterizing, seed storage, cleanup or preparation for a future occupant.

Water connects survival, cultivation, discovery and fuel. The same crops and spacecraft make the storm and departure consequential. An outgoing observation and a delayed reply can provide warmth without radio tokens or a passive companion. Mara's recorded messages convey purpose and human reactions, not a list of recipes. The first emergency is handled locally; Earth never directs a seconds-long rescue.

## Time and gameplay scope

The player advances through completed operations and changed physical conditions. Do not impose calendar markers, crop-growth waits, fuel-production durations or resource accounting. Real growth, soaking, cooking and production are implicit in their complete operations. Results show the readiness that matters—dry pods, soaked beans, tender food, collected propellant—without requiring the player to imagine a schedule. The only explicit departure hold is the user-requested rationale for the celebratory meal; no waiting action is authored.

Approximately 200 worthwhile combinations is a length hypothesis, not a quota. A stronger short operation should remain short. User direction sets a **maximum of 50 primitive item identities**. Stocks and genuine integrated assemblies can reduce incidental supplies, but must not become bags of unrelated hidden capabilities. The current inventory is audited separately; the count describes game identities, not launch mass or a complete engineering bill of materials.

Imported precision equipment remains imported. Ordinary supports, covers, trays, handles, housings, simple heat exchangers and workshop tools should use the established material stocks where physically sensible. Do not add a special Earth item solely to make one recipe possible. Do not invent a named former or mold for every product.

## Mission and farm premises

The mission establishes a partly self-supplying research station for later expeditions. It deliberately allocates payload to reusable cultivation and manufacturing equipment rather than every planned meal. This is a mission choice, not a claim that a greenhouse necessarily saves mass during its first programme. Stored food bridges commissioning and interruptions and supplements the crops. The thirty-square-metre growing room supplies a substantial share of food under the stated optimized conditions, not a complete diet by itself.

Precursor robots surveyed an accessible cavern near suitable shallow layered ice. That particular favorable site is fictional. The excavator clears loose infill and grades ground; it does not tunnel through hard basalt. The cave provides shielding, not breathable air. Its imported deployable pressure enclosure is one real assembly with attached airlock and service spine containing air handling and regulated heating. A dedicated bank of breathing-gas cylinders fills the habitat and farm without transferring air out of the small cabin. The player constructs the floor, padding and lighting, then makes a sheltered berth using the existing cabin mattress before fitting the growing beds. The mattress and under-deck insulation can be fitted in either order; both routes use the same steel frame and end at the same complete berth. The enclosure’s heater already warms that dry alcove; sleeping does not depend on later process-heat recovery. Bed structures, liners, drains, aerated reservoir and root medium follow.

The three crops share the environment, lighting, nutrient system and tested porous medium. Chosen compatible cultivars are an explicit horticultural premise. Ordinary mineral nutrient provision, including soybean nitrogen, is assumed as part of the delivered growing system. It needs no separate item or mixing action; watering the unwatered beds starts the shared loop. The potato root zone is deep and dark beneath illuminated foliage. Soybean stems receive support. Lettuce occupies a shallow tray with a clean fabric wick. Their different tasks sit on one common preparation sequence.

The initial pressure fill uses stored breathing gas. Once power returns, one atmospheric processor replenishes oxygen through carbon-dioxide capture/compression followed by an oxygen-making electrochemical stage. A separately controlled takeoff upstream of the oxygen cell later supplies carbon dioxide to the fuel plant; its gas never mixes with the carbon-monoxide exhaust. Selective capture, scalable capacity and compatible takeoff are explicit future-machine assumptions. The oxygen branch keeps operating during ordinary industrial use. During the storm the hot processor shuts down; stored breathing gas and the running scrubber bridge the interruption. This integrates two uses of the same machine rather than adding a second atmospheric unit.

The initial array is sized for the farm and baseline station. Factory expansion modules later support industry. The delivered reserve is specified for essential air handling, reduced electric heat, laboratory refrigeration and reduced farm operation through the forecast regional storm. It does not maintain full production. The fixed crop beds remain in the cavern. A locally made screen and reservoir cover reduce losses while the established pump changes mode. The water buffer supplements heating; it neither makes energy nor reaches cooking temperatures. Sample cooling is already essential and never becomes an emergency substory.

The original fuel cargo cannot support the revised return plan. The modular materials/life-support equipment provides a reviewed local-production contingency, not a finished fuel plant. The available orbital vehicle and approved trajectory are explicit future logistics. Producing surface ascent propellant does not remove orbital-transfer constraints.

## Current material and equipment boundaries

Steel means known low-carbon steel sheet and lengths from expendable shipping braces. Their grade and form do not silently change between supports, a magnetic core, thermal panels and the specified grounded brace. The tool kit contains ordinary cutting, drilling, fastening and metal-forming tools, including a drill/driver, metal cutter, spanners and tools for sheet and tubing. It is a collection of compatible workshop tools, not one universal powered device. Clean cloth, foil, felt, mesh, hose, electrical conductor and liner sheet are reusable material stocks, not unique objects cloned between recipes. Different fresh pieces are used for food and industrial work.

The furnace's imported core incorporates its receiver, tracking base and temperature controller. Mirror petals remain imported optical surfaces. The press forms supported powder blanks; the furnace then sinters them into non-pressure ceramic. A made electromagnet separates the assayed magnetic fraction of the regolith. The imported retort reduces that concentrate under controlled heat and hydrogen, leaving porous iron; the heated press consolidates and shapes it. The compact machinery, exact feedstock and useful material properties are declared future/site assumptions. Ordinary red dust is not pure iron, a catalyst or ready growing soil.

The electrolysis core includes dedicated outlet separation and drying; its rack has separate dedicated hydrogen and oxygen receivers. Its ordinary controls stop when a buffer is full. Gas cylinders are compatible, oxygen-clean factory containers, each assigned its own gas and keyed connection. The hydrogen outlet feeds the retort; the oxygen outlet later feeds its distinct cryogenic store. Other vessels collect captured carbon dioxide and the contained commissioning batch. No unique bottle is used simultaneously for different gases.

The retort's mineral liner is exchanged for a clean compatible Sabatier cartridge. Standardized factory valve/control blocks serve the distinct installations. A test precedes the completed purification and return circuit. The actual reactor joins the supplied condenser/separator/dryer/recycle core; its cooled water return then joins treatment. That complete assembly—not an emptied test bottle alone—supplies purified methane. Ordinary mounting, spare control blocks and the separate test cylinder are included with the real installation; no unique vessel is duplicated. The hydrogen connection seats the retort at the already constructed furnace, and the water-return connection uses the established treatment port directly.

Qualified coolant tubing and locally formed metal panels provide the designed heat-rejection surface. The supplied equipment design includes adequate thermal contact, radiating surface and area for the Mars setting; this does not rely on strong atmospheric convection. The player makes a vented tank from the existing steel sheets and fits a separate tubing coil inside it. Insulation and water complete the heat store in the pressurized farm service alcove. Sealed coolant lines pass through the enclosure’s service spine; the tank contains non-potable low-temperature water at room pressure. The room-facing insulation can fold back after the outdoor coil valves close, releasing stored warmth directly from the tank wall into the room. It is a finite supplement to electric heating, with no unexplained room-heating loop. No dedicated imported drum is needed.

Two qualified cryogenic vessels and two factory refrigerator units form a prepared equipment batch. Separate members are assigned to methane and oxygen. Each has its own pressure/vacuum boundary, operating connections, cooling, vent and transfer route; common controls do not mix propellants. Local material supplies outer shells and insulation only. Cryogenic capacity, compatibility, throughput and the overall energy balance are declared future-system assumptions, not a validated present-day mission design.

## Continuity and departure

The lander's grounded lower support is diagnosed and stabilized at the opening. The emergency rack is accessible from the ground, but the main stores remain aboard; temporary cribbing prevents further settling before repeated boarding and heavy unloading. Its upper cabin, tanks, avionics and engine mounts survive. The made braced leg and footing restore the lower support; ordinary lifting, cribbing and load transfer are bundled with installation using the spacecraft’s service equipment. No separate jack primitive, engine or pressure-tank fabrication is needed. Known ceramic blocks and steel form the footing and brace together after the ground is prepared. The actual foot, brace and lower stage stay on Mars when the original upper crew cabin departs.

The original membrane remains sealed in its holder. The source core stays in its liner and freezer cradle. The remote core stays in its liner/case through the optical examination; only the external optical face is prepared. The field refrigeration insert includes its qualified transfer buffer. Both actual core liners enter separate installed refrigerated cradles inside the ascent cabin. Qualified transfer containment bridges the move from laboratory cooling and keeps the ice cold and sealed until flight refrigeration takes over. The player places the water-source core in the upper cradle and the remote band core in the lower one; either can be loaded first. The original membrane transforms into its flight-secured state in its own locker. The membrane and either core can be secured in any order; each result represents only its own specimen. Food and scientific containment remain separate. The body is exceptional fictional evidence, not a demonstrated age, terrestrial taxon, DNA sequence or native origin.

Recipe order must respect physical prerequisites. Hopper and power can be fitted to the excavator in either order; a startup test precedes digging. Drains and reservoir can be fitted in either order, as can berth padding and insulation. Heat-tank filling and insulation also permit either order after the coil is fitted. Independent methane and oxygen connections remain separate in both the storage bank and spacecraft. No fixed machine is dragged onto another fixed machine.

The station array stays outdoors throughout deployment, cleaning and reconnection. A portable service key operates the three installed storm selectors; the panel never contains the actual machines. Restart visibly reconnects the cleaned outdoor array and performs the ordinary warming, checking and priming sequence. The original industrial array has its own left-hand footing; the separately prepared right-hand pad is for process equipment. A drop must build at its actual receiving place.

The press, furnace and process equipment stand on individual supports on Martian soil. There is no finished workshop building, tent, roof, storage shelving or room that appears without being constructed. The supplied outdoor-rated machinery retains its factory mechanisms; the player constructs the modest supports and connections.

The crop beds remain in the same farm positions through tending and harvest. Their mature harvest-ready states become available in chapter 33; this gates fresh harvesting without making seedlings harvestable earlier. Supper lettuce is a new harvest, separate from the first meal in chapter 11.

Steel and Tool kit are compact representational icons for material stock and available tools. The tool symbol does not imply that only the two pictured tools exist. Structural equipment remains literal and fixed at its scene position; the symbols do not license dragging entire installations.
