# Mars v12: minimal-payload engineering proposal

Fresh consultant proposal, 4 November 2026, for the user's requested revision. Read the current manuscript, design brief, project instructions and supplied request; did not consult earlier reviews or outcomes. This proposes an engineering and visual direction, not acceptance of an implementation or certification of a mission. The inline lander reference was described by the production director: the contact is beneath the low chassis crossmember immediately right of the ladder, between the feet.

**Priority decisions.** Make local steel immediately before the fuel-equipment construction. Let the astronaut build visible frames, collars and protective casings around supplied precision apparatus. Keep the cryogenic stores in one geographical location throughout production and fueling. Change the opening rock to an unloaded catch and replace it with a short pedestal at exactly the same crossmember contact, without moving the lander. Use a small three-selector wall plate and one lightweight cooking pot.

## What can actually be made locally

Ordinary steel can make useful structures; merely calling it Steel does not establish a hydrogen receiver, oxygen-clean pressure vessel or cryogenic tank. Hydrogen can impair metal properties, and vessel design and testing must address its service conditions. Cryogenic material selection must address low-temperature toughness, seals and joining; NASA also treats oxygen cleanliness as a separate ignition concern. These are reasons to import the difficult boundaries while making the surrounding installation locally. [DOE hydrogen handling](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/fuels/hydrogen-safe), [NASA cryogenic engineering course, material selection](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20150000199/downloads/20150000199.pdf), [NASA oxygen-service cleaning](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/white-sands/oxygen-service-cleaning/).

| Part | Recommended origin and visible treatment |
| --- | --- |
| H₂ and O₂ buffer receivers at the electrolyzer | Small supplied, separately rated receivers. They buffer flow; they are not the entire ascent supply. |
| Sabatier reaction and separation hardware | Supplied compact core with actual reactor, thermal control, separation, drying, recycle and compatible connections. Remove the big decorative cabinet. |
| Reactor stand, cold-store cradles and collars | Local steel: short open sections, narrow straps, obvious simple joints, generous empty space. Ceramic blocks provide low broad foundations. |
| Cylindrical outer covers | Local steel may form vented protective covers. Show a seam/open underside and exposed service neck, making their non-pressure role legible. |
| Methane and oxygen cold inserts | Supplied self-supporting qualified vessels, including the insulation, thermal isolation and any required vacuum boundary. A bare liner which depends on the homemade casing for pressure strength is insufficient. Supplied refrigeration, vents and controls remain real apparatus. |
| Open heat-buffer tank | A local, room-pressure water vessel remains reasonable under the story's forming/joining capability. It is separate from the cryogenic stores and cannot cook food. |

NASA has demonstrated compact Sabatier reactor development, but that does not miniaturize the entire propellant plant by itself. Its Mars development work explicitly includes heat/flow management and post-reaction gas separation. [Compact reactor research](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20120016419/downloads/20120016419.pdf), [NASA methane-production project](https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/93847).

**Visually compact cold stores are an explicit future premise.** Draw comparatively slim, unpainted cylinders with restrained foil insulation, narrow local collars and small identifiable service fittings. Do not promise that two hand-carried bottles contain all the ascent propellant. Capacity, production duration, heat rejection, power and return trajectory have not been sized. “Qualified inner flasks” must mean the complete cold inserts described above. Their locally fabricated protective shell is neither the gas boundary nor an improvised vacuum vessel.

An even leaner architecture fills the lander's own tanks directly. NASA has studied both storage in vehicle tanks and separate surface stores. That alternative removes duplicated tanks but changes the requested two-store gameplay, so retain stationary stores for this revision. [NASA surface liquefaction architecture study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180002101).

## A concrete construction and continuity plan

Move **Metal from the red ground** to immediately before the fuel installation. Retain electrolysis beforehand and its clear future purposes. The steel chapter still distinguishes reduction from refining: hydrogen removes oxygen from the mineral as water, leaving sponge iron which needs further processing. Real hydrogen steelmaking uses distinct reduction and melting stages; the story's single compact solar furnace doing both remains a declared future capability. [HYBRIT's demonstrated process](https://www.hybritdevelopment.se/en/fossil-free-steel-production-ready-for-industrialisation/).

Proposed physical operations, to adapt to the manuscript's existing combination syntax:

1. **Steel → ceramic equipment footing: open process frame.** The frame has short upright sections and a small equipment tray; it is visibly made by the astronaut.
2. **Supplied process core → frame: mounted fuel apparatus.** The imported core becomes part of a locally built rig rather than a whole painted factory module appearing finished.
3. **Steel → methane cold insert: protected methane store**, and **Steel → oxygen cold insert: protected oxygen store**. Each complete operation forms and fits its own vented outer jacket, retaining collar and low cradle, then places it on its prepared ceramic support. The cold insert itself is unmodified. If fewer actions are desirable, construct both cradles in the frame operation and retain one cover operation for each store.
4. Retain catalyst installation and the two genuinely separate gas connections. These commission real equipment; a catalyst does not supply the missing reactor.

The delivered materials kit must explicitly support modest cutting, forming, joining and inspection. Near-net short cast sections and ordinary finishing fit the existing premise better than a large rolling mill. Thin locally made cover sheet additionally needs a stated sheet-forming capability: do not pretend a casting crucible alone yields thin finished sheet. This can be bundled equipment, without new tool tokens. The astronaut's steelwork can look plain and economical without depicting poor joints, leaking equipment or dirty oxygen fittings.

Establish one site layout now: methane store left foreground, oxygen store right foreground, small process core offset between/behind them, lander farther back with both service ports readable. In later fueling, move the camera closer or change its angle, not the tanks. Retain distinctive collars, ceramic patterns, service necks and ground landmarks. Only dedicated transfer lines extend toward the lander. Local covers, blocks and store positions do not move when a line is connected. The full stores remain stationary; empty-component handling does not establish filled-store portability.

## Make the two gases' jobs explicit

Suggested short result text at electrolysis:

> Water gives me two separate gases. Hydrogen will free iron from the minerals, then help make methane. The oxygen will supply the return engine.

Suggested text at the fuel apparatus:

> Hydrogen from the electrolyzer joins carbon dioxide captured from the air. The reactor makes methane and water. Oxygen comes through its own line from the electrolyzer; it never enters this reaction.

The visible paths should agree with the words:

| Source | Destination |
| --- | --- |
| Treated well water + electricity | Electrolyzer |
| Electrolyzer H₂ | Furnace reduction service; later methane processor |
| Captured atmospheric CO₂ | Methane processor |
| Processor methane, after separation and drying | Methane liquefaction/store → methane lander port |
| Electrolyzer O₂ | Separate oxygen liquefaction/store → oxygen lander port |
| Recovered reaction water | Water treatment and reuse |

Use the same H₂, O₂ and CH₄ labels at the sources, lines and receiving equipment; distinguish them by shape/label as well as color. The early small receivers remain flow buffers when continuous production starts. Avoid “hydrogen from the well,” which hides the electrolysis step. Water recovery reduces new water demand; it does not create a water-closed propellant supply. NASA's reaction and system descriptions support these separate processes. [Sabatier research](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20120016419/downloads/20120016419.pdf), [NASA methane-production project](https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/93847).

## Rock, crossmember and permanent pedestal

**Opening:** the existing legs still carry the spacecraft. Roll the selected stone into the already available gap beneath the specified crossmember. No jacking, levering, lifting, leveling or controlled load transfer occurs. Keep the cabin, feet and crossmember identical between images. The stone is a close-fitting catch against further settlement, not proof that arbitrary leg failure can be arrested safely. Suggested result: “The stone fits the gap beneath the frame. If that corner settles, it cannot fall far.” Prefer **Catch beneath the frame** over **Rock-supported lander**.

**Later:** retain that contact location. Place a low broad ceramic footing beside the unloaded rock, under the same crossmember region. Fit a compact steel pedestal to the available gap and remove the still-unloaded stone as part of that operation. The drawing should show a shallow top saddle immediately beneath the sound member, a very short hollow or formed-channel compression section, and a modest load-spreading footplate lying flat on the ceramic. A small gusset or retaining detail may be present where the repair design needs it. There are no ground bolts, tall legs, heavy chassis or foot repair. The ceramic footprint and background remain registered between states.

Load-path drawing brief: **sound lower crossmember → saddle → short compression section → footplate → broad ceramic footing → firm ground**. The original feet remain in place. The pedestal stays with the lower stage. Do not change the lander's tilt, raise it, or describe it as level again. **Ground support ready** is a better result name than **Level launch platform**.

This is a proposed geometry, not a calculated “lightest safe support.” Real blocking depends on support placement, ground bearing and stability; steel compression members also require buckling checks. [OSHA explanation of blocking and load distribution](https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/laws-regs/federalregister/2010-08-09.pdf), [AISC research on compression-member buckling](https://ej.aisc.org/index.php/engj/article/view/527).

Necessary mission premises: the original legs remain stable; the rock never becomes trapped under load; the specified crossmember is a suitable bearing point; the supplied repair design covers material properties, local bearing, ceramic strength, soil support, buckling, lateral stability, thermal conditions, fueling mass and launch/separation effects. It uses accessible assembly without anyone moving a loaded stone. No invented load, wall thickness, capacity or certification is warranted. A large-gap impact catch would require additional dynamic analysis; show the intended close fit.

## Small controls and ordinary cooking

**Storm selectors:** use one thin wall plate with three small supplied key-operated selectors and a shallow enclosed electronics backing, mounted to the existing service spine. It commands the installed power, well and fuel controllers; it does not contain those machines or switch high power through a homemade exposed circuit. Clear icons: lightning/battery, droplet with well pipe, and paired CH₄/O₂ vessels. Put the service key on the nearby floor with a short bright lanyard. A close camera makes three targets usable without making the physical box enormous. The local plate may be steel; the protected electrical internals stay supplied.

**Supper:** show one thin-walled lidded camping pot with folding handles, on a small supplied electric hotplate over a ceramic rest. This replaces the heavy stockpot and needs no cupboard, range, gas canister or cooker-building chapter. Lightweight one-person pots are ordinary hardware: MSR's 900 ml titanium example is listed at 126 g, with folding handles, and doubles as an eating vessel. That is a visual size/mass reference, not a claim that this exact model or portion serves the mission. [Manufacturer's pot specification](https://cascadedesigns.com/en-eu/products/titan-kettle-900ml).

Use the requested order: **soybeans at sink → washed/soaked soybeans; prepared soybeans + potatoes → cooked stew in the small pot; cooked stew + lettuce → finished supper**. The first action includes soaking and draining of the current manuscript's dry mature beans. Cooking is bundled in the second action: beans cook first, potatoes enter later, and both finish thoroughly; lettuce is washed and stays fresh alongside. Dry soybeans take substantial cooking, so washing or warm soaking alone must not be shown as sufficient. [University of Alaska Fairbanks preparation guidance](https://www.uaf.edu/ces/publications/database/food/beans-peas-and-lentils.php).

Keep the pot, handles and lid recognizable between raw preparation and cooked result; a small glimpse of soft potato and plump beans demonstrates cooking better than a huge steam cloud. The enclosed habitat has cooking-compatible pressure, water and electrical service. The thin heater's controllability and the meal's energy allocation are supplied capabilities, not demonstrated minimum-mass optimization. Do not borrow low-temperature heat from the open buffer as the cooking source.

**Evidence boundary:** the chemistry, separate gas paths, materials-service concerns, ordinary cooking and general support principles are established. The proposed compact Mars manufacturing plant, cold-store capacity, installation tools and spacecraft support design are declared future/site capabilities. Whole ascent propellant in casually portable homemade steel bottles, pressure qualification by appearance, arbitrary local alloy suitability, safe launch merely because a thin strut looks plausible, and a proved minimum-payload mission remain unsupported.
