Inspected the v12 manuscript/brief, installed planting and storm-bed images, and garden construction, tending and wall-screen masters. Updated for the authorized reclaimed water cartridge and subsequent austerity decisions: reserve bed headroom, hill without a collar, and reuse light cargo netting with existing habitat attachment tabs. ## Visible ice and a small manual water operation

**Use a low, dusty ice exposure about fifteen metres from the starter habitat.** Pale grey-white massive ice shows below a red regolith cap; one distinctive rusty band and a neighbouring rock identify it in both Water and the later source-core scene. NASA/JPL has observed exposed layered midlatitude water ice. The published scarps are often large, steep and retreating: a convenient low, stable, non-overhanging bank beside a firm habitat foundation is our local fictional site, not an observed mission location or a universal safe setback. No bore, extraction head or hidden underground reservoir is needed. [JPL observed ice exposure](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia22077-underground-martian-ice-deposit-exposed-at-scarp/).

**Adopt the small manual operation:** use the reusable heater at the visible bank to collect and melt ice in a repurposed, latched galley pot; strain and treat the meltwater with fabric around a reclaimed drinking-water cartridge; carry the clean batch to the tap tank. The heater operation bundles ordinary chipping, loading the pot, closing its lid and controlled heating. The pot and its suitable pressure-retaining seal belong to this small supplied galley assembly, without becoming another player token. Show the heater against the closed pot, not melting an exposed puddle from the face. Reuse the heater and cleaned pot for the later stew.

The filtering action includes carrying the closed pot through the airlock before opening and pouring through the filter. The final tank action carries the clean covered batch the remaining distance to the tank and fills it. These routine movements do not require a separate chapter or carrying token. Ice harvesting and refills repeat over story time; one small pot does not fill the entire farm permanently.

Mars pressure is close to water's triple point and varies with altitude and season; some places exceed it. Therefore “water can never be liquid anywhere on Mars” is too strong. Nevertheless, exposed warm water loses vapour rapidly into the dry atmosphere, and can boil or freeze as conditions change. A protected vessel, or the habitat, gives the manual operation a reliable liquid environment. The need is containment and sufficient heat, not necessarily a huge high-pressure plant. [Primary experimental study of water stability under simulated Mars conditions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8588207/).

**Use a small reclaimed drinking-water cartridge from the lander's cabin services.** Fresh fabric wrapped over its inlet catches grit; the cartridge handles the remaining water treatment. An appropriate perchlorate-selective ion-exchange medium and a microbial treatment/barrier function can be included inside the single future cartridge. Its compatibility with this relatively clean ice deposit, treatment capacity, suitable flow and accepted output are explicit story premises authorized by the user. No large treatment unit, pump-building chain or additional treatment token is needed. The clean tank stores this output and dispenses it by tap.

Fabric itself does not remove dissolved perchlorate, and melting or cloth-straining is not sterilization. The real precedent is selective treatment inside the cartridge: EPA identifies ion exchange as a perchlorate-removal process, while CDC distinguishes cloth prefiltration from disinfection. These sources support the mechanisms; they do not qualify our fictional compact cartridge. Avoid describing a generic charcoal cartridge as a universal salt remover. Player text can simply say: “The cloth catches grit. The cartridge from the lander's drinking-water line finishes the treatment.” Keep the lander's **air CO₂ scrubber** distinct: the recovered component comes from drinking-water service, not its breathing-air sorbent. [EPA treatment explanation](https://19january2021snapshot.epa.gov/sdwa/perchlorate-drinking-water-frequent-questions_.html), [CDC cloth prefiltration](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/).

The four player operations are:

```text
Fabric + Reclaimed water cartridge → Water filter
Heater @ Visible ice → Meltwater
Water filter + Meltwater → Clean water
Clean water @ Tap tank → Stored water
```

Preserve the heater, filter and ice bank as reusable equipment/resource identities. Fabric fitting and the first melting can proceed independently. The filter cartridge is the sole added small treatment supply, salvaged from an already useful system. Recovering it and cleaning its housing can be included when it first appears.

**Make the later fault belong to this actual operation.** Immediately after the first harvest, the next meltwater batch stalls in the fabric prefilter around this same cartridge. A mat of brown flakes clogs it. Replacing the cloth restores filtering; rinsing the old cloth into the existing clean sample vessel retains the residue. The laboratory membrane then catches small particles for microscopy, as before. The tank can run low while refilling is blocked, but a blocked prefilter must not magically stop an already-full tank's tap. Replace “pumped through the well” with “melted, poured and strained.” The recognizable fragments remain fictional evidence, and morphology alone still does not establish life or origin.

The source core comes from **the untouched part of this same visible bank**, beside the now-obvious hand-harvest marks. The corer penetrates the interior away from the chipped/weathered surface and any heater contact. Preserve the same bands, cap, rock, perspective and habitat distance. Delete the former heated-cavity boundary: the new process never creates a cavity under the habitat. Later remote ice remains a separate site and specimen.

## Fabric, sheet and prepared mineral medium

Use two plain payload names with genuinely different capabilities:

| Stock | Meaningful uses | Required limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Fabric** | Coarse water strainer, basket liner, cleaning cloth, backing for foil, supported crop cover | Permeable, washable, low-lint stock suitable for the assigned contact; fresh or properly cleaned pieces for food/water. It neither holds water nor forms a pressure boundary. |
| **Sheet** | Waterproof bed liner, splash protection, temporary waterproof containment | Compatible flexible polymer sheet; supported by smooth ceramic. An impervious sheet cannot also be a cloth strainer. |

NASA has investigated cargo bags designed to unfold into partitions, blankets and other useful articles, so repurposed soft goods are well motivated. That does not qualify arbitrary flame-retardant cargo cloth for food contact. Define the selected general stock once in author notes; keep the player labels generic. Never reuse the dirty ice-straining piece for lettuce or the solar-panel wiping piece for beans. [NASA multipurpose cargo transfer bags](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140016963).

**Cargo mesh is strong, light synthetic cargo-restraint net recovered from packing.** The same flexible knotted grid can support the chosen soybean cultivar, carry a cloth-lined harvest load and support a light canopy. Draw tension and textile fibres: it cannot stand upright by itself. Use existing interior attachment tabs and reusable ties, with the modest suspended loads included in those tabs' stated purpose. No brass poles, finials, buckles, timber trellis, freestanding frame or newly supplied curtain hardware is needed. Soybean support helps prevent lodging; it does not turn this crop into a climbing vine. This is a material/payload choice informed by NASA's reuse approach, not a claim that NASA has qualified this exact garden assembly.

**Do not fire finished bricks only to crush them at the sink.** The same furnace can fire a separate batch of small granules, using suitable local feed already selected for the ceramics programme. The complete operation forms/sizes loose granules before firing, using the furnace's existing trays. At the sink the player grades, rinses and checks those granules, producing growing medium. This needs one derived material identity, **Fired granules**, rather than another primitive or a dedicated apparatus. Alternatively, supply the spare granules from the earlier firing batch when the garden chapter begins, with their origin explained.

If a visible preparation step is useful, use **Clean water + Screened regolith → Granule charge**, then **Granule charge @ Furnace → Fired granules**. A little water enables granulation of the selected suitable fine feed; shaping, sizing and drying before firing are bundled operations. This makes the reused water supply useful without introducing a binder or machine token. The charge is deliberately shaped as small granules before firing, not as bricks that will be smashed afterward. Favorable feed cohesion and the fired material's porosity remain part of the existing material premise.

The firing serves two purposes: making a stable granular substrate and thermally decomposing the measured oxychlorine contamination. Rinsing removes remaining soluble salts and fines; a batch leach check determines whether it is suitable. A 2026 primary experiment demonstrated effective thermal treatment and repeated leaching of magnesium-perchlorate-spiked JSC Mars-1; its leaching route also needed water recovery. That is evidence for the mechanism, not for universal treatment of all Martian minerals. Recipe text should therefore say that **this batch passes**, rather than that fire makes all Mars dirt safe. Washing alone could work for soluble perchlorate but spends scarce water, leaves contaminated washwater, and does not necessarily provide useful particle structure. [Coker, Denvir and Howe experiment](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/saj2.70201).

The fictional feedstock must yield a mechanically stable, sufficiently porous material under the selected firing schedule. Dense vitrified shards would be poor substitutes. The rinse/leach check includes salinity, relevant contaminants and pH; wastewater stays out of drinking and crop circuits. Use clean porous granules as **root support**, with crop-specific nutrients supplied by the drippers, including soybean nitrogen. This is mineral-medium cultivation, not instant fertile Earth soil. NASA's plant systems use porous clay substrate with added fertilizer and controlled water/air balance; this supports the architecture, not our locally fired material's qualification. [NASA plant growth systems](https://www.nasa.gov/exploration-research-and-technology/growing-plants-in-space/).

## Give the potato cover an actual job

The earlier installed `one-room-for-roots-plantable-growing-beds.png` puts dark cloth outside an already opaque brick wall while leaving the growing medium exposed. It adds almost no darkness where tubers are vulnerable.

For the first action, spread an **opaque removable surface cover across the open potato bed**, with overlapping planting flaps/loose openings for shoots and drippers underneath. Show its broad horizontal surface covering the medium, rather than another sleeve around the brick. Leave clear headroom below the existing brick rim when filling the bed. The cover can rest loosely across the rim and later lift away; do not wrap stems tightly or shade leaves.

**Omit the collar and its construction step entirely.** Later, `Growing medium @ Growing potato bed → Hilled potato bed` lifts the loose cover, adds a modest mound around lower stems within that reserved headroom, and loosely replaces the cover around the shoots. Added medium buries developing shallow tubers; the existing brick bed contains it. Do not increase bed height, raise the plants or produce a large mound that requires another wall. If a drawing begins with medium already at the rim, it must be revised to leave headroom. Hilling does not require a rigid collar, and the exact mound size follows the chosen bed and cultivar rather than copying field-scale quantities. Leaves need light; tubers remain covered to prevent greening. [University of Minnesota potato guidance](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/growing-potatoes).

Expose the soybean and lettuce planting receivers as soon as the drippers/medium are ready. Potato cover completion is an independent garden requirement, not a dependency of those seed slots. Even potato planting can proceed before or after the loose cover if its planting flaps are explicit. Preserve already-planted overlays when the cover changes. No horticultural mechanism justifies making soybean or lettuce sowing wait for potato darkness.

## Storm shelter above the crop

Replace the quilted bed-wall padding with **a light fabric cover over tensioned cargo net**, tied to existing interior habitat attachment tabs above the foliage. There are no new poles, canopy furniture, rods, rails or rings. The net supplies support; fabric supplies the cover. Let soft side folds descend toward the bed edges with a deliberate circulation gap. A flat roof with every side wide open offers less protection than a sheltered pocket of air, so show the soft folds as well as the ventilated opening. The cover reduces radiative exchange and air movement; the reserve heater and warm substrate supply the heat.

Keep the full end-wall curtain as plain packing foil backed by fabric, tied to those existing tabs, with its access overlap and ventilation path retained. A loose drape and air gap can reduce heat loss; it is not a new thick insulated wall. Do not depict a newly fabricated curtain rail, brass rings or heavy padded construction. The canopy and curtain remain independent, useful uses of the same supplies.

The smallest consistent use of generic fabric is a **night cover**: lower it during the dark period, lift/roll it back during the shortened lamp period. Depict the lowered nighttime state with its roll-up ties and tell the player the daily opening is part of the complete storm operation. Do not leave opaque foil or dense cargo cloth between the existing ceiling lights and the plants throughout the storm. If the image instead shows a permanently lowered canopy, the lamps must visibly be underneath it with suitable clearance and circulation. A translucent specialist row cover could transmit light but would weaken the multipurpose-payload idea. Earth row-cover guidance confirms the insulation/light tradeoff and the need to ventilate; its frost-temperature figures do not establish performance inside this Martian hab. [University of Minnesota supported row covers](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/extending-the-growing-season).

Lower light means slower water demand and growth. Keep reduced drip pulses, continued essential heat and a finite storm endurance premise. This is survival through a temporary interruption, not sustained crop production without energy.

## One harvest basket and a mandatory bean preparation

Use the same cleaned **cloth-lined cargo-net basket or carrying sling** for all three harvests. Its flexible synthetic grid must match the net used for support; do not introduce woven wicker, timber or a heavy wire basket. The liner keeps small beans from escaping. Each action includes routine work appropriate to that crop: uncover/lift potatoes, pick and shell mature soybean pods, cut/pick lettuce. No separate shovel or cloth harvest token is necessary. The adopted sequence unloads potatoes and lettuce onto the clean counter before gathering beans last; repeated basket pictures represent those successive loads, not three newly made baskets. Drain soaked beans before returning them to the lined net: an open mesh sling cannot hold a bath of water.

**Soybean harvest → sink → washed/soaked beans → potatoes and beans cooked together → lettuce alongside.** The sink action consumes/transforms the harvested-bean identity, so raw harvested beans cannot bypass it. Bundling washing, soaking and draining is reasonable; soaking does not replace thorough cooking. The same small heater and pot introduced for water can cook the stew after proper cleaning, with raw meltwater collection kept distinct from cleaned food preparation. Keep the final cooking operation and elapsed time in prose. [University of Arkansas dried soybean preparation](https://www.uaex.uada.edu/farm-ranch/special-programs/Education_in_Agriculture/Docs/access%202019%20_3%20Cooking%20Soybeans.pdf).
