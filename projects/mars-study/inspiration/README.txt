# Mars icon-set

Goals:
- Clean and distinctive
- Clearly readable as game objects
- Not cartoony
- Not generic modern sci-fi
- Not too pristine
- Not too Star-Wars grimy

## Phase 1 studies

In directory "phase 1 studies", exploring 12 different ideas
- Apollo-era technical illustration
- Polar expedition field sketchbook
- Architectural and industrial isometric drawings
- Photorealistic icons
- Mid-century industrial catalogues and service manuals
- Societ space-age book illustration
- Golden age sci-fi cover illustration style
- 1970s paperback sci fi art
- Star Trek
- Star Wars
- Raygun Gothic
- 2001 Space Odyssey

**Verdict**
- I like 1960s star-trek TV best. It feels clean and distinctive and recognizable. Although I worry it might be a turn off to some players who consider it too old or corny
- Apollo-era was the next best. Still very clear. But the pencilness of it made it seem someone disconnected from what w're actually doing, which is being in a game.
- Axonometric was nice, and less pencil than the appollo, which was good. But somehow it had lost too much color, reduced to just white and grey and orange

I want the objects to feel like physical things you can pick up and manipulate, not illustrations of those things.

Takeaways: study the star trek direction further, but also explore a combination of apollo and axonometric

Takeaway: The 1960s look is a bit too much like a "prop", made of cheaper equipment available to a low-budget TV show, and shot in studio lights that give reflections that are too clean.

## Phase 3 study
- 1960s star trek v2

This is a version of 1960s star trek which uses less cheap prop-like materials, which has lighting appropriate to what we'd get on mars, and which isn't so pristine. I don't want to go full-on Star Wars gritty used feel. But just enough to take off that fresh-out-of-the-wrapping shine.

**Verdict**
- We have a good direction here (although the sealant still had reflections from studio lights)
- The shapes are simple and iconic. The materials are believable but restrained. Slight dust, wear, and outdoor lighting help a lot. The set has a coherent industrial language.
- Cream housing, orange-red accents, exposed fittings, practical hardware — all of that ties the world together.
