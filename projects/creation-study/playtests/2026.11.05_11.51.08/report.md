# Report

Story: projects/creation-study/creation.md

Play style: --detailed

**Definitions**
- Worthwhile: counts actions supported by meaningful decision-making or a valued story payoff or reason to keep playing, counting each action once. Show the average credited actions / average total gameplay actions per player, not an average percentage.
- Fun: a narrower count of meaningful decisions: worthwhile deliberation or intentional experimentation. Measured by --detailed, including inspections and unsuccessful well-formed attempts; --fast reports it as not measured. Fast menu-assisted diagnostic ratios are not comparable.
- Hooks: the players' reported desire to continue: "high" means specific eagerness, "medium" means mild or conditional interest, and "low" means indifference or reluctance

## Chapter 1: Create the heavens and the earth

Worthwhile: 11/13 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 7.7/13 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium/high (2 medium, 1 high)

Strongest moments: Sky stones + Rocky world → Moon was the shared standout. Every player singled out the collision, gathering debris and reflected sunlight as a valued explanatory discovery; each also made a credited inquiry into possible moon material before the automatic result. Moon + Rocky world → Steady axial tilt extended that payoff for all three players. Players 1 and 3 valued the qualification that stabilization does not freeze the tilt; Player 2 found the companion's additional role satisfying even though its automatic execution received no meaningful-decision credit.

Weakest moments: Finding the lesser night light repeatedly devolved into empty compatibility lists. All three explicitly reported frustration or an unrewarding search, although their graders credited some inquiries as meaningful. The weakness is sparse recipe-discovery feedback, not a demonstrated rejection of the creation imagery. Earth + Waters of the deep → Sky stones was an opaque bridge to the Moon for every player. Compatibility automation supplied the route rather than a predicted recipe. Players 1 and 3 still welcomed the resulting lead, so the discoverability weakness should not erase their renewed curiosity.

## Chapter 2: A place for the rain

Worthwhile: 7.3/8.7 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 4.7/8.7 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium

Strongest moments: Water + Moon producing Tides, followed by Tides + Ocean producing Tidal shore, combined appreciated explanation with satisfying closure across all three players. Players 1 and 2 especially valued uneven lunar gravity and the Sun's contribution; their original Moon–Ocean attempts also demonstrated meaningful reasoning despite compatibility friction. Water + Stone crust producing Steam cloud and Steam cloud + Air producing First rain made the weather sequence intelligible. All three explicitly valued the vapour/droplet distinction; Player 3 particularly valued and named the rain-producing inspection as the most worthwhile action.

Weakest moments: The Moon–Ocean attempt exposed an arbitrary-feeling Water/Ocean distinction for Players 1 and 2. Reversing operand order yielded Waves for one and Tides for the other, weakening the connection between a sensible physical hypothesis and its outcome rather than the value of the eventual tide explanation. Air + Water's uninformative failure and the repeated Water + Stone crust attempt weakened Player 3's causal exploration. The former remained a meaningful experiment despite being personally least worthwhile; the latter unexpectedly supplied Tides rather than an ocean, although its explanation was still appreciated.

## Chapter 3: Water learns the way home

Worthwhile: 8/8 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 6.7/8 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium

Strongest moments: Rivers + Earth → Sediment, followed by Sediment + Rivers → Delta, was all three players' most worthwhile sequence. Each separately valued understanding the material source and seeing erosion become land-building; Players 2 and 3 emphasized satisfaction or tangibility. Delta creation remained rewarding despite automatic recipe correction. Rivers + Sun → Water cycle provided shared explanatory closure. Player 1 valued the integration of processes, while Players 2 and 3 explicitly found the solar-energy/gravity summary satisfying. This was worthwhile payoff even though every grader classified the action as routine execution.

Weakest moments: Producing Rainfall through Clouds + Water conflicted with every player's expectation: Players 1 and 2 tried land, while Player 3 reused the earlier Air pattern. All described the required pairing as somewhat arbitrary or initially unclear; Player 3 named it least worthwhile. The explanation partly repaired recipe friction, but automatic correction bypassed choosing the revised combination. The rejected Rivers + Ocean pairing frustrated a shared river-mouth deposition model and was least worthwhile for Players 1 and 2. Sediment + Ocean later encountered a similar boundary. These were meaningful tests under the independent grades, and the eventual delta payoff was valued, but compatibility rules repeatedly interrupted otherwise plausible causal reasoning.

## Chapter 4: The stone is not finished

Worthwhile: 10.7/11 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: 9.3/11 meaningful decisions (average across 3 players)

Hook: medium

Strongest moments: Volcano + Water → Hydrothermal vent: all three valued the heated-seawater explanation, and Players 1 and 2 selected it as their highlight. It joined an independently inferred combination to an experienced educational payoff. Weathered rock + Rivers → Mineral-rich sea: all three explicitly found the completed land-to-sea process satisfying; Player 3 selected this pathway as the highlight. Its payoff remains valuable despite disagreement over whether the final combination supplied a new decision.

Weakest moments: Inspecting Hydrothermal vent for the mineral goal: all three pursued its explicitly described dissolved chemicals, then found no partners. Their reports identify an unproductive or arbitrary-feeling restriction in the interaction model, despite valuing the vent's creation. Trying to release or transport minerals directly from Stone crust: Player 1's Rainfall + Stone crust failed, Players 2 and 3's Rivers + Stone crust failed, and Player 3's crust inspection also yielded nothing. These motivated tests exposed unclear recipe boundaries. Later weathering partly resolved the distinction for Player 2, while Player 1 still questioned the crust-versus-continent restriction.
