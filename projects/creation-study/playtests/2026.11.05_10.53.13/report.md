Play style: --fast

**Definitions**
- Worthwhile: counts actions supported by meaningful decision-making or a valued story payoff or reason to keep playing, counting each action once. Show the average credited actions / average total gameplay actions per player, not an average percentage.
- Fun: a narrower count of meaningful decisions: worthwhile deliberation or intentional experimentation. Measured by --detailed, including inspections and unsuccessful well-formed attempts; --fast reports it as not measured. Fast menu-assisted diagnostic ratios are not comparable.
- Hooks: the players' reported desire to continue: "high" means specific eagerness, "medium" means mild or conditional interest, and "low" means indifference or reluctance

## Chapter 1: Create the heavens and the earth

Worthwhile: 6.3/8 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium

Strongest moments: Sky stones striking the Rocky world to create the Moon was the shared standout: all three identified the impact, gathered debris and reflected sunlight as worthwhile. Players 1 and 3 also received independent credit for predicting impact-driven Moon formation. Moon + Rocky world producing Steady axial tilt gave all three a separately valued explanation: the Moon moderates changes rather than fixing the angle. Each linked this material to curiosity about continuing.

Weakest moments: Fire in Water producing a Starfield lacked a convincing prospective connection for Players 1 and 2, who called it arbitrary. The disagreement matters: Player 1 still appreciated the ending's imagery, while Player 3 found the image striking and received independent decision credit. Earth in Water on the route to a night light was Player 3's least clear choice: Moon material was largely a guess. Player 1 pursued a similar hypothesis and received meaningful credit, while Player 2 reached the action only after exhausting the alternative. All three nevertheless found the resulting threat of impact intriguing.

## Chapter 3: Water learns the way home

Worthwhile: 6/7 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium

Strongest moments: Rivers + Earth → Sediment, followed by Sediment + Rivers → Delta: all three players value erosion supplying material that becomes branching new land. Sediment creation supports causal inference; delta creation delivers satisfying payoff even though its execution was independently classified nonmeaningful. Rivers + Sun → Water cycle: all three connect the completed circulation to their willingness to continue. Players 1 and 3 explicitly find the conclusion satisfying; Player 2 values the integrated explanation. The river-versus-rain source comparison also supplies the graders' borderline decision credit.

Weakest moments: Clouds + Water → Rainfall: all three identify this as their weakest action. Players 1 and 2 emphasize predictability; Player 3 additionally finds the separate water ingredient abstract. The weakness is limited discovery and, for one player, recipe clarity—not documented interface friction.

## Chapter 4: The stone is not finished

Worthwhile: 6.3/7 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium

Strongest moments: Volcano + Water → Hydrothermal vent was the shared standout. All three players named it most worthwhile for connecting heated rock, circulating seawater and dissolved chemicals; Players 1 and 2 explicitly called it satisfying. Its payoff survives the graders' disagreement about whether its execution was another meaningful decision. Weathered rock + Rivers → Mineral-rich sea completed a valued causal connection rather than merely a checklist. Players 1 and 3 explicitly found the land-to-sea sequence satisfying; Player 2 valued its useful geological connections. Player 3 additionally wondered what the resulting sea could support next.

Weakest moments: Moving plates + Earth → Continent imposed sole-option progression with delayed purpose. Player 1 found it somewhat arbitrary, Player 2 later resolved that concern, and Player 3 found it procedural despite useful reshaping information. The weakness was constrained agency and initially opaque relevance, not an interface malfunction. Moving plates + Fire → Volcano was Player 2's least worthwhile moment because it was predictable, although its rising-magma explanation remained useful. Players 1 and 3 did not identify the volcano as a comparable weakness.
