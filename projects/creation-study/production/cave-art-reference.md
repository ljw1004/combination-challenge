# Paleolithic cave painting revision

The selected animal treatment draws specifically on Upper Paleolithic paintings at Lascaux and Altamira. It preserves the established illustrated cave and torchlight while giving the marks on its wall a distinct mineral-pigment language. The [canonical brief](../../../stories/creation/design-brief.md) owns this adopted direction.

## References and decision

- The French Ministry of Culture’s [Lascaux stag photograph](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/lascaux/en/mediatheque/der-hirsch), credited to the Ministry/Centre National de la Préhistoire/Norbert Aujoulat, supplied economical dark contours, expressive branching antlers, bare stone within the animal and granular pigment marks.
- The Altamira Museum’s [art collection page](https://www.cultura.gob.es/mnaltamira/cueva-altamira/arte.html), particularly its photograph of the hind, supplied broad partial ochre fields and the relationship between animal shape and uneven stone. Its [museum guide](https://www.cultura.gob.es/mnaltamira/dam/jcr%3A178b1057-f52d-42db-852f-5ccf77855875/folleto-en-neocueva-altamira.pdf) identifies charcoal black and ochre red in that figure.
- Lascaux’s documentation of the [rock support](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/lascaux/en/rock-support) and [construction of images](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/lascaux/en/construction-images) explains how wall grain and relief affect techniques and figures. These sources support deliberate, accomplished marks; Paleolithic does not mean clumsy drawing.

The previous animal’s uniform outline, smooth body shading and carefully finished details read as modern wildlife illustration. The replacement uses stronger variable contours, spare tapering legs, larger antlers, partial rusty pigment and visible stone. It is an original illustrative adaptation, not a replica of one archaeological work or a universal Paleolithic style.

The second state was generated as an edit of the selected deer. It adds irregular charcoal-dark and red-ochre dots above the animal, replacing the decorative starbursts. Dots represent remembered stars in this invented story; this does not assign a proven astronomical meaning to actual cave signs. The result reading now states that the painters use dots for stars.

Both selected masters and their exact built-in image-generation prompts, reference links and source hashes are recorded in [special-scene-requests.json](special-scene-requests.json). Their registered runtime exports retain the existing geometry and alpha masks in [scene-state-layout.json](scene-state-layout.json). No recipes, goals, receivers or engine behavior changed.
