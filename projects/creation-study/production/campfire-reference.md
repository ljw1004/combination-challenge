# Campfire: people and night sky

The selected scene keeps five people small beneath a large night sky, with the fire near the middle of the full portrait. Two women and three men have varied ages and individual, weathered faces. Deep blue surroundings make the campfire and faint torch inside the cave welcoming. The scene uses early Homo sapiens as its visual reference; it does not reconstruct a named population, date or location.

The Natural History Museum’s [early modern human and Neanderthal reconstructions](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/bringing-a-neanderthal-to-life-the-making-of-our-model.html) supplied comparisons of anatomy and natural facial character. The selected direction follows early Homo sapiens rather than combining diagnostic Neanderthal anatomy with modern faces. Its [Cheddar Man reconstruction](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/cheddar-man-mesolithic-britain-blue-eyed-boy.html) challenges a default pale European appearance. These reconstructions combine evidence with interpretation; their clothing, poses and museum settings were not copied.

For the Milky Way, [A. Trigo/ESO’s Paranal photograph](https://www.eso.org/public/images/potw2607a/) and [Alexandre Santerne’s La Silla photograph](https://www.eso.org/public/images/santerne-3_6-milkyway/) supplied the broad, uneven star clouds and branching dark dust lanes. The first generated sky had a plausible direction but was too narrow and uniform. The selected edit changes that structure while retaining the approved landscape and gathering. Photographic exposure and colour are not a naked-eye calibration; this is an illustrative night sky rather than an exact astronomical reconstruction.

The final source, exact sky-edit request, preceding composition request and reference links are retained in [special-scene-requests.json](special-scene-requests.json). Only the selected painting is retained in production. Research downloads, rejected candidates and browser captures are disposable scratch.
