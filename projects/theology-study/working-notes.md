Introduce the Trinity as a different kind of claim; move from its explanatory limits to bodily rites, then ask why water and bread are given different roles. The instrument comparison is a reason for the transition, not another occasion to repeat the same general caution.
- **9–12: keep the household and the disputed benefit in view.** Use the supper case to make free service matter, then let Marburg and Trent change the question about gift, reception and later deeds. Chapter 11 already demonstrates that a limited agreement can be a satisfying destination. Aim for similar progress in 12 without promising reconciliation.
- **13–14: give the investigation a bounded ending.** The record should change the player’s account of the fragment even though it cannot settle every religious question. End on what became better understood and which disagreement survives; avoid ending with several procedural reminders. High hooks here support retaining the inquiry, not its existing amount of work.

These are editorial hypotheses from local reports and the manuscript. They do not establish that a continuous player currently experiences the proposed arc problems in exactly this way.

## Revision order and evidence that would change the plan

1. Draft the **10 and 14** changes first. Preserve their current evidence as the baseline. Inspect downstream identity uses, run the shared lint/checkpoint checks, and preview the affected working sets. Obtain fresh authenticity review for changed attributed claims and fresh narrative review for changed transitions, using the [reviewer-independence rules](../../docs/fun-and-evaluation.md#reviewer-independence).
2. Run three fresh **`--fast`** chapter players per revised chapter with the same educational guidance. For 10, require evidence that the household action and writer comparison earn distinct value, with fewer reactions calling service or discipline a repeat. For 14, require that players distinguish custody, identity, recognition and permission while still valuing both the record and the surviving disagreement. A shorter route that loses those distinctions fails even if its percentage rises.
3. If those prototypes work, apply the demonstrated method to **2, 5 and 7**, then 4 and 13. Use the reports' actual reasons to decide whether to merge a result, replace an input or improve an explanation; do not apply a universal recipe-count target.
4. Test the arc proposals through fresh readings of the relevant transitions and, when ready, continuous human play. Local high hooks cannot establish cumulative attachment. Use `--detailed` later for the separate meaningful-decision question; do not compare its Fun with a fast diagnostic ratio.

Retain a longer or predictable action when a fresh player can identify its distinct application or satisfying contribution. Reconsider the reduction strategy if players lose the case, attribution or reason a limit matters.
