# H — A solid that changes the light

Concept candidate for [Before the Cloud Grew Thin](../../narrative/round-3/h-dust.md). Proposed scope: **two combinations**. The structure is making a material, then letting its new physical property matter at a much larger scale.

**Player action and question.** The player joins the constituents of a solid in an expanding stellar remnant, then brings that solid into the remnant's light. The question is **how can anything acquire a lasting boundary while everything around it is dispersing?** The setting remains the ruined star: no vessels, tools, human observer or workshop metaphor. The player's combinations enact natural encounters.

**Complete chain.**

1. A short opening follows the oxygen-and-silicon band escaping the star. The stalled shock, neutrino heating and successful ejection establish how these constituents reached the cloud; they are narrated causes, not a preliminary particle puzzle. The first playable scene begins after expansion and radiation have lowered the temperature, inside a knot still dense enough for repeated encounters.
2. **Silicon atoms + Oxygen atoms, within the cooling dense knot → Silica dust.** Silicon and oxygen each contribute actual constituent atoms. The knot contributes the bounded physical conditions: suitable composition, temperatures permitting structures to survive, and sufficient local density while the cloud expands. It contributes no missing mineral ingredients. The single result represents a population of silicon–oxygen grains, not one grain made from two individual atoms. The operation includes intermediate molecules, growing clusters, broken bonds and repeated encounters. Its reading makes the crucial distinction: the star altered nuclei to make elements; chemical bonding now assembles those existing atoms into solids. Other material remains gaseous.
3. The view widens while keeping those grains within their original knot. **Silica dust + Light passing through the knot → Warm dust.** Dust contributes the absorbing solid; the light contributes energy from the glowing gas behind it. The incident light is a fixed, local radiation context, not a movable packet or a universal source of power. The action represents interception along that established path, not transporting grains from outside the remnant.
4. Automatic narration completes the exchange: the grains emit at longer, infrared wavelengths. The far-side glow remains present but is partly hidden. Uneven dust produces the dark seam of the ending, while gaps remain bright. Expansion makes further growth increasingly difficult; the existing grains keep their internal bonds as the surrounding gas disperses.

**Continuity and supplies.** Only two movable starting supplies are needed: representative populations of silicon and oxygen atoms already in the escaping band. Their abundance is abstracted; there is no atom counting or repeated production loop. The fixed dense knot is the specified setting for the first operation. In the wider scene the created dust is carried forward, and the remnant supplies the explicitly identified incident light. Warm dust is the same material after gaining energy. Neither the cloud nor its illuminating gas is consumed by the recipe.

**Vocabulary and scope.** “Silica” names the chosen silicon–oxygen solid and prevents “mineral” from silently implying magnesium, iron or other unsupplied constituents. Molecule, chemical bond and infrared appear in explanations; they are not extra primitives. This narrow material choice is consistent with supernova chemistry models that include silica formation, but does not specify a single elementary reaction or make every ejecta knot productive ([Sarangi and Cherchneff, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201424969)). Cooling, time, pressure and neutrinos never become collectible ingredients.

**Main risk and falsifier.** The first gesture bundles the passage's central molecular history, and the second could be mistaken for deliberately arranging dust as a screen. Reject the concept if the simple actions teach that cold atoms invariably make dust, that an individual silicon–oxygen collision makes a grain, or that absorption extinguishes the background gas. It contains no timing challenge: the thinning cloud explains the physical opportunity without imposing a player deadline.
