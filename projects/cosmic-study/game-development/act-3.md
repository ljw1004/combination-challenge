## `c-ch1` What the young stars leave outside
background: ![Temporary cosmic background](../assets/backgrounds/background-03.webp)

While the rocky worlds were gathering, other material was leaving the young stars. At the nearby tail-base and nursery boundary, the new lights meet gas they never consumed; material lost by the planetary disk reaches a cold neighbouring cloud.

> NOTE: This scene joins the concurrent early tail-base and nursery-outskirts consequences. a-tail-stars, tail-cloud, outflowing-gas, a-dust-poor-gas and b-dusty-outflow are actual earlier results, never replacement supplies. Different portions of these extensive reservoirs meet the stated recipients. The selected dense cold recipient contains surviving grain seeds and can radiate the added heat; most surrounding gas remains hot and dilute. Its dust can include surviving grains from the disk and later growth from gas. The two cold-cloud producers are alternatives, not required collections. Sources and limits are recorded in research.md. Later cluster survival is one selected possible outcome: https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasas-hubble-traces-string-of-pearls-star-clusters-in-galaxy-collisions/ .

**Goals**

- What do the new stars change outside their birthplace?
    - both: `c-tail-shell`
    - and: `c-wind-born-stars`
- Can expelled material join a cold cloud again? -- `c-dust-rich-cloud`

**Items**

- `tail-cloud` Gas in the long tail
- `a-tail-stars` Young stars at the tail's base
- `outflowing-gas` Gas flowing out of the nursery
- `c-tail-cloudlets` Small clouds crossing the tail
    Portions of gas are converging here, near the tail's base and the nursery's outskirts. The paths cross inside the long stream drawn from the galaxies.
- `c-cold-neighbour` Cold dusty cloud beside the nursery
    A large, dense reservoir at the nursery's boundary with its thin surroundings. It contains surviving grain seeds. Separate portions meet the outgoing nursery flow, dust-poor gas and material escaping the small planetary disk. In a limited mixture, radiation can remove heat while most surrounding gas remains hot and dilute.
- `c-lit-tail-gas` Glowing gas around the tail cluster
    Ultraviolet light from the population's massive stars strips electrons from nearby hydrogen. The gas shines as electrons rejoin atoms. The already-made stars are changing the cloud around them, even though that material never became stars.
- `c-tail-shell` Swept-up shell beyond the cluster
    Radiation and stellar outflows drive surrounding gas outward into neighbouring cloudlets. Their meeting sweeps material into a shell. The stars remain behind; their influence now reaches beyond their birthplace.
- `c-wind-born-stars` Stars born along the swept shell
    The nursery's outflow strikes the dense neighbouring cloud. After shock heating, crowded portions radiate energy, contract and form stars. Only part of the material does so. A flow that ended one gathering has helped another begin.
- `c-dust-rich-cloud` Cold cloud with growing dust grains
    Material lost by the nursery or its planetary disk meets a much larger cold cloud. This selected dense mixture radiates its added heat. Some grains survive; atoms can gather on existing seeds. The departing material has joined another cold reservoir, carrying its earlier history with it.

## `c-ch6` Light outside the darkness
background: ![Temporary cosmic background](../assets/backgrounds/background-03.webp)

Deeper inside one of the merging centres lies a massive black hole. The approaching gas does not fall straight into darkness. It carries the motion of its long journey.

> NOTE: This is an already-existing central black hole, not another object manufactured from a gas token. The recipe models gas settling into an external accretion disk through angular-momentum transport over time, not direct radial infall. The disk represents a reservoir with different portions entering the hole, radiating and supplying an external jet. Jets originate outside the event horizon; nothing shown here escapes from inside it. Background physics: https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/anatomy/ . A feeding disk need not launch a jet; this selected case has a spinning hole and a suitable magnetized inner flow. Simulated gas-rich-merger jet context: Talbot, Sijacki and Bourne, https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07316 . No black-hole merger, jet power or radiative efficiency is asserted.

## `c-end` The lights that remain

The winding arms are gone. So is the dark lane by which the cloud could once be followed.

The old orange star remains. So do stars made from that cloud and the cluster born beyond the spiral. The younger sun still carries its moon-bearing rocky world and ringed giant; the small rubble world travels without that sun. Between them lies material that has been heated, scattered, gathered and given light more than once.

Their shared galaxy did not exist when any of those journeys began. It contains what survived the encounter, and what the encounter made possible.
