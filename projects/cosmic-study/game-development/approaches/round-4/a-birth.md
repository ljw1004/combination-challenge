# Three ways to make the opening carry less preparation

The expanded fast cohort valued the contraction-light revelation and the later gas illumination. It found the separate growing-star-to-fusing-star gesture predictable, and the jet's collision with the hot bridge spatially unclear. Two small chapters also ended with a forced second action. The next revision must preserve physical causality without making every stage of stellar development an inventory item.

## A — One birth, then consequences

The large gas gathering and the smaller turning knot each become stars through a complete operation. The result explains the initial contraction light and subsequent sustained fusion in one short history. Remove both hidden-body identities and the two additional gas-feeding transformations. One substantial following scene combines the jets, glowing gas, escaping loose cloud and sheltered pillars. Its two purposes concern light and the cloud's possible futures. The smaller system retains its disk and actual jet; the pillars still supply the tail's later stars.

This reduces two recipes and two result identities, and combines the two feedback scenes. It does not change the material exports or introduce a process receiver. Its risk is a fuller board and less pause between birth and feedback; verify entry count and the actual available actions. The complete formation result must still say that light begins before sustained fusion.

## B — Follow the two births separately

Follow the large nursery through formation, illumination, outflow and pillars first. Then return to the adjacent small knot, make the star with its disk, and follow its jet into surrounding gas. This is a sequential pair of short, complete stories rather than a mixed nursery board. The first story ends with escaping material; the second carries forward the selected planetary system. Each small scene has several consequences instead of two matched preparation actions.

This offers clearer local identity and can use fewer simultaneous objects. Its cost is repeating the initial act of formation after the large nursery has already developed. A clear concurrent cue is needed: the second star is forming during the same early interval, not being triggered by the first unless that is separately established. It also postpones the two outflow histories' meeting near the tail.

## C — Make the jet the small star's coming-of-age event

Retain the physically interesting first contraction light and the two hidden growing bodies. The larger nursery's next operation makes sustained stars. The smaller body's next operation includes continued feeding, sustained fusion and an escaping jet as one star–disk–jet arrangement. That same composite supplies the actual gas shock without a separate jet-production gesture. The nursery's feedback and the smaller system's outflow can remain interwoven across the existing chapters.

This makes the second stage change what the small system can do rather than merely rename it. Its cost is a more complicated composite icon and a need to keep the actual jet portion distinct from its surviving star and disk. It saves one operation, but preserves more of the growth-stage vocabulary that prompted the user's criticism.

## Selection for the next test

Choose A. It answers the recurring criticism most directly: stellar development belongs within a complete physical operation, and the player's next work concerns what the newly made stars do to actual surroundings. B is a viable fallback if the combined feedback board becomes crowded or confusing. C retains an interesting scientific distinction at too great a representational cost for this game.

This is an authoring choice, not a reviewer verdict or a claim that the revised passage has passed playtesting. The actual revision needs semantic checks and fresh fast/detailed records.
