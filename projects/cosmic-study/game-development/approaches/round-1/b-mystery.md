# B — The Galaxy With Missing Arms

Independent game architecture, 6 November 2026. A proposed departure in narrative order, governed by the [active brief](../../../design-brief.md). This is a design candidate, not a manuscript, scientific approval or playtest result.

## The invitation

*The stars are still here. Their galaxy is not.*

Begin with the surprising end: a broad, rounded galaxy, a faint ribbon curving beyond its light, and a compact gathering of stars whose brightest members have already vanished. An ancient orange star passes through the scene. Nothing looks like a smashed object. Yet two immense spiral patterns have disappeared.

The question is **what was destroyed, what survived, and what became something else?** The player reconstructs this particular history by bringing its material through the encounters that explain those three different fates. No detective, telescope, specimen tray, clue inventory or answer card stands between the player and the cosmos.

The principal pleasure is a change in interpretation. A bright streak first seems to be debris; later it becomes an intact population on altered journeys. A dark cloud first seems to hide the interesting event; later it becomes the event. The apparent wreckage contains both survivors and beginnings. The final galaxy becomes familiar because the player knows several of its constituents, rather than because it resembles a domestic place.

## A reconstruction, not a reversed collision

The order follows questions raised by the remnant, rather than the approach, first passage, separation and final merger. Three short physical histories interleave:

- **The missing arrangement:** how intact stars can lose their shared spiral pattern.
- **The missing cloud:** how some diffuse material becomes compact stars, while much is dispersed.
- **The unexpected survivors:** why a new star subsequently responds to an encounter differently from the gas that made it.

The opening panorama and brief returns to it belong to narration. They do not award completed future objects. Play always enacts an event forward in physical time. A flashback changes the setting; dragging never reverses an orbit, unburns a star or separates a galaxy into ingredients.

The player has no authority to select the laws of physics. They bring actual material into an established physical encounter. Local branches allow independent work and contrasting outcomes. Choosing which thread to pursue first within a scene should reveal something different, even when both eventually matter.

This is a causal mystery, not an inference simulator. The selected history is supplied by the story; a shape alone does not prove a unique past. If play becomes merely a sequence of illustrations of an already delivered explanation, this candidate has failed.

## Twenty-eight chapter purposes

The chapter list is an exploratory long-form envelope. Each row earns its place through a question or consequence; it has no per-chapter recipe quota. Adjacent rows should merge when the playable work does not support a separate scene. Short present-day passages between blocks are prose, not additional chores.

| Chapter | Question and physical purpose |
|---|---|
| 1. A light beyond the edge | In the late return, carry the orange star's stream into the remnant's outskirts. Discover a changed journey without a shattered star. |
| 2. Crossing the unfamiliar | Bring a second, separately established stellar population through the same region. Their paths can interpenetrate rather than make a compact pile. |
| 3. The scattered nursery | Carry a loosely grouped young population through the changing galaxy. Its gathering can be lost while the stars survive. |
| 4. A gathering that holds | Contrast a sufficiently compact cluster with the loose population. Internal binding can preserve one arrangement while the larger galaxy changes. |
| 5. Where the brightest went | Enter an earlier epoch of that same nursery. Its surrounding gas meets the radiation and outflows of its short-lived massive members. The bright stars matter through what they do. |
| 6. The empty birthplace | Follow gas out of the nursery and leave its smaller stars behind. Establish why the present cluster need not retain its natal cloud. |
| 7. Before the lights | Move earlier again, into the dark lane during first passage. Join two extended clouds and make their collision physically unlike the stellar crossing. |
| 8. Room to cool | Follow compressed gas in a specified region where radiation can escape. The important consequence is that crowded gas can gather further; cooling is included in the operation. |
| 9. Several beginnings | Feed distinct condensations from separate parts of the cloud. Establish a population forming in several places, not one star made from an entire galactic cloud. |
| 10. Light under the dust | Continue the fed condensations into young stars while remaining gas still surrounds them. Contraction and fusion belong to the complete result explanation. |
| 11. The edge and the hollow | Bring dense and diffuse neighboring gas into the same young stellar environment. One can retain a substantial dense region while the other is dispersed. |
| 12. The cloud cannot all become stars | Carry the expelled remainder into surrounding gas. The nursery's success changes its own supply and ends this gathering. |
| 13. A cloud outside the galaxy | Shift to a distinct portion of gas in the long outward tail. Its location raises the next question: how did so much star-making material get outside? |
| 14. A gathering in the ribbon | Join converging gas within that tail and follow a selected region into a young stellar group. This is the sole proposed second nursery, justified by a different location and later fate. |
| 15. The ribbon comes back | Bring returning tail material into the surrounding galaxy. Stars and extended gas again have different physical encounters. |
| 16. Gas meets gas again | Join returning gas to an established receiving gas stream. The result changes both its distribution and its prospects for gathering. |
| 17. The second birthplace | Follow this new crowded region where it has a different consequence from the first nursery. Cut the chapter if it only repeats the first star-making chain. |
| 18. Lights without an arm | Carry the tail-born stars through the returning encounter. They now cross as compact stars, rather than repeating their gas-phase behavior. |
| 19. Before the ribbon | Move to the earliest outward encounter. Let the approaching companion redirect the outer stellar population and stretch its old arrangement. |
| 20. The near side and the far side | Work with two physically separate regions of the same disk. Their unequal deflections explain stretching without treating gravity as a portable ingredient. |
| 21. The dark lane bends too | Bring the original lane's gas through the companion's influence. The same large disturbance delivers gas into encounters unavailable on its former path. |
| 22. Through, but not unchanged | Cross an explicitly stellar region and an explicitly gaseous region. This is the original separation of fates, now understood through its eventual consequences. |
| 23. The long outward road | Follow the bound outer material to its turning path. Do not require separate “slower,” “slower still,” and “turning” tokens. |
| 24. Nothing to return to | Follow the old stream inward while the two centers themselves are returning. Its destination has changed during its journey. |
| 25. The newly made stars cross | Reunite the nursery thread with the stellar thread in the final return. The player predicts the new stars' behavior before seeing it. |
| 26. One changing center | Bring the returning material through the combined remnant while the large-scale merger is completed in the surrounding account. No final “galaxy + galaxy” assembly manufactures a settled shape instantly. |
| 27. A pattern comes apart | Interleave the two established populations in the remnant's volume. Loss of coherent structure becomes legible without annihilating its constituents. |
| 28. The lights that remain | Make the last consequential return of the old and younger populations to the shared expanse. End on the opening scene with a different understanding of what its light contains. |

The central reconstruction order is therefore **late survivors → their vanished nursery → a second material thread in the tail → the original disruption → the late crossing where the threads meet**. It does not reset to the beginning and replay the entire collision chronologically.

## Twelve concrete operation proposals

These are complete natural operations, not final manuscript syntax. The right-hand location is fixed when written with `@`. All are subject to scientific and forward-understanding review. Each receiver must visibly contain the matter and physical situation that make it consequential.

1. **Returning old stellar stream @ Remnant's outer reaches → Old stars on inward paths.** The stream supplies the followed stars; the extended galaxy redirects them. The tutorial reveals the movement and leaves the result unknown.
2. **Loose young group @ Changing inner galaxy → Dispersed young stars.** A selected loosely bound population loses its grouping. The result retains those stars; it is not a new population supplied afterward.
3. **Compact young cluster @ Changing inner galaxy → Cluster on a new orbit.** A distinct, sufficiently bound cluster survives this selected encounter. This must not claim that compact clusters survive every possible passage.
4. **Dark-lane cloud + Crossing gas cloud → Crowded star-forming cloud.** Both contribute gas and their relative motion. Shock heating, radiative loss and subsequent gathering are explicitly included; touching clouds does not instantaneously ignite stars.
5. **Crowded cloud + Feeding gas filament → Embedded young cluster.** Additional material joins the forming population; the result includes young stars and their surrounding residual cloud as one physical region. If this adds nothing beyond operation 4, bundle them.
6. **Diffuse nearby gas @ Exposed young cluster → Outward-moving gas.** Radiation and stellar outflows act on the gas. Light and wind are not inventory supplies.
7. **Dense neighboring cloud @ Exposed young cluster → Cloud with a compressed, illuminated edge.** This contrasts with dispersion without promising automatic triggered star formation. Retain it only if that surviving dense material has a later role.
8. **Outward-moving gas + Surrounding gas → Expanding gas shell.** The outgoing material sweeps up a physical surrounding reservoir. The resulting shell can disperse or meet a later inflow; it is not merely a diagram of feedback.
9. **Tail gas + Converging tail stream → Crowded gas in the ribbon.** A distinct location brings gas together far from the bright centers. The later young group supplies the essential transfer-of-understanding case.
10. **Returning tail gas + Inner gas stream → Mixed, slowed gas cloud.** Direct gas interaction changes the material's motion and distribution. It does not simply repaint its orbit.
11. **Outer stellar population @ Approaching companion's outskirts → Lengthening stellar tail.** The companion contributes a different pull across the established disk. The tail contains the earlier followed orange star; it is not an explosion fragment.
12. **Tail-born young stars @ Returning stellar outskirts → Young stars on changed paths.** This is the payoff: material that once collided as gas now passes among other stars. The player should predict why before receiving the explanation.

The strongest six-operation prototype is 1, 4, 5, 6, 11 and 12, with appropriately ordered narrative transitions and real prerequisite identities. It tests nonlinear orientation, the material distinction, the missing-cloud consequence and transfer before a large manuscript is attempted. Operations 2–3 and 7–10 are expansion hypotheses, not automatically earned content.

## What the player handles

Use a small recurring vocabulary: **old stars, young stars, a loose group, a tight cluster, gas cloud, dark cloud, gas stream, feeding filament, shell**. Conditions such as crowded, diffuse and returning need visible physical consequences. The player does not receive a tray of shock fronts, cooling curves, orbital energies, molecular species, dynamical friction or fusion thresholds.

Keep roughly four to seven material portions and at most two receiving regions on a board. A stellar stream is many stars following related paths; a gas stream is extended material. Distinguish them in names before artwork exists. Scale changes are explicit in chapter orientation. A whole galaxy remains the setting when working inside a cloud.

Individual material lineages occur once in playable history. The initial final-state panorama supplies no future tokens. A later flashback introduces an earlier region, not a duplicate of an already manipulated final object. Keep the original old-star stream and the first nursery's descendants as the two principal carried threads; the tail nursery is the only substantial addition. At returns to a previously visited epoch, use the actual surviving result. Do not create a second copy from a new primitive supply.

Chapter-local fixed contexts distinguish first passage, returning passage and remnant. Globally available recipes must not allow a late crossing in the earlier scene. Future narration cannot serve as a mechanical gate. Never carry old and new epoch versions of one unique population onto the same board.

## How much game this honestly supports

Approximately 200 worthwhile combinations remains the full-length ambition. This document does **not** establish that the collision can support that number. The twelve operations above establish several distinct jobs, not two hundred jobs hiding behind interchangeable adjectives.

A plausible first manuscript might reach roughly 80–120 combinations across a substantially compressed version of these chapters, including independent material portions and necessary joins. That is a planning estimate, not a count or duration finding. Reaching 180–210 would require evidence that the second nursery, feedback consequences and returning gas each support substantial new play. Do not preallocate seven recipes to every chapter.

The first deletions are chapters 2, 8, 17, 20 and 23 when their distinction exists only in narration; operations 4–5 should merge if gathering and ignition are only consecutive reveal cards. Delete the second nursery if it feels like another copy of the first. Combine the final three chapters if their actions repeatedly rename mixed stars. Never add separate heat, cooling, contraction and fusion tokens to rescue the count.

This is the candidate's decisive risk: its mystery may be an excellent shorter game while the requested long game needs a broader material premise. Select B only if prototypes show that reconstructing contrasting physical histories supplies enough enjoyable work. A compelling short result is preferable to claiming that an outline has already earned 200 combinations.

## Tests before expansion or artwork

First run fresh `--fast` players through the six-operation prototype. Record whether they can place the current event relative to the opening, identify the same followed material, and describe what question makes the next scene inviting. An interesting ending does not excuse disorientation in the middle.

Develop two or three divergent solutions to each substantial failure: for example, interleaved histories, one complete material history at a time, or a present-day action followed by its causal flashback. Compare those solutions with fresh participants. Do not merely shorten the same exposition and call it a new approach.

After a promising structure emerges, run repeated whole-story `--fast` cycles and focused revisions of weak chapters. Then use `--detailed` on the actual playable scenes, including the strongest passages, and expand to whole-story coverage. Follow the existing independent player/grader workflow; completion, correct prediction and reported fascination are separate findings. Artwork waits until those loops stop finding a confused timeline or routine repetition as the chief experience.

The sharpest falsifier is a player who explains the final galaxy correctly but says the combinations were merely unlocking the next explanation. A second is a player who treats the late young stars as colliding gas despite having made them. A third is a player who wants the mystery resolved but dreads another indistinguishable cloud sequence.

## Scientific starting points and limits

The central stellar/gas distinction and merger-induced star formation are supported by [NASA's account of NGC 6745](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/hubble/a-birds-eye-view-of-a-galaxy-collision/) and its [galaxy evolution overview](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/galaxies/evolution/). Cloud clearing by young populations is described in [the Hubble Antennae account](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/hubble-reveals-stellar-fireworks-accompanying-galaxy-collisions/). These sources support the broad mechanisms, not this proposed recipe graph.

The extra tail nursery is a candidate grounded in the range of merger environments found in [the SMUGGLE merger study](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/514/1/265/6575935). Specific survival of a compact cluster, cloud-edge compression, tail fallback, timings and the selected rounded endpoint require an informed science pass against the eventual operations. This is one chosen encounter history; neither every merger nor every cloud follows it.
