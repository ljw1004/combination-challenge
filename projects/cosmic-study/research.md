# Scientific boundaries for the narrative alternatives

Sources checked on 5 November 2026. These support selected physical histories, not exact reconstructions of named objects, universal outcomes, or the quality of the stories. [Iterations](iterations.md) identifies which versions received independent scientific review. The original study's [research](<../../archive/2026.11.05 cosmic study/research.md>) remains historical and does not dictate the new scope.

## Galaxies: G and its predecessor D

A bound, gas-bearing major merger provides the chosen setting. Widely separated stars mostly avoid direct physical collisions; changing gravity rearranges their orbits, while extended gas can shock, cool and form stars. Tidal streams and new stellar populations are observed in interacting systems. The narrative's followed star and gas parcel are illustrative selections. [NASA: Galaxy Evolution](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/galaxies/evolution/), [NASA: Antennae Galaxies](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/hubble/antennae-galaxies/).

A rounded remnant is one possible outcome. Gas content, mass ratio and geometry affect how much disk survives or reforms; merging does not always erase a disk. The selected old star remains bound and lives through the encounter. Its exact path is invented. Broad mixing does not erase all chemical or orbital evidence of progenitor populations. [Hopkins et al., How Do Disks Survive Mergers?](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.1739).

Contraction already heats and lights protostars before sustained hydrogen fusion becomes their continuing energy source. The story must preserve that distinction. [NASA: Stars](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/).

## Stellar escape and dust: H and its predecessor E

The selected massive star undergoes a successful core-collapse explosion, leaving a neutron star. Collapse alone does not guarantee ejection. Neutrino heating and multidimensional flow can revive a stalled shock; models specifically support assistance from disturbances in the pre-collapse oxygen shell. This is a chosen model-supported history, not a complete theory of every explosion. [Müller et al., The Last Minutes of Oxygen Shell Burning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01393), [Müller et al., Supernova Simulations from a 3D Progenitor Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.00620).

Nuclear synthesis makes elements; chemical bonds later join atoms into molecules and grains without changing their nuclei. Cooling ejecta can form dust, with dense clumps helping growth. Expansion also reduces encounter rates. The story's deadline is this changing opportunity, not a literal countdown or a measured rate for an invented parcel. [Sarangi and Cherchneff, Condensation of Dust in the Ejecta of Type II-P Supernovae](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.5522), [Matsuura et al., A Stubbornly Large Mass of Cold Dust](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.7381), [Wesson et al., The Timing and Location of Dust Formation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.7386).

Dust can absorb visible light and reradiate in infrared. Formation within a remnant does not promise survival through later shocks or eventual planet formation. [ESO: ALMA Spots Supernova Dust Factory](https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso1401/), [Slavin et al., Dust-Grain Destruction and Survival](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210013378).

The conceptual recipes introduced additional material-specific constraints. Pure silica cannot simply inherit generic dust's absorption behaviour; its visible/near-infrared absorption can be low. The cited Sarangi–Cherchneff model explicitly omits silica condensation and estimates it from precursors. The second concept instead specifies magnesium silicate/forsterite, whose chemistry and coagulation are modeled, and changes the proposed effect to scattering. Scattering-size grains alone do not establish a dark region: the size distribution, column, illumination and forward-scattered light must be checked for the selected geometry. [Olofsson et al., Warm Debris Disks](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/2012/06/aa18735-11.pdf), [Sarangi and Cherchneff, model limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/1412.5522v1), [Bladh et al., Forsterite Scattering Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.6572). These later findings constrain the concepts; they do not invalidate the original prose's generic mixture of dust.

## Late light: I and its predecessor F

Brown dwarfs retain much ordinary hydrogen because they do not sustain the ordinary hydrogen fusion of stars. A suitably selected pair can lose orbital energy through gravitational radiation, join much later, and yield a long-lived low-mass star. This far-future possibility is theoretical; the exact binary, survival, delay, mass loss and thermal evolution in the story have not been simulated. It is neither the literal last star nor a reversal of cosmic cooling. [Adams and Laughlin, A Dying Universe, sections III.B–C](https://arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/9701131), [NASA: Types of Stars](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/types/).

Enough retained mass **and an appropriate thermal history** matter. Slowly adding matter to an old cold brown dwarf need not ignite it. Near-equal substellar pairs can undergo unstable transfer, while other pairs transfer stably; an observed interacting pair is not proof of this selected far-future merger. [Forbes and Loeb, Overmassive Brown Dwarfs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12143), [Whitebook et al., Substellar Mass Transfer](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17040v1), [Whitebook et al., An Observed Mass-Transferring Pair](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17038).

Merger brightness initially spends energy from motion; sustained fusion is a different source. The narrative follows a successful case. It must not turn that into an unconditional rule that any two dark bodies make a star.

## Earlier unselected human-scale candidates

A used an invented chondritic meteorite history, with separate stellar origins, later mineral formation, asteroid assembly and atmospheric survival; its child and exact itinerary were fiction. [NASA: Meteors and Meteorites](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/meteors-meteorites/facts/), [Lunar and Planetary Institute: Meteorite Structure](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/science/kring/epo_web/meteorites/structure/), [Washington University: Fusion Crust](https://sites.wustl.edu/meteoritesite/items/meteorite-fusion-crust/).

C selected one debris-disk version of a Moon-forming impact. Theia's exact state, impact geometry and assembly history remain uncertain; a familiar Moon is not the intact surviving Theia. [NASA: Moon Formation](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/formation/), [Salmon and Canup: Lunar Accretion](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.0932).

B's future-body premise survives in F/I, but its rocky witness, detailed fracture and shelter were unnecessary invented framing. Those surface events are not carried into the current concept work.
