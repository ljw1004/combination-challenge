# Report

Story: projects/cosmic-study/encounter-pilot.md

Play style: --fast

**Definitions**
- Worthwhile: counts actions supported by meaningful decision-making or a valued story payoff or reason to keep playing, counting each action once. Show the average credited actions / average total gameplay actions per player, not an average percentage.
- Fun: a narrower count of meaningful decisions: worthwhile deliberation or intentional experimentation. Measured by --detailed, including inspections and unsuccessful well-formed attempts; --fast reports it as not measured. Fast menu-assisted diagnostic ratios are not comparable.
- Hooks: the players' reported desire to continue: "high" means specific eagerness, "medium" means mild or conditional interest, and "low" means indifference or reluctance

## Chapter 1: Almost nothing to strike

Worthwhile: 2/2 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium/high (1 medium, 2 high)

Strongest moments: Following the old stars through the other galaxy produced the strongest shared payoff: all three players valued learning that gravity can reshape a galaxy without smashing its stars, and each selected this as most worthwhile. Combining the two gas clouds produced crowded, cooling material and a shared question about what it becomes. Players 1 and 2 explicitly enjoyed its contrast with the stars; Player 3 expressed milder continuation interest.

Weakest moments: Combining the two gas clouds was every player's least engaging decision because it was the only remaining gameplay action. This was limited agency, not reported interface friction, arbitrary causality or rejection of the explanation; its result still earned supported story/hook value.

## Chapter 2: The darkness makes light

Worthwhile: 4/4 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium/high (2 medium, 1 high)

Strongest moments: Combining crowded gas with central inflow to form new stars was all three players' stated most worthwhile moment. The distinction between contraction-powered light and sustained fusion delivered an explicitly valued revelation despite routine menu selection. Combining the new stars with the dense cloud to produce a bright rim gave all three players a memorable correction: illumination does not guarantee another star will form. Each identified this as a surprise, and it supplied context for the subsequent loose-gas comparison.

Weakest moments: Forming the young star and dusty disk exposed shared goal-signposting weakness: all three reached it as the only remaining combination without understanding its connection to 'what happens beside those lights.' This was not a reported failure of physical plausibility; Player 1 nevertheless gained a specific onward hook from the disk. Combining new stars with loose surrounding gas was comparatively weak for all three: Player 1 found the still-open goal unsatisfying, Player 2 found the investigation repetitive, and Player 3 called it least engaging. Its informative feedback contrast retained value, and the independent graders disagreed about whether its execution demonstrated meaningful inference.

## Chapter 3: Collisions within a collision

Worthwhile: 3.3/5 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium/high (2 medium, 1 high)

Strongest moments: Fast-crossing pebbles shattering loose rocky pebbles: all three players predicted the reversal from gentle growth, found it satisfying and selected it as most worthwhile. It combines the unanimously credited meaningful test with an experienced causal-story payoff. Combining shattered fragments with ice-bearing grains: all three valued or reported surprise at material re-entering growth instead of reaching a dead end. This supplies story value despite routine, sole-productive-option execution.

Weakest moments: Placing fine rocky grains in the disk: all three identified it as mere setup or least worthwhile, with no separately evidenced payoff. The weakness is preparatory interaction overhead rather than an arbitrary story event. Turning rock-and-ice clumps into small bodies: the sole-option action limited agency for everyone, and Player 3 found it initially underspecified. Its explanation mitigated that weakness: Player 2 explicitly found the outcome more meaningful, while Players 1 and 3 reported improved plausibility.

## Chapter 4: The new lights cross

Worthwhile: 2/2 worthwhile decisions (average across 3 players)

Fun: not measured (--fast)

Hook: medium

Strongest moments: Following the cloud-born stars through the returning galaxy was the shared strongest payoff: all three explicitly valued how the same material changed from colliding clouds into widely separated stars capable of passing through. Following the old stars into a wider galaxy supplied a smaller shared payoff: survival did not restore their former orbits. All three specifically appreciated this distinction, even where they criticized the crossing's familiarity.

Weakest moments: The old stars' crossing repeated an established collision-free outcome. Players 2 and 3 named it weakest, while still appreciating the changed-orbit detail; Player 1 instead emphasized that detail as satisfying and surprising. The cloud-born stars' crossing left only routine execution once the old stars had crossed. Player 1 explicitly named this predictability weakest, although all three considered its explanation the strongest payoff. The limitation was shallow choice, not reported interface friction.
