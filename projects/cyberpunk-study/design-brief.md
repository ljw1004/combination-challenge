# The Travelling Harbour

## Purpose and player promise

Make a small ship, give it a harbour, then turn that relationship inside out. In cyberspace a creation can carry its former surroundings, and the behaviour of one working object can become material for another. The story follows the lives of things the player makes. Its pleasure should come from recognisable transformations, useful invention and a growing attachment to a few creations.

This is the single active brief for `harbour.md` and its development. The earlier `cyberpunk.md` and `assets/` remain an unreleased baseline; the [original development archive](<../../archive/2026.11.05 cyberpunk study/README.md>) describes that version. It is no longer the production direction. The user found its small icons and numerous subtle concepts difficult. The new work must respond through its actual objects, working sets and visual presentation, not explanatory prose alone.

## Player, setting and tone

The player makes a touring exhibition inside a wholly fictional digital world. Sera is its curator, a conversational AI with a practical sense of humour. People visit and talk but are never combination ingredients. The exhibition is planned from the opening; objects do not create visitors or their approval. Quiet construction can provide momentum without an invented crisis.

Use direct, economical prose, specific discoveries and occasional dialogue. Let the player perform the transformations that advance the story. Keep introductions short and make the visible task and objects sufficient to begin. No knowledge of programming is assumed. The transferable ideas are composition, containment, independent copies and reusable behaviour; only teach distinctions the actual actions enact.

## Governing fictional rules

- Objects retain their recognisable parts when combined into larger things. The white ship, striped sail, bent flag, warm lantern, clear bottle and crescent pier are continuity anchors.
- Visitors can enter digital models at their own scale. Editing a complete model does not transport or duplicate visitors. Visitors leave before a major enclosure transformation.
- The Inside-out arch reverses a visible outside-to-inside enclosure chain while keeping each layer's attachments with it. Harbour(Bottle(Ship)) becomes Ship(Bottle(Harbour)). Introduce the rule and its actual availability together. Do not pretend this is the only possible way to transport digital objects.
- The Merging frame explicitly combines related versions: their common base once, their nonconflicting additions together. Ordinary joining elsewhere does not silently deduplicate ancestry.
- Borrowed movement copies an explicitly displayed outward route. A host pauses at its stops, then returns along the complete route. Appending a segment keeps earlier stops; two compatible ribbons can also join directly into the same composed route. The path is translated to a receiver's starting point without changing its shape, distances or stops; source place names are illustrative, not transported locations. A copied route supplies motion, not the source's body, light, strength or intentions.
- Accept simpler useful constructions and sensible assembly orders. Do not manufacture a need for ornate choreography when raising a light would suffice.

The longer work follows several connected digital commissions, each with a substantial payoff and reuse of earlier creations. Construction is not justified solely by adding another attraction.

The structure uses an early reversal followed by continuing useful lives. Preserve the resulting hook and willingness to cut routine preparation; neither a chapter count nor any particular garden or theatre is protected from removal in response to player evidence.

## Scale, presentation and production

Aim for about 200 authored combinations, with a shorter chosen completion route and useful alternatives. Never retain an operation solely to reach the target. Keep local work focused: one principal creation, a source or tool and a small number of plainly distinct parts. Avoid strings of certificates, plans, test results and near-identical state tokens. No new engine mechanics are assumed.

The adopted visual direction is **a paper theatre in cyberspace**: painted gouache scenery with precise layered SVG objects, using ivory, deep teal, vermilion, cyan and warm gold. Circular objects use a strong size hierarchy: 320/448/640-pixel source drawings display at 80/112/160 CSS pixels on a 390-pixel-wide phone. Fixed stages use 576-pixel canvases and other fixed constructions use 768; their placement leaves the working surface available below. Preserve generous space and broad silhouettes.

Enclosure reversals, reordered routes and different copy histories receive separate large reading panels with normal text captions. This uses the existing reader while keeping each comparison legible within its image-height limit. Shared drawing components retain exact parts across generations. This is digital model-making; apparent placement must agree with that fiction. Decorative scenery establishes place, while the circles represent the models being combined. Keep exact geometry, generation prompts and production methods in the [production record](production/README.md).
