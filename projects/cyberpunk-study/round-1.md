# Round 1 — Three meanings of combining

Concept treatments, not a playable manuscript. Each is a proposed departure from the published story. The player manipulates a few large, named creations; no existing artwork or quantity target is assumed. The existing authored combination/chapter format must suffice. Ordinary people may use places in result scenes but are never ingredients. All digital operations below are fictional world rules, not claims about a real platform.

## A. A City Made of Elsewhere — join spaces

You inherit an empty cinema in an abandoned online world. You want somewhere to meet the friends who still log in to scattered places. You begin by making one room usable; it becomes the centre of a neighbourhood that could never fit on a physical map.

**Grammar.** Joining a room to a doorway brings that actual room next door, regardless of distance. Light and people can cross an open connection. A link is not a copy: changing the room changes what every connected entrance reaches. Whole architectural cutaways make the rooms recognisable. This is a finite authored set of constructions, not a promise of arbitrary 3D editing.

| Combine | Created thing and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Empty cinema + Workshop doorway | Backstage workshop: a workbench opens directly into the hall. |
| Backstage workshop + Garden doorway | Garden court: the same dark cinema now opens onto a living garden. |
| Garden court + Paired window frames | Daylight passage: one window faces the garden sky; the other lights the old screen. |
| Daylight passage + Giant cutout bird | Shadow theatre: the light path gives a workshop-made shape a new scale and purpose. |
| Garden court + Market doorway | Public arcade: the garden and hall become the market's sheltered frontage. |
| Public arcade + Railway doorway | Station square: a distant line now arrives in the place we have made. |
| Station square + Workshop return doorway | A circuit through the rooms: the first workshop is useful as a shortcut, not just the beginning of a corridor. |
| Shadow theatre + Station square | A performance in the station: the first improvised light becomes the centre of a gathering. |

**Story through the creations.** The first connection admits daylight; daylight makes performance possible; the performance gives people a reason to use the growing place. Adding rooms makes the first route awkwardly long; closing a circuit changes how the whole creation works. The ending returns to the same cinema, but it is now a place you can reach from several incompatible worlds. The screen, workshop bench and garden tree must survive every view.

**Questions.** Does adjacency do enough interesting work, or is this merely add-a-room city building? Does the performance actually motivate the later construction? The first three operations currently conceal rather a lot in the words “doorway” and “paired window.” Directly performing these joins must be understandable. No autonomous crowd or transport simulation is assumed.

## B. Ghost Band — copy and recombine patterns

You make music from the sounds of a dismantled orbital station. Its public announcement voice, lift rhythm and closing-door chord have survived as recordings. The work starts as one instrument and becomes something people can play after you leave. The recordings are sounds, not trapped minds.

**Grammar.** A recording can be copied without taking it away from an earlier work. Combining recordings in a supplied instrument layers or sequences their patterns. The same pattern can drive a different output, such as light, through an explicit instrument that supplies that capability. No sound-recognition or live composition engine is assumed; a finite set of authored pieces would be needed. Whether silent illustrations can deliver the premise is a major open question.

| Combine | Created thing and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Station announcement recording + Sampler keyboard | Voice keyboard: familiar words become playable syllables and notes. |
| Lift rhythm recording + Loop recorder | Drum loop: the station's old machinery becomes a musical pulse. |
| Voice keyboard + Drum loop | First song: two earlier creations become one recognisable work. |
| First song + Complete rooftop transmitter | Rooftop broadcast: the made work acquires an audience beyond the workshop. |
| Drum loop + Lamp sequencer | Pulse lantern: the earlier rhythm becomes visible movement. |
| Pulse lantern + Empty platform | Dance floor: a former waiting place becomes somewhere to take part. |
| Voice keyboard + Blank sample cartridge | Playable station archive: the organised sounds are copied, while the original instrument remains. |
| Playable station archive + Public sampler console | Open instrument: another maker can change the work instead of merely replaying our song. |
| Open instrument + Dance floor | Music room: the two created branches reunite in a place that can keep producing new work. |

**Story through the creations.** The announcement becomes an instrument rather than evidence about the past. The rhythm first holds the song together, then makes a silent platform dance. The work's last transformation gives away the means to make another work. The ending is not “our song is now perfect”; the original sounds acquire futures we do not author.

**Questions.** Without hearing the layers, does the whole premise become invisible conceptual work again? Can an illustrated waveform be a meaningful thing to combine rather than a disguised label? “A new song brings a crowd” cannot substitute for a middle the player actually makes. Copying is distinctively useful here, but perhaps not sufficiently dramatic by itself.

## C. The House That Was an Umbrella — give a finished thing another life

You arrive from a long voyage with a striped landing canopy. Before building a settlement, you make one common house. Its temporary shelter becomes the form of its permanent roof, then turns upside down to catch the water for what grows inside.

**Grammar.** Physical shaping and adaptive reuse. A reusable form imparts its shape to a new object without being consumed; the form then changes function. The setting supplies a fictional cold-setting shell material and an engineered removable rib frame. No general disassembly system or automatic multi-output recipe is required.

| Combine | Created thing and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Landing fabric + Folding ribs | Striped umbrella: immediate shade and a recognisable central object. |
| Shell material + Striped umbrella | Cast dome: the form is reused; the finished roof remembers its ribs. |
| Cast dome + Translucent roof panel | Daylight room: a permanent place to work and grow plants. |
| Striped umbrella + Gutter collar | Rain funnel: the same temporary roof is inverted and gains another job. |
| Rain funnel + Elevated cistern | Rain store: catchment becomes a usable supply. |
| Rain store + Gravity hose | Watering line: the former roof can serve the room it formed. |
| Seed trays + Prepared beds | Planted beds: the voyage's living cargo gets room to grow. |
| Planted beds + Daylight room | Nursery room: both branches become one place. |
| Watering line + Nursery room | Common garden: the umbrella sustains life beneath its cast descendant. |

**Story through the creations.** Shade permits the first work. The umbrella lends its form to a lasting room. Once that room exists, the temporary shelter can be changed rather than cherished as an untouchable achievement. The final view can tell the story: inverted striped umbrella, water line, ribbed roof, garden below. The ship's destination and carried seeds are established at the opening.

**Questions.** Is this a satisfying short construction without enough story to grow? Is the fictional material doing too much? Casting must be credible at the chosen scale. This is a physical contrast to A and B, not a commitment to keep the future story physical.

## Comparative assignment

A combines **where things are**. B combines **patterns that can survive and travel between works**. C combines **materials and shapes whose jobs change**. Compare their actual operations and continuing creations. Do not choose by setting, title, diagram size or a vote alone. The next round must contain different responses to what is learned, rather than three versions of the highest-ranked pitch.
