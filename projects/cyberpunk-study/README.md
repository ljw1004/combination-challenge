# Cyberpunk reconsideration

The user played **No Fixed Address** and found the many small circular illustrations and subtle technical distinctions hard to follow. On 5 November 2026 they asked to set that approach aside and brainstorm a futuristic story whose narrative unfolds through the continuing lives of things the player combines and creates. The question is what combination can *mean*, before choosing a setting or producing a full game.

The [live brief](../../stories/cyberpunk/design-brief.md) remains the single authority for the published version and identifies this reconsideration. Its old plot, AI curriculum, circular-token system and approximately 200-combination scope do not constrain these explicitly proposed alternatives. The [first development archive](<../../archive/2026.11.05 cyberpunk study/README.md>) describes the earlier work and its evidence limits; successful AI checks did not establish that its human experience worked.

[Exploration questions](exploration.md) define this exercise. [Iteration outcomes](iterations.md) preserve the candidates, questions, independent findings and decisions. Develop two or three substantially different candidates in every round, carrying lessons forward through different solutions. Reviewers are fresh for every assignment. Concepts and narrative traces are design deliverables; raw reviewer packets/replies are disposable under `runs/cyberpunk-rethink/`.

This is an active concept study. No replacement manuscript, graphics production, publication or new combination-count target is assumed.

## Current result

[Four rounds of brainstorming and review](iterations.md) produced these [current treatments](round-4.md), each with its own rules, story beats, continuing creations, representative recipes and remaining risks:

| Candidate | What combination means | Continuing life | Present judgment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Harbour in the Bottle** | Reverse the containing relationships in a digital world. | Small ship → bottled beacon serving a harbour → ship carrying that same bottled harbour. | Preferred compact story prototype; strongest ending image. |
| **Borrowed Moves** | Copy and compose behavior independently of its body. | Toy's route → useful carrier → longer route → copied pattern or whole carrier serving illumination. | Strongest basis for a longer construction game; test the actual route diagrams. |
| **The Kite With Two Histories** | Fork a creation and merge its independently developed descendants. | One light sculpture → two local compositions → one new descendant, with both earlier works surviving. | Expressive alternative; its two lives need more narrative weight before expansion. |

The reviewers' strongest distinction is between a memorable transformation and a reusable game grammar. A currently supplies the first most convincingly; B supplies the second. Neither has human play evidence yet. The earlier length target is not a reason to pad either one.

The next discriminating experiment is a small playable sequence using a few large whole-object drawings or clear placeholders. For A, test whether players can anticipate and care about the change from harbour-around-bottle-around-ship to ship-around-bottle-around-harbour. For B, test whether the path strip explains stops and return, and whether players naturally choose between copying, composing and carrying. C should first earn more than decorative investment in its two local versions. Those are subsequent development tests, not completed evidence in this concept exercise.
