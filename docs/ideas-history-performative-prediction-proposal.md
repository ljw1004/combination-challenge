# Forecasts that change the society they describe

26 October 2026. **Paper comparison only, not a prototype, playtest or selected next implementation.** Original speculative concepts; no claim about actual contemporary societies or SMAC rules.

## The future-idea hypothesis

An ordinary prediction describes a future. A public prediction can become an input to the people it describes. Combining prediction with communication therefore changes the system, not merely the accuracy label on an administrative record. A society might develop institutions that publish conditional futures, make voluntary commitments, or protect the ability not to become someone else's forecast.

Possible original chain:

- **Behavioral forecasts + public communication → Reflexive Almanacs.** A forecast explicitly includes how its publication changes behavior. Citizens can inspect the predicted response to the announcement, not just a recommendation.
- **Reflexive Almanacs + voluntary commitments → Rendezvous Futures.** People can create a shared plan that becomes predictable because they enact it together, rather than because an authority predicts their compliance.
- **Reflexive Almanacs + protected refusal → Unprediction Commons.** A person may decline the role assigned by an individually identifying forecast. The system can still publish conditional consequences without falsely promising a single settled personal future.
- **Rendezvous Futures + Unprediction Commons → Plural Tomorrow.** Some shared plans are settled while other lives remain explicitly open. This is an alternative to universal individual prediction, not its mandatory upgrade.

Lived consequence: neighbors can arrange a shared event while another person can change their own plan without being treated as an error to correct. A predictable city and an open-ended city are different possible projects, not truth-ranked civilizations.

## Small exact model that exposes both the insight and the weakness

Use two places, garden (0) and studio (1), and two actors with public response laws. These are invented toy preferences, not psychological claims.

- Ada wants to meet Ben. On receiving a public prediction of Ben's destination, she chooses that destination.
- Ben wants a quiet visit away from Ada. On receiving a public prediction of Ada's destination, he chooses the other destination.

Let a broadcast predict `(a,b)`. Its enactment produces `F(a,b) = (b, 1-a)`. All results are visible:

| Published forecast | Actual response |
| --- | --- |
| garden, garden | garden, studio |
| garden, studio | studio, studio |
| studio, garden | garden, garden |
| studio, studio | studio, garden |

No row predicts itself. Repeatedly replacing the forecast with the last actual response creates a four-state cycle. This follows from the transition law, not a hidden answer or a surprise refusal. Installing more copies of the same broadcaster does not create a fixed point; without explicit message precedence it instead makes the model underspecified.

**Important:** Ada's wish to meet Ben and Ben's wish to be apart cannot both hold in the same visit, independently of prediction. Do not present that contradiction as emergent depth or require the player to defeat somebody's preference. It is a diagnostic of reflexivity, not a complete social game.

## What combinations must actually change

A commitment must change a future response rule for a declared event, with visible consent and scope; it cannot be another completed-permission badge. If Ada voluntarily commits to garden regardless of forecast, Ben can choose studio and the forecast `(garden, studio)` becomes self-consistent. It fails Ada's original meeting purpose, so it is not a universal social improvement.

A non-identifying conditional forecast may publish the full table above without asserting that a particular row will be enacted. This is truthful useful information, but does not itself make a meeting or quiet visit occur. Protected refusal must visibly preserve Ben's option to change course; do not require a compulsory refusal click every round.

A richer scenario could include a willing meeting partner, shared plans spanning several events, and changing uses of the same places. That could permit meaningful compatible purposes. It is not earned merely by adding more actor names or response bits. The concrete purpose must explain why coordination and openness both matter and what actually changes afterward.

## Universal-policy challenge

The strongest baseline is **give everyone conditional forecasts, optional commitments, and unrestricted private choice**. It may solve every reasonable compatible goal with one general institution plus a short event schedule. Keep it legal. Discovering all concepts should never itself force actors to adopt contradictory plans.

Also test:

1. Publish nothing; let an agreed schedule coordinate willing participants.
2. Publish only aggregate or conditional statements, avoiding self-defeating personal certainty claims.
3. Give each actor an individually scoped channel, commitment and opt-out.
4. Predict a distribution rather than a certain destination. This can represent uncertainty but cannot automatically certify a guaranteed meeting.
5. Commit willing participants to one recurring plan and leave everyone else free.
6. Provide every facility at both places. If only facility access matters, this may erase spatial conflict entirely.

Do not block these policies with attention points, finite channel stock, forced universal publication, mandatory compliance or hidden preferences. If one is a generally good answer, its conceptual achievement can be worth a short construction without establishing sustained gameplay.

## Comparison with previous failures

Unlike the six-action family-authority kernel, the broadcast changes the next actual behavior; iterating it changes subsequent inputs. A conditional forecast and a commitment have different causal roles. Unlike the V3 causal inquiry, the method's communication enters the modeled process itself rather than only selecting an external stimulus.

But the tiny version is still a public Boolean dynamical system. Once the four rows are understood, choosing a scoped commitment or conditional publication is likely another very short configuration. Calling it a future society does not establish the user's imagined intellectual world. An observation that the loop is interesting is evidence of an explanatory insight, not evidence of a long game.

## Selection gate

Do not implement the two-actor table as another fresh pilot. First specify a coherent shared ontology of public claims, contingent plans, voluntary commitments and enacted events, with at least two genuinely different social purposes that reuse an earlier institution. Show complete legal trajectories under the universal optional-policy baseline. Remove clerical proposal/accept steps unless they are actual choices with inspectable consequences.

Proceed only if there is a concrete purpose for a playable evolving world beyond illustrating the four-cycle, and a reason construction contributes more than entering the already-understood policy. Preserve comfortable enactment and speculative wonder as possible value; do not require every step to surprise or dilemma. If no such purpose emerges, retain Reflexive Almanacs and Plural Tomorrow as ontology ideas, not a gameplay mechanism proven by prose.
