# Six independent Mars levels: preserved alternative

**Direction update, 01:41 UTC:** the human explicitly welcomes a growing scrollable catalogue, leaving unused things behind and dragging useful things into new areas. The preferred plan is therefore again the continuous two-mission world. This six-level experiment is preserved as an alternative, **not a necessary clutter fix**. Total retained item count is not a blocker by itself; active viewport density, discoverability and retrieval costs require separate evidence. No independent-level fresh player is being recruited.

**Design and authored gesture replay only. No new UI, player, fun, duration, or optimum claim.** Deployed `src/mars-named-*` and the Site remain frozen. The continuous and transfer-boundary candidates remain preserved evidence, not silently rewritten.

## What the player is doing

Six clearly labelled, separately authored puzzles share an 87-recipe/126-kind ontology and learned recipe knowledge, with 20 introduced objectives across the collection. These are catalogue/goal counts, not discovery or decision counts. Completing one saves its board and a completion record. Opening the next announces its own starting equipment and task; it is **not a journey with the same conserved possessions**. A water canister in two different puzzles is no more a secret duplicate than a chess piece in two saved games. Novelty is board-local: no second water canister can be created on either board. No packing, retrieval, freight accounting, hidden disposal, reset masquerading as world continuity, or generic kit exists.

Starts can contain intact inventions. These are explicitly supplied scenario premises, not rewards falsely claimed to have been constructed or earned on another board. A player who used binoculars in Travel still receives the declared field camera in Ice. Learned concepts are useful recognition, not magically accessible tokens. Each completed board remains its own saved state; replay would be another openly identified attempt, outside these counts.

The story is a collection of practical Mars work scenes, not a continuous survival simulation. Each scene has a clear satisfying endpoint. Starting the next level, reading goals, automatic supply introductions, and choosing a route are not credited as additional actions. The warm meal is a completed episode, not an obligation to leave food uneaten forever.

Cultivation, heating and photography denote conventional game episodes, not elapsed-time, oxygen, yield or chemistry simulation. Ice photographs make no magical inference about life or passability. ## Repeated mastery, not manufactured duration

The ordinary sequence reconstructs just two outputs already constructed in earlier levels: solar charger in Shelter, now supporting a lamp rather than a radio; solar radio in Travel, now supporting long-range relay. The simple sequence reconstructs solar radio only. These counts concern earlier **constructed outputs**, not every initially supplied invention recognized by the player. They are disclosed repeated gestures, not new discoveries. Ice directly starts with its camera/light so it does not rebuild their infrastructure. Garden begins with propagation equipment so it does not repeat the germination episode.

Comfortable progress can be enjoyable. Recognizing the old charger recipe for a different practical purpose can be mastery; physically doing it again can also feel like busywork. No static graph decides that question. Repeated placement of finished tools, especially pump delivery and relay installation, may likewise feel satisfying or administrative. We should observe that rather than require every gesture to be a dilemma or assume named nouns make every action meaningful.

## Where choices actually arise

With all combinations inspectable, this baseline offers route selection (optical/radio, filtered/distilled water, hand/powered transport, panorama/binocular survey, soup/solar baking), ordering and target-placement reasoning. Reused components connect otherwise different tasks. Choosing where to place the canister to germinate versus cook and orienting the greenhouse harvest combine are genuine state reasoning, though usually easy once understood.

**Most choices are not difficult tradeoffs.** Inputs are inexhaustible, and simple answers often achieve exactly the same stated goal in fewer actions. Longer constructions therefore mainly offer expression, exploration and a sense of making a capable place, not demonstrated superior strategic value. The six headings do not themselves establish six distinct decision structures. Nor do 70 operations establish 70 interesting decisions. The 62-operation control is particularly important when assessing whether bigger equipment is pleasant optional construction or a padded ladder.

The baseline is intentionally calm and clear. It is complete as a six-level collection, but far short of an evidenced roughly 200-decision experience. No alternative-route sums, future level multipliers, unplayed catalogue nodes, supply animations or completion clicks close that gap.

### One bounded prospective comparison: moving sunlight in Garden

Keep this baseline unchanged. A separate **unimplemented** Garden variant could disclose before cooking that the sunny patch will move once from the east ledge to the west ledge when the growing episode finishes. Both ledges and the exact transition are visible; no surprise timer, damage, lost ingredients or finite energy. The actual cooker target must occupy the currently sunlit ledge. A player can wait to set up solar cooking at the west ledge, relocate an already placed cooker, or use the existing galley for soup. The meal objective remains the same; no extra recipe census or forced bake-and-soup checklist.

This tests simple anticipation and adaptation to an intelligible environmental change, rather than guessing recipes. It might merely add a predictable relocation chore; that is a real negative outcome, not a reason to call it depth. It requires an explicitly authored environmental-state rule beyond the present static context model and is **not part of these routes/counts or authorized for implementation here**. Compare prospective whole-Garden experiences against the calm baseline, with inspectable recipes and legal soup shortcut retained. Evidence sought: whether players understand the change before committing, choose a meaningful response, and experience the resulting gesture as adaptation rather than tax. If the best-informed route is indistinguishable from baseline soup and players find relocation annoying, retain the baseline instead. No minimum action quota or hidden penalty should rescue the variant.
