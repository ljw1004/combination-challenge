# The Far Bank: a proposed continuation

**Superseded as the current direction.** After trying Bell and Mneme, the owner clarified that narrative must emerge from game primitives, with little written story. This prose-led campaign was stopped before a completed playtest. Its useful rights/lineage ideas may be reconsidered as reusable laws; its cast and stage budget are preserved as proposal history.

Designer proposal for a separate Confluence version. The played `confluence-2` model, story and captures stay frozen. This is a proposed experience engine and mechanical contract, not implemented content or a claim of sustained appetite.

## What the two plays changed

The [interest player](../results/logical-players/fun-confluence-interest-1.json) stopped after 20 interventions with a personally coherent settlement: quiet exact memory, public living accounts and Ilex’s garden. The [long diagnostic](../results/logical-players/fun-confluence-long-1.json) ended after 42 interventions and 12 annotated decision episodes. Each actor could perform one independent act; afterward they had no next concrete ambition. More local forms, invitations and quotations would have been feature tourism.

Keep the best episode: public village speech reached a separate exact witness record and caused rain. The player retained publication, changed the relation, then erased the local exact copy while retaining the original archive. A creation mattered in an unanticipated place, and the player authored a response in service of an ambition they still valued. The extension should make that pattern recur through new actors and uses, rather than repeatedly flooding a solved village.

## The proposed promise

**Something you made has reached someone you did not make it for. What will they make of it?**

After the valley settles, Ilex wants to build something no single mind can author. His garden gave him an act of his own; he now wants an act that remains his while becoming part of another person’s work. He proposes carrying an actual creation beyond the valley. The player may close the story here, export a creation, change what is offered, or pursue another local ambition.

What crosses is not a generic chapter key. It is a creation with a causal history: which voices it carries, whether they remain exact, whether the creation is living, who may quote it, and what purpose travels with it. The first receiver can therefore inherit the player’s public living account, an exact private mark, a common chorus, a protected garden, or a deliberately constructed mixture. The receiver’s first response and later ambitions depend on that inheritance.

## One continuing opposition

The Far Bank’s speaker, **Rill**, wants a community that can answer as one without being reduced to somebody else’s account. She takes the arriving work seriously enough to repurpose it. Ilex’s first mark becomes, for her, a return address: evidence that an absent speaker can be reached. This is her interpretation and ambition, not a revelation that the mark was secretly a different object all along.

Rill wants a shared call that can cross the separation between communities. Ilex wants to help make it. Mneme wants a singular account to survive the shared call. The Judge wants to know whom its answer can obligate. These are independently attractive purposes. Rill may oppose a perfectly safe, plural arrangement because it cannot speak or remember as the collective person she is trying to become. The player need not accept her project or declare her defective for wanting it.

The call’s success should bring a new actor into the relationship: the shared speaker itself. Its first unassigned reply is a consequence of the institutions the player actually constructed. It can disagree with Rill as well as the player. Thus completing Ilex’s proposal produces a next intention inside the same relationship, instead of exhausting his one autonomous act again.

The core designer’s proposed mechanism supplies an important positive use for a promise: **a recipient will begin an independent work only with an enforceable right to continue it**. The binding constrains the creator’s later unilateral edits. It can enable a recipient rather than compel one. An invitation can be sincere yet leave the invited person dependent on the inviter’s continuing permission; a grant can change that relationship. Later promises can conflict over one inherited work without becoming a finite supply to spend.

## Six receivers and a homecoming

These are people encountered through the consequences of a creation. They do not announce six independent errands. The core should name the actual received work, its relevant source and the right currently at stake in each scene. The right labels below are proposed semantic roles; the core owns their exact rules.

Ilex has become a source someone can answer, rather than the sole maker at the end of a route. |
| **Sen, the Quay of Names** | Rill’s living reply, with its actual lineage | “If we can answer someone absent, let us know whom we are answering.” Sen wants a reliable return address and attribution; a generic public chorus cannot stand in for a particular speaker. | An exact return relation can reconnect singular origins. Its first reply is not selected by choosing a finished output. It can disagree with Tal or Rill. |
| **Ada, at Lantern Reach** | The collective speaker’s work | “An invitation only your members can hear is not an invitation to us.” Ada wants genuine public access for people the shared project did not initially represent. | A beacon extends the work’s reach. Publishing the living answer and exposing its inherited exact addresses must remain distinguishable; earlier quotation and custody choices matter again. |
| **Neri, of the Unmoored House** | Ada’s invitation and the collective work offered through it | “I want what we made to remain part of my life without every later word of mine becoming its word.” Neri wants a right to leave, retain a lineage and begin a new work without misrepresenting the old speaker. | A departure can become another beginning. | **Eno, the Night Orchard** | A departed work whose source may still be publicly addressable | “Let a life continue here without requiring it to become an explanation of where it came from.” Eno wants protected continuation, including a durable right not to disclose a singular origin. | A living creation can return without every part of its history returning publicly. That answer puts Sen’s attribution, Ada’s access and Mneme’s custody into a concrete relation. |

**Homecoming returns to Ilex, Mneme and the Judge.** Ilex receives a work that descends from his first act but has acquired another intention. Mneme receives a request to distinguish preserving an origin from requiring every descendant to disclose it. The Judge faces commitments granted to others that the creator cannot simply retract. The player chooses what to admit, transform, quote, keep apart or leave unanswered in the retained valley. A shared call, a federation of distinct answers, a protected private return, an acknowledged separation and a deliberately unfinished exchange can all provide closure if the actual rules support them.

Each receiver can have a continuing project and answer relevant changes after its first adoption. “I granted the right once” is not an exhaustion flag. Nor should a speaker repeatedly demand a new right on a schedule: a new claim needs an actual new use, recipient or conflict involving its work.

## Four connected movements

| Movement | An ambition worth adopting | What combining does differently | What the earlier world contributes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Inheritance** | Let another community receive something without prescribing its whole use | Translate a creation through a receiver’s living or institutional context; preserve the distinction between source and descendant | The actual exported creation, old exposure, custody and compulsion histories |
| **The shared call** | Make a work that can address an absent community and receive an answer | Couple two continuing processes; a return relation must work in both directions, and public fragments cannot substitute for an exact address | The old paths become parts of a reciprocal circuit; an exact record may be useful while remaining dangerous to publish |
| **Another first person** | Decide what the new collective speaker can remember, promise and speak for | Constitute representation: a shared voice can carry a continuing intention, but its members and recipients need not all share it | Commons, witness and custody now concern a character’s identity, not only packet filtering; the Judge’s and Mneme’s past experiences alter their willingness |
| **Return** | Bring the answer home without making the first valley merely an instrument of the later work | Combine inherited obligations with later authorship; admit, translate, quote or decline a return that has acquired a purpose of its own | Ilex’s first mark is now used by someone else to address him; the original garden and archive remain actual places the returning call can change |

These are movements of one project, with closure after reception, a successful exchange, the shared speaker’s first act, or its return. The original valley remains playable and causally connected. There is no requirement to exhaust each community’s configurations before continuing.

## Minimum mechanical contract

1. **Carry a work, not only a bit mask.** A work records its source, current form, lineage, audience and continuing purpose. Translation can create a descendant while retaining a reference to its source. Distinct origins are not automatically independent testimony.
2. **Give recipients a policy and a project.** A recipient responds to relevant changes in its received work, jurisdiction or accepted purpose. Its project has public functional conditions and an effect on the world. Dialogue alone cannot make Rill independent. Once a project is achieved, her response must be capable of changing what she seeks or what the shared speaker does; not another fixed “own act” flag.
3. **Add a return relation, not a reset map.** A later community is connected to the retained valley. What passes one way need not pass back in the same form. A living interpretation may answer a source without reproducing it; an exact address may reach a particular original that an undifferentiated chorus cannot. All these laws are inspectable.
4. **Make grants and a shared speaker actual capabilities.** A durable grant changes who can revise, withhold or transform a work, and can let the recipient safely undertake its own project. Stable common reference can enable a joint act no isolated local will can perform. Binding can preserve that reference across incompatible local changes, while limiting unilateral revision. A revisable association can preserve local authorship but cannot promise the same singular answer under every disagreement. These are causal distinctions to implement and counterexample, not points awarded for authoritarianism or consent. If free invitation performs every attractive joint act with all the same guarantees, discard the supposed dilemma. Likewise, test whether making a separate copy with every possible right trivially satisfies everyone: preserving a lineage and sharing one accountable identity must have different effects if the model claims a dilemma between them.
5. **Allow authored arrangements.** The player may separate exact private addresses from public interpretations, combine local and shared jurisdictions, route a reply through translation, or decline the shared project. Do not reduce the new operator to choosing “unity” or “pluralism” in a menu. The world must expose intermediate constructions and consequences.
6. **Resolve, then rest.** Responses follow a relevant changed relation or creation. No per-action production, calendar, quotas, forced degradation or endlessly recurring storm. Actor memory prevents simply undoing a disclosure from restoring the prior relationship.

The smallest useful implementation needs one new receiver, one actual exported work, one reciprocal connection, one joint project and one shared speaker capable of an unassigned reply. Build and test that episode before multiplying communities.

## Scope and the next long test

The 180–240 target needs a longer experience than the current eight-place field can credibly supply. The proposal expands both the persistent world and the operations, with the same work repeatedly entering a genuinely new relation. A rough production budget is four movements containing six to ten construction episodes apiece, often with several interventions per episode, plus the opening valley. That is a content budget to challenge, not evidence that those episodes will be wanted or that their actions will total 200.

Before committing a large scene list, the core designer should demonstrate a concrete causal chain from export through reception, a reciprocal act, the shared speaker’s response and a changed return to the original valley. At least one later choice should reverse the usefulness of an earlier relation, and at least one inherited choice should change an actor’s actual policy. The first continuation test asks whether the player adopts Rill’s or Ilex’s project after a valid valley ending. The mandated long test then checks whether the middle and return sustain new intentions rather than permutations or cleanup.

Exact future personal replies need not be spent in previews. Functional effects, legal combinations, project conditions and the possibility of refusal remain explicit. The question is what these people will choose to make of the player’s creation, not which undocumented combination the author meant.
