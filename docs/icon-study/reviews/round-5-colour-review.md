## Woodcut direction

**The revision makes a visible, useful move toward the requested woodcut treatment.** Removing the little ground shadows and stray staging marks is the clearest improvement. The objects feel more like printed symbols on the page and less like miniature props arranged on an invisible surface. Stronger dark shapes and flatter colour also give the set more weight. The hut, botanical page, gear frame, and window benefit from this treatment.

**The broader-cut effect is uneven.** The soil heap has conspicuously larger light marks, and the spool has bolder bands, but much of the mat, thatch, bread, and vessel texture still reads as orderly fine hatching. In places, the change feels like thicker outlines around a carefully rendered illustration. The reference's characteristic marks are broader, less regular pale cuts through large black or coloured ink masses, with broken edges and visibly uneven printing. The revision moves closer without fully adopting that mark vocabulary.

**Flatter colour helps, although the set retains a polished illustrated quality.** Its earth colours and muted blue remain compatible with the reference. The clean silhouettes, consistent dark perimeter, and neatly separated colour regions can still suggest outlined game assets. The reference feels rougher and more materially printed. I would not solve that difference merely by adding random distress: the more important difference is the shape and scale of the ink masses and cuts.

**The game-inventory impression is reduced but still present; cuteness is mild.** The lack of shadows reduces the display-prop effect. The warm cottage, fruit, food, and tidy little objects still produce a cozy mood, and the centered numbered grid itself encourages an inventory reading. The revision feels more earnest and graphic than round 4, but I would describe it as a woodcut-influenced illustrated inventory rather than a close match for the reference's bold, irregular print character.

The reference was displayed substantially larger than the numbered sheets, which limits comparison of fine texture. These comments therefore put more weight on broad silhouettes, ink coverage, colour treatment, and presentation. This is one repeatedly exposed reviewer's judgement, not a blind test or player study.
