## Verdict

**No substantive mechanical or disclosure blocker found for a bounded public-interface playtest.** The two goal predicates inspect different actual consequences of the same public physical laws. Neither the receiver nor the optical model branches on the selected goal. The deliberately admitted universal lit-clear-shell solution is genuine, as is the twelve-combine build-everything policy. Their simplicity is evidence for the later experience comparison, not grounds to invalidate it in advance.

The model is a heavily abstracted construction toy, not an engineering account of Mars survival. It supplies complete shells, fixed reflecting geometry and built-in sealed cable fittings. This is clear in the public rules and protocol. The player does not design seams, pressure systems, mirror angles, arbitrary shapes or a transmitted code. Do not attribute those accomplishments to a successful trace.

- Three additional exact recorded traces replayed to identical states. The thirteen authored focused tests also passed independently.

## What the model really retains

Concept recipes establish canonical reusable blueprints. A copied lit shell contains its shell, solar panel, cable and lamp, with separate installation identity. In-world changes remain attached to that particular installation: additional shell layers, individual source covers and an attached lamp are not silently overwritten by its original `builtFrom` name.

**A blueprint is not the previously installed object.** Copying it does not preserve an earlier installation's identity or custom shading/layers. This version does not support taking an arbitrary modified installation as a whole ingredient in a new higher assembly. Thus it tests canonical structural composition and in-place repurposing, not general nested-world-object transport or procedural capture. Public wording and the prospective protocol should retain this boundary.

## Causal consequences versus an experience claim

- A clear shell encloses a daylight-lit tray; it does not send the east-going sunlight to the northern receiver.
- A reflective shell encloses the tray and sends sunlight north, leaving the interior dark until an actual lamp supplies light.
- A bare solar lamp can signal without a shell. A lit clear shell can satisfy both goals unchanged.
- Adding reflective cover to a previously clear shell really changes the same installation: its enclosure remains, sunlight no longer reaches the tray and a northward ray reaches the observer.

These are legitimate functional differences, not names that merely set `gardenComplete` or `signalComplete`. Whether choosing and performing these short constructions feels ingenious, worthwhile, routine or over-prescribed remains untested. Complete result previews may expose the answer immediately; the planned player comparison must report that experience honestly rather than count mechanical success as enjoyment. Assigned two-board exposure is not voluntary appetite. No human, duration, long-game or general Mars-design conclusion follows from this review.
