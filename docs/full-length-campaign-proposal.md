# Mars Workshop: a first full-length campaign candidate

26 October 2026. **Prospective design, not an implemented game or an enjoyment finding.** This supersedes neither frozen models nor their protocols. It answers the owner's 14:41 UTC instruction to keep building, playing, evaluating and publishing toward a combination game judged fun that lasts about 200 decisions, preferably across several scenarios.

## Recommendation

**Build and play one complete twelve-job campaign next, not another two-to-five-combine wrapper.** Use familiar objects, a common inspectable ontology, and short explicit goals. Give the player shelters, growing things, optical instruments and movable constructions to combine and repurpose. Routing is one activity among others, not the assumed answer to every level.

Working candidate: **Mars Workshop**. Twelve finite jobs share 15 familiar starting concepts, 27 authored composite designs, and player-created assemblies. They span four kinds of work on six retained maps: enclose/expose, transform/separate, carry/support, and aim/record. The proposed scope is approximately 180–220 deliberate combines along a first-visit campaign. This is a **content/workload hypothesis to falsify**, not a measured length or a minimum number of moves. Known designs and entire assemblies can make it substantially shorter. Their shortcuts must be implemented and attacked before recruiting a long-session player.

The experiential proposition is a campaign rhythm: understandable progress; a construction taking shape; a few places where its arrangement matters; a useful completion; then another purpose for familiar things. Not every combine has to be a dilemma, teach a rule, acquire a new meaning, or earn its place through a later payoff. Conversely, compulsory completion and an attractive Mars label cannot stand in for an agent's reported experience.

**Immediate scope:** the whole catalogue and all twelve goal predicates and six maps, one pure headless engine, public previews, an append-only play adapter, mechanical witnesses and shortcut attacks. Implementing the first two jobs can be an internal engineering milestone, but is not the next research deliverable. Freeze UI and authored narrative work.

## Why the old slice cannot simply be multiplied

The recovered Living Atlas documentation reports privileged five-goal witnesses of 37 and 38 combines. The checkpoint separately records a prior assigned play of 29 combines; its missing original capture must not become a new raw observation. Atlas demonstrates a useful way to share knowledge and retain separate boards. It does not supply a 200-decision campaign.

Mars Objects discloses a five-combine ordinary-catalogue universal camp, or one complete-catalogue placement. The progressive-camp wrapper can reveal how a player experiences continuity, but cannot create substantial scale from those same sufficient objects. Eight copies of “pick the useful shell” would not solve that problem.

The response is **more things worth constructing and different contexts for actually using them**, not hiding recipes, withholding the obvious prefab, increasing a wait, demanding a particular sequence, or counting supplied goals as player decisions. A larger catalogue is allowed. It should be implemented as a connected set of reusable structures rather than unrelated password pairs.

## Non-negotiable interaction and economy

- All recipes, properties, placement conditions, sources, goals and immediate effects are inspectable. A public effect catalogue is not a walkthrough: it does not select the player's larger construction.
- Concepts are inexhaustible. No quantities, fuel, oxygen budget, water stock, battery charge, cargo capacity, production rate, action allowance or depletion timer enters this candidate. This is fictional object logic, not real Mars engineering or survival guidance.
- The player chooses **actual operands**. `combine(a,b,context)` covers making an assembly, putting it at a place, containing an actual object, attaching a component, joining actual interfaces, or bringing a movable construction to terrain. A command such as `build("working greenhouse")` is not an action.
- A newly made composite is immediately available and manipulable. Its child objects, connections, exterior parts and provenance remain inspectable. Do not replace it with a success flag.
- Learned canonical designs can be used directly thereafter. A player can also reuse a construction they actually made as a whole. It is not necessary to rebuild its prerequisite tree, cable by cable, on another board.
- An assembly copy preserves its actual relative arrangement, internal connections, exposed interfaces and component properties. It creates new physical identities, not copies of achievements, observed location knowledge, records of events that did not happen there, or an automatically fitted answer. Copying never auto-connects external ports or finds a route to a target.
- No charge for selecting a board, inspecting, previewing, naming/selecting an existing assembly, looking at phases, or deciding to stop. Do not make selecting a subassembly an elaborate paid “packaging” ritual.
- Each scenario has a finite ending. Navigation can offer a suggested order but all twelve cards and requirements are visible from the start. No mandatory `next` combine. Inactive boards pause and retain their actual constructions; concepts and learned designs travel globally.
- Temporary mistakes are recoverable. Undo is available as editing, logged separately and not credited as length. There are no irreversible surprise deaths. A complete, elegant early answer remains complete.

## A small engine, not a general simulator

Implement five related forms of object interaction. This is the bounded law budget for version 1; do not add chemistry, weather simulation, pressure equations, economy, NPC dialogue or a programmable circuit language.

### 1. Composition, containment and exposure

Objects are shallow trees of actual parts with a footprint, named attachment surfaces and exposed interfaces. A frame supports attached parts. A clear covering encloses and transmits light; an opaque reflective covering encloses and blocks transmitted light; mesh blocks dust while admitting light and passage of air. A hinged covering has a public open/closed pose. A covering affects what is really inside it, not every object at that site.

An exterior solar panel stays outside its shell. A newly installed opaque layer between a source and that panel can shade it. Adding a lamp does not magically make every nearby enclosure lit. Broad permissive attachment is preferable to bespoke “this one place accepts this one noun” exceptions. Context eligibility is a disclosed physical/property predicate.

### 2. Source, direction and functional contact

A few directional rays and explicit contacts carry four boolean effects: light, electric power, warmth and water. These are present/absent effects with source provenance, not stored units. All sources recur inexhaustibly. Light can power an exposed solar cell; its cable can power an attached lamp, heater or camera. A reflector changes the direction of actual light; a guide carries it through its declared entrance and exit. No source-free solar-lamp feedback loop creates energy.

Connections are normally **whole interfaces**, not tile-by-tile plumbing: an object plus another object's compatible interface makes one visible connection when the disclosed geometry permits it. The player can combine a whole known greenhouse with a location in one action. Long identical pipe/cable corridors are not part of the action budget.

### 3. Transforming conditions at a real place

Warmth at an ice face produces a water effect there. A still separates water from a displayed dust/salt tag; its actual outlet carries the clean effect. A rootbed is growing when enclosed, lit and supplied with clean water in its required exposure phases. A drying tray instead needs light and air, without ongoing water contact. These are intentionally coarse toy laws.

Use actual places as operands: ice is a place to which the heater or still assembly is joined, not a limited collectible item. A named result is not itself success: a “greenhouse” kept in permanent shade does not grow. Recipes explain these laws; the goals inspect resulting state.

### 4. Whole-object mobility and fit

Wheels make a supported assembly movable; a hinge changes the footprint of its attached part between two explicit poses. A constructed cart can be combined with a reachable destination. It travels the disclosed terrain route in one command; traversing six empty edges is not six decisions. An arch can block the actual tall unfolded shelter; a gap can require a real supporting span. A movable construction remains the same object across travel and later modification.

No inventory slots or mass points: fit is literal small geometry (for example, one/two cells wide and low/high), and support is a relation between actual spans and endpoints. Do not introduce dozens of size values. The elementary collision/support rule applies to every object with the corresponding shape, not only an authored “bridge” token.

### 5. Observation and four public exposure phases

A camera records only an actually visible, illuminated subject from its actual position; the plate retains that event's subject/source identity. A screen can protect a sample from dust while blocking a desired view if it is opaque. A marker or beacon conveys a record through a real unobstructed light path; the receiver retains what actually arrived. This supports surveying and signaling without authored scenes.

Where a job needs changing conditions, it supplies **four fixed public environment rows**, such as east-lit / west-lit or clear / dusty. This is a small truth table, not weather prediction. There is no automatic switch or programmable control branch in version 1. Use actual static alternatives—two sources, two views, a reflector, a movable assembly—or choose another configuration. This keeps the first candidate out of circuit-language development.

A construction is certified by checking the required rows automatically. No waiting beats count toward length. Phase-invariant jobs can be solved statically; other jobs ask for a covered camera that sees in one phase, or a garden that works in both. First-certificate and current satisfaction remain different. Four rows do **not** mean four repeated repairs.

Geometry is the potentially expensive implementation. Keep it to the stated finite categories and explicit anchor paths. Do not turn this into a giant simulator to save the proposal.

## Ontology breadth: 42 named concepts, plus actual assemblies

Start with these 15 inexhaustible familiar things:

`frame, hinge, clear film, reflective foil, mesh, tube, cable, solar cell, lamp, heater, tray, seed, wheels, lens, record plate`.

The following **27 canonical composites** are the implementation manifest. These are toy assembly recipes, not real fabrication claims. Each output retains its actual parts and applies the same five law families. Generic attachment/containment allows unnamed larger assemblies too; the catalogue is not a restriction on the legal combinations of actual objects.

| --- | --- |
| Covering/support (7) | frame + clear film → clear shell; frame + foil → reflective shell; frame + mesh → mesh screen; clear shell + hinge → hinged shell; tube + clear film → sealed passage; tube + mesh → filtered passage; frame + hinge → folding frame |

A reflective shell's exposed facet reflects light according to its public orientation, while its body blocks light. It is not a second hidden recipe named “reflector.” A camera is a lens/plate instrument; external power is needed only for its declared active illumination, not to invent a second kind of record. Sample trays acquire site tags only through actual contact with that site. Parts cannot gain recorded history merely by copying the design.

Provide each composite's part tree, public ports, footprint/pose and exact default effects in content data. The ordinary campaign begins with the 15 primitives; a complete-catalogue condition begins with all 42 designs. The broad route may use most composites, but no goal requires all 27 syntheses. Do not pad the catalogue with synonyms if some prove redundant.

## Twelve jobs, four families, six retained maps

**This is the revised recommendation after a scope critique of an eight-job/64-name draft.** More named prefab variants did not credibly supply more work. Twelve explicit constructions reuse a leaner set of laws; none needs its own simulation subsystem. The following is a complete first candidate, not a list from which to implement only one small opening.

Each map has 8–12 functional anchors. All anchors, source/target positions, routes, cover restrictions, phase rows and goal predicates are public. Two jobs share each map; both are visible from the start. Their achievements are recorded separately, and each card distinguishes historical achievement from current satisfaction. All map cards are immediately accessible. Suggested order is only onboarding.

These jobs cover four families, with overlaps: enclose/expose, transform/separate, carry/support, aim/record. Later jobs need not each teach a new law. Familiar fitting and enactment can be valuable. No goal prescribes a named prefab, recipe count or exact action order.

**Exact first implementation bounds:** six maps; 8–12 anchors per map; maximum four exposure rows; two height categories, two width categories and cardinal directions; boolean light/power/warmth/water/dust; actual part/record identities. Start with a readable graph rather than a tile simulator. Geometry constraints come from shared laws and displayed terrain, never secret place-specific eligibility.

**Optional continuity, not required complexity:** boards retain physical state; knowledge and saved assembly designs are global. Actual travel is within a board. No cross-board historical object transfer is needed for this first candidate. A copied design has new identities and no invented record history. This makes the architecture directly implementable without a campaign transport simulator.

## Why approximately 200 is plausible—and presently unproved

The workload proposition is **27 possible one-time canonical syntheses plus approximately 12–16 contextual combines per job**, with first-use synthesis shared rather than charged again: a broad route of roughly **171–219 combines**. This is a prospective content budget, **not a derived lower bound or an executed witness**. Twelve job names do not establish twelve valuable experiences. A single action may satisfy two cards and is counted once.

Each contextual combine must have an actual object/relationship purpose: place a whole working assembly, fit its exposed source, combine with the local source or target, add another functional part, reorient it, join a target interface, carry an actual assembly, or repurpose a retained object. These are categories to count in actual traces, not twelve compulsory steps per job. No count assumes mistakes, repairs, individual wire segments, traversing empty edges, waiting, or rebuilding a known chain.

An implementation team should now **execute fluent ordinary, complete-catalogue and saved-assembly witnesses across all twelve jobs** before believing this range. That is the cheapest decisive next experiment. This proposal is more plausible than eight tiny shell goals because the actual work includes observation, separation and mobility, but it may still collapse to 100–140 actions. Report that outcome promptly rather than embellishing maps until the number looks right.

A generic lit/warm covered cart, a universal outpost and a portable instrument are explicit attacks. Keep all useful copying. If most goals reduce to placing one of those at a marked target, the campaign is chiefly prefab selection. If the ordinary route approaches 200 but complete-catalogue play takes 20, name the actual mechanism: an ontology-unlock campaign, not extensive contextual construction. That can be played and evaluated honestly; it cannot receive unsupported depth credit.

If 100 good actions is the natural result, preserve that short experience and continue the search: replace weak jobs with different functional goals, add genuinely distinct goals using existing laws, or choose another substrate. Do not split connections into clicks, reset knowledge, disallow assemblies, hide results, add maintenance or impose a move minimum. A fast valid solution is both a successful play and evidence against the proposed length.

## What is counted as a decision

Use three ledgers rather than one flattering number:

1. **Committed player interventions.** Each accepted operand choice that changes the construction or its actual world relationship is one intervention. Mark synthesis, contextual join, reconfiguration, actual travel and observation-producing combine separately. A submitted batch containing ten separately chosen joins contains ten interventions; a single whole-assembly placement is one, however many automatic internal effects it causes.
2. **Decision opportunities and intention episodes.** Record actual opportunities to select operands/context, including welcome obvious steps. Do not relabel every intervention as an independent strategic problem. Retrospectively group a few runs into plans/episodes when supported by the trace or player; leave boundaries unknown rather than inventing 200 dilemmas.
3. **Nonconstruction activity.** Inspections, previews, navigation, free selection, automatic phase checks, rejected commands, undo and reporting are visible in the audit but contribute zero toward the approximately-200 construction claim. Count a deliberate wait/timing choice separately if a later version introduces one; this version needs none.

The desired practical target is **roughly 180–240 actual player-controlled construction decisions in a first ordinary campaign**, with the experience judged positively in the empirical agent loop. This is an operational hypothesis for the owner's “about 200,” not a promise that 200 meaningful dilemmas or 30–40 human minutes have been measured. Report actual totals, the played scope and reported value together.

An efficient 130-combine completion is a real complete campaign, not a failed play. It supplies counterevidence about length. A 230-combine struggle with unwanted assembly is not a successful long game. A player who finishes ten jobs satisfied has completed a smaller experience; record the two unvisited jobs without inventing failures or appetite.

## Required shortcut and variety attacks before a public freeze

A separate mechanical screen should execute, save and replay:

- One legal ordinary-catalogue witness for **every** job, in the suggested and an alternative order, counting shared unlocks only once. Provide actual action lists, not this table as evidence.
- A complete-catalogue witness, and a second campaign with previously learned whole assemblies. Show how much first-session length came from discovering structures versus contextual use.
- A build-all/copy-all strategy. Record first success and effects of adding more, including when apparently helpful layers obstruct light or movement. Do not select only the moment it fails.
- A generic “lit warm covered cart” and generic “universal outpost” used wherever legal. Do not ban either. If one wins all jobs through only target selection, that is the result.
- An aggressive shortcut search: reorder goals, move one camera instead of making three, join a whole assembly instead of parts, use an ordinary object as a span, and skip irrelevant branches. Exact shortest solutions are useful where feasible; otherwise call the result a **best found upper bound**, not a proved minimum.
- At least two cases where the same concept or actual assembled structure serves a changed functional role, and two cases where familiar same-role enactment remains legal. These are coverage examples, not forced player behaviors or pleasure findings.
- No count padding from long paths, repeated phase checks, automatic records, template selection, duplicated known recipes or redundant objective badges.

The source includes no author witness or preferred next move in a public observation. All functional state still appears there. Test arbitrary parameterized legal actions; comparing only a fixed happy-path list is insufficient for reliable previews.

## Empirical scaling and rejection decisions

The parent is preparing a separate long-play protocol. This proposal supplies the candidate, not a competing recruitment protocol. Useful tests are public-only agent campaigns, a complete-catalogue/assembly-carryover challenge, and held-out goals or layouts using the same objects. Freeze sources and conditions first; preserve raw prompts/actions/reports, including short valid completions and negative reports. Do not give a fresh player this analyst proposal.

Evaluate stage and scope:

- **Keep/scale the candidate:** the full campaign has actual substantial construction near the requested scale; reports identify valued progress/construction/completion rather than only complying; later jobs reuse knowledge without a dominant universal routine; and at least some purported contextual inferences are borne out in play. Comfortable familiar actions may contribute value without new insight. This warrants another iteration, not a human-fun claim.
- **Keep the short game, continue the search:** runs are appreciated but naturally finish far below the target. Preserve the ending and publish the short result. Implement a materially different next project or add distinct goals justified by their functional work, then test the revised campaign—not another tiny isolated trial presented as the destination.
- **Revise:** jobs work but attention clusters around a few constructions while others are an unwanted checklist, disclosure overwhelms, or whole-object reuse is awkward. Remove the burden; replace content instead of making every step difficult.
- **Reject this macro architecture:** complete-catalogue play repeatedly reduces most jobs to the same insensitive prefab routine; added breadth mainly creates label selection; the only way to approach 200 is manual duplication or repeated setup; or actual campaign reports consistently describe no worthwhile construction despite coherent rules. Retain any genuinely useful short constructions. Choose another logic family rather than escalating the simulator.

No single score decides these gates. Positive agent reports are evidence about these agent plays, not human enjoyment. Human testing would add evidence when available, but its absence is **not** a reason to stop the authorized build–play–evaluate loop.
