# Combination is the player's action

This contract records the user's corrections after the first flood prototype. It is a current design constraint, rather than a conclusion inferred from automated scores.

## Current correction: the world carries its history

The owner clarified after trying the written Bell Orchard and Mneme prototypes: “I don't really want much text narrative at all” and “Any narrative we find must be emergent in the primitives of the game.” Narrative sweep, beauty, philosophical concepts and curiosity remain motivations. The desired vehicle is the game's building blocks: terrain, an avatar, a foe indicator, a changing block or another visible process. Authored dialogue and successive prose scenes are not the current direction. Preserve those experiments as history rather than treating their agent appreciation as evidence of owner fit.

The owner specifically finds combining onto terrain promising. A place can participate in the operation, including as a catalyst in **A + B at P**. Its properties may enable a combination or change its effect; the place persists. This is a hypothesis to test, not a mandatory ternary grammar for every level. Place and time should contribute actual causal rules, with earlier constructions changing later possibilities and leaving a readable history. Time may be a process or context; it need not become an action allowance.

Extend the praised discoverability to places. Holding or hovering an item can expose compatible places and results; inspecting a place can expose what its current conditions enable. All eligibility rules remain public. The intended challenge is constructing and acting within those conditions, rather than guessing arbitrary location recipes.

The next logical tests should expose the blocks, relations and functional transitions from which an interface could communicate the unfolding history. Compact labels and explanations of actual state are useful. A dramatic paragraph cannot substitute for a world event or a persistent actor's changed behavior. UI polish remains deferred while the game laws are investigated.

## Three givens; disposable implementations

The project keeps three givens from AGENTS.md13–17: combination, fun through interactive play, and an empirical loop testing that fun. A flood, a factory, turn pacing, an ontology, historical setting or research metric earns its place by helping those givens. The original prototype's implementation effort does not justify retaining it. The user's subsequent explicit constraints below further narrow acceptable implementations.

## Gods, not grocers

The user categorically rejects finite physical material supplies, stockpiling and inventory management. Discovered things are inexhaustible capabilities: once established, they can be reused directly. There are no material quotas or depletion costs to reintroduce under another name. World things can be transformed by actual combinations, while access to the concepts remains.

The desired experience is creating a universe from its elements. Creating a capability should expand the ways the player can shape what happens. Challenge may arise from consequences, relationships, placement, sequence or execution. Managing supplies must not become a surrogate for that challenge.

The first finite-supply joining experiment violated this preference. Its resource-dependent planning results are rejected evidence for the requested game, and are archived separately. Unlimited joining can still be inspected as a spatial hypothesis, but an abstract shape puzzle has not established the desired creation experience. It remains a staged experiment.

## The player performs the combination

Ingredients are the primary visible objects and the primary input. Pick them up, bring them together, inspect a reaction and commit the join. The result appears where the join happens, remains manipulable and can enter the next combination. A finished-output card followed by an instruction to manufacture it does not satisfy this requirement, even when it executes the correct recipe internally.

The first handling experiment used a free tabletop and a shelf. The latest candidate unifies them: one movable node for each concept, with both parents remaining and a child appearing beside the contact. No material is consumed or counted. Selecting, dragging or touching actual things replaces the recipe-selection/deployment dashboard. Undo restores arrangement without deleting discoveries.

The shared canvas follows a user proposal that remains open to experiment. It is not a reason to reject every future level with a different physical representation. Pan, zoom and offscreen rendering support a growing board; filling it with thousands of arbitrary nouns has not earned its place.

## Legality is information, not the source of challenge

Hovering or holding a thing reveals compatible partners and the resulting possibilities. At contact the specific combination is explicit before release. The user reports this removes the frustrating search for an author's intended ontology. Preserve that discoverability.

**Rejection test: a challenge must remain worthwhile when every legal combination and its result can be inspected.** If explaining the recipes dissolves the challenge, the design is relying on the low-level search this project intends to remove. The worthwhile uncertainty should concern choosing when, where, in which order or under which constraints to combine; predicting and adapting to the consequences; or executing a manipulation skill.

This does not imply showing a giant recipe catalogue or putting output instructions ahead of ingredients. The current hover behavior exposes possibilities through the object already under the player's attention. Its presentation can be clarified without suppressing the information.

## A human correction to the grader

The first fresh browser agent found pervasive result previews too directive. The user independently praised that same cross-icon hover revelation as a major advance in discoverability, while finding the cross-icon placement slightly confusing. These are different reports, not a consensus to average.

A removal of per-partner result names was queued from the agent report, then cancelled before implementation in response to the user. The original affordance is retained. This is a concrete error signal for the evaluator: agency/curiosity proxies must not automatically reject information that a player finds enabling. Keep the authored-ontology problem distinct from tactical uncertainty.

The user feedback is real human design-review evidence about this feature. It is not a recruited multi-person playtest, proof that the whole game is fun, or evidence that any particular challenge works. Earlier reports stating human participants0 describe the earlier experiment period; they must not erase this subsequent feedback.

## What we test next

The current priority is logical development under the correction above. On-screen discoverability, truthful highlights, handling and immediate reuse remain later interface obligations. Browser-only agents can reveal affordance errors; mechanical API success cannot validate those obligations. When interface testing resumes, record picked ingredients, contact preview, committed pair, result location and the next manipulation.

Then compare distinct challenges around that same interaction. Candidate families include arrangement/joining, process/order/conditions, and rule/idea composition. Explain each local relation, expose legal moves and preserve the possibility of several plans. A paradigm change should supply a new inference, rather than hide a new vocabulary's recipe passwords. [Research across nine games](combination-systems.md) and [transformations/ideas](transformations-and-ideas.md) supply experiments, not mandatory mechanics.

Reject a candidate if its challenge vanishes with full recipe information, the player mainly issues output instructions, or the added system makes handling busy without a useful decision. Human pleasure in the combining gesture and enjoyment of the surrounding challenge remain empirical questions.
