# Interesting decisions, meaningful combining, and work that earns its place

Research return, 25 October 2026. This reads the new AGENTS.md13–20 as a set of hypotheses to investigate. It does not turn the user's shorthand descriptions into verified quotations. The current work remains wholly logical: no interface changes are proposed here.

The practical change is to evaluate **what makes a combination worth doing for this player in this situation**. Informed tradeoffs are one strong answer. Discovering a construction, gaining mastery, expressing an intention, enjoying a meaningful enactment, and sharing a social moment are other possible answers. A grader that recognizes only one of these will reject some worthwhile games; one that labels every repeated action meaningful will preserve chores indefinitely.

## What the linked sources actually support

### Sid Meier: a useful lens with limits

Read the [lecture transcript](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dklassic/GDC-transcript/main/static/src/transcript/WggIdtrqgKg.txt) and [Leigh Alexander's contemporary report](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-sid-meier-on-how-to-see-games-as-sets-of-interesting-decisions). The transcript is third-party; I did not watch the video or authenticate its transcription verbatim. Meier's phrase describes games as a series of interesting decisions; it is not an equation identifying all fun with decisions.

Meier contrasts an automatic best choice with arbitrary choice. He connects interest to situational tradeoffs, consequences lasting long enough to matter, different play styles, understandable information, and feedback. His speed-versus-handling example already supplies a tradeoff without inventory spending. He also warns against more choices merely for their quantity. Importantly, he explicitly limits the lens: rhythm games and some puzzles may be better understood differently. His repetition answer seeks advancing capability without proportionally expanding micromanagement. His iteration answer includes returning to saved positions and abandoning designs that do not work.

**Our inference:** inspect a concrete decision and its alternatives, rather than require every logical combination to be a novel strategic dilemma. Preserve complete recipe information. A unique-solution insight is not automatically a failure, and a repeated verb can serve a changing situation. These are tests to run, not Meier's universal definition of enjoyment.

### Soren Johnson: density is a design warning, not a calibrated score

Read Johnson's [8 Things Not To Do, Part I](https://www.designer-notes.com/8-things-not-to-do-part-i/), including the update and his reply in the comments. His expression is **“Fun Factor = Interesting Decisions / Actual Time Played.”** It appears in criticism of repetitive tasks after their outcomes cease to be in doubt. He also criticizes insufficient play variety and content quantity without decisions. The article acknowledges an error: the criticized game's selling shortcut did exist. That correction matters; verify whether a purported chore is actually mandatory before deriving a design conclusion.

**Our inference:** investigate settled execution and avoid extending it merely to increase play length. Do not substitute raw command count for interesting decisions, or tool latency and model computation for a person's play time. A one-command macro could artificially inflate such a ratio while leaving the same experience. The formula is an argumentative heuristic, not an empirically calibrated estimator. Fewer combinations alone cannot establish improvement.

### Characteristics of Games: access boundary and a related firsthand source

The [MIT Press page](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262542692/characteristics-of-games/) verifies the authors and editions; the [authorized Google Books listing and contents](https://books.google.com/books?hl=en&id=Yyw3AgAAQBAJ) identify chapter6 as *Player Effort*. The accessible preview did **not** expose the relevant chapter pages through these tools. I therefore have not verified the user's chapter6 wording against an authorized full chapter, and do not claim to have read it. Uncontrolled full-book mirrors appeared in search results; they are not the basis of this report.

A useful directly accessible primary source is coauthor Richard Garfield's [Removing Busywork](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/removing-busywork-2006-11-27). He describes necessary activity outside the fun, including finishing already decided play, while allowing gray areas: tactile activity, seeing a beginning unfold, or appreciating an elaborate setup may have value. He advocates reducing burdens without indiscriminately removing the structure supporting play.

**Our inference:** work must be evaluated relative to the player's understanding and desired reward. Tag a reported change from exploration to mastered use to unwelcome transcription; do not infer that sequence just from success. The user's book paraphrase remains a sensible hypothesis, not an independently authenticated quotation here.

### Oliver Kiley: distinguish purposes, without legislating which games count

Read [This Thing Called Strategy: An eXposition](https://explorminate4x.wordpress.com/2016/07/15/this-thing-called-strategy-an-exposition/), especially action categories, complexity, and uncertainty. Kiley distinguishes choosing an objective, actualizing it, optimizing internal systems, and routine maintenance. He argues that complexity may enlarge calculation without improving strategic interaction, and distinguishes opponent uncertainty from mere randomness. He explicitly presents a personal theory/preference and a narrow game taxonomy influenced by Keith Burgun; it is not a consensus that puzzles or simulations are invalid games.

**Our inference:** classify the role of an episode, not the surface verb. Combining could choose a long-term commitment, adapt it locally, solve a construction, or carry out a settled plan. Record those differences. Hard calculation can be the intended pleasure of a puzzle, so an optimization label is not a veto. Additional variables and larger tables do not establish a new skill or a richer strategic problem.

### Xu and colleagues: chores can support social play, in a specific setting

Read the [DiGRA paper landing page](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/view/591) and the [full paper](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/591/591/588), including methods, examples, and discussion. The study analyzed eight sessions across four board games, with nine distinct university students and262 minutes of recorded play. It used qualitative analysis of social events, interpreted through Interaction Ritual theory. Bookkeeping, object handling, learning rules, and waiting supported conversation, reflection, public performance, and shared attention. The authors also acknowledge that excessive chores can be tedious and some should be automated.

This narrow qualitative sample does **not** show that arbitrary repetition in a solo logical game is enjoyable. Nor does an agent saying “satisfying” reproduce their observed group interactions. The paper supports investigating what an activity enables before deleting it, especially when it gives a shared event significance. The user's further hypothesis—that combining can acquire a different meaning through understanding—is promising, but extends beyond this particular evidence.

## Changes the logical fun evaluator should make

These are proposed operational tests, not claims that they already ran. They should augment mechanical verification and replay integrity, rather than collapse them into an enjoyment number.

| Evaluation question | Evidence to seek | Rival explanation or failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Was the choice informed? | Before commitment the player can identify relevant effects, consequences and uncertainty; the information was actually available. | Correct syntax, author knowledge or an oracle preview substitutes for understanding. Recipe secrecy creates difficulty. |
| What kind of interest is claimed? | Separate tradeoff/strategy, construction insight, mastery, expression, and meaningful enactment claims. | Reject a puzzle for lacking two equally good answers, or call any victory strategic. |
| Is there a real tradeoff? | Identify what each plausible continuation enables and impairs. Inspect future joint achievability as well as immediate outcomes. | Two incomparable immediate numbers can both be acquired harmlessly; learn/install every safeguard first dominates. |
| Does context change the decision? | A changed relation or world condition changes why the player prefers a plan, with an inspectable cue. | Renamed letters, changed score weights or a different prescribed output masquerade as a fresh situation. |
| What does the repeated combination mean now? | Player-specific evidence of rehearsal, a new use, a commitment, adaptation, expression or payoff. Preserve a trace showing that role. | Mere repetition is treated as bad, or a designer's story declares meaningless repetition profound. |
| Has execution become settled? | A player announces a complete plan, later feedback is equivalent, and they report remaining work adds nothing they value. | Treat all upfront insight or every correct later move as boredom. A brief execution may enact the reward. |
| Does the combined intermediate matter? | A prior result enables a subsequent inference or behavior; compare using that result with rebuilding or bypassing it. | It is only a token required by a named prerequisite list. The same state is reachable through a shorter obvious action. |
| Is complexity doing useful work? | New relations, interactions or counterplans change the reasoning. | Larger tables, more modifiers, more nouns and longer construction paths create effort alone. |
| Does further play remain attractive? | Ask what *different* situation the player would choose to investigate, and test one rather than solicit general praise. | An instructed agent agrees to continue; a familiar method handles every proposed level unchanged. |

The profile should distinguish **decision opportunity** in the model from **experienced interest** in a player trace. A Pareto frontier is useful screening evidence for multiple objectives, but it neither demonstrates uncertainty nor establishes meaningful preferences. Test whether a simple policy reaches all benefits, whether one global choice is effectively required, and whether a plausible alternative supports a genuinely different continuation.

For density, report raw episode/action counts with definitions and limitations. Mark human elapsed play time unknown in logical agent runs; exclude model/tool delays from any claimed human pacing measurement. Do not optimize a numerical density by making every action irreversible, adding nuisance choices, or bundling an entire solved chain into a macro. Decision quality and enjoyable enactment require separate evidence.

Repetition needs at least three possible judgments: **unresolved**, **supported meaningful reuse**, or **supported burden**. An automatic repetition penalty would erase the owner's central hypothesis. An automatic repetition bonus would retain grind. Ask what changed in intention, consequence, or understanding, then seek an observable example. A report of meaning without a changed relation remains a report; an actual changed consequence without a positive report remains mechanical evidence.

## Tradeoffs that do not require stocks or a clock

These are original design proposals. They do not follow as historical or psychological facts from the sources above. Every local combination and its rules should remain inspectable; concepts remain inexhaustible.

| Tension | A small logical combination experiment | Crucial falsifier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coupling versus independence | Connect two reasoning communities: a correction reaches both, but a shared error source also reaches both. Preserve separate channels for independent checks. | All channels can be coupled and independent at once without changing any consequence. |
| Precision versus coverage | Combine criteria into a rule: adding a condition protects against one failure while excluding a legitimate exception; loosening it admits both. | One safe condition accepts every benefit and excludes every failure in all contexts. |
| Stability versus revisability | Bind an institution to a principle: it preserves intent across disruption but can keep applying a rule after its premise ceases to hold. | An optional revision safeguard can be added everywhere with no effect on stability. |
| Transparency versus influence | Publish a plan so others can coordinate; an opponent can also anticipate it. | Secrecy is merely a hidden recipe, or public commitment has no strategic response. |
| Reversible exploration versus commitment | Combine capabilities into a persistent world relationship whose consequences cannot simply be unmade by learning another capability. | Irreversibility only punishes uninformed first moves or replaces materials with a disguised quota. |
| Order preserves one property while transforming another | A process composition changes which properties survive into the next transformation. Compare different orders under the same public laws. | There is only one mandatory chain, an unrelated fusion advances a timer, or unlimited fresh attempts erase all substantive choice. |

Finite concept access is unnecessary for these tensions. However, merely naming opposing virtues is insufficient. The logical world must make their interaction causal: a connection transmits something, a binding rule excludes something, or a transformation changes a property. If all virtues can simply be accumulated, discard the alleged tradeoff rather than add an arbitrary budget.

## Qualitatively different levels: change the law of combination

The following are candidate paradigms, not a requested architecture or a six-level campaign to implement wholesale.

- **Material transformation:** order-dependent operations change surviving properties. Skill: predicting transformations and useful intermediate states.
- **Formal construction:** objects combine by a transparent spatial or logical algebra. Skill: decomposition, equivalence, factoring and reuse. A single surprising insight can be sufficient for a short puzzle.
- **Causal connection:** combining establishes links in a network; the same link carries beneficial and harmful propagation. Skill: predicting nonlocal consequences and structuring independence.
- **Epistemic synthesis:** combine a hypothesis and evidence, or combine experimental conditions, to separate explanations. Skill: choosing a discriminating observation, revising a model and avoiding a redundant test. Unknown world truth is distinct from unknown legal recipes.
- **Institutional commitment:** combine intentions and rules that change how later intentions interact. Skill: designing commitments and exceptions with persistent consequences.

To call two levels philosophically different, specify the changed combination law and the new inference it asks of the player. A new set of nouns over the same truth-table problem is not enough. Shared inputs can aid transfer, but each paradigm needs a coherent explanation and a small successful/failed inference before expanding it. The ideal learning question is whether a familiar action acquires a new role, not whether the player remembers another authored vocabulary.

## Adversarial fixtures for the expanded evaluator

| Fixture | Required evaluator behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Twenty named choices with identical continuations | No decision-depth credit. |
| All safeguards harmlessly learned/installed first | Reject the claimed tradeoff, even if lesser policies lose. |
| A unique-solution puzzle with one new construction insight | Evaluate the insight claim; do not fail solely for lacking tradeoffs. |
| A mastered combine changes a new world's dependency | Preserve contextual reuse; repetition alone is not burden. |
| A long fixed chain with a moving meter and no new consequence | Mark settled-work concern; animation or progress is not learning. |
| A player deliberately enacts a known result and values that moment | Keep the report and inspect its meaning; do not automatically automate it or declare human enjoyment proved. |
| An eloquent claim of profundity with equivalent state transitions | Report-only support, with mechanical counterevidence. |
| A social-paper citation used to justify solo grind | Reject the transfer of evidence; the relevant social mechanism is absent. |
| A one-command macro increases measured decision density | No automatic fun improvement. |
| Randomness breaks a fixed script but exposes no usable cue | Unpredictability is not informed decision quality. |
| One strong AI solves a familiar algebra immediately | Record pre-existing expertise; do not infer universal human triviality. |
| Greater rule complexity increases play time but no changed inference | Effort/overhead concern; no depth credit from complexity alone. |

## Independent review of what-is-fun.md revision 1, before application

Read the draft after completing the source work above. Overall it is coherent and substantially actionable: it keeps several kinds of pleasure separate, allows an upfront puzzle insight, preserves player-relative reports, and does not treat a Pareto frontier or a short action count as enjoyment. No game was graded in this review.

Four revisions would make it ready for an executable evaluator:

1. **Resolve a system-versus-action ambiguity.** “Combination must do intellectual work” can conflict with the later allowance for meaningful enactment, ritual and expression. Specify that the combination system must constitute the game's meaningful activity; every individual combine need not introduce new reasoning. Otherwise an implementation could quietly fail all mastered execution despite the rest of the document.
2. **Replace source placeholders with the access-qualified findings above.** Distinguish the main decision-making hypothesis from a universal theory of fun, and distinguish direct social-study evidence from the owner's further solo-combining hypothesis. Preserve the book chapter's access limit; do not upgrade its paraphrase into a verified quotation. These qualifications affect which evaluator gates apply.
3. **Define the unit of a decision account.** Before grading, declare the candidate's claimed pleasure and a decision episode boundary. A plan-plus-three-combines may be one construction episode, while one combine might create a persistent commitment. Record a concrete alternative, relevant consequence and remaining uncertainty at that boundary. Counts at different units must not be compared as a density measure. A field for “unknown/unobserved” is necessary when no annotation or report establishes the player's interpretation.
4. **Turn meaningful repetition and tradeoffs into falsifiable tests.** For repetition, ask what would be lost if a settled chain were resolved automatically, while keeping tactile/social pleasure outside a solo logical verdict. Seek a real changed role or preserve the report as report-only evidence. For tradeoffs, compare future joint achievability: immediate incomparable advantages are insufficient if both can be obtained and applied harmlessly later. Report the search bound if full continuation analysis is incomplete.

Suggested additional evaluator fixtures:

- A player values enacting a fully known short result; the evaluator must not infer burden from determinism alone.
- A repeated-looking chain is prescribed by the author but changes no functional role; a flourishing description alone must not establish meaningful reuse.
- Two immediate outcomes appear nondominated, but a third safe continuation obtains every benefit; the tradeoff claim must fail.
- One useful setup action preserves all future endings; the evaluator should locate the later commitment rather than invent one at setup.
- A no-tradeoff insight puzzle and a two-outcome commitment game receive separate claim types before either is evaluated.
- The same action counts are packaged into different command batches; the attention verdict must not improve merely from a smaller denominator.

The draft's largest strength is that it does not demand a dilemma at every action. Preserve that during implementation. Its largest remaining practical risk is that thoughtful prose turns into unconditional heuristic penalties—repetition, upfront planning, optimization, or a unique answer—when the source-informed document explicitly allowed them.

## Narrative, beauty and conceptual resonance: a Psychonauts 2 return

The owner now identifies narrative sweep, beauty, philosophical concepts and discovering what comes next as personal reasons for loving Psychonauts 2. That is direct preference evidence for this project. It is not a universal account of fun, and the designer sources below do not prove what caused the owner's experience.

Two primary accounts were read. They describe design intentions, not controlled evidence of player enjoyment:

- In the official [Xbox Wire interview with Tim Schafer, 26 August 2021](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2021/08/26/getting-psyched-for-psychonauts-2-with-tim-schafer/), Schafer says the mental-world premise permits different styles, locations and rules, then explains that the team narrows them to support a level's themes and understanding of its host. He describes Mental Connection as linking ideas in ways that change behavior, including unexpected consequences. He also connects the game's scale and pacing to telling an extended tale. - The studio's [Chatting With The Doctor, 24 August 2022](https://www.doublefine.com/news/chatting-with-the-doctor) introduces a Schafer discussion and summarizes five level considerations: host, mental condition, aesthetics, setting and a twist. Its written examples ask how an experience can become a gameplay metaphor and how the role of a Psychonaut constrains exploration. I read the article, not the hour-long embedded discussion; no claim here depends on having watched it. The studio's written account supports thematic coherence as a design constraint. It does not make evocative subject matter automatically effective.

The [GDC design-postmortem page](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027887/-Psychonauts-2-Design) redirected to a maintenance notice during this pass. Its search description was not treated as a watched talk or a source for detailed claims.

### What this changes in our earlier interpretation

The earlier cosmetic-variety tests guard against a false claim of new *strategic structure*. They must not become tests that erase narrative or conceptual value. Two mechanically isomorphic encounters can differ in whose story they reveal, what a familiar action means, what the player hopes to discover, or what they find beautiful. Conversely, changing labels does not demonstrate that anyone experienced those differences.

For this project, keep two distinct accounts:

1. **Mechanical/construction development:** new laws, options, skills, consequences and ways to reuse a result.
2. **Narrative/conceptual development:** questions, revelations, changing interpretations, attachment, imagery, resonance and appreciation.

Either account may support a limited pleasure claim. Neither automatically supplies evidence for the other. A linear revelation can matter without creating a new tactical branch. A familiar construction can become an act of remembering, reconciliation or responsibility without requiring a new algorithm. Whether that meaning is actually experienced remains a playtest question.

### Actionable principles for a logical written-world prototype

These are our design hypotheses, informed by the sources and the owner's taste:

- **Start with someone or something worth understanding.** Before presenting a combination task, provide a concrete place, person, institution or idea carrying an unresolved question. An abstract target may be sufficient for a puzzle; the narrative hypothesis needs an actual object of curiosity.
- **Let the combination disclose or reinterpret something.** Its result can change what the player understands, rather than only the world's numerical state. A revelation should refer back to something already encountered, so the player can notice what changed in meaning.
- **Choose concepts for their associations as well as their computational roles.** Combining memory and a voice, or a promise and an institution, invites different expectations from combining anonymous bits. Do not assume the association worked merely because the author chose a grand noun.
- **Use a coherent local metaphor.** The relationships among the concepts, the world's response and the encounter's theme should help explain one another. A twist can complicate that understanding; it should not require guessing an unannounced legal recipe.
- **Write a small arc, not isolated reward blurbs.** Seed a question, let one construction reveal or transform part of it, and make another aspect newly interesting. Some arcs should resolve satisfyingly. A new emergency or an endless cliffhanger is not the only reason to act again.
- **Allow beauty to be a reason to linger.** In this phase it may be an image in prose, an elegant relation, a resonant contrast or an unfolding conceptual structure. Visual/tactile beauty remains untested. Appreciation need not justify itself through a win-rate improvement or a new decision.

Logical-only development permits authored descriptions, dialogue and world facts. It excludes interface work, not language or imagination. This does not require copying Psychonauts' setting, using mental-health subjects, or giving every level the same emotional intention.

### Evaluator questions and useful comparisons

Before play the designer should state the narrative promise and one plausible revelation. After play the critic should ask what specific question, relationship, image or passage the player noticed, what they now understand differently, and what they want to encounter next. An aesthetic report remains a report, but it is not invalid simply because the underlying state graph is unchanged. A vague compliment is weaker than a response tied to an actual part of the encounter; inability to explain a feeling analytically is not proof that it was absent.

Record forward intentions separately: wanting to gain a power, wanting to know what happened, wanting to understand a concept, wanting to revisit an appreciated place, and completing assigned work are different accounts. At a genuine stopping point, an optional next action can corroborate curiosity; it cannot establish voluntary human absorption when performed by an instructed agent.

Useful tests include a shared logical structure with different written-world contexts, or an encounter with a connected revelation versus an unrelated completion message. Use fresh or counterbalanced exposure where possible: once a player knows a revelation, replay is a different experience. Do not infer that stripped labels reveal the “real game” while story is worthless decoration. Compare the promised experiences, and preserve the owner's response when it differs from an agent's taste.

Keep legal-combination information separate from undiscovered narrative facts. The player may know that a combination will reveal a memory while not yet knowing its contents. That is uncertainty about the world, not an opaque recipe ontology. If a preview already reveals the full story fact, record that exposure rather than crediting the later action with a new discovery.

### Pre-application review of the temporal and narrative extension

Revision5 of what-is-fun.md was available during this pass. Its intention–construction–response–renewal loop now admits narrative questions, appreciation and satisfied closure. It also correctly separates mechanically new reasoning from new significance. This resolves the main limitation of interpreting every encounter through decision difficulty.

Two implementation checks remain important:

- Treat an **interpretive revelation** as a possible consequential response even if the world's transition graph does not change. Otherwise an evaluator may verbally accept narrative value and then exclude it by requiring a changed mechanical frontier.
- Do not require **renewed instrumental intention** to validate every aesthetic moment. “I want to understand this world” and “I want to stay with this image” can be intentions; satisfied appreciation can also close an arc. An actual continuing question supports a continuation claim, while appreciation supports a narrower value claim.

The next reviewer should inspect whether the evaluator really keeps these distinctions in its output, rather than merely listing narrative words in a heading. No game was graded as part of this source and doctrine review.
