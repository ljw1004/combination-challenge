# Two primitive-level proposals: places remember what happened

These are **designer inventions, not playtest findings or established fun claims**. Neither has evidence for 180–240 worthwhile opportunities. They replace authored scenes with visible state changes. All recipes, conditional legality and local update rules are inspectable. Concepts are inexhaustible. An action is physically combining **A + B at P**, including existing things as operands; it never commissions a finished remote product. UI work remains frozen.

## 1. Resonance garden: give places a past, then combine with that past

**Hypothesis:** laying out processes becomes more interesting when the resulting traffic changes what the same places can subsequently become. Familiar combinations acquire different uses as one construction becomes another construction's environment.

Seven laws for a first logical model:

1. **Places and fronts.** Square cells are empty, conductor or stone. A pulse has polarity `+` or `−`, a heading and an ingress side. It advances one conducting edge per world beat, splitting at junctions; it never immediately reverses onto its ingress edge. Beating time and waiting are free. They are causal phases, not a turn quota.
2. **Recovery.** A traversed cell is refractory for one beat. Two opposite fronts entering a resting cell on the same beat annihilate. Equal fronts coalesce. These are observable local laws; no strength/ammunition quantities exist.
3. **Ordinary construction.** `Earth + Water at P` makes P conducting. `Earth + Fire at P` makes P stone. `Air + Fire at conducting P` injects a `+` front through an inspected adjacent exit chosen by the player; `Air + Water` similarly injects a `−` front. A built route determines which things that pulse can actually reach. The unused concepts persist.
4. **Scars.** Each successful passage leaves its ingress direction in P. This orientation persists after the pulse and survives rebuilding. Stone does not erase history. Hover exposes it. A virgin place has no scar.
5. **History is a catalyst.** `Memory + Stone at scarred P` makes a diode admitting pulses only from the recorded side. The same pair at virgin ground has no directed product. `Memory + Water at scarred P` makes an echo: every arriving pulse also emits a copy toward the recorded side. The pair is known; *the place's history supplies its direction*.
6. **Recurring forms become things.** A closed route can maintain a circulating front because each cell has recovered before the front returns. A settled repeating connected component is inspectable as one thing with its exact footprint, period and phase. `that Form + Memory at P` lays its footprint around P with the same relative phase; existing stone blocks the copy there, shared conductors are shared, and copied pulses obey the same recovery/collision laws. It is an inexhaustible capability, not a harvested object. This adds a scale of combination rather than more named elemental recipes.
7. **Contact is reciprocal.** Shared conductor cells are shared by every system. A new form can drive, synchronize, suppress or break an old form through those cells. Insulation prevents all traffic through its cells. There is no selective “protect the good result” flag.

A small causal example, executable with coordinates:

- Prepare a four-cell square loop A(0,0) → B(1,0) → C(1,1) → D(0,1) → A. Inject one `+` at A. It circulates every four beats. Let its branch from B enter E(2,0); E records ingress from west. This is an existing creation the player elects to keep.
- Connect another route from N(2,−1) to E and send a `−` timed to arrive with that branch. The E fronts annihilate. Both recipes were legal; the consequence is the two constructions sharing a place and phase.
- Combine `Memory + Stone at E`. Its west scar makes a west-admitting diode, so B's branch survives and N's incoming front fails.
- That same diode rejects a desired return from the east. If E was intended to join a larger return loop, saving the first pattern prevents the larger one. The player can reroute the return around E, change one loop's period/phase, or remake E after a different passage. None preserves every previous behavior automatically.
- Later, `Memory + Water` at E would echo west, sending the branch back toward B. A formerly protective place becomes a coupling/feedback instrument. Its directional role comes from the actual history, not a story unlock.

**What the player wants/works out:** create and retain particular visible recurrent forms, make two forms communicate without extinguishing one, turn interference into a new stable rhythm. Prediction concerns route, period, phase and the remembered directions of real places. A test board should offer an already moving form and genuinely different possible continuations, not a required sequence of circuits.

**What makes a next situation:** every added shared route changes existing rhythms; preserving a result creates boundaries that affect further construction; a recurring component becomes a new operand. New scale is the speculative longer-horizon engine. It must not merely require re-laying the same four cells.

**Trivial-policy attacks:** stone/diode everywhere prevents the reciprocity needed to join forms; a diode that saves one branch can kill its return. Copy-all produces phase conflicts at shared cells. Always-expand adds junctions and new paths that alter recurrence. However, unlimited empty space permits forever-isolated copies: that is valid peaceful closure. Do not pretend it is a compelling long game. Kill this candidate if players regard connected forms as arbitrary instructor demands, or if “always separate the loops” supplies everything they actually want.

## 2. Growing opponents: constructions have autonomous fronts

**Hypothesis:** a visible rival can renew a map without authored errands. The challenge is composing spatial behavior under reciprocal growth, rather than paying for units. This is a stronger game-pressure candidate, but a weaker candidate for qualitatively different later combining.

Seven laws:

1. **World.** A square map contains traversable ground, water and stone. A lineage is a visible moving tip plus its persistent trail. The initial map has one player lineage and one rival lineage. No population, food, ammunition or stockpile is counted.
2. **Inexhaustible faculties.** Known things supply three mutually exclusive tip temperaments: Root, Flame and Shell; and path faculties Straight, Left, Right and Fork. Their product is literal: `Root + Left at P` creates/turns a Root tip at P. The terrain is a catalyst: water turns an arriving tip one quarter-turn left; stone blocks Root but permits Flame. Every context-specific result is shown before combining.
3. **Local influence.** The player can combine at tips or ground adjacent to its own trail. More distant placement requires extending an actual creation there. There is no free remote counter-placement.
4. **Autonomy.** After a combination, or a freely chosen wait, every tip executes its visible path rule and extends its trail one cell. There is no defeat countdown. This candidate explicitly uses alternating construction and autonomous growth; tempo may become its dominant challenge. A collision of the same lineage's tips merges them; a tip entering its own trail follows that trail instead of generating an unlimited stack.
5. **Reciprocal opposition.** At a hostile contact Root defeats Shell, Shell defeats Flame, Flame defeats Root. Equal types halt each other. Victory converts that contacted trail cell; it does not award spendable material. The rival follows these same local movement/contact rules, including its publicly visible fork pattern.
6. **Places inherit contact.** Converted cells retain both headings as a visible crossing. At a crossing `Memory + Root` produces a fork following those historical headings; `Memory + Shell` produces a barrier across them. At ordinary straight trail those same pairs produce a single continuation or a single transverse barrier. A battle literally changes the future construction surface.
7. **Composite things.** Any closed trail component can be combined with Memory to become an inexhaustible whole-pattern faculty. Deploying it at an eligible place inherits that place's terrain transformations. It still contains exposed tips and can meet the rival. Wrapping a creation does not grant immunity.

Hand example: player Root tip at (0,0), eastbound; rival Shell at (1,−2), southbound. After one beat they occupy (1,0) and (1,−1). On the next, Shell enters the Root trail at (1,0) and loses. That place now retains east and south headings. Combine `Memory + Root` there: its two children follow those headings. Put water at (1,1) and (2,1); the southern child turns east, then north, and rejoins the friendly trail at (2,0), where it merges. The other child advances east. Meanwhile farther, already visible rival Flame and Root fronts approach different sides. Turning the frontier into Shell defeats Flame but exposes it to Root. Saving the old trail, advancing the fork, or reconnecting a safe loop require different spatial interventions. The history consists of visible territories, crossings, lineages and lost/reconnected loops.

**What the player wants/works out:** keep a chosen living structure while making room for its descendants and redirecting a competing ecology. A foe icon can express this without character dialogue. A short test must find whether the rival creates attachment and changing plans, or only reflexive cleanup.

**What makes a next situation:** both constructions continue to grow; contact writes new catalysts into the map; the player can capture a resulting configuration as a new faculty. These are causal continuations, not new chapters or new enemy cards.

**Trivial-policy attacks:** all-Shell loses to Root; all-Fork clogs into its own trails and exposes more contact; copy-all shares the copied frontier's weaknesses. But “always counter the nearest foe” may dominate, and fully observable RPS is particularly vulnerable. A player may also seal a peaceful enclave and reasonably stop. Reject the candidate if the response is obvious at every contact, or if a desired defeat can be prevented forever by building one universal wall. Do not rescue it with scarce actions or spawning enemies on a timer.

## Recommendation and discriminating test

Test **Resonance garden first**, using one 10–15-cell logical board and two recurrent forms. Its key claim is a qualitative transition: inject a pulse → route it → use the route's remembered past → combine whole recurrent forms. Place and time supply actual operands and consequences. The opponent candidate risks replacing combination play with a familiar stream of local counters.

The first test should explicitly permit an isolated/insulated ending. Ask a fresh player to create something they want, then show the exact available combinations and let the two systems run. Record whether they voluntarily connect them, why they keep a damaged old form, which earlier place they repurpose, and whether a whole-form operand creates a new intention. If the only renewed intention is supplied by the evaluator, or the entire attractive world is solved by disconnection, discard or substantially change the mechanism. No length claim before that result.
