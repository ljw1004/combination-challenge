# Interaction and visual guidance

This document owns player interaction and visual presentation. Preserve the old workshop's successful feel: warm paper palette, outlined cards, square hotspot frames, high-contrast partner/drop states, native reading dialogs and explicit chapter arrows. Game semantics come from the [Markdown specification](story-format-spec.md).

- The work surface is a compact portrait panel with readable labels, vertical scrollback and visible scene artwork. The reference size is 390 × 844. Phone width must not create horizontal scrolling or tiny touch targets.
- Text-based things are rounded cards; hotspots are fixed square frames. Text slots live inside a named construction, use compact rows, and show named empty-slot hints only when authored. Installed parts are labelled. No result catalogue or click-to-build menu belongs in player mode.
- Hover or press shows compatible existing partners. Keep applicable highlights through a drag, including over rich artwork, and for the full accepted double-click interval after release wherever the pointer is released. A second click must not create an off/on flash between taps. Opening a reader or choosing another item replaces this feedback appropriately. A creation drop must look different from an ordinary placement. Do not preview the future result name.
- Partner hints answer **“Will doing a drag between these two things help progress anything?”** Progress includes making something, installing a part, fulfilling a placement goal, or setting up an unfinished recipe or goal by putting an ingredient at its required hotspot. It does not require completing the whole recipe now. For `Dough + Wild starter @ Warm cellar`, Dough and the cellar highlight each other before Wild starter exists; Flour does not highlight the cellar merely because it can eventually become starter.
- Preparatory placement remains useful before a compound has all its fittings, or before a later `next:` placement goal is displayed. Consider unfinished authored recipes and goals, with missing ingredients/fittings allowed when they can still be prepared on a compatible branch. Only highlight existing items and visited locations; do not reveal future results. Exclude permanently unavailable prerequisites, excluded or already-created results, completed goals and placements the item already satisfies. An item already at a hotspot can still highlight it if dropping there performs an available operation.
- A single-item hotspot operation is highlighted when it can execute. Parking its unfinished input there does not satisfy a separate receiving-placement requirement; it needs another recipe or goal to justify a preparatory placement hint.
- Hover, press and keyboard focus show a useful drag in either direction; once dragging starts, show only targets useful for that dragged item. Two ingredients that require a hotspot highlight each other only when one can actually be dropped onto the other there. Actual combinations retain their full ingredient, fitting and context checks. Amber drop feedback identifies a creation or useful placement; ordinary repositioning alone does not earn that cue.
- A hotspot accepts a contextual combination through its actual occupants. Drag feedback highlights both the hotspot and the uniquely selected receiving item as creation targets. A direct compatible-item drop selects that item; an unrelated card must not mask a hotspot beneath it. Multiple compatible occupants remain highlighted for a deliberate item drop, with no arbitrary combination or move on release. The [specification](story-format-spec.md) owns priority and chapter-surface rules.
- Dragging a thing moves it; dragging empty space scrolls. Recognize drag travel from both move and release coordinates, retaining the pickup offset even when a quick release arrives before an intermediate move event. Small movements within click tolerance remain clicks. Double-tap (or desktop double-click) opens the item’s encyclopedia detail page, even before the goals-screen encyclopedia unlocks; holding never opens one, because a hold can be the prelude to a drag. Keyboard activation remains available. Pointer cancellation must not commit a move or combination.
- Both text cards and icons use their full measured rectangles for board-edge limits, retaining the pickup offset. A dropped tile's whole rectangle stays within its destination panel; right-edge dragging and release agree, and reloading preserves the placement. Measure text wrapping and compound slot rows as part of the card's height, including at narrow screen widths.
- On a successful combination, anchor the result at the drop destination using the inputs' final computed dispositions; see the [result-placement matrix](#result-placement). Animate the birth without forcing a wait. Never duplicate an existing result, including an incorporated part.
- Earlier panels remain in scrollback. Carry uses actual existing objects and preserves their exact board coordinates when no new primitive supplies arrive. New arrivals appear below carried things with a dotted divider when both groups exist. Ordinary creation, resize and reading must not scramble settled cards.
- Center terse goals at the bottom of the screen, without an “Objectives” heading. Reserve space for a checkbox beside each goal so completion does not shift its text. Use a light translucent backing rather than opaque badges, keeping the text and checkboxes readable over artwork. Do not dim the goals while dragging. Items take pointer precedence where they cover a goal. Clicking unobstructed goals reopens the chapter text.
- When all goals are complete, keep them in place while any creation wonders are open. After the last wonder closes (or immediately if none opens), animate the goals away and the chapter arrow into the same bottom-center position. Its entrance should make the change visible, distinct from its ongoing attention animation. Do not replay the entrance on ordinary redraws or when reloading an already-ready chapter. A new background uses a down arrow; an inherited background uses a right arrow. Advance only through that arrow. Reduced-motion users get the state change without movement or a forced wait.
- Described items show a small muted open book (half the goals glyph’s dimensions) centered over their top edge only while pressed or dragged, including the existing 400 ms release-feedback window. For icon artwork, place the book just inside that edge to leave room for the name above. Hover and compatible partners do not show this book. It enters once briefly, then stays still; reduced motion skips its entrance. The cue is available before the encyclopedia unlocks.
- Wonder and encyclopedia-entry readings have no book decoration; the glyph belongs only on the pressed-item badge and the chapter’s Encyclopedia control.
- Reading and wonder popups render only their authored Markdown paragraphs and images. Do not append an item-name caption, title or attribution; an italic name appears only when explicitly authored. Preserve authored paragraph order and emphasis, including on later renamed items. Primitive reading is deliberate. Created-item wonders appear once per applicable configuration/run, can be revisited, and survive saving.
- Save errors must be visible; avoid gameplay toasts for ordinary movement.

## Result placement

For a two-item combination, A is the dragged item and B is the receiving item, regardless of their order in the Markdown recipe. Use their **final computed dispositions**, after the engine protects ingredients still needed later. A preserved A returns to its exact source position and placement; a preserved B stays put.

| Dragged A | Receiving B | What remains | Where C appears |
|---|---|---|---|
| preserve | preserve | A returns; B stays | Near the drop, clear of B |
| preserve | transform | A returns; B disappears | Replaces B in place |
| preserve | consume | A returns; B disappears | Occupies B's vacated position |
| transform | preserve | A disappears; B stays | Near the drop, clear of B |
| transform | transform | Both disappear | Replaces B in place |
| transform | consume | Both disappear | Occupies B's vacated position |
| consume | preserve | A disappears; B stays | Near the drop, clear of B |
| consume | transform | Both disappear | Replaces B in place |
| consume | consume | Both disappear | Occupies B's vacated position |

Centre a replacement on B's former centre, using the actual dimensions of both tiles and adjusting only enough to keep C on the board. When B survives, use nearby space clear of B, allowing room for the new icon's name; leave the player's other placements intact. If exceptionally large tiles leave no clear neighbouring position, retain the result within the board. This applies equally to text cards and icons, including drops between visited chapter panels. Logical chapter and hotspot placement follow the [specification](story-format-spec.md).

Compound installation adds the component to the existing compound, which stays in place even when it was the dragged item. Single-item hotspot operations create their result at the receiving hotspot's chapter and location.

An applicable hotspot `place:` instruction takes precedence over the default matrix for ordinary placement and newly created results. Try its exact centre using measured item dimensions after input retirement; occupied or off-board rectangles fall back to the matrix/ordinary drop. Existing items stay put. Single-item hotspot results without a usable authored destination are centred using their actual dimensions. Subsequent drops can snap again, including after goal completion; redraws, resizing and reload do not reapply the instruction. Syntax and containment checks belong to the [specification](story-format-spec.md).

## Item artwork

- An authored icon replaces the text card, preserving the image's transparent outline and its own backdrop. At the 390px reference width, display it at one quarter of its intrinsic pixel dimensions; narrower screens scale the board proportionally. Unusually wide artwork fits within the panel while retaining its aspect ratio. Keep a usable touch area for small images. The [specification](story-format-spec.md) owns icon syntax and consistency checks.
- A hotspot may also use transparent artwork at quarter size, centred on its authored image-relative point. It stays fixed and receives the same contextual combinations and placements as a text hotspot. Both icon and text hotspots use a plain black dotted outline on hover, press, keyboard focus or compatible-item highlighting, with no white edging or shadows, to distinguish fixed locations from draggable items. Show the icon hotspot's name above its artwork; use amber feedback for an accepted drop. Preserve its connected image when the board updates, and fall back to its readable name if loading fails.
- A fixed location must belong to the scene: a headland is the actual cape, a quarry is its rock face, and a kiln stands in its yard at the scene's scale and perspective. Paint the feature into the background when that integrates it better, then use a fully transparent icon to define its interactive rectangle. Fit the rectangle to the feature and keep it large enough to touch; its name and normal hotspot feedback still reveal it. Preserve open working space and the location's geography on later visits.
- Show the item's current name when hovering, pressing, dragging or keyboard-focusing it, and show the names of compatible partners at the same time. Item and hotspot name badges sit immediately above the image, clear of a finger dragging it: white sans-serif lettering, a faint light border, rounded corners and a translucent black backing. Use the same feedback lifetime as text cards, including the double-tap interval. Keep the accessible button name available while the visual badge is hidden.
- A newly created icon shows its name automatically for 1.5 seconds, then fades it out over 200 ms. Redrawing or resizing the board preserves this lifetime; reloads, chapter jumps and resets do not replay creation feedback. Hover, press, drag, partner and keyboard-focus feedback keep the name readable independently of that fade.
- A selected or compatible icon has a dotted outline within its full image rectangle. An authored `(circular)` icon instead gets a circular outline within those dimensions. Accepted creation targets have a distinct amber outline and glow. Preserve transparent artwork rather than filling the whole image rectangle with highlight color.
- A compound overlays its fitted slot images on the unchanged base, in declaration order. Every layer shares the base's extent. Show only the compound's name badge; fitted parts do not add labels or ghost rows. Retain the base and already fitted image nodes when another slot fills.
- Measure icons before chapter-entry layout and leave space between rows for their transient badges. When new supplies arrive, use available space below the carried icons first; translate the carried group only as far as needed before resorting to compaction. Authored arrivals reserve their own space. Resizing, reading and ordinary creation preserve settled positions. Missing or failed artwork falls back to a readable text card, including fitted parts.
- Primitive icons with authored starting centres use those points on their first arrival. Arrange automatic arrivals around them; carrying or reloading an existing item preserves its normal placement behaviour. See the [specification](story-format-spec.md) for syntax and restrictions.

## Minimal chapter flow

- A fresh story opens on a cover: its leading standalone description image, otherwise chapter one’s background, top-aligned with a centered **Start** button. Show no items, hotspots, goals or popup yet, and no added title/caption/instructions. Start reveals normal chapter-one play and its intro. Cover visits and reloads before Start create no progress save or history action. Existing saved games resume directly. Editing or resizing on the cover must not reveal play; pre-start edits establish a fresh initial inventory from the current draft. Debug remains available, and explicit checkpoint jumps bypass the cover.
- Do not include a “How to play” screen or instructions control. The natural flow should not pause to explain the interface or ask the player to choose navigation options.
- A chapter popup appearing automatically at the start of a story or after advancing has exactly one button: **Begin**. It has no reset, restart, story picker or chapter-navigation controls.
- Clicking goals deliberately reopens the chapter popup. This is the place for recovery controls and the encyclopedia, rather than the automatic chapter introduction:
  - Show **Encyclopedia** with an open-book glyph after at least three distinct encountered items have applicable nonempty descriptions. Its white-background scrollable index shows compact plain text links indented under chapter headings, ordered by the chapter in which each item first appeared and then by encounter order within that chapter. Current readable names open the existing authored reading; renaming or moving an item does not reorder its history. Include supplied primitives and consumed, transformed or installed items, not just available cards. Filter current tokens by applicable readings, using immutable first-encounter metadata for ordering: never include future items or a recipe catalogue. Chapter/story reset naturally restores the corresponding list.
  - The encyclopedia stays in one popup, showing one full book page at every viewport width. Its left page is titled **Index**, with a single crease line and soft shading deepening toward it, plus a slim edge of the right page at the right. Selecting an item shifts the entire book, including headings and controls, to the authored-detail page; the fold and a slim edge of the index then sit at the left. No fixed outer title or two-full-page desktop layout remains. A top-left **Index** control on the detail returns to the index, retaining its scroll position and selected entry. Both pages have a top-right **×** icon with the accessible name **Close**, moving with the page and exiting directly to the game, not the chapter popup. There is no bottom Close control. Escape/cancel returns from detail to index, then closes from the index. Text entries remain keyboard-accessible links without button boxes; compact rows retain at least 28px hit height. Encyclopedia controls and links never draw rectangular focus outlines or focus shadows. Keep real focus and keyboard navigation: focus-visible uses a contrasting text colour and underline instead, including the close glyph. Inactive pages (including their visible slivers) are inert and excluded from accessibility navigation. Index navigation restores focus to the selected link without jumping its scroll; resize and live edits retain the active page without stealing editor focus. Reduced motion changes pages instantly. Direct item readings open this same book on its detail page and show every currently applicable authored reading for that item. Its Index remains available below the three-entry goals-button threshold. Automatic first-creation wonders remain separate popups with their existing Continue and pending-notice flow.
  - After a real move or combination, offer **Restart this chapter** with the conventional circular-arrow icon. Reading, pressing Begin, cancelled gestures and moves that change nothing do not make it available.
  - Reset restores the inventory, placements and progress captured after the chapter's arrival layout, retaining progress from earlier chapters. Reset clears its own eligibility; saving and reloading preserve both the arrival snapshot and whether a change has occurred. Older saves without a snapshot reconstruct a legal entry layout rather than recover historical pixel positions. Normal chapter entry captures the actual arrival layout.
  - If no chapter reset is available and this is not the first chapter, offer **Restart story** instead. Never show both recovery actions together. Both are visually subordinate to Close: quiet text-style buttons without peer-sized boxes, retaining accessible names, focus treatment and usable touch targets.
  - Restarting the story requires a separate confirmation explaining that it discards this story's progress and returns to its cover. Restarting a chapter, even chapter one, instead returns to that chapter’s popup. Cancelling leaves the story unchanged. The final confirmation remains an explicit destructive decision, not a quiet recovery link.
- Reading earlier chapters means scrolling upward to their existing panels. Ordinary play does not need a chapter selector or a within-game story menu.
- Each story has its own URL, with the catalogue outside the game. Loading routes and deployment layout belong to the [README](../README.md#run-the-game).

## Debug pane

- One control at the game's top right toggles the pane. Show a subtly shaded bug glyph for the original story, or a pencil on a red square when local edits exist. The draft alone determines the icon; opening the pane does not change it. Keep it keyboard accessible, with an accessible name, expanded state and usable touch target. Game items take precedence where they overlap it.
- The desktop pane contains the Markdown editor, live diagnostics and draft recovery controls. Chapter-heading lightbulbs supply checkpoints, including restarting at the first chapter. Use browser navigation for story switching. Chapter jumps have no persistent checkpoint label on the game surface. Synthesized checkpoints do not count as fresh-play evidence.
- Keep the header compact: no title, story URL, copy/download buttons or routine saved-status indicator. Select and copy text using the editor's normal keyboard commands. Display actual save failures and recovery notices.
- On desktop, the game occupies its natural 390px width on the left and the pane fills all remaining space on the right. Wrap Markdown within that width. Closing or responsively hiding the pane preserves its text, selection, scroll and editor undo history.
- Below 900px, keep the game at full available width and replace the editor with a compact **Jump to chapter** dialog. List all chapters with the current chapter marked; tapping one creates its checkpoint. Closing the picker leaves gameplay unchanged. Do not auto-open it for a cached draft or a desktop open preference. Local drafts still load and retain the red pencil. Widening the window restores the previous desktop pane visibility without discarding its session.
- On desktop, open the pane automatically if the story URL has a saved draft or its pane was last left open. Remember explicit open/close choices separately from draft text; a saved draft takes precedence over a closed preference.
- Game readings stay within the game area so their text can be edited while they are open. Updating a reading must not steal editor focus. Preserve connected game/image nodes, settled item positions and both panes' scroll positions during ordinary text updates.
- Incomplete syntax still produces a usable preview. If the current chapter disappears, make the temporary fallback clear; restoring it recovers the earlier position until a deliberate game interaction establishes a new one.
- Diagnostics have hoverable squiggles and a scrollable list below the editor, written as `56: [mixed-fates] Stone has mixed fates: preserve (8), consume (5)`. Keep source order and the clicked row's viewport position stable. Clicking selects a diagnostic and shows only its related locations; clicking it again toggles those locations while retaining selection. Related locations are compact clickable bullets such as `61: preserve (default): Fruit + Stone → Fruit juice`. Preserve selection and expansion during updated results where the diagnostic survives. Map source spans through text changes while analysis is pending.
- All bright cyan outline lightbulbs overlay their row without reserving space, increasing line height or changing wrapping. Anchor them to the bottom-right of the applicable text span, including its final wrapped fragment, never to an editor or diagnostics-pane edge. Fixable diagnostics have bulbs beside both their source span and their diagnostic text. Multiple bulbs at the same text endpoint remain individually clickable.
- Every lightbulb opens a popup menu, even for a single action. Cmd+. (Ctrl+. on other platforms) opens quick actions on the caret's row; if that row has several fix bulbs, include their labeled choices. Focus the first enabled option, use Up/Down and Home/End to choose, and Enter/Space to activate. Escape dismisses and restores focus; Tab exits the menu. Pointer dismissal, editor scrolling, resizing and text changes close it. The popup title names the affected item or action, truncating long titles with the complete title available on hover.
- Mixed-fate actions read **always preserve**, **always transform** or **always consume**, limited to present fates. Explain incompatible implicit slot constraints and apply each fix as one editor Undo step. Keep normal autosave and disable stale fixes while updated diagnostics are pending. The three-goal suppression action inserts the [specified acknowledgment](story-format-spec.md) and selects its reason placeholder for immediate replacement; obsolete acknowledgments offer removal.
- Hovering a chapter heading, placing the caret there, or keyboard-focusing its control reveals its lightbulb at the heading text's bottom-right. Its popup offers **Jump to checkpoint**; opening the menu alone never jumps. Use the edited text for checkpoint generation and preserve the draft and editor position.
- Keep the text selectable and copyable after a save failure. Closing the panel and deleting its saved draft are separate actions; deletion requires explicit confirmation. The [README](../README.md#edit-a-story-live) owns storage and recovery instructions.
