# Current UX decisions

This is the authoritative record of all current player and authoring UX decisions: interaction, presentation, navigation, accessibility, tutorials, editing and recovery. It describes the intended experience and includes motivations where known. Game syntax and semantics belong to the [Markdown specification](story-format-spec.md); content-writing guidance belongs to the [authoring guide](story-authoring.md). Link to those rules rather than maintaining competing definitions here.

## Maintaining this document

- Update this document in the same change whenever a UX decision is adopted, revised or removed, including decisions made in conversation. Record small interaction details and failure/recovery behavior as well as major screens. A decision does not need a code change to belong here.
- State the latest decision directly in its relevant section. Replace or remove superseded text and reconcile any affected sections; do not append a dated amendment, keep rejected alternatives, or accumulate contradictory rules. Organize by the experience, not by implementation date or commit.
- Accompany a decision with its known motivation when it helps explain the tradeoff. Do not invent a rationale or turn an assumption into evidence of comprehension or enjoyment. Unknown motivation can remain unstated.
- Put chronology, commit references, research provenance, implementation/validation records and evidence limits in [working notes](../notes.md) or the [archive](../archive/README.md). Put unresolved proposals and evaluation work in the [backlog](../projects/backlog.md). None belongs here as a historical log.
- Consult this document before changing UX and check the final change against it. An implementation discrepancy does not silently supersede an agreed decision: fix the discrepancy or track it in the backlog. Other documents may explain usage or implementation, but should link here for the current UX contract.

## Overall presentation

Let the [game's philosophy](../README.md#philosophy) guide player and authoring UX.

Use a warm paper palette, outlined cards, square hotspot frames, high-contrast partner/drop states, reading dialogs and explicit chapter arrows. Keep scene artwork visible and leave natural working space for the objects. The interface should support recognizing, trying and discovering combinations without requiring an instructional screen or administrative steps. The [experience criteria](fun-and-evaluation.md) govern claims about whether that succeeds.

## Catalogue and story identity

- Keep the story catalogue outside the game, on its own vertically scrollable page. Present each manuscript using its authored title and rendered description, including authored images and emphasis. Filenames, folder names, inventory counts and debugging metadata are not player-facing story labels. The manuscript's directory supplies the base for its relative artwork, so catalogue and game show the same illustrations.
- Open each story at its own URL. A missing manuscript shows a visible load error with **Try again**; an unavailable catalogue shows its failure. Do not silently substitute another story. The [README](../README.md#run-the-game) owns route syntax and deployment layout.
- Identify browser progress, drafts and tutorial completion by the resolved full manuscript URL. Player routes that resolve to the same manuscript share that state; different manuscripts remain independent even when they share an artwork directory. A changed website origin or manuscript path has a separate identity; do not treat filenames as display titles or silently move saved data between stories.

## Work surface and dragging

- The work surface is a compact portrait panel with readable labels, vertical scrollback and visible scene artwork. The reference scene is 390 × 844; the panel is at most 390px wide and exactly as tall as the available viewport. Phone width must not create horizontal scrolling or tiny touch targets. Keep labels and touch targets physically readable as the panel narrows; do not hide objects or shrink text to disguise an overcrowded scene.
- Covers and chapter backgrounds fill that panel using the same uniform scale, horizontal centering and top alignment. Crop the sides and bottom as needed, without stretching the artwork, exposing a blank strip or making the background itself scrollable. When the cover and first chapter share an image, **Start** must leave its framing unchanged. Keep authored hotspot coordinates aligned with their scenery as it scales and crops; unpositioned hotspots use the visible panel’s lower layout. Text cards retain their phone-width sizing and readable type independently of extra scenery scaling.
- Arriving at a chapter shows its whole viewport at the end of scrollback. There is no downward scrolling beyond the current chapter, including during dragging or when the phone's available height changes. Earlier chapters remain above it. Resizing keeps the current chapter at the bottom, or preserves the relative reading position when the player has deliberately scrolled into history; opening and closing readings does not change that position.
- Text-based things are rounded cards; hotspots are fixed square frames. Text slots live inside a named construction, use compact rows, and show named empty-slot hints only when authored. Installed parts are labelled. No result catalogue or click-to-build menu belongs in player mode.
- Hover or press shows compatible existing partners. Keep applicable highlights through a drag, including over rich artwork, and through the accepted double-click interval after release until another target is chosen. Hovering or keyboard-focusing another item or station immediately replaces the previous feedback; the old release timer must not clear the new target. If the pointer remains over the released item, expiry returns it to ordinary hover feedback. Moving into empty space during the release interval retains the released item's feedback until expiry. A second click on the same item must not create an off/on flash between taps. Opening a reader clears ordinary partner feedback. Double-click recognition keeps its own timing independently of hover replacement. A creation drop must look different from an ordinary placement. Do not preview the future result name.
- Partner hints answer **“Will doing a drag between these two things help progress anything?”** Progress includes making something, installing a part, fulfilling a placement goal, or setting up an unfinished recipe or goal by putting an ingredient at its required hotspot. It does not require completing the whole recipe now. For `Dough + Wild starter @ Warm cellar`, Dough and the cellar highlight each other before Wild starter exists; Flour does not highlight the cellar merely because it can eventually become starter.
- Preparatory placement remains useful before a compound has all its fittings, or before a later `next:` placement goal is displayed. Consider unfinished authored recipes and goals, with missing ingredients/fittings allowed when they can still be prepared on a compatible branch. Only highlight existing items and visited locations; do not reveal future results. Exclude permanently unavailable prerequisites, excluded or already-created results, completed goals and placements the item already satisfies. An item already at a hotspot can still highlight it if dropping there performs an available operation.
- A single-item hotspot operation is highlighted when it can execute. Parking its unfinished input there does not satisfy a separate receiving-placement requirement; it needs another recipe or goal to justify a preparatory placement hint.
- Hover, press and keyboard focus show a useful drag in either direction; once dragging starts, show only targets useful for that dragged item. Two ingredients that require a hotspot highlight each other only when one can actually be dropped onto the other there. Actual combinations retain their full ingredient, fitting and context checks. Amber drop feedback identifies a creation or useful placement; ordinary repositioning alone does not earn that cue.
- Drop targeting generously interprets intent: a target qualifies when it contains the cursor or has positive-area overlap with the dragged item's final, board-constrained rectangle. Use measured tile rectangles, excluding floating names, outlines and shadows; fixed stations use their actual circle or rectangle. Among targets supporting an available combination or useful placement, prefer those containing the cursor, then the frontmost target within each group. Incompatible items never mask compatible ones, even when the cursor is on the incompatible item. Apply the same selection during preview and release, with amber feedback identifying the selected useful target. If none qualifies, use ordinary placement, including station membership when applicable. This makes combinations less dependent on where the player grabbed the item.
- A hotspot accepts a contextual combination through its actual occupants. When the hotspot is selected, drag feedback highlights both it and its uniquely selected receiving item as creation targets. A geometrically targeted compatible item can resolve a station's multiple compatible occupants using the drop-target rules above. Without such a target, an ambiguous station leaves the incoming item in place and highlights the alternatives; never choose an occupant by recipe order. The [specification](story-format-spec.md) owns occupant and chapter-surface rules.
- Dragging a thing moves it; dragging empty space scrolls. Recognize drag travel from both move and release coordinates, retaining the pickup offset even when a quick release arrives before an intermediate move event. Small movements within click tolerance remain clicks. Double-tap (or desktop double-click) opens the item’s encyclopedia detail page, even before the goals-screen encyclopedia unlocks; holding never opens one, because a hold can be the prelude to a drag. Keyboard activation remains available. Cancellation, window focus loss, resize and lost pointer capture while a button is still held must not commit an unfinished move or combination. If capture ends after the button is released but before the release event arrives, preserve the gesture so that release completes the drop. Prevent native image dragging from taking over the gesture. Allow dragging between visited panels, with edge scrolling to reach them.
- Drags should be predictable: when no useful target is selected, dropping A onto incompatible B leaves A where it was released, even when their rectangles overlap. Keep the pickup offset and ordinary board-edge limits; an incompatible item does not send A back to its starting position. A pause before releasing must never be required to commit an ordinary move.
- Keep a stable back-to-front order for movable items. Picking up an item brings it foremost immediately and leaves it there after release; newly created items and new supplies appear foremost. Hover, partner/drop highlights, keyboard focus, reading, redraws and resizing never reorder items. Highlights stay in the highlighted item's layer: if B is partly behind B2, B2 also covers that part of B's combination highlight. Save the order with their placements; chapter restart restores its arrival order. If A covers B, the overlap belongs to A: grab B through an exposed part or move A away. Fixed hotspots remain behind movable items. This makes overlapping drags and subsequent pickups predictable.
- Both text cards and icons use their full measured rectangles for the visible panel's board-edge limits, retaining the pickup offset. A dropped tile's whole rectangle stays within its destination panel; right-edge dragging and release agree, and reloading preserves the placement. Resizing temporarily brings otherwise cropped movable items within reach without overwriting their saved scene coordinates; expanding the viewport restores their arrangement unless the player moves them. Measure text wrapping and compound slot rows as part of the card's height, including at narrow screen widths.
- On a successful combination, anchor the result at the drop destination using the inputs' final computed dispositions; see the [result-placement matrix](#result-placement). Animate the birth without forcing a wait. Never duplicate an existing result, including an incorporated part.
- Earlier panels remain in scrollback. Carry uses actual existing objects and their latest positions. The [chapter arrival layout](#chapter-arrival-layout) reconciles objects brought from different chapters while protecting fixed working places and space for new supplies. Ordinary creation, resize, reading and reload preserve settled positions.

## Chapter arrival layout

- Use the same measured layout for icons and text cards, including wrapped labels and compound rows. Work within the visible panel and above the measured goals, with breathing room above the goals and below new supplies. Do not shrink artwork or text to disguise crowding.
- Place newly supplied primitives with authored starting centres exactly at those points. These anchors reserve space before automatic placement and may overlap hotspots. Their authored starts apply only on first arrival; subsequently they are ordinary retained objects.
- When a chapter has no explicit background, it inherits the previous background and hotspots. Items already occupying those inherited hotspots retain their exact positions and hotspot membership. Treat them as fixed obstacles for other items. A chapter with an explicit background does not receive this exception.
- Plan a concrete arrangement for all remaining new supplies near the bottom before placing retained objects. Reserve its region and a gap above it. Try alternative row arrangements and higher reservations when stations obstruct the bottom. If a separate lower group cannot fit, use other clear pockets before compromising hotspot clearance.
- Place retained objects by their last occupied chapter, starting with the previous visited chapter and working backwards. Use their latest coordinates, not their original creation positions. Within each group prefer the least displacement; among equally distant placements prefer one closer to the visible panel centre. Deterministic alternative arrangements help avoid an early small object blocking the only space for a large one.
- Normally allow at most 10% of any item's measured rectangle to intersect the union of other item rectangles. Check the existing objects' budgets as well as the arriving object's: several small overlaps must not conceal much of a small object. Try alternative arrangements before gradually relaxing item overlap. New-supply reservations remain protected. Fixed authored arrangements can already exceed this allowance; automatic placement must not increase that excess.
- If any new primitives arrive, retained objects from before the previous visited chapter must fit entirely within the visible panel's upper two-thirds, subject also to the supply reservation. Keep valid old positions where possible; relocate rectangles extending below that boundary into available upper space. If an object cannot fit there, use the remaining unreserved working area. Without new primitives, omit the two-thirds restriction and supply reservation, but still reconcile collisions.
- Automatic arrivals must clear hotspots completely. Only new authored primitive starts and occupants of inherited hotspots are exempt. Item crowding, optional spacing and failure of one placement attempt are not reasons to cover a hotspot; only a station arrangement leaving no possible space for the item permits that last-resort failure. Circular hotspots use their circular interactive area for this check; other hotspots use rectangles.
- After placing retained objects, lift the planned new supplies to a short distance below them when possible, checking the new positions against hotspots and items again. Keep a dotted divider labelled **new items** only while all new supplies are below retained objects with a clear gap. The divider identifies actual new supplies. Save the final positions and capture them for chapter restart; ordinary redraw, reload and reading do not re-run arrival layout.


## Goals and readings

- Center terse goals at the bottom of the screen, without an “Objectives” heading. Reserve space for a checkbox beside each goal so completion does not shift its text. Use a light translucent backing rather than opaque badges, keeping the text and checkboxes readable over artwork. Do not dim the goals while dragging. Items take pointer precedence where they cover a goal. Clicking unobstructed goals reopens the chapter text.
- Show the current stage of each sequential goal chain, replacing an achieved intermediate with its next task and retaining a checked final stage. Keep the wording of a partly satisfied multi-condition goal intact. Gold on an item identifies a current witness of a displayed completed goal in the active chapter, not permanent achievement status; moving a placement witness away removes that visual status without undoing the recorded achievement. The [specification](story-format-spec.md) owns goal latching, chain requirements and simultaneous-goal limits. Staged goals keep attention on the next useful work without prohibiting early discovery.
- When all goals are complete, keep them in place while any creation wonders are open. After the last wonder closes (or immediately if none opens), animate the goals away and the chapter arrow into the same bottom-center position. Its entrance should make the change visible, distinct from its ongoing attention animation. Do not replay the entrance on ordinary redraws or when reloading an already-ready chapter. A new background uses a down arrow; an inherited background uses a right arrow. Advance only through that arrow. Reduced-motion users get the state change without movement or a forced wait.
- Described items show a small muted open book (half the goals glyph’s dimensions) centered over their top edge only while pressed or dragged, including the existing 400 ms release-feedback window. For icon artwork, place the book just inside that edge to leave room for the name above. Hover and compatible partners do not show this book. It enters once briefly, then stays still; reduced motion skips its entrance. The cue is available before the encyclopedia unlocks.
- Wonder and encyclopedia-entry readings have no book decoration; the glyph belongs only on the pressed-item badge and the chapter’s Encyclopedia control.
- Reading and wonder popups render only their authored Markdown paragraphs and images. Do not append an item-name caption, title or attribution; an italic name appears only when explicitly authored. Chapter prose likewise gets no generated chapter heading or duplicated goal checklist. Preserve authored paragraph order and emphasis, including on later renamed items. Use readable serif prose and fit illustrations within the reading area without cropping their content. Primitive reading is deliberate. Created-item wonders appear once per applicable configuration/run, can be revisited, and survive saving.
- Keep readings within the game area, scroll long content, and leave the desktop editor usable alongside them. Dismissal and returning from a reading preserve the board's arrangement and scroll position. Manual reading does not create another discovery or acknowledge an unrelated pending wonder. Creation wonders have a clear **Continue** action and never require waiting for an animation. Avoid gameplay toasts for ordinary movement; visible failures belong to [persistence and recovery](#persistence-and-draft-recovery).

## Result placement

For a two-item combination, A is the dragged item and B is the receiving item, regardless of their order in the Markdown recipe. Use their **final computed dispositions**, after the engine protects ingredients still needed later. A preserved A returns to its exact source position and placement; a preserved B stays put.

| Dragged A | Receiving B | What remains | Where C appears |
|---|---|---|---|
| preserve | preserve | A returns; B stays | Near the drop, clear of B |
| preserve | transform | A returns; B disappears | Replaces B in place |
| preserve | consume | A returns; B disappears | Occupies B's vacated position |
| transform | preserve | A disappears; B stays | Near the drop, clear of B |
| transform | transform | Both disappear | Replaces B in place |
| transform | consume | Both disappear | Occupies B's vacated position |
| consume | preserve | A disappears; B stays | Near the drop, clear of B |
| consume | transform | Both disappear | Replaces B in place |
| consume | consume | Both disappear | Occupies B's vacated position |

Centre a replacement on B's former centre, using the actual dimensions of both tiles and adjusting only enough to keep C on the board. When B survives, use nearby space clear of B and other available items, allowing room for the new icon's name; leave the player's other placements intact. This keeps a newly made ingredient from concealing a different ingredient needed for the next operation. Search nearby free space before accepting an overlap; if exceptionally large tiles leave no clear position, retain the result within the board. This applies equally to text cards and icons, including drops between visited chapter panels. Logical chapter and hotspot placement follow the [specification](story-format-spec.md).

Compound installation adds the component to the existing compound, which stays in place even when it was the dragged item. Single-item hotspot operations create their result at the receiving hotspot's chapter and location.

An applicable hotspot `place:` instruction takes precedence over the default matrix for ordinary placement and newly created results. Try its exact centre using measured item dimensions after input retirement; occupied or off-board rectangles fall back to the matrix/ordinary drop. Existing items stay put. Single-item hotspot results without a usable authored destination are centred using their actual dimensions. Subsequent drops can snap again, including after goal completion; redraws, resizing and reload do not reapply the instruction. Syntax and containment checks belong to the [specification](story-format-spec.md).

## Item artwork

- Settlement uses rough, rustic coloured woodcuts with generous natural blank space in its backgrounds. Keep its opening orchard, cooking clearing, shelter and house on the home bank; the footbridge crosses away from that ground. This geography keeps later visits coherent. Other stories retain their authored visual identities; the [icon reference collections](../references/icon-sets/) are author references, not a player-facing style selector.
- Cookbook introductions are brief captions. A title normally occupies its own italic paragraph, without a trailing full stop; put the menu, occasion or expression of appetite in the next paragraph. A title earns its place by naming the subject or marking an occasion, especially when the following text is only a menu or an invitation. It may be omitted when the opening already provides that orientation; do not invent one merely to fill a template. The opening follows the user’s wording: “Write a cookbook. Make doughs, sauces, fillings, pastries and cakes.” Let play convey the methods; do not narrate combinations, explain mechanisms or append instructional commentary. Only occasionally add a further, separate paragraph with a useful principle of culinary judgment, such as “Add variety through texture” or “Balance rich with plain.” Keep it to one or two easy sentences, each earning its place. A principle should help the player judge food beyond the current recipe. Roughly one every five to ten chapters is a sparsity check, not a quota; most chapters need none. Keep these thoughts in chapter prose, without new creation popups. Its serving beats are breakfast, a tiered afternoon tea about one-third through the story, and a final feast table. The early tea spread is intended to give the player an accomplishment before the longer cooking sequence ahead. Preparation chapters produce foods rather than a preassembled tea plate. Afternoon tea has two independent goals: fill the compound stand and brew a pot of tea. It uses one heating hotspot and no delivery target; the pot stays separate from the stand. The final compound table receives the dinner dishes in their own visible places, with completion determined by the spread itself rather than a trolley delivery. Keep that completed table visible on the closing page. Preserve the observational Cook’s Pen drawings and restrained washes. Its ingredients, preparations and apparatus follow a physically informed size hierarchy: small standalone objects have a 60px minimum longest edge at the reference viewport, while bowls, jugs and long tools are larger. Compress unusually large equipment; the 320×260px feast table and 220×240px tiered tea stand use smaller fitted servings within their registered canvases. Keep each serving distinct, including both optional feast cakes. Use finished Cook’s Pen illustrations for the revised dishes and apparatus. Each cooking chapter has a background named for its culinary theme, drawn from its description and goals: ingredients, techniques, finished dishes or related possibilities. Loose, faint thematic border art runs along the top, replacing isolated upper objects that can look draggable. A shallow cookbook spread occupies the bottom, leaving a broad quiet working field; its height may vary naturally between pages. Both ornamental areas use similarly subdued pen lines and washes. Compose the lower spread as a connected setting with shared dishes, utensils, overlapping forms and food traces. Its foreground continues beyond the bottom edge, with substantial forms visibly cropped there; do not leave a blank strip beneath a complete still life. Use tablecloths sparingly to connect arrangements that otherwise resemble separate icons; boards, trays, plates, marble and other natural settings can provide that context. Vary the pale working surface and its temperature, including cool marble for pastry, while preserving icon contrast. Keep the sides open and both ornamental areas tolerant of cropping. Afternoon tea and the final feast are exceptions to the cookbook-page composition: these are serving occasions in a home parlour and home dining room. Draw furnished rooms across the whole background, with architecture, textiles and domestic details, while keeping the working surfaces quiet enough for the movable food. The tea stand starts on a painted tea table; the dinner table remains the movable compound, so its background leaves the central floor open. These places mark the reward for the cooking and should feel hospitable rather than like additional recipe pages. Cookbook’s fixed-station treatment uses spare monochrome pencil schematics in the upper page, visually distinct from the coloured movable ingredients. Keep each chapter to at most two hotspots. Fixed apparatus such as the fruit press and churns are hotspots; the breakfast cooker is a simple cookstove. Name hob and oven uses explicitly. Where the same stove drawing serves either role, place saucepan results toward its top and baked results toward its lower part. Keep sauce intermediates in saucepans. Contextual heat should represent a meaningful or otherwise confusing step, such as adding milk to the hot roux, without adding heating gestures to every warm preparation. Center a single station above the working area; separate a pair enough to leave a clear gap between their drawings and receiving rectangles. Do not print station names into the background: ordinary game feedback supplies the name without a competing label. Register each transparent receiving rectangle to its own equipment contour, leaving comfortable space around the drawing rather than hugging its strokes. Rectangular shelves and benches need room on all four sides; judge the visible padding as well as the numeric bounds. Check the outline and name on hover and touch. Ordinary names and recipe context supply distinctions that food appearance cannot establish, such as fermentation, exact powder identity or processing history.
- An authored icon replaces the text card, preserving the image's transparent outline and its own backdrop. At the 390 × 844 reference viewport, display it at one quarter of its intrinsic pixel dimensions; other viewports use the scene's uniform scale described in [work surface and dragging](#work-surface-and-dragging). Unusually wide artwork fits within the panel while retaining its aspect ratio. Keep a usable touch area for small images. The [specification](story-format-spec.md) owns icon syntax and consistency checks.
- A hotspot may also use transparent artwork at quarter size, centred on its authored image-relative point. It stays fixed and receives the same contextual combinations and placements as a text hotspot. Both icon and text hotspots use a plain black dotted outline on hover, press, keyboard focus or compatible-item highlighting, with no white edging or shadows, to distinguish fixed locations from draggable items. Show the icon hotspot's name above its artwork; use amber feedback for an accepted drop. Preserve its connected image when the board updates, and fall back to its readable name if loading fails.
- A fixed location must belong to the scene: a headland is the actual cape, a quarry is its rock face, and a kiln stands in its yard at the scene's scale and perspective. Paint the feature into the background when that integrates it better, then use a fully transparent icon to define its interactive rectangle. Fit the rectangle to the feature and keep it large enough to touch; its name and normal hotspot feedback still reveal it. Preserve open working space and the location's geography on later visits.
- Show the item's current name when hovering, pressing, dragging or keyboard-focusing it, and show the names of compatible partners at the same time. Item and hotspot name badges sit immediately above the image, clear of a finger dragging it: white sans-serif lettering, a faint light border, rounded corners and a translucent black backing. Use the same feedback lifetime as text cards, including the double-tap interval. Keep the accessible button name available while the visual badge is hidden.
- A newly created icon shows its name automatically for 1.5 seconds, then fades it out over 200 ms. Redrawing or resizing the board preserves this lifetime; reloads, chapter jumps and resets do not replay creation feedback. Hover, press, drag, partner and keyboard-focus feedback keep the name readable independently of that fade.
- A selected or compatible icon has a dotted outline within its full image rectangle. An authored `(circular)` icon instead gets a circular outline within those dimensions. Accepted creation targets have a distinct amber outline and glow. Preserve transparent artwork rather than filling the whole image rectangle with highlight color.
- A circular hotspot receives pointer hits and drops within its centred circle, whose diameter is the smaller displayed image dimension, enlarged when necessary to retain a 44px minimum touch diameter. Its rectangular corners do not activate it. Use the same shape for arrival clearance and authored destination validation. Other hotspots retain rectangular hit areas; transparent pixels remain interactive within the applicable shape.
- A compound overlays its fitted slot images on the unchanged base, in declaration order. Every layer shares the base's extent. Show only the compound's name badge; fitted parts do not add labels or ghost rows. Retain the base and already fitted image nodes when another slot fills.
- Measure icons before [chapter arrival layout](#chapter-arrival-layout) and leave space between supply rows for their transient badges. Missing or failed artwork falls back to a readable text card, including fitted parts.
- Primitive icons with authored starting centres use those points on their first arrival. Arrange automatic arrivals around them; carrying or reloading an existing item preserves its normal placement behaviour. See the [specification](story-format-spec.md) for syntax and restrictions.

## Minimal chapter flow

- A fresh story opens on a cover: its leading standalone description image, otherwise chapter one’s background, top-aligned with a centered **Start** button. Show no items, hotspots, goals or popup yet, and no added title/caption/instructions. Start reveals normal chapter-one play and its intro. Cover visits and reloads before Start create no progress save or history action. Existing saved games resume directly. Editing or resizing on the cover must not reveal play; pre-start edits establish a fresh initial inventory from the current draft. Debug remains available, and explicit checkpoint jumps bypass the cover.
- Do not include a “How to play” screen or instructions control. The natural flow should not pause to explain the interface or ask the player to choose navigation options.
- A chapter popup appearing automatically at the start of a story or after advancing has exactly one button: **Begin**. It has no reset, restart, story picker or chapter-navigation controls.
- Clicking goals deliberately reopens the chapter popup. This is the place for recovery controls and the encyclopedia, rather than the automatic chapter introduction:
  - Offer the [encyclopedia](#encyclopedia) when its discovery threshold is met.
  - After a real move or combination, offer **Restart this chapter** with the conventional circular-arrow icon. Reading, pressing Begin, cancelled gestures and moves that change nothing do not make it available.
  - Reset restores the inventory, placements and progress captured after the chapter's arrival layout, retaining progress from earlier chapters. Reset clears its own eligibility; saving and reloading preserve both the arrival snapshot and whether a change has occurred. Older saves without a snapshot reconstruct a legal entry layout rather than recover historical pixel positions. Normal chapter entry captures the actual arrival layout.
  - If no chapter reset is available, offer **Restart story**, including at the start of chapter one. This allows returning to Start and replaying onboarding without first progressing through the story. Never show both recovery actions together. Both are visually subordinate to Close: quiet text-style buttons without peer-sized boxes, retaining accessible names, focus treatment and usable touch targets.
  - Restarting the story requires a separate confirmation explaining that it discards this story's progress, clears both of its tutorial flags and returns to its cover and **Start** button. Keep its Markdown draft and other stories' progress. Restarting a chapter, even chapter one, instead returns to that chapter’s popup and preserves tutorial completion. Cancelling leaves the story unchanged. The final confirmation remains an explicit destructive decision, not a quiet recovery link.
- Reading earlier chapters means scrolling upward to their existing panels. Ordinary play does not need a chapter selector or a within-game story menu.
- Chapters whose goals were achieved before entry follow the specification's skip/bridge rules: do not force an already-solved task or routine acknowledgment. Preserve applicable authored bridge prose in the next unfinished introduction. A partly complete chapter still presents its remaining work. At completion, show the authored ending when present and **The End** without automatically restarting or adding a new task.
- Story selection and URL identity follow [catalogue and story identity](#catalogue-and-story-identity).

## Encyclopedia

- Show **Encyclopedia** with an open-book glyph in the deliberately opened chapter popup after at least three distinct encountered items have applicable nonempty descriptions. Direct item readings open the same book on the detail page even below that threshold, with Index available. Automatic first-creation wonders use their separate Continue and pending-notice flow.
- The white, scrollable index contains compact plain text links indented under chapter headings, ordered by the chapter in which each item first appeared and then encounter order. Include supplied primitives and consumed, transformed or installed items with currently applicable readings. Use current readable names; renaming, carrying or moving an item must not reorder its discovery history. Never include future items or a recipe catalogue. Reset restores the corresponding discoveries. When saved or edited history cannot identify an encounter chapter, group it under **Earlier discoveries** rather than guessing from its present location.
- Show one full book page at every viewport width. The left page is titled **Index**, with a single crease line, soft shading deepening toward it, and a slim edge of the right page. Selecting an entry shifts the entire book, including headings and controls, to the authored detail; the fold and a slim edge of the index then sit at the left. Keep headings on their pages and show no fixed outer title. Reduced motion changes pages instantly.
- The detail's top-left **Index** control returns to the list with its scroll position and selected entry retained. Both pages have a top-right **×** with accessible name **Close**, moving with the page and exiting directly to the game. There is no bottom Close control. Escape/cancel returns from detail to index, then closes from the index.
- Entries remain keyboard-accessible links without button boxes; compact rows retain at least 28px hit height. Controls and links use contrasting text colour and an underline for visible keyboard focus, including the close glyph, with no rectangular focus outlines or focus shadows. Inactive pages and their visible slivers are inert and excluded from accessibility navigation; their scrollbar must not appear as an active control.
- Index navigation restores focus to the selected link without jumping its scroll. Resize and live edits retain the active page without stealing editor focus. Show every currently applicable authored reading for the selected item, preserving the authored content rules in [goals and readings](#goals-and-readings).

## Contextual tutorials

- Each manuscript supplies exactly one `(tutorial)` recipe in its first chapter, immediately executable with its opening inputs and no preparation. This ensures the demonstration offers a valid first move. The [specification](story-format-spec.md) owns marker syntax, directed operand selection and lint errors for count, chapter and readiness violations.
- After Start and the first chapter introduction have closed, demonstrate that recipe's directed drag unless this story's drag tutorial is already complete: for `A + B -> C`, drag A onto B; an item/hotspot recipe targets the hotspot. A thin curved arrow connects the live source and target, a translucent copy of the source demonstrates pickup/travel/release, and the target gets a restrained outline. The real objects remain still and usable; the demonstration never creates or previews the result. Use the object itself to demonstrate the action without obscuring play with a large animated hand or instructional text.
- Start each demonstration with the translucent copy exactly over the original, matching its bounds, text alignment, slot rows and artwork before travelling. Preserve fractional dimensions and refresh sizing and typography after resizing so the copy still appears to lift from that object on narrow screens.
- Start's pointer input selects a small mouse cursor or a touch/pen contact ring. Later pointer input updates the cue for hybrid devices. Keyboard or unknown activation starts with a neutral contact ring until pointer input is available; this does not introduce new keyboard combination controls.

Ordinary movement does not complete it.
- Next, gently brighten the goals' backing and demonstrate a single click/tap in place, with a quiet pause before repeating. - Remember the two accomplishments independently in browser local storage for each resolved manuscript URL, separately from chapter/game snapshots and manuscript fingerprints. Reloads, chapter restart and live edits preserve completion. Confirmed story restart clears both; cancelling it does not. For older saved games, an actual recorded combination supplies evidence for drag completion; never infer an unrecorded goals click. Explicit debug checkpoint jumps suppress cues across reload without awarding completion for their generated actions. Story restart returns to ordinary tutorial behavior. Storage failures retain session behavior and show the usual save warning.

## Debug pane

- One control at the game's top right toggles the pane. Show a subtly shaded bug glyph for the original story, or a pencil on a red square when local edits exist. Game items take precedence where they overlap it.

Synthesized checkpoints do not count as fresh-play evidence.
- Whenever the desktop pane opens, place the caret at the end of the current gameplay chapter's heading and scroll that heading into view, so the editor and chapter dropdown start at the chapter being played. This makes chapter navigation directly available while retaining the author's position when already editing there. This does not reflow carried objects during ordinary play.
- Keep the header compact: no title, story URL, copy/download buttons or routine saved-status indicator. Select and copy text using the editor's normal keyboard commands. Display actual save failures and recovery notices.
- Cmd+F (Ctrl+F on other platforms) opens a compact Find/Replace panel with literal text search, optional **Match case**, previous/next arrows, replacement controls and Close. Omit regexp, whole-word and select-all-matches controls to keep finding text simple. Each change to the Find text immediately selects and scrolls to the first occurrence at or after the current editor selection's start, wrapping at the end. Starting at the current match lets added letters extend that selection when they still match; otherwise search advances to the next occurrence. Keep focus and the input caret in Find while typing. **Replace all** applies the replacement to all occurrences. Show the selected match's position as **36 of 52**. Moving the caret away from a selected match changes this to **? of 52**; previous/next searches from the current editor selection and wraps at either end. Keep counts current after query, case, selection, replacement and text changes. An empty query has no count; a query with no matches shows **0 of 0** and disables match actions. Neither moves the editor selection. Keep source indentation and wrapping, and use tabular digits for aligned gutter line numbers. Color parsed item and hotspot references, including declarations and qualified slot names, blue so authors can distinguish them from surrounding prose and grammar. Keep this color visible without holding a modifier. Render `> NOTE:` author-comment blocks, including their quoted continuation lines, in a muted grey italic font to reduce their prominence while retaining readability. Ordinary quotations retain their Markdown styling. Update these styles as the draft changes; matching words in prose or notes do not become references.
- Hovering an item or hotspot reference shows its name/identifier, authored descriptions and conditional reading requirements, relevant properties, combinations that create it or use it, and goals mentioning it. Descriptions are shown as authored text, without loading their embedded artwork. Hover does not move the caret or change gameplay.

## Persistence and draft recovery

- Save accepted play locally and resume valid progress on reload, including item arrangements, discovery acknowledgments and chapter-restart eligibility. Report save failures visibly without replacing normal play with routine saved-status messages. Tutorial completion follows its [separate reset rules](#contextual-tutorials); a successful game save must not conceal a failed tutorial save.
- A local draft shadows the original manuscript for its URL, including an empty or invalid draft. Save the raw text immediately before parsing, linting or previewing it; these operations must not endanger the author's buffer. Closing the pane, resizing, reloading and restarting a chapter or story keep the draft. Editing is local and nothing uploads automatically.
- Preserve concurrent work from other tabs under **Saved copies**, without replacing the open buffer. Show a notice when another tab saves different text. Each editing session retains its latest saved buffer; offer copies that differ from the open text, including raw recovery data for damaged records. Loading a saved copy first preserves the current buffer and must refuse replacement if that safeguard fails. Keep retained session copies without automatic pruning.
- **Delete draft…** is separate from closing the editor and restarting gameplay, and requires explicit confirmation. Retrieve the original manuscript before deleting the draft; a failed fetch or a draft changed during retrieval cancels deletion and keeps the text. Successful deletion returns to the original manuscript.
- After a failed draft save, keep the text open, selectable and copyable, show the failure and warn before leaving with unsaved work. Export uses the editor's normal select-all and copy commands, without separate copy/download controls. Browser storage is local, not an uploaded backup; saved copies protect editing sessions but do not survive clearing all browser data. The [README](../README.md#edit-a-story-live) gives the recovery and backup instructions.
